## Facts the applicability table could not settle on the message alone

- **Austria and other non-Germany EU member states.** The skill's references state the EU directive floor (Consumer Rights Directive, UCTD, DCD) at the directive level, with Germany worked out as the reference member state in full implementing detail (exact wording, button labels, statutory forms). Austria's own transposition of the Consumer Rights Directive (the Austrian equivalent of the Muster-Widerrufsbelehrung, the Austrian FAGG) is not merged into this skill's references. The subscription-terms and withdrawal-information drafts use the directive-level EU text for Austria and flag the Austrian-specific wording and form as something your counsel needs to verify against the current Austrian FAGG text before publication.
- **Whether California's CCPA applies to Example Fitness GmbH at all.** The statute's thresholds are over $25,000,000 in prior-year gross revenue, or buying/selling/sharing personal information of 100,000 or more California consumers or households, or deriving 50% or more of revenue from selling or sharing personal information (`references/us-states/ca-ccpa.md`). Your stated facts (about EUR 2 million annual revenue company-wide, about 40,000 total users, 3,000 in California) look well under both the revenue and the volume thresholds, but the skill's rules bar estimating a statutory threshold from unit sales, user totals or market-size figures, and "annual revenue about EUR 2 million" is not necessarily the same figure as "gross revenue" under the statute's own definition, nor necessarily in the right base year or currency conversion. This is left undetermined; no CCPA-specific clause was drafted into the delivered documents on the strength of an assumed non-applicability.
- **Whether CalOPPA, Virginia/Colorado/Connecticut/Texas-style privacy statutes, or New York's own (non-auto-renewal) data law reach you.** Out of scope for this delivery (you asked for subscription terms and withdrawal information, not a privacy notice), but flagged here so it is not lost before that document is drafted.
- **Whether the app-store channel (Apple App Store, Google Play) changes who owes the California/New York same-medium-as-consent cancellation duty, or the German cancellation-button duty.** Both `references/us-states/ca-arl.md` and `references/de/bgb-fernabsatz.md` raise this without settling it: where billing runs through a third-party platform rather than the seller's own checkout, it is unresolved whether that platform's own cancellation path can stand in for the statutory duty, or whether Example Fitness GmbH needs its own contractual arrangement with Apple/Google to secure it. This is not something the references resolve in the abstract; flagged as a product and legal question together.
- **Whether a specific jurisdiction's court has tested California's McGill public-injunctive-relief rule, or Illinois BIPA, Washington's MHMDA, or any state beyond California and New York** against this specific business. Out of scope: the subscription document drafted does not include a dispute-resolution or arbitration clause, since your message asked only for subscription terms and withdrawal information plus the flow requirements, not a full terms-of-service with dispute resolution.

## Rules that take effect later than the law-as-of date, or whose outcome is pending

- **BGH ruling on login steps in the German cancellation flow, due 5 November 2026** (I ZR 272/25 and I ZR 275/25). The Kammergericht held that routing the cancellation button through a login (customer number and password), or asking for a password on the confirmation page, breaches § 312k Abs. 2 Satz 3 and 4 BGB. Until the BGH rules, the subscription-terms and implementation-requirements drafts assume **no login step** in the German cancellation flow (beyond the identification data the statute itself lets the flow collect). Recheck this before your checkout ships, since the ruling is due within two weeks of this delivery.
- **BGH ruling of 16 July 2026 (I ZR 200/25)**, already decided and reflected in the drafts: the German cancellation confirmation page may carry no retention offer, pause option or other content beyond the five statutory fields. This is final law as of the law-as-of date, not pending, and is built into both `subscription-terms.md` and `implementation-requirements.md`.
- **16 C.F.R. Part 425 (the federal Negative Option Rule) is in a settled-but-unstable state.** The 2024 amendment that would have extended disclosure/consent/cancellation duties to every negative-option pattern (not just prenotification plans) was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on a procedural ground; the FTC restored the pre-2024 text effective 12 February 2026. A 2026 advance notice of proposed rulemaking is asking whether to bring some or all of the vacated rule back; the comment period closed 13 April 2026 and no decision has been announced. Stridewell Plus (a pure digital subscription, no merchandise shipped) sits outside Part 425's prenotification-plan scope either way, so this mainly affects how confidently you can describe "federal compliance" in any marketing or investor-facing material; ROSCA and FTC Act section 5 remain your actual federal floor, and both are drafted into the documents.
- **FTC's open question on retention offers ("Saves")** before honoring a cancellation request is unresolved at the federal level; the 2021 guidance's narrower single-offer-versus-repeated-pitch standard is what the US module drafts to, not a settled Saves-specific rule.

## Decisions counsel has to make

- **Whether to accept the recommended module split** (one combined subscription-terms document with EU-consumer, Germany and US sub-modules) versus three separate documents. Drafted as one combined document per the stated assumption in open point 8; easy to split later if counsel prefers separate files per market.
- **Whether Stridewell's 7-day free trial should be restructured so no billing information is collected until the trial actually converts**, which would simplify the ROSCA/CA/NY pre-billing timeline considerably (see open point 6). This is a commercial and product decision the drafting cannot make for you.
- **Whether to drop US pricing in USD entirely and bill every market in EUR**, or keep the dual-currency model. Nothing in the references requires one approach over the other, but a dual-currency model raises a question (not resolved here, and not covered by any file in this skill) about whether showing a different net price by detected currency/location could brush against the EU geo-blocking Regulation's non-discrimination rule for electronically supplied services, since Art. 4(1) of Regulation (EU) 2018/302 bars price differentiation tied to a customer's nationality, residence or establishment for that service category. This is flagged, not resolved: the regulation reaches discrimination by customer location, and a single published EUR-or-USD toggle the customer picks themselves is a different fact pattern from geofenced pricing, but the distinction was not tested against your actual checkout design. Treat as a question for counsel before finalising the checkout's currency-selection logic.
- **Whether the German cancellation-button duty and the EU withdrawal-function duty can, as a commercial matter, be secured from Apple and Google by contract** for the in-app-purchase channel, or whether Stridewell needs a parallel compliant path through its own web checkout as the backstop for every EU user regardless of how they originally subscribed.

# Stridewell Plus Subscription Terms

Drafting aid for counsel review. Not legal advice. Law as of 2026-10-20. Seller: Example Fitness GmbH, Hauptstrasse 1, 89073 Ulm, Germany, registered at Amtsgericht Ulm, HRB 000000, represented by managing director Jana Beispiel, support@example-fitness.test ("Example Fitness," "we," "us").

This document is built as one text with labelled modules: a core paragraph that applies to everyone, followed by a module for **EU consumers** (Germany stated separately where its own rule differs from the EU floor) and a module for **US consumers** (California and New York stated separately where their rules differ from each other). Keep every module that reaches a market you actually sell into; do not delete a module just because most of your users will not see it, since which module a given user sees depends on where they are, not on which module is "default."

## 1. What this agreement covers

These terms govern your subscription to Stridewell Plus, the paid tier of the Stridewell mobile application for iOS and Android ("Stridewell," "the app," "the subscription"). Stridewell also offers a free tier that these subscription terms do not govern except where they say so.

*Open point: whether a general terms-of-use document also exists for the free tier and for app use generally, separate from these subscription terms, is not settled by your message; these subscription terms assume a separate general terms-of-use document exists or will exist, and do not duplicate acceptable-use, intellectual-property or dispute-resolution content that belongs there.*

By tapping the button labelled **"Start my 7-day free trial"** (free trial sign-up) or **"zahlungspflichtig bestellen"** (direct paid sign-up without a trial, for German users) or **"Subscribe now"** (direct paid sign-up, other markets), you agree to these Subscription Terms and to our Privacy Policy [link].

We keep a copy of these terms and your order details and make them available to you on request. This agreement, your signature and your order are not denied legal effect only because they are in electronic form.

**EU consumers.** Immediately before the order button, and with nothing else between the two, we show: the technical steps to conclude the contract, whether we file the concluded contract and whether you can access it, the technical means to spot and correct an input error before you submit, and the languages the contract can be concluded in.

**Germany.** The order button for a direct paid sign-up (no trial) carries only **"zahlungspflichtig bestellen"** (PW-DE-1), with nothing else on it. The price, the plan's main characteristics, the initial term (one month) and the renewal mechanism sit directly before that button, highlighted, not behind a link.

*Open point: where the sign-up flow is "start free trial" rather than an immediately paid order, German law's exact-wording order-button duty under § 312j Abs. 3 Satz 2 BGB still applies at the point the trial converts into a paid obligation if a card is charged automatically on day 8: see the Free trial conversion section below. The label "Start my 7-day free trial" is not itself the payment-obligation button; the payment-obligation disclosure sits at trial sign-up only if the user is told, next to that same control, that continuing creates a future payment obligation they must act to avoid. Confirm with counsel that this phrasing clears the Fuhrmann-2 words-only test before it ships.*

**US.** We place this assent sentence on the same screen as the control it names, in a size and color comparably prominent to the surrounding text, directly next to the control, visible without scrolling. Where sign-up spans more than one screen, we repeat the notice-and-control pairing on the screen where the subscription itself actually starts; we do not rely on a notice shown earlier in the flow. We do not use a pre-checked assent box.

*Legal basis: Art. 8(2) CRD; § 312j Abs. 2, 3 BGB; Case C-249/21 Fuhrmann-2; Meyer v. Uber Technologies; Berman v. Freedom Financial Network; Chabolla v. ClassPass.*

## 4. Term, renewal and cancellation

This agreement starts on the date we confirm your order (or, for a free trial, the date your trial period ends and converts to a paid subscription) and continues for one month. Unless you cancel before the end of that month, it renews automatically for successive one-month periods, at the price and on the terms in effect at the time of each renewal, until you cancel or we end it as this section allows.

Before you are charged for the first time, and before each renewal, we show you, or send you, the price you will be charged, how often you will be charged, and how to cancel.

You can cancel at any time. Cancelling stops the next renewal; it does not refund the month that has already started, except as the EU withdrawal right in section 6 below separately allows, or as this section otherwise states.

Stridewell Plus offers a **7-day free trial**. If you do not cancel before the trial ends, it converts automatically into a paid Stridewell Plus subscription at the price stated above, charged to the payment method you provided at sign-up.

Before we collect your payment details, we clearly disclose, in text, next to the control you use to start the trial: that the trial converts to a paid subscription unless you cancel, the price that will be charged, when it will be charged, and how to cancel before that happens. We obtain your express, separate affirmative action confirming you understand this before the trial starts; a consent box used for this is never pre-checked.

**EU consumers.** `references/eu/uctd.md` sets the EU-wide floor: an automatic trial-to-paid conversion needs a reasonable, not unreasonably early, cancellation deadline, and cancelling is at least as easy as signing up. **EU business customers.** Not applicable: Stridewell Plus is sold to consumers only.

**EU consumers.** Where you are a digital-service subscriber, a change is permitted only where this contract itself allows it and states a valid reason, the change carries no extra cost for you, you are told about it clearly, and, where it does more than minimally and negatively affect your access or use, you are told reasonably in advance, on a durable medium, with at least 30 days to terminate free of charge or keep the unmodified version instead.

**Germany.** A clause letting us change these terms, including price, through your silence or continued use, with no substantive limit on what can be changed, is invalid, even where we also offer a matching special termination right.

This notice explains what Shutterline stores on or reads from your device (cookies, local storage, and similar technologies), why, and how to control it. It works alongside our [Privacy Notice].

## 2. EU and UK users: how our consent banner works

Before we store or read anything on your device for a purpose that is not strictly necessary, we ask for your consent. Our banner:

- names each purpose separately, with its own on/off control that starts off;
- gives you a **reject** option on the same screen as **accept**, equally prominent;
- lets you keep using Shutterline without accepting anything beyond what is strictly necessary;
- names [any named third party] individually if we use one, not just "partners" in general;
- tells you whether data is processed outside the EEA (see our [Privacy Notice], Section 6); and
- lets you change your mind at any time from **[Settings → Privacy → Cookie settings]**, just as easily as you gave consent in the first place.

We do not label a cookie "essential" or "strictly necessary" unless it meets that standard, and we keep a current list able to justify each label on request.

### Germany

At the point we ask for consent, we also name: whether we build and enrich an individual profile of you using data from other sites, whether any data is processed outside the EEA, and how many parties your data is disclosed to, if any. Where one button grants both this storage consent and a related data-processing consent under the GDPR, we tell you this clearly at the same point, rather than only on a deeper screen.

## 3. United States: how opt-out works

For a US user, we honour an opt-out of "sale" or "sharing" of personal information for cross-context behavioural advertising, to the extent our personalised-advertising category above falls within that definition. You can opt out by [link to opt-out mechanism], or, where you use a recognised opt-out preference signal in your browser, we honour that signal automatically.

**[Open point: whether the personalised-advertising category as actually built counts as a "sale" or "share" of personal information under the CCPA's own definitions turns on the mechanics confirmed in the open point above (item 6) and on the CCPA threshold question (item 4); this section is drafted to offer an opt-out regardless of that determination, as the more conservative approach, but the exact regulatory label (sale, share, or neither) should be confirmed with counsel.]**

---

## 4. Changing your choice later

You can change your cookie choices at any time from **Settings → Privacy → Cookie settings**, or the link in the footer of every page. Changing your choice takes effect immediately and takes the same number of steps as giving consent in the first place.

# Photo community with user content

## The request

```text
We are Lumenfolk Media GmbH, Spichernstrasse 12, 50672 Koeln, Germany, legal@lumenfolk.test, managing director
Theresa Brandt. We run Shutterline, a free online community for hobby photographers: people upload their own
photos, comment on other people's photos, and send each other direct messages. We do not charge users
anything; the business is funded entirely by advertising, both plain contextual ads next to the photo feed and
ads personalised to what a user has looked at and liked. About 2 million monthly active users in the EU,
almost all of them in Germany, France and Spain, and about 1.5 million monthly active users in the United
States. We have 120 employees and about EUR 20 million in annual turnover. Minimum age to create an account is
16, checked by self-declared birth date at signup. Photos, comments, direct messages and profile data are
stored on our own servers in the EU. We want the terms of service, the content and moderation policy covering
what happens when someone reports a photo or a comment as infringing or against the rules, the notice and
complaint flows a user or a rights holder can use, and a clear list of what our product (the upload flow, the
report button, the complaint and appeal screens, the ad labelling) actually has to implement. Our outside
counsel reviews everything before launch.
```

## What the skill asked back

1. Which EU member states beyond Germany, France and Spain, if any, Shutterline actually sells into or directs marketing to.
2. Whether Shutterline's turnover or headcount has changed, and whether it qualifies as a micro or small enterprise under the EU recommendation that sets those categories.
3. How personalised advertising is actually built: from on-platform activity only, or also from cross-site tracking, app SDK data, or bought third-party data.
4. Whether Shutterline processes payment data, runs a mobile app with its own SDKs, or stores data outside the EU.
5. Whether Shutterline has crossed the CCPA's own revenue or data-volume thresholds.
6. What the actual age-verification mechanism is beyond self-declared birth date, and whether any feature makes the product attractive to, or predominantly used by, under-16s.
7. Who the actual contact points are for DSA authorities and users, and whether there is a named data protection officer.
8. Whether Shutterline wants a US arbitration and class-action-waiver clause.

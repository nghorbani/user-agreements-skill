A change does not apply to a dispute that already exists between you and us before the change takes effect. We will not ask you to accept a change through silence or continued use alone where the change affects a right you already have; where the law lets us treat continued use as acceptance, we will still tell you clearly, before the change takes effect, what continuing to use Shutterline after that date means, and what you can do instead. If you do not agree to a change, your option is to stop using Shutterline and delete your account before the change takes effect.

### EU users

A term letting us change these terms without a valid, contract-stated reason is itself treated as unfair and void against you; so is a term letting us change a characteristic of Shutterline without a valid reason, however that term is framed (Annex points (j), (k) Directive 93/13/EEC). We state the reason and the method for a change in these terms themselves, not only in the notice we send when a change happens.

This clause relies on the carve-out that lets any business reserve the right to alter an indefinite-duration contract's terms unilaterally, provided you get reasonable notice and stay free to leave; it still needs a valid, contract-stated reason for a change falling outside that carve-out, and for any change to a characteristic of Shutterline (Annex point 2(b), second sentence, Directive 93/13/EEC).

### Germany

A clause letting us change these terms through your silence or continued use, with no substantive limit on what can change, unreasonably disadvantages you and is invalid, even where a separate statute also permits a deemed-consent mechanism for that kind of change (BGH, judgment of 27 April 2021, XI ZR 26/20). This is why the wording above rests on actual notice and your own choice to continue or leave.

### United States

No change to these terms applies to a dispute that has already accrued before the change takes effect, and no change binds a person who has only browsed Shutterline without creating an account to a changed arbitration or forum term (9 U.S.C. §§ 401, 402; Heckman v. Live Nation Entertainment, Inc., 120 F.4th 670 (9th Cir. 2024)). We do not promise that a change takes effect only on explicit re-acceptance and then apply it through mere continued use instead.

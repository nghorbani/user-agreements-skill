# Acceptable use policy

Law as of: 2026-10-20

A fill-in template for the clause that states what a user agrees not to do with the service, and the separate
clause that states what happens when a user breaks that rule: how a notice of illegal content is handled, what
statement of reasons the user gets, how an internal complaint runs, and how a repeat-abuse suspension policy
works. Keep the two as separate sections even inside one terms document: the conduct rule and the enforcement
process answer different questions and carry different duties.

## How to fill this in

- Draft one prohibited-conduct list for every market, in plain language concrete enough to meet the EU clarity
  duty, rather than a separate US list and EU list (Art. 14(1) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).
- The EU modules in both sections apply regardless of whether the recipient is a consumer or a business, since
  the Digital Services Act's recipient-facing duties are not limited by the recipient's status (Art. 3(b)
  Regulation (EU) 2022/2065). Add the EU business customers module in the enforcement section only where the
  recipient is also a business user of an online intermediation service under the P2B Regulation.
- Check whether the service is an online platform under Art. 3(i) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, given the
  minor-ancillary-feature carve-out in that definition, and whether it is a micro or small enterprise: both
  decide whether the fuller internal-complaint, out-of-court-settlement and trusted-flagger duties (Art. 20 to
  24) apply at all, or whether only the Art. 14 to 18 notice-and-statement-of-reasons duties apply. A hosting
  service that is not an online platform drops the Art. 20 to 24 module; a micro or small enterprise drops it
  too. Treat the Art. 14(5), (6), 24(2) to (4) and Art. 33 onward duties for a very large online platform or
  search engine as an open point for counsel; this template does not draft them.
- Add the CAN-SPAM paragraph in the conduct section only where the service itself is a channel for sending
  email: an email marketing feature, an invite-a-friend function, or a forwarding or sharing tool. A service
  with no such feature leaves that paragraph out; the Section 230 and Digital Services Act modules still apply
  on their own.
- Where the provider is a US service, add the parental-control notice in the onboarding flow itself at the point
  a customer enters an agreement for the service, in addition to this document (47 U.S.C. § 230(d)).
- Confirm every bracketed reference to another clause (the licence and publisher-immunity clause, the cancellation
  clause, the dispute-resolution clause) points to the document's actual section numbering before publishing.
- For a German-facing agreement, run the prohibited-conduct list and the enforcement wording through the German
  standard-terms-control check (§§ 305c, 307 BGB; `references/checklists/de-agb.md`) before use; this template
  does not run that check itself.
- Check every citation in this template against the cut legal text before relying on it; the citations above were
  checked at the time this template was written.

## Section 1: acceptable use

### Core wording (every market)

[User name] agrees not to use [the service] to post, upload, transmit or otherwise make available any content
that is illegal, or to use [the service] for any purpose that is illegal. [User name] agrees not to attempt to
circumvent a restriction [trader name] has placed on the use of [the service], not to probe, scan or test the
vulnerability of [the service] or any system connected to it, and not to access or use [the service] by any
automated means (scraping, crawling or similar) except to the extent [trader name] has made a documented
interface available for that purpose.

[Trader name] states this policy, with examples of what counts as a breach, in these terms so that [user name]
can read it before using [the service]; see [the enforcement section below] for how a reported or detected
breach is reviewed and what happens to the account.

### Module: commercial email features (include only where the service is a channel for sending email)

[User name] agrees not to use [the service] to send, or to help send, an unsolicited commercial electronic
message that misstates who it is from, carries a subject line [user name] has reason to think would mislead a
reasonable recipient, or lacks a working way for the recipient to ask not to receive further messages (15
U.S.C. § 7704(a)(1), (a)(2), (a)(4)(A)(i)). [User name] agrees not to use an automated means to register for
multiple accounts in order to send messages that would otherwise break this paragraph, and not to relay such a
message through a system [user name] does not have authorization to use (15 U.S.C. § 7704(b)(2), (b)(3)). A
reference to a commercial entity, or a link to its website, inside a message does not by itself make the
message a commercial electronic mail message under this paragraph; the test looks at the message's contents
and circumstances as a whole (15 U.S.C. § 7702(2)(D)).

### EU module

State the restrictions on use of the service, in clear, plain, intelligible, user-friendly and unambiguous
language, in the terms and conditions themselves, publicly available in an easily accessible, machine-readable
format (Art. 14(1) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065). Tell users of a significant change to this clause once it is live
(Art. 14(2) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065). Where the service is primarily directed at, or predominantly used by,
minors, write the restrictions so a minor can understand them (Art. 14(3) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).

Apply and enforce this clause diligently, objectively and proportionately, weighing the rights and legitimate
interests of everyone affected, including a user's freedom of expression (Art. 14(4) Regulation (EU)
2022/2065). Do not draft the clause, or apply it in practice, as a licence to screen content before it is
posted or to actively hunt for breaches: no general obligation to monitor the information transmitted or
stored, or to seek out facts or circumstances indicating illegal activity, applies to an intermediary service
(Art. 8 Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).

Where [trader name] operates an online platform and suspends a recipient for repeatedly breaching this clause
with manifestly illegal content, that policy, its examples and its duration sit in [the enforcement section
below], not here (Art. 23(1), (4) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).

### US module

[Trader name] may restrict access to or availability of material it considers, in good faith, to be obscene,
lewd, lascivious, filthy, excessively violent, harassing or otherwise objectionable, whether or not that
material is constitutionally protected, and may give [user name] the technical means to restrict such material
too; this is a right [trader name] holds at its discretion, not a promised or guaranteed screening service (47
U.S.C. § 230(c)(2)(A), (c)(2)(B)).

### Must not

- Do not state or imply that this policy, or the Digital Services Act, requires or authorizes blanket
  pre-screening or general monitoring of what users post (Art. 8 Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).
- Do not promise guaranteed or complete screening of content against this policy before it is posted (47 U.S.C.
  § 230(c)(2)(A)).
- Do not draft or enforce the commercial-email restriction in a way that would itself require materially false
  header information, a misleading subject line, or ignoring an opt-out request inside the 10 business day
  window; the restriction is on the user, not an excuse for [trader name]'s own obligations where [trader name]
  itself initiates such messages (15 U.S.C. § 7704(a)(1), (a)(2), (a)(4)(A)(i)).

## Section 2: enforcement (moderation, notices and suspension)

### Core wording (every market)

We set out, in plain language, the restrictions we place on using [the service] in connection with content you
or another user provides, and the policies, tools and procedures we use to apply them, including whether a
decision is made by a person, by an automated process, or both. We will tell you if we make a significant
change to this clause.

We may remove, restrict the visibility of, or disable access to content, or restrict, suspend or terminate your
access to [the service] or your account, where we reasonably consider the content or your use of [the service]
is illegal or breaches these terms. Where we do, and we have a way to reach you, we will give you a statement of
reasons covering what we decided, why, whether a notice or our own review led to it, and how to challenge it,
except where the law does not require this or a narrower rule in a module below applies.

If you disagree with a decision we made about your content or your account, you can raise an internal complaint
through [the complaint channel]; we will not decide it solely by automated means, and we will reverse our
decision where your complaint shows we had it wrong. [EU module: add the out-of-court settlement paragraph for
a recipient located in the EU.] [Business-user module: add the P2B notice-period, reinstatement and
complaint-system paragraphs for a business user of an intermediation service.]

Anyone can tell us about content they believe is illegal through [the notice channel]; we will confirm we
received a notice that identifies the content and explains why it is said to be illegal, and tell the notifier
what we decided.

Where you, or an entity submitting notices or complaints, do this so often, and so far out of proportion to
genuine notices or complaints, that it becomes clearly abusive, we may warn you first and then suspend, for a
period that fits the pattern, our handling of what you send us or your access to [the service]; [worked
examples of what we treat this way and how long a suspension of this kind typically lasts, filled in below].

### EU consumers module (hosting service / online platform)

Build the notice-and-action channel to be easy to access, user-friendly, usable only by electronic means, and
able to take a notice that gives a substantiated explanation of why the content is said to be illegal, the
content's exact location, the notifier's name and email address (except for the narrow offence categories the
file exempts), and a good-faith, accurate-and-complete statement (Art. 16(1), (2) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).
Confirm receipt without undue delay where the notifier gave contact details, and tell the notifier the decision
taken, with redress information, also without undue delay (Art. 16(4), (5) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065). Say in
that notification whether the decision used automated means (Art. 16(6) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065). A notice
meeting all four of those elements gives us actual knowledge of the content for liability purposes, so route it
to a timely, diligent decision rather than a generic queue (Art. 16(3) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).

Give a statement of reasons to a recipient affected by a visibility restriction, a payment restriction, or a
suspension, termination or restriction of the service or the account, whether the restriction started from a
notice or from the provider's own review, from the moment it takes effect and wherever the provider holds the
relevant contact details (Art. 17(1), (2) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065). Cover in it: what was decided, its scope
and duration; the facts relied on and whether they came from a notice or an own-initiative review; whether
automated means were used; the legal or contractual ground and why the content or conduct fails it; and the
redress routes, written clearly enough for the recipient to actually use them (Art. 17(3), (4) Regulation (EU)
2022/2065).

[Insert only where the service is an online platform, not a micro or small enterprise, and not merely a hosting
service: Art. 20 to 24 go beyond the Art. 14 to 18 duties above.]

Keep the internal complaint system open for at least six months from the notification, let a complaint be
lodged electronically and free of charge against any of the Art. 20(1) decisions, decide every complaint in a
timely, non-discriminatory, diligent, non-arbitrary way, reverse the decision without undue delay once the
complaint shows sufficient grounds, and never decide it solely by automated means (Art. 20(1) to (4), (6)
Regulation (EU) 2022/2065). Tell the complainant the reasoned outcome and the out-of-court settlement route
without undue delay (Art. 20(5) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).

You can bring an unresolved complaint to any certified out-of-court settlement body; we engage with it in good
faith, and will refuse only where the same dispute has already been settled this way. Going to court stays open
to you at every stage. If the body decides in your favor, we pay all its fees and your other reasonable
expenses; if it decides against you, you owe nothing for our costs unless you clearly acted in bad faith (Art.
21(1), (2), (5) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).

[Fill in the misuse-suspension worked examples here: the facts and circumstances considered, and the duration
each triggers, with the prior warning given before any such suspension (Art. 23(1) to (4) Regulation (EU)
2022/2065).] [Where the provider gives priority handling to a trusted flagger's notices, state that the
priority runs only within the flagger's designated area of expertise and that a quality problem is reported to
the awarding Digital Services Coordinator rather than resolved by [trader name] unilaterally de-prioritising the
flagger (Art. 22(1), (6), (7) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).]

### EU business customers module (business user of an online intermediation service)

[Insert where the recipient is a business user of an online intermediation service under the P2B Regulation.]

We give you a reasonable, proportionate notice period of at least 15 days (longer where you need more time to
adapt) before a terms change takes effect, and a right to terminate within that period or to waive it by a
clear statement or action (Art. 3(2) Regulation (EU) 2019/1150). [This 15-day minimum does not apply where we
are subject to a legal or regulatory obligation requiring a terms change on a shorter timeline, or must
exceptionally change the terms to address an unforeseen and imminent cybersecurity danger such as fraud,

We give you a statement of reasons on a durable medium no later than when a restriction or suspension of
individual goods or services takes effect, and at least 30 days before a whole-service termination, except for
[the three narrow grounds the Regulation itself lists], each of which still gets a statement without undue

### US module

We are not treated as the publisher or speaker of information you or another user or information content
provider supplies, and reviewing, filtering, labeling, restricting or removing it does not change that (47
U.S.C. § 230(c)(1)). [This sentence covers only content we did not ourselves create or develop in whole or in
part (47 U.S.C. § 230(f)(3)).]

We may restrict access to material we consider, in good faith, to be obscene, lewd, excessively violent,
harassing or otherwise objectionable, as a voluntary exercise of discretion, not a promised or guaranteed review
of every item; where we give you or another information content provider the technical means to restrict such
material yourself, such as blocking, filtering or personal moderation settings, that is the same kind of
voluntary, good-faith action (47 U.S.C. § 230(c)(2)(A), (c)(2)(B)).

limit a minor's access to material that is harmful to minors; current providers of such protections are [listed

Nothing in this section limits the enforcement of federal criminal law, including the obscenity and
child-exploitation statutes, or any other federal criminal law; nothing here expands or limits

similar state law (47 U.S.C. § 230(e)(1), (2), (4)). [Neither the publisher/speaker immunity above nor the

### Must not

- Do not claim the Section 230 publisher, speaker or good-faith-moderation immunity for content [trader name]
  itself creates or develops, in whole or in part (47 U.S.C. § 230(f)(3)).
- Do not promise full or guaranteed review of user content (47 U.S.C. § 230(f)(3)).
- Do not state or imply that Section 230 displaces a state or local law consistent with it, or that it limits
  federal criminal law, intellectual-property law, or the Electronic Communications Privacy Act (47 U.S.C. §
  230(e)(1), (2), (3), (4)).
- Do not claim the Section 230(c)(1) or (c)(2)(B) immunity as a defense to a civil sex-trafficking claim tied to
  18 U.S.C. § 1591, or to the matching state criminal prosecutions (47 U.S.C. § 230(e)(5)(A) to (C)).
- Do not suggest that EU law requires, or that [trader name] will run, blanket pre-screening or general
  monitoring of content (Art. 8 Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).
- Do not narrow the statement-of-reasons duty to notice-triggered restrictions only; it runs equally to an
  own-initiative restriction, with only two carve-outs: deceptive high-volume commercial content, and an
  authority order (Art. 17(2), (5) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).
- Do not shorten the six-month minimum internal-complaint access window, treat a sufficiently-grounded reversal
  as discretionary, or decide a complaint solely by automated means (Art. 20(1), (2), (4), (6) Regulation (EU)
  2022/2065).
- Do not describe the misuse-suspension policy only in generic terms, with no concrete examples or stated
  duration, and do not skip the prior warning or the case-by-case assessment for a suspension of this kind (Art.
  23(1) to (4) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).
- Do not let a terms change, restriction, suspension or termination of a business user's account proceed without
  the Art. 3(2) notice period or the Art. 4 statement of reasons it is owed, except the narrow grounds that
  Regulation itself states, each still needing a statement without undue delay (Art. 3(2), (3); Art. 4(1), (2),
  (4), (5) Regulation (EU) 2019/1150).
- Do not claim a trusted flagger's notices keep priority handling once the flagger's status is suspended or
  revoked, and do not unilaterally de-prioritise a flagger instead of reporting a quality problem to the
  awarding Digital Services Coordinator (Art. 22(1), (6), (7) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).

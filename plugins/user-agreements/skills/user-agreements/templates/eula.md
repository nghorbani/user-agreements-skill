# EULA / software and SaaS terms

Law as of: 2026-10-20

A fill-in skeleton for a software licence document: a shrink-wrap or click-wrap EULA, a SaaS or cloud
terms-of-service document that licenses rather than sells access, or a mixed goods-plus-software
product. It assembles the sign-up, licence, content, conduct, data, AI-feature and back-of-document
clauses this kind of document needs into one ordered skeleton; fill in the bracketed placeholders and
keep only the modules (EU consumers, EU business customers, Germany, US) the actual product and
audience need. Each section points to the clause file it was built from, so a change to the underlying
rule is made there and carried into this template on the next assembly, not patched here alone.

## How to fill this in

- Classify the transaction before drafting anything else: licence or sale (a one-off fee paired with
  an unlimited-duration right to use the copy is a sale under EU law whatever the document calls it,
  and exhausts the EU distribution right for that copy); goods, digital content or digital service;
  hosted access with no copy transferred, or a copy the end user actually receives; and consumer or
  commercial end user. Each of these drives a different module below; see the "Before drafting:
  classify the product and the transaction" section of `references/checklists/eula.md` for the full
  list and its citations.
- Work through the sections in order. Each one names the clause file it is drawn from; open that file
  for the model wording's full modules, its own list of what a clause must not do, and its legal
  basis before filling in the brackets, since this template gives the assembled skeleton, not a
  substitute for the clause file itself.
- Keep every jurisdiction module the audience needs and delete the rest; do not blend an EU consumer
  module and a US module into one paragraph, since the two routinely reach opposite results from a
  similar-looking clause (see each section's own drafting notes for where this matters most: the
  licence grant's sale-versus-licence classification, the change-to-terms silence mechanism, and the
  liability cap's floor).
- If this document also needs a software-specific conformity, repair, or connected-product-data
  section beyond what is included here, build it from `references/eu/software-directive.md`,
  `references/de/urhg-software.md`, `references/eu/repair.md`, and the warranty-and-conformity and
  connected-product-data clause files directly; this template already includes both.
- Run `references/checklists/eula.md` against the finished document before using it: the checklist
  states the classification questions, the user-exception checks, and the Open points this template
  does not resolve on its own.
- Where the product also collects data through cookies or similar device storage, add that notice from
  `references/clauses/marketing-communications.md` and the dedicated cookie-notice template; this
  document's own privacy-and-data and connected-product-data sections do not cover device-storage
  consent.

## 1. Acceptance

See `references/clauses/acceptance-and-formation.md` for the full clause, its modules and legal basis.

By [clicking the button labeled "[exact button label]" / continuing to [the action that completes
sign-up or checkout]], you agree to these Terms of Service and to our Privacy Policy.

[Link to the full terms, placed immediately next to this sentence and the control it names, in a
color and weight that contrasts with the surrounding text.]

Before you complete your order, you can review and change the items in your cart, your delivery
address and your payment method. Once you submit the order, we will send you an electronic
confirmation to the email address you provided.

We will keep a copy of these terms and your order details and make them available to you on request.
This agreement, your signature and your order are not denied legal effect only because they are in
electronic form.

**EU consumers:** add, immediately before the order button and with nothing else between the two, the
four-point Art. 10(1)(a) to (d) Directive 2000/31/EC disclosure (the technical steps to conclude the
contract, whether the business files the contract and whether the consumer can access it, the
technical means to spot and correct an input error, and the languages available), paired with a
working, not merely described, correction step. Keep the full terms storable and reproducible in
every channel, including an email-only channel, and send an electronic order acknowledgment without
undue delay.

**EU business customers:** keep the model wording's assent sentence and confirmation mechanics unless
the two parties have actually agreed otherwise and neither is a consumer; a one-sided "this is a
business" label does not switch the duties off by itself.

**Germany:** make the order button, or any other control that places a paid order, carry wording that,
read on its own with nothing else on the page supplying meaning, tells an average reasonably well
informed and observant consumer that activating it creates a payment obligation; use PW-DE-1
("zahlungspflichtig bestellen") or an equally unambiguous substitute (§ 312j Abs. 3 Satz 2 BGB).
Immediately before the order button, in a highlighted manner and not behind a link, give the EGBGB
Art. 246a § 1 Abs. 1 Satz 1 Nr. 1, 5 to 8, 11a, 14 and 15 information. Send a confirmation on a
durable medium within a reasonable time, at the latest on delivery or before the service starts.

**US:** pair the assent sentence with whichever control actually completes the step, name that
control by its real, current label, and place the sentence on the same screen, visible without
scrolling, with no unrelated question or control between the sentence and the button. Do not rely on
a pre-checked box. Do not send the notice only after sign-up is already complete.

## 2. Changes to these terms

See `references/clauses/changes-to-terms.md` for the full clause, its modules and legal basis.

The business may update these terms from time to time. Where a change is more than a minor
clarification, correction of an error, or adjustment required to keep the service lawful, safe or
secure, the business will tell the user about it in advance, state the reason for the change and when
it takes effect, and give the user a reasonable period before the change applies. [EU consumers and
digital-content module] Where the change is more than a minor negative effect on the user's access to
or use of the service, the user may end the contract free of charge within the notice period
described above. A change does not apply to a dispute that already exists before the change takes
effect. The business will not ask the user to accept a change through silence or continued use alone
where the change affects a right the user already has or a payment the user already makes.

**EU consumers:** state a valid reason and the method for any change in the terms themselves; a change
clause with no stated reason and no stated method is exposed as unfair under Annex points (j) and (k)
UCTD even where a notice and exit right are offered case by case. For digital content or a digital
service supplied over a period, add the Art. 19 DCD four-point test (contract-stated reason, no extra
cost, clear notice, and, where the negative effect is more than minor, advance durable-medium notice
with a free termination right of at least 30 days or a genuine unmodified option); none of this applies
to a change required for conformity, such as a security update.

**Germany:** do not let a change clause, including a price or fee change for a continuing obligation,
rest on the customer's silence or continued use with no substantive cap on what can change; this fails
even where a separate statute permits deemed consent and a matching termination right is offered, since
the two are not separable parts of one clause (BGH, judgment of 27 April 2021, XI ZR 26/20).

**US:** no change clause may apply to a dispute that already accrued, or bind a mere site visitor who
never transacted, and no clause may promise one acceptance mechanism while an incorporated document
applies the change through continued use alone (Heckman v. Live Nation Entertainment, Inc., 120 F.4th
670 (9th Cir. 2024)).

## 3. Licence grant

See `references/clauses/licence-grant.md` for the full clause, its modules and legal basis.

[Trader name] grants [licensee name] a [personal, non-exclusive, non-transferable] right to install
and run [the software] on [licensee name]'s own devices, for [licensee name]'s own use, for as long as
this contract runs. [Trader name] does not transfer ownership of [the software] or any copy of it;
[trader name] keeps every right this clause does not expressly grant.

[Licensee name] may make one copy of [the software] where that copy is needed to install or run it, or
to keep as a backup, and may correct an error in [the software] where that is needed to use it for its
intended purpose. [Licensee name] may observe, study, or test how [the software] works to work out the
ideas and principles behind any part of it, through acts [licensee name] is otherwise entitled to
carry out.

[Licensee name] must not copy, adapt, translate, or create a derivative work from [the software] beyond
what this clause allows; must not distribute, rent, lease, sublicense, or make [the software] available
to a third party; and must not reverse-engineer, decompile, or disassemble [the software], except
where [licensee name] needs specific interoperability information not already available, and asked
[trader name] for it first. Information obtained this way may be used only for that interoperability
purpose and must not be used to build a competing program.

[Where the software is supplied under a one-off fee for an unlimited period: once [licensee name] has
paid in full, [licensee name] may transfer this licence as a whole to another person, provided
[licensee name] stops using [the software] and deletes or destroys every copy, including any merged
update, at the time of transfer.]

**EU consumers and EU business customers:** state the three restricted acts together (Art. 4(1)(a) to
(c) Directive 2009/24/EC: reproduction including incidental reproduction; translation, adaptation and
alteration; distribution including rental) and keep the three user-side exceptions open: a back-up
copy (Art. 5(2)); observing, studying or testing (Art. 5(3)); and decompilation for interoperability on
the three stated conditions (Art. 6(1) to (3)). Where the arrangement combines a one-off fee with a
permanent, unlimited-duration right to use the copy, this is a sale for EU distribution-right purposes
regardless of the licence's own label, the right exhausts within the Union, and a non-transferable
label does not block resale (Case C-128/11, UsedSoft GmbH v Oracle International Corp.); add the
transfer paragraph above for that arrangement and require the transferring party to render its own
copy unusable at the time of transfer. Drop the transfer paragraph where the arrangement is genuinely
time-limited or charges a recurring fee. Where the licence is a group or volume licence, it may be
resold as a whole, or in whole surplus units, but the licensee cannot divide it below the contracted
minimum unit and resell part of it (Case C-128/11, paras. 69 to 71, 86).

**Germany:** track the same exceptions under UrhG's own numbering (§ 69c Nr. 1 to 4, § 69d Abs. 2, 3,
§ 69e UrhG); a contract term in conflict with any of them is void, not merely unenforceable against the
licensee who invokes it (§ 69g Abs. 2 UrhG). State who holds the economic rights where the software is
employee or contractor work product (§ 69b Abs. 1, 2 UrhG).

**US:** state plainly that the software is licensed, not sold, where that recital is meant to do real
work; the US copy-owner exception under 17 U.S.C. § 117 is available only to a copy owner, not a
licensee as such, and a copy or adaptation made under it may be transferred only with the original copy
and only as part of a transfer of all rights.

## 4. Term, renewal and cancellation

See `references/clauses/term-renewal-and-cancellation.md` for the full clause, its modules and legal
basis.

This agreement starts on the date [trader name] confirms your order and continues for [initial term].
Unless you cancel before the end of that term, it then renews automatically for successive [renewal
term] periods, at the price and on the terms in effect at the time of each renewal, until you cancel or
we end it as this clause allows.

Before you are charged for the first time, and before each renewal, we will show you, or send you, the
price you will be charged, how often you will be charged, and how to cancel. You can cancel at any
time. Cancelling stops the next renewal; it does not refund a period that has already started unless
this agreement or the law says otherwise.

**EU consumers:** the EU-wide floor is a reasonable, not unreasonably early, objection deadline before
automatic renewal (UCTD Annex point (h)) and cancellation as easy as sign-up; treat the gap between
that floor and the Germany module's specific term lengths as an open point outside Germany.

**Germany:** where the terms are preformulated AGB, state the renewal mechanism so it extends the
contract onto an indefinite term and gives the customer a right to end the renewed contract at any time
on at most one month's notice (§ 309 Nr. 9 Buchst. b BGB); where the contract instead binds the
customer to an initial term, keep it at two years or less and any pre-end notice period at one month or
less; a contract for goods customarily sold together, and an insurance contract, fall outside this
whole paragraph. Where [trader name] lets customers conclude the continuing paid contract through a website, add
the cancellation-button paragraph: to cancel, the customer uses the cancellation button, labelled
PW-DE-2 ("Verträge hier kündigen"), permanently available and easily accessible, which takes the
customer to a confirmation page labelled PW-DE-3 ("jetzt kündigen"). That page asks only for: the
cancellation type and, for an extraordinary cancellation, the reason; data identifying the customer;
data identifying the contract; an optional end date the customer is never required to fill in; and an
electronic channel for the confirmation. [Trader name] confirms receipt immediately, electronically,
in text form, stating the content, the time stamp, and the resulting end date.

This duty binds [trader name] even where a third-party platform runs the website the contract is
concluded through (§ 312k BGB). A missing or non-compliant button or confirmation page lets the
customer cancel at any time without any notice period (§ 312k Abs. 6 BGB).

**US:** before taking billing information for any auto-renewing or continuous-service plan, disclose,
in visual proximity to the control that records consent: that the plan continues until cancelled, the
cancellation mechanism, the charge and whether and how it may change, the renewal term length, and any
minimum-purchase obligation (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17601(a)(2)(A) to (E); N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law
§ 527-a(1)(a)). Obtain affirmative consent before the first charge, send a retainable acknowledgment of
the offer terms and the cancellation policy, and describe a cancellation mechanism reachable through at
least the same medium the customer used to consent.

## 5. Price and payment

See `references/clauses/price-and-payment.md` for the full clause, its modules and legal basis.

The total price for [product or service] is [price], including all taxes. [Where the contract is a
subscription: this is the price for each [billing period].]

We accept payment by [payment methods]. [Where any method carries a fee: using [payment method] costs
an extra [amount], which reflects what it costs us to accept it.] [Germany: a customary, reasonable,
free payment option must remain available alongside any surcharged method.]

We will not charge you anything beyond the price above unless you have separately and expressly agreed
to it. We do not treat your failure to uncheck a box, or your silence, as that agreement.

**EU consumers:** state the total price inclusive of all taxes, or the method used to work it out, and
every extra freight, delivery or postal charge, before the consumer is bound (Art. 6(1)(e) Directive
2011/83/EU). Where the price was set by automated decision-making based on the consumer's behaviour,
disclose that fact. Do not charge a payment-method fee above what accepting that method actually costs
the trader (Art. 19 Directive 2011/83/EU), and seek the consumer's express, separate agreement to any
payment beyond the agreed price; a pre-ticked option the consumer has to reject is never that agreement
(Art. 22 Directive 2011/83/EU).

**Germany:** a payment-method surcharge clause is void where a customary, reasonable, free payment
option exists alongside the surcharged one, or where the fee exceeds the trader's own cost of that
method (§ 312a Abs. 4 BGB). State the price and payment information directly before the order-submission
button, not behind a separate terms link (§ 312j Abs. 2, 3 BGB).

## 6. Withdrawal (EU and Germany)

See `references/clauses/withdrawal-eu.md` for the full clause, its modules and legal basis. Add this
section only for a distance or off-premises contract with an EU consumer; drop it for a business
customer and for a US-only document, since no comparable right exists there under these references.

[Trader name] gives the consumer 14 days to withdraw from this contract, without giving any reason and
without any cost beyond what this clause states. The withdrawal period runs for 14 days from [the day
the consumer takes physical possession of the goods / the day this contract is concluded], depending on
what the consumer bought.

To withdraw, the consumer tells [trader name] of the decision to withdraw by a clear statement, for
example a letter sent by post, a fax, or an email to [contact details]. The consumer may use the
withdrawal form at [link], but does not have to.

If the consumer withdraws, [trader name] refunds every payment received, including the standard
delivery cost, within 14 days of being told of the withdrawal, using the same payment method the
consumer used, charging no fee.

[For digital content not supplied on a tangible medium, or a digital service: if the consumer asked for
performance to start before the withdrawal period ends, the consumer pays a share of the agreed price
for what was supplied up to the point of withdrawal, owing nothing where [trader name] did not get the
consumer's express request and prior acknowledgment for the early start, or did not give the
information on the withdrawal right this clause describes.]

**Germany:** where the trader concludes the contract through an online interface, add the two
labelled controls for the online withdrawal function, worded exactly as PW-DE-4 ("Vertrag widerrufen")
and PW-DE-5 ("Widerruf bestätigen") give them, permanently available and distinguishable from ordinary
footer links (§ 356a Abs. 1 to 3 BGB). Use the complete Muster-Widerrufsbelehrung (PW-DE-6) as the
statutory safe harbor for the withdrawal-information duty.

Name every withdrawal exception this contract actually relies on, drawn only from the Art. 16 Directive
2011/83/EU closed list (goods made to specification, sealed goods unsealed, digital content whose
supply already started with express consent, and the rest), stated by its own facts rather than its
label alone.

## 7. Conformity and warranty

See `references/clauses/warranty-and-conformity.md` for the full clause, its modules and legal basis.

[Trader name] supplies [the goods / the digital content / the digital service] so that it matches what
this contract describes and promises, and, on top of that, has the quantity, quality, and performance a
consumer may reasonably expect for a product of this kind, including its usual functionality,
compatibility, durability, and security.

[For goods with digital elements or continuously supplied content: [trader name] keeps [the product]
updated, including with security updates, for [the contract's stated period / the period a consumer may
reasonably expect]. [Trader name] tells the consumer when an update is available and what happens if
it is not installed.]

If [the product] does not match what this clause promises, the consumer can ask [trader name] to fix
it. [For goods: the consumer chooses between repair and replacement; trader name may decline the
consumer's choice only where it is impossible or clearly disproportionate, and must then offer the
other option instead.] The fix is free of charge, happens within a reasonable time, and causes the
consumer no significant inconvenience. If the fix does not happen in time, fails, or [trader name]
refuses or will clearly not do it, the consumer can ask instead for a price reduction, or can end the
contract unless the problem is only minor.

**EU consumers:** state both conformity layers together, the contract's own promise and the objective
floor a consumer may reasonably expect, never presenting the first as if it replaces the second (Art. 6
to 9 Directive (EU) 2019/770; Art. 5 to 9 Directive (EU) 2019/771). State the liability period as at
least two years from delivery or supply, extended to the whole supply period for continuous supply if
that runs longer; for a contract concluded on or after 31 July 2026, disclose that choosing repair
extends the period once by 12 months. Name any product category covered by Annex II of Directive (EU)
2024/1799 where a separate manufacturer repair duty may apply; see `references/eu/repair.md`.

**Germany:** use § 434 Abs. 1 to 3 BGB (goods) and § 327e Abs. 2 BGB (digital products) for the two
conformity layers; state the limitation period as two years from delivery (§ 438 Abs. 1 Nr. 3 BGB),
with the five further consumer-sale floors in § 475e BGB layered on top where they apply.

**US:** draft a written warranty only where the business chooses to give one; the Magnuson-Moss
Warranty Act does not require one. Caption it conspicuously as either "full (statement of duration)
warranty" or "limited warranty" (PW-US-FED-4), with no other designation, and do not use the full
designation unless the warranty actually meets the federal minimum standards (15 U.S.C. 2304(a)(1) to
(4), 2304(e)). For a limited warranty costing the consumer more than $15, include the nine Part 701
disclosure items. Where an informal dispute settlement mechanism under Part 703 is named in the
document, disclose its availability, name and contact channel, whether resort to it is a precondition
to suing under the Act, and that resort is not required for remedies arising outside the Act
(16 C.F.R. 703.2(b)(1) to (4)).

## 8. Liability and disclaimers

See `references/clauses/liability-and-disclaimers.md` for the full clause, its modules and legal basis.

Nothing in these terms limits or excludes a liability that the law does not let [trader name] limit or
exclude. In particular, nothing here limits [trader name]'s liability for death or personal injury, for
its own fraud or intentional wrongdoing, for a grossly negligent breach of duty, for a negligent breach
of a duty essential to this contract, or for liability a specific statute states cannot be contracted
away.

Outside what the paragraph above protects, [trader name]'s liability for a negligent breach of a duty
that is not essential to this contract is limited to the loss that was foreseeable and ordinary for a
contract of this kind. [Trader name] is not liable for indirect or consequential loss, including lost
profit or lost data, except where the paragraph above says this clause cannot limit that liability.

[Trader name] is not liable for a problem caused by [the consumer's / the user's] own misuse of [the
product], by unauthorized changes to it, or by a cause outside [trader name]'s control, unless the law
says this clause cannot exclude that liability.

**EU consumers:** state the floor first: nothing reaches liability for death or personal injury caused
by the trader's own act or omission (Annex point (a) UCTD), and nothing unduly excludes the consumer's
legal rights for non-performance (Annex point (b) UCTD). Where the product is a defective product
within the Product Liability Directive's meaning, state plainly that nothing in these terms limits the
trader's, or any other liable economic operator's, statutory liability toward an injured natural person
under that Directive (Art. 15 Directive (EU) 2024/2853). Record in the annotations that software and
firmware are products under that Directive, and that a manufacturer that keeps the ability to supply
updates is not exempt for a defect caused by an update or by a missing security update within its
control (Art. 4(1), 11(2), point (c), Directive (EU) 2024/2853).

**Germany:** no clause may exclude or limit liability for death, personal injury, or health harm
arising from a negligent breach, or an intentional or grossly negligent breach by the trader's
representative or vicarious agent, with no carve-out for any contract type (§ 309 Nr. 7 Buchst. a BGB);
no clause may exclude or limit liability for other damage from a grossly negligent breach, apart from
the two narrow statutory carve-outs (§ 309 Nr. 7 Buchst. b BGB).

**US:** draft a conspicuous as-is or as-available disclaimer and a liability cap paired with a
disclaimer of incidental and consequential damages. For California, do not draft a clause whose effect
is to exempt a party from its own fraud, willful injury, or a violation of law (Cal. Civ. Code § 1668),
and do not require a California consumer to waive a Consumers Legal Remedies Act right (Cal. Civ. Code
§ 1751).

## 9. Indemnity

See `references/clauses/indemnity.md` for the full clause, its modules and legal basis. Use the
business-to-business module for a commercial counterparty; drop this section for an EU consumer.

[Counterparty name] covers [trader name] for a loss, cost, or claim a third party brings against
[trader name] because of: content [counterparty name] uploads, posts, or sends through [the service];
[counterparty name]'s own breach of this contract; or [counterparty name]'s use of [the service] in a
way this contract does not allow. This covers [trader name]'s reasonable costs of defending the claim
and any amount [trader name] has to pay because of it, once the claim is confirmed or settled with
[counterparty name]'s agreement.

This does not cover a claim that comes from [trader name]'s own breach of this contract, from [trader
name]'s own negligence, or from [trader name] not following [trader name]'s own published rules.
[Trader name] tells [counterparty name] promptly about a claim this clause might cover, lets
[counterparty name] take part in defending it, and does not settle it in a way that admits fault on
[counterparty name]'s part without [counterparty name]'s agreement.

[Business-to-business module: add a stated cap on the amount covered and a stated time limit for
bringing a claim, set out in the pricing or order document the parties actually negotiate.]

**EU consumers:** drop this clause. An indemnity running from the consumer toward the trader risks the
Annex point (o) pattern of binding the consumer to obligations the trader does not mirror, the point
(b) and (q) patterns of shifting a burden of proof, and, for Germany, the outright void burden-shift
under § 309 Nr. 12 Buchst. a BGB.

**US:** a California-governed indemnity must not, however labeled, have the effect of covering the
trader's own fraud, willful injury, or violation of law (Cal. Civ. Code § 1668), and must not make a
California consumer give up a CLRA right or remedy (Cal. Civ. Code § 1751).

## 10. Intellectual property

See `references/clauses/intellectual-property.md` for the full clause, its modules and legal basis.

[Trader name] and its licensors own all right, title, and interest in [the service], including its
software, databases, trademarks, logos, and all other material [trader name] or its licensors make
available through it, apart from content [users] post, upload, or submit. Nothing in this contract
transfers any of that ownership to [licensee name] or any other party; [trader name] reserves every
right it does not expressly grant elsewhere in this contract.

Where [the software] is protected by copyright, the acts reserved to [trader name] as rightholder are:
reproducing it, including the incidental reproduction loading, displaying, running, transmitting, or
storing it needs; translating, adapting, arranging, or otherwise altering it; and distributing the
original or copies of it to the public, including by rental. [Licensee name] must not carry out any of
these acts except as section 3 (Licence grant) expressly allows.

[Where the service stores, transmits, caches, or links to material at a user's direction, add the
notice-and-takedown and counter-notice module from `references/clauses/intellectual-property.md`,
including the designated-agent filing.]

**EU consumers and EU business customers:** state the three restricted acts the same way section 3
states them for the licence grant itself (Art. 4(1)(a) to (c) Directive 2009/24/EC), so the two do not
drift apart; state the trader's trademark and other non-software IP ownership as a separate sentence,
since the Software Directive does not extend to trademarks at all.

**Germany:** track the UrhG's own four restricted acts (§ 69c Nr. 1 to 4 UrhG); state who holds the
economic rights for employee- or contractor-created software, since this clause's ownership statement
rests on that agreement, not on a default assumption of trader ownership (§ 69b Abs. 1, 2 UrhG).

**US:** include the designated-agent and notice-and-takedown module only where the product in fact
stores, transmits, caches, or links to material at a user's direction. Publish the designated agent's
contact details on the service and separately file them with the Copyright Office; both steps are
required together (17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(2)(A), (B)).

## 11. User content

See `references/clauses/user-content.md` for the full clause, its modules and legal basis. Include this
section only where the product lets users post, upload or submit their own content.

[User name] keeps ownership of the content [user name] posts, uploads or submits through [the service].
By posting it, [user name] gives [trader name] a non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free licence to
host, store, reproduce, adapt for technical formats, publicly display and distribute that content, only
to the extent needed to operate, promote and improve [the service]. This licence ends when [user name]
deletes the content or closes the account, except for a copy kept as required by law, for a backup kept
on the ordinary backup cycle, or for content other users have already shared further within [the
service] before the deletion.

[User name] confirms that posting the content does not infringe a third party's rights and that [user
name] has the rights needed to grant this licence. [Trader name] does not check content before it is
posted and is not responsible for it; [trader name] reviews, restricts, labels or removes content under
the process in section 12 (Moderation and suspension), on [trader name]'s own initiative or in response
to a notice, and doing so does not by itself make [trader name] the author or publisher of that
content.

**EU consumers and EU business customers:** where the service is a hosting service under the Digital
Services Act, state in the terms any restriction the provider imposes for user-provided information,
and the policies, procedures, measures and tools used for content moderation, including whether
algorithmic decision-making or human review applies (Art. 14(1) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065). Do not
draft this section as a blanket disclaimer of liability: the hosting exemption applies only while its
own conditions hold. Do not draft it as a general monitoring duty or a promise to screen content before
posting (Art. 8 Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).

**US:** state that the provider is not treated as the publisher or speaker of information a user
provides, and that reviewing, filtering, labeling, restricting or removing content does not change that
(47 U.S.C. § 230(c)(1)); keep that statement limited to content the provider did not itself create or
develop. Do not promise full or guaranteed review of user content (47 U.S.C. § 230(f)(3)). Where the
product stores, caches, links to, or transmits material at a user's direction, keep the licence and this
sentence consistent with the DMCA module in section 10.

## 12. Acceptable use

See `references/clauses/acceptable-use.md` for the full clause, its modules and legal basis.

[User name] agrees not to use [the service] to post, upload, transmit or otherwise make available any
content that is illegal, or to use [the service] for any purpose that is illegal. [User name] agrees not

## 22. Severability and entire agreement

See `references/clauses/severability-and-entire-agreement.md` for the full clause, its modules and
legal basis.

these terms stays in effect. Where the law that applies to the finding supplies its own rule for what
replaces the invalid part, that rule applies instead of anything stated here. These terms are the
entire agreement between the user and the business about the subject matter they cover, and replace any

**EU consumers:** once a court finds a term unfair, it has no power to revise or reduce it to an

**Germany:** where a standard term is not included or is invalid, the rest of the contract stays valid
and the gap is filled by statutory law, not by whatever the severability clause itself proposes (§ 306
Abs. 1, 2 BGB).

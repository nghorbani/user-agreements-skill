# Privacy notice checklist (GDPR and Germany)

Law as of: 2026-10-20

For a controller writing or reviewing the privacy notice itself, the cookie and tracking consent
flow that sits beside it, and the rights-handling process the notice promises. Covers the GDPR
transparency, lawful-basis, rights and liability rules, the ePrivacy Directive's device-consent
rule, and Germany's TDDDG and BDSG layers on top of them. Member states other than Germany
transpose the ePrivacy rule and narrow GDPR rights in their own way; check the national law before
relying on this file outside Germany.

## Before drafting: classify the processing

- [ ] Every distinct processing purpose listed separately, each with its own Art. 6(1) basis
      named by letter, not a blanket reference to processing as permitted by law (EU: Art. 5(1)(a),
      (b), 6(1) GDPR)
- [ ] Each purpose's basis checked against the Art. 6(1) picks: consent, contract necessity, legal
      obligation, vital interest, public task, legitimate interest; none is a fallback used only
      because nothing else fits (EU: Art. 6(1) GDPR)
- [ ] Any Art. 9 special-category data (racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or
      philosophical belief, trade union membership, genetic data, biometric data used to identify
      a person, health data, sex life or sexual orientation) flagged for its own Art. 9(2) ground,
      checked separately from the Art. 6(1) basis for the same processing (EU: Art. 9(1), (2) GDPR)
- [ ] Criminal-conviction or offence data checked for official-authority control or an authorising
      law with safeguards before any processing starts (EU: Art. 10 GDPR)
- [ ] Any processing offered directly to a child, where the basis is consent, checked against the
      Member State's own age floor (13 to 16), not a single hard-coded figure (EU: Art. 8(1), (2)
      GDPR)
- [ ] Whether the data was collected from the data subject (Art. 13 notice) or from elsewhere
      (Art. 14 notice) decided per source, since the two content lists and timing rules differ (EU:
      Art. 13(1), Art. 14(1) GDPR)
- [ ] For data obtained elsewhere: the specific source and whether it was publicly accessible
      recorded at intake, or, only where naming it genuinely proves impossible, the type and sector
      of source instead (EU: Art. 14(2)(f) GDPR)
- [ ] For the BDSG layer specifically: whether the data was collected from the data subject
      (§ 32 Abs. 1 BDSG) or obtained elsewhere (§ 33 Abs. 1 BDSG) checked activity by activity, the
      same split as the Art. 13/Art. 14 bullet above, since the BDSG grounds that narrow the notice
      duty attach to one section or the other on that basis (DE: § 32 Abs. 1, § 33 Abs. 1 BDSG)
- [ ] Every cookie, tracking pixel, client-side script that sends data off-device, IP-based
      tracking, or persistent device identifier inventoried, independent of whether it carries
      personal data, since the device-access rule applies regardless (EU: Art. 5(3) Directive
      2002/58/EC; DE: § 25 Abs. 1 TDDDG)

## The notice content itself

- [ ] Controller's identity and contact details, and its representative's where it has one, stated
      (EU: Art. 13(1)(a), Art. 14(1)(a) GDPR)
- [ ] Data protection officer's contact details stated, where the controller has one (EU:
      Art. 13(1)(b), Art. 14(1)(b) GDPR)
- [ ] Purpose of each processing operation and its specific legal basis stated, in concrete terms
      with no open-ended qualifier such as "may", "might", "some" or "possible" unless the
      controller can show why the qualifier could not be avoided (EU: Art. 12(1), Art. 13(1)(c),
      Art. 14(1)(c) GDPR)
- [ ] Where the basis is legitimate interest: the specific interest pursued named for the
      controller or the third party relying on it, not a generic reference to the article (EU:
      Art. 13(1)(d) GDPR)
- [ ] Recipients or categories of recipients named specifically where possible, not only by broad
      category (EU: Art. 13(1)(e), Art. 14(1)(e) GDPR)
- [ ] Any intended third-country or international-organisation transfer disclosed, with the
      adequacy decision or the Art. 46/47/49(1) safeguard named and how to get a copy of it (EU:
      Art. 13(1)(f), Art. 14(1)(f) GDPR)
- [ ] Storage period stated, or, where a fixed period is not possible, the criteria that set it, in
      enough detail that the data subject can work out roughly how long their data is kept; a bare
      statement that data is kept as long as necessary, with no further detail, does not meet this
      (EU: Art. 13(2)(a), Art. 14(2)(a) GDPR)
- [ ] Existence of the access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and portability
      rights stated (EU: Art. 13(2)(b), Art. 14(2)(c) GDPR)
- [ ] Where the basis is consent or explicit consent: the right to withdraw at any time stated,
      with no effect on the lawfulness of earlier processing (EU: Art. 13(2)(c), Art. 14(2)(d)
      GDPR)
- [ ] Right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority stated (EU: Art. 13(2)(d),
      Art. 14(2)(e) GDPR)
- [ ] For data collected from the data subject only: whether providing it is a statutory or
      contractual requirement or needed to enter a contract, whether the data subject must provide
      it, and the consequence of not doing so, stated (EU: Art. 13(2)(e) GDPR)
- [ ] For data obtained elsewhere only: the categories of personal data concerned stated, since the
      data subject has no existing awareness of what the controller holds (EU: Art. 14(1)(d) GDPR)
- [ ] Existence of Art. 22(1) or (4) automated decision-making, including profiling, disclosed with
      meaningful information about the logic involved and the significance and envisaged
      consequences for the data subject (EU: Art. 13(2)(f), Art. 14(2)(g) GDPR)
- [ ] Direct-marketing objection right stated separately from the rest of the notice, flagged
      explicitly no later than the first communication with the data subject, never folded into a
      general rights paragraph (EU: Art. 21(4) GDPR)
- [ ] Right to erasure section carries the fixed title "Right to erasure ('right to be forgotten')"
      in English, or its German equivalent; see PW-EU-11 in references/prescribed-wording.md (EU:
      Art. 17 heading GDPR)
- [ ] Written in a concise, transparent, intelligible and easily accessible form, in clear and plain
      language, with extra care where the information targets a child (EU: Art. 12(1) GDPR)
- [ ] No fee charged for the notice itself or an ordinary rights request, and access never made
      conditional on a purchase or other financial transaction (EU: Art. 12(5) GDPR)
- [ ] Icons, where used, only supplement the written Art. 13 or 14 content and never stand in for it
      (EU: Art. 12(7) GDPR)

## Timing of the notice

- [ ] Data collected from the data subject: notice given at the time of collection, with no later
      alternative (EU: Art. 13(1) GDPR)
- [ ] Data obtained elsewhere: notice given within a reasonable period and at the latest within one
      month, brought forward to the first communication with the data subject or the first
      disclosure to another recipient, whichever comes sooner (EU: Art. 14(3)(a) to (c) GDPR)
- [ ] A new purpose beyond the one originally disclosed: the new purpose and the matching Art. 13(2)
      or 14(2) information given before that further processing starts, with a gap before it begins
      that is longer the more intrusive or surprising the new use is (EU: Art. 13(3), Art. 14(4)
      GDPR)
- [ ] An Art. 14(5) exception (impossibility, disproportionate effort, a barring law, professional
      secrecy) claimed only with the specific fact it rests on documented, since the burden sits on
      the controller (EU: Art. 14(5)(a) to (d) GDPR)

## Surfacing the notice (layering and delivery)

- [ ] A first layer gives the purposes, the controller's identity, the rights available, and
      whichever part of the processing most affects or could surprise the data subject, with full
      detail reachable from there (EU: Art. 12(1) GDPR, per the EDPB transparency guidance it draws
      on)
- [ ] The first layer surfaces at the point of collection itself, beside the form field it
      explains, not only inside a separately linked document
- [ ] A screenless device has a notice channel beyond an online page, such as a URL on packaging or
      an audio alert where the device has audio output

## Keeping the notice current

- [ ] A change to processing purpose, controller identity, or how rights are exercised is treated as
      a material change and triggers a dedicated, active notification sent well before the change
      takes effect, separate from marketing content (EU: Art. 12(1), Art. 5(1)(b) GDPR)
- [ ] No clause tells the data subject to check back periodically for policy updates in place of an
      active notification of a material change (EU: Art. 5(1)(a) GDPR)
- [ ] Where a change is fundamental, such as a materially wider recipient category or a new
      third-country transfer, the data subject gets a real opportunity to react, by objecting or
      withdrawing consent, before the change takes hold (EU: Art. 5(1)(b) GDPR)
- [ ] A typo fix or stylistic correction is not treated as a material change needing active
      notification

## Consent, where consent is the basis

- [ ] Each distinct processing purpose has its own control; a declaration mixing several purposes
      carries a separate opt-in per purpose, not one bundled tick (EU: Art. 7(2) GDPR)
- [ ] Consent is a statement or a clear affirmative act, never inferred from silence, a pre-ticked
      box, inactivity, or scrolling or swiping through a page (EU: Art. 4(11) GDPR)
- [ ] Consent is not obtained through the same motion by which the data subject accepts a contract
      or general terms (EU: Art. 4(11) GDPR)
- [ ] Contract performance is not made conditional on consent to processing that is not necessary
      for that contract (EU: Art. 7(4) GDPR)
- [ ] Before asking: the controller's identity, the purpose of each processing operation, what data
      will be collected, the right to withdraw, any Art. 22 use, and any transfer risk absent an
      adequacy decision or safeguards, disclosed; where several controllers share the same consent,
      all of them named (EU: Art. 7(2) GDPR, per the EDPB consent guidance)
- [ ] Withdrawal is as easy as giving consent, through the same interface, free of charge, with no
      service downgrade beyond what the withdrawn processing itself enabled (EU: Art. 7(3) GDPR)
- [ ] Explicit consent, not merely an affirmative act, used wherever Art. 9 special-category data,
      an Art. 49 transfer, or Art. 22 automated decision-making is the ground (EU: Art. 9(2)(a),
      Art. 22(2)(c), Art. 49 GDPR)
- [ ] For a consent clause recorded in a signed form: the clause is not pre-ticked by the
      controller's own staff before signing with no evidence it was read, does not suggest the
      contract cannot be concluded without that consent where the processing is not in fact
      necessary for it, and refusing is not made harder than accepting (EU: Case C-61/19, Orange
      Romania, para. 52 and operative part)
- [ ] An affirmative-consent sentence pattern is available for a document that needs one; see
      PW-EU-12 in references/prescribed-wording.md (EU: Art. 4(11), Art. 7(2) GDPR)
- [ ] A child's consent to an information-society service offered directly to them is verified by
      reasonable effort, or, where the service states it is offered only to adults, that statement
      is not undermined by other site content or marketing (EU: Art. 8(1), (2) GDPR)

## Cookies and tracking: the consent flow

- [ ] Purposes of storing or reading information on the device disclosed clearly and in full before
      the storage or reading happens (EU: Art. 5(3) Directive 2002/58/EC)
- [ ] Where the stored items collect information for advertising or a comparable third-party
      purpose: the operating duration of each item, and whether and which categories of third party
      can access it, also stated (EU: Art. 5(3) Directive 2002/58/EC, per the Planet49 reading of
      the Art. 10 Directive 95/46/EC disclosure floor)
- [ ] Each purpose has its own control and its own accompanying text; a tick on one purpose's
      control is never read as covering a different purpose (EU: Case C-673/17, Planet49)
- [ ] Every non-essential control starts unticked, off, or otherwise not selected, requiring a
      distinct affirmative action to turn on (EU: Case C-673/17, Planet49)
- [ ] A genuine reject or refuse option sits on the same layer as any accept button, with
      prominence comparable to it, not buried in running text or placed outside the banner's
      visual frame
- [ ] The site remains usable without consenting to non-essential processing, with no design that
      gives the impression access depends on accepting
- [ ] A withdrawal mechanism is reachable at any time, through the same interface and with the same
      ease as giving consent, and visibly reflects the change once used rather than appearing to do
      nothing
- [ ] Where a cookie's further processing also relies on Art. 6(1)(f) legitimate interest, for
      example personalised advertising, the objection to that processing is presented on the same
      layer and with the same prominence as the cookie refusal, not one layer deeper
- [ ] No humour, wordplay, or styling that distracts from the data-protection meaning of the request
      or blends it into unrelated content
- [ ] A consent toggle's colour and position do not contradict each other (an "on" state is not
      coloured to look like "off" or vice versa)
- [ ] No consent prompt a user already declined is repeated through a recurring pop-up that blocks
      ordinary use of the service
- [ ] Consent records exist showing, per user, that the information was presented and an
      affirmative action taken, since the burden of proof sits with the controller (EU: Art. 7(1)
      GDPR)
- [ ] No item is labelled "essential" or "strictly necessary" to skip consent unless it meets that
      standard; the burden of justifying the label sits with the site owner, documented per item
      (DE: § 25 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 TDDDG, per the German supervisory guidance's test)

## Cookies and tracking: German-specific checks

- [ ] Terminal equipment scope checked against the German definition, reaching laptops, tablets,
      phones and connected devices such as smart-home appliances, connected vehicles and smart TVs
      (DE: § 25 Abs. 1 Satz 1 TDDDG; § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 6 TDDDG)
- [ ] Where one banner button grants both the § 25 storage consent and a later GDPR-governed
      processing consent: the follow-on processing purposes named at the same point, with it made
      clearly recognisable that one act grants more than one consent (DE: § 25 Abs. 1 Satz 1, 2
      TDDDG)
- [ ] At the first layer where consent is requested: the concrete processing purposes, whether
      individual profiles are built and enriched with data from other sites, whether data is
      processed outside the EEA, and how many controllers the data is disclosed to, all shown; where
      third-party services are used, each one named individually rather than referred to only as
      partners in general (DE: § 25 Abs. 1 TDDDG, per the German supervisory guidance)
- [ ] A reject option, where one is required (the banner blocks some or all content pending a
      decision), carries the same visual and functional weight as accept, and a reject button is
      never demoted to a combined settings-or-reject label that only leads to a further layer (DE:
      § 25 Abs. 1 TDDDG, per the German supervisory guidance)
- [ ] A claimed strict-necessity exemption is documented per item: which function it supports, when
      storage or access may start (only once the user actually invokes that function), the content
      stored or read, how long it persists, and who can read it; a multi-purpose item is exempt
      only if every one of its purposes independently meets the test (DE: § 25 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 TDDDG,
      per the German supervisory guidance)
- [ ] A cart or payment cookie is not claimed exempt before the matching user interaction has
      actually occurred; a cookie carrying a unique identification marker is not claimed exempt
      without checking whether the function actually needs individualisation (DE: § 25 Abs. 2 Nr. 2
      TDDDG, per the German supervisory guidance)
- [ ] A single navigational act, such as typing a URL, is not treated as expressly requesting every
      subpage or additional function behind it; an additional function or service integrated into
      the base service (a shopping cart, a chat box, a wish list) is expressly requested only once
      the user actually invokes it (DE: § 25 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 TDDDG, per the German supervisory
      guidance)
- [ ] No withdrawal mechanism requires an email, fax, letter, contact form, or a search through the
      privacy notice; a withdrawal link inside the notice is permissible only where it leads
      directly to the withdrawal mechanism with no search step (DE: § 25 Abs. 1 TDDDG, per the
      German supervisory guidance)
- [ ] Full Art. 13/14 GDPR content disclosed for any processing that follows a § 25 access, since the
      § 19 Abs. 3 TDDDG information duty is in addition to, not a substitute for, that content (DE:
      § 19 Abs. 3 TDDDG)
- [ ] Consent to a third-country transfer arising from regular or systematic behavioural tracking is
      not relied on as the Art. 49(1)(a) transfer mechanism; an adequacy decision or standard
      contractual clauses, backed by a case-by-case assessment and, where needed, supplementary
      measures, used instead (DE: § 25 Abs. 1 TDDDG, per the German supervisory guidance, citing
      CJEU, judgment of 16 July 2020, C-311/18, Schrems II)

## Rights requests: the handling process

- [ ] A rights-request channel exists for access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection
      and portability, answered within one month, extendable by two further months for complex or
      numerous requests with a logged reason sent within the first month (EU: Art. 12(3) GDPR)
- [ ] A request that will not be acted on is refused within one month, with reasons, and the data
      subject told of the right to complain to a supervisory authority and to seek a judicial
      remedy (EU: Art. 12(4) GDPR)
- [ ] The channel is free by default; a fee or refusal is available only where the request is shown
      to be manifestly unfounded or excessive, with the burden on the controller (EU: Art. 12(5)
      GDPR)
- [ ] Identity verification sits in front of a request only on reasonable doubt, and does not
      justify holding data beyond its normal retention period merely to verify a possible future
      request (EU: Art. 12(6) GDPR)
- [ ] An access response includes the Art. 15(1)(a) to (h) information list, with the first copy
      free and a reasonable administrative fee only for further copies (EU: Art. 15(1), (3) GDPR)
- [ ] Erasure is actioned without undue delay on any of the six Art. 17(1) grounds, checked against
      the Art. 17(3) exceptions (freedom of expression, a legal obligation, public-task processing,
      public-health reasons, archiving or research where erasure would seriously impair the
      purpose, or legal claims) before the request is granted (EU: Art. 17(1), (3) GDPR)
- [ ] Restriction, not erasure, applies in the four Art. 18(1) situations (contested accuracy,
      unlawful processing where the data subject opposes erasure, data no longer needed by the
      controller but needed by the data subject for legal claims, or a pending Art. 21(1)
      objection), with the data subject told before a restriction is lifted (EU: Art. 18(1), (3)
      GDPR)
- [ ] Every recipient of a rectification, an Art. 17(1) erasure, or a restriction is notified unless
      impossible or disproportionate, with those recipients named to the data subject on request
      (EU: Art. 19 GDPR)
- [ ] Portability is offered only where the processing rests on consent or contract and happens by
      automated means, never for public-task or legitimate-interest processing (EU: Art. 20(1),
      (3) GDPR)
- [ ] A general Art. 21(1) objection and the unconditional direct-marketing objection are both
      reachable at any time, distinguishable from each other; the marketing one needs no
      justification from the user and has no override (EU: Art. 21(1), (2) to (4) GDPR)
- [ ] Where a decision is solely automated with a legal or similarly significant effect and rests on
      contract necessity or explicit consent: a human-intervention path, a chance to state a view,
      and a chance to contest the decision are all available (EU: Art. 22(3) GDPR)
- [ ] Where the product scores, ranks, or otherwise automatically evaluates a person and hands that
      output to a third party who draws strongly on it to decide about that person: the scoring
      step itself is treated as its own Art. 22(1) decision, with its own Art. 22(2) legal basis and
      its own Art. 15(1)(h) access duty, independent of the downstream party's decision (EU: Case
      C-634/21, SCHUFA, paras. 43, 53, 56, 63)
- [ ] Where a national-law basis under Art. 22(2)(b) is relied on for an automated output: checked
      that the statute itself regulates the act of establishing the output, not merely its later use
      (EU: Case C-634/21, SCHUFA, paras. 65, 71, 72)
- [ ] A complaint-and-remedy section names the supervisory-authority complaint, the judicial remedy
      against that authority, and the separate judicial remedy against the controller or processor
      as three distinct routes, not folded into one contact-us line (EU: Art. 77, 78, 79 GDPR)
- [ ] Where a German controller skips the individual Art. 13 or 14 notice under a BDSG ground that
      allows it: a public-facing privacy page carries the Art. 13(1), (2) or Art. 14(1), (2) content
      instead, and the withholding reasons are recorded in writing (DE: § 32 Abs. 2, § 33 Abs. 2
      BDSG)
- [ ] Where a BDSG ground displaces an individual notice because of a temporary obstacle: the
      make-up notice is sent within a reasonable period, at the latest within two weeks of the
      obstacle lapsing (DE: § 32 Abs. 3 BDSG)
- [ ] An access refusal under a § 34 Abs. 1 BDSG ground is documented, with reasons stated to the
      data subject unless doing so would itself defeat the refusal's purpose (DE: § 34 Abs. 2 Satz
      1, 2 BDSG)
- [ ] An erasure request met with restriction instead under § 35 Abs. 1 BDSG first checks that the
      underlying processing was lawful; restriction is not available where it was not (DE: § 35 Abs.
      1 Satz 3 BDSG)
- [ ] An automated decision under an insurance contract not granted in full carries the
      human-intervention, own-view and contest-the-decision notice together with, and no later
      than, the refusal itself (DE: § 37 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 BDSG)

## Compensation and liability wording

- [ ] No clause states that any GDPR infringement by itself creates a payable compensation claim;
      an infringement, actual damage, and a causal link between them are all required (EU: Case
      C-300/21, Österreichische Post, para. 42, operative point 1)
- [ ] No internal remedy or compensation process conditions payment on the damage crossing a
      seriousness or materiality threshold framed as the Art. 82 standard itself (EU: Case
      C-300/21, Österreichische Post, para. 51, operative point 2)
- [ ] No clause promises a single, fixed EU-wide compensation formula or figure; quantum is set by
      each Member State's own rules (EU: Case C-300/21, Österreichische Post, para. 59, operative
      point 3)
- [ ] No clause suggests a data subject can recover punitive or exemplary damages (EU: Case
      C-300/21, Österreichische Post, para. 58)
- [ ] A joint-controller or joint-processor liability clause does not cap any one party's exposure
      to data subjects below the entire-damage standard; an internal apportionment is a matter
      between the parties, never a limit enforceable against the data subject (EU: Art. 82(4), (5)
      GDPR)
- [ ] No clause claims adherence to a code of conduct or certification mechanism exempts the
      controller from a fine; it is one mitigating factor among several, not a safe harbour (EU:
      Art. 83(2)(j) GDPR)

## Records

- [ ] A record of what notice content was shown to a given data subject, and when, is kept, since
      any Art. 14(5) exception or Art. 12(1) qualifier used needs to be justified after the fact
      (EU: Art. 5(2), Art. 14(5) GDPR)
- [ ] A record of which lawful basis was disclosed for which purpose, and of when and how each
      consent was obtained, is kept, since the burden of proof for both sits with the controller
      (EU: Art. 5(2), Art. 7(1) GDPR)
- [ ] For a cookie or tracking-technology inventory: records are kept together with the banner or
      consent-management-platform configuration in effect at the time, not only the current
      configuration (DE: § 25 Abs. 1 TDDDG, per the German supervisory guidance)
- [ ] A stated retention period exists for both the inputs collected and any score or similar
      automated output produced from them (EU: Case C-634/21, SCHUFA, para. 67)

## Not covered here

Member states other than Germany, their own ePrivacy transposition and narrowing of GDPR rights,
processor and international-transfer duties (see references/eu/gdpr-processors-transfers.md), and
US state privacy law (see references/checklists/privacy-notice-us.md).

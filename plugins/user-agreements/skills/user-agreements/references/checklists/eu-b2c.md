# EU B2C checklist

Law as of: 2026-10-20

For a trader selling goods, digital content or digital services to a consumer anywhere in the
Union. Covers the directive-level floor only: the Consumer Rights Directive, the Unfair Contract
Terms Directive, the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, the Digital Content Directive, the
Sale of Goods Directive, the e-Commerce Directive, the geo-blocking Regulation, Rome I and
Brussels Ia, and the repair Directive. National transposition, in particular Germany's, decides
the exact wording and sanction and is checked separately (see the de-agb checklist and the
references/de files for Germany).

## Before drafting: classify the contract

- [ ] Trader, consumer and contract classified, including a dual-purpose contract where the trade
      purpose is not predominant (EU: CRD Art. 3(1), Recital 17)
- [ ] Distance, off-premises or on-premises channel identified, since each carries its own
      information list and formal requirements (EU: CRD Art. 2(7), 2(8), 5, 6, 7, 8)
- [ ] Goods, digital content (a single act of supply) or digital service (ongoing access) sorted
      correctly, since the two carry different withdrawal regimes and genuine doubt defaults to
      the service rule (EU: CRD Art. 9(2); DCD Art. 3)
- [ ] Payment in money, voucher, loyalty points or personal data identified: a nominally free
      product that processes the consumer's data for anything beyond supplying the content is in
      scope on that ground alone (EU: CRD Art. 3(1); DCD Art. 3(1))
- [ ] Member states the product is sold into named, since every duty below operates through that
      state's own transposition, not through directive text directly (EU: CRD Art. 4; UCTD Art. 8;
      DCD Art. 4; SGD Art. 4)
- [ ] Sector exclusions checked (financial services, healthcare, gambling, immovable property,
      package travel) before applying the rest of this checklist, since an excluded contract needs
      a different or narrower article set (EU: CRD Art. 3(1b), 3(3))

## Offer and product page

- [ ] Main characteristics, trader identity and a second complaints address if different, shown
      before the consumer is bound (EU: CRD Art. 6(1), points a to v)
- [ ] Total price including taxes, with the billing-period total for a subscription and the
      monthly total for a fixed-rate contract, shown as one figure, not assembled screen by screen
      (EU: CRD Art. 6(1), points a to v; UCPD Art. 6(1)(d), 7(4)(c))
- [ ] Whether the price was personalised by automated decision-making, disclosed (EU: CRD
      Art. 6(1), points a to v)
- [ ] Payment, delivery and performance arrangements, and the complaint-handling policy, disclosed
      (EU: CRD Art. 6(1), points a to v)
- [ ] Withdrawal conditions, time limit and procedure given under Art. 11(1), with the Annex I(B)
      form or a correctly completed Annex I(A) notice; see PW-EU-4, PW-EU-5 in
      references/prescribed-wording.md (EU: CRD Art. 6(1)(h); Art. 6(4))
- [ ] Return-cost liability and liability for early-performance costs disclosed, and, where no
      withdrawal right exists or it can be lost, that fact disclosed (EU: CRD Art. 6(1), points a
      to v)
- [ ] Conformity-guarantee reminder and the durability-guarantee label shown where the producer
      offers one, using the harmonised notice once the implementing act sets its content (EU: CRD
      Art. 6(1)(l), (la); needs source: the Art. 22a implementing act, not yet in a declared file)
- [ ] After-sale assistance, after-sales services and commercial guarantees disclosed where the
      consumer has a legitimate interest in that information to decide whether to contract (EU: CRD
      Art. 6(1)(m); CJEU C-179/21 absoluts -bikes and more-)
- [ ] Contract duration, termination conditions and minimum obligation period shown (EU: CRD
      Art. 6(1), points a to v)
- [ ] Existence and conditions of any deposit or financial guarantee the trader requires, disclosed
      before the consumer is bound (EU: CRD Art. 6(1), points a to v)
- [ ] For goods with digital elements, digital content and digital services: applicable
      functionality, technical protection measures, and known compatibility and interoperability
      disclosed (EU: CRD Art. 6(1), points a to v)
- [ ] Reparability score, or spare-parts information where no score applies, shown (EU: CRD
      Art. 6(1), points a to v)
- [ ] Free trial, gift or intro price: how and when the price changes stated in the same place as
      the headline offer, before the consumer commits, not on a later screen (EU: CRD Art. 6(1),
      points a to v; UCPD practice on subscription disclosure)
- [ ] No "gratis", "free" or "without charge" framing where the consumer must pay anything beyond
      the unavoidable cost of responding to the offer and of collecting or paying for delivery (EU:
      UCPD Annex I point 20)
- [ ] A reference, crossed-out or recommended retail price states, as prominently as the price
      itself, whether it is the trader's own genuine prior price or a third-party comparison (EU:
      UCPD Art. 6(1)(d))
- [ ] A generic environmental claim such as eco-friendly, sustainable or green carries its
      qualifying detail in the same place as the claim, not an extra click away, and the trader
      keeps dated substantiation ready to produce on request (EU: UCPD Annex I point 4a; Art. 12)
- [ ] A sustainability label rests on a certification scheme meeting minimum transparency
      conditions or on a public authority, not an unbacked self-created badge (EU: UCPD point 2a)
- [ ] Consumer reviews: disclosed whether and how the trader checks that reviews come from
      consumers who actually used or purchased the product; negative reviews not suppressed
      without a stated, valid, content-neutral reason (EU: UCPD Art. 7(6); Annex I point 23c)
- [ ] A keyword-search or comparison feature carries a dedicated, directly accessible section
      disclosing the main ranking parameters and their relative importance, separate from the
      general terms (EU: UCPD Art. 7(4a); CRD Art. 6a(1) for an online marketplace)
- [ ] A marketplace listing states whether the third-party seller is a trader or not, based on
      that seller's own declaration (EU: CRD Art. 6a(1); UCPD Art. 7(4)(f))
- [ ] Provider's name, geographic address, an email address for rapid direct contact, trade
      register entry, supervisory authority where relevant, and VAT number where applicable,
      rendered on a standing, permanently accessible page, before and after sign-up (EU: ECD
      Art. 5(1))
- [ ] Every price statement states clearly and unambiguously whether tax and delivery costs are
      included (EU: ECD Art. 5(2))
- [ ] Commercial communications clearly identifiable as such, and the person they are made on
      behalf of clearly identifiable (EU: ECD Art. 6(a), (b))
- [ ] A durability-limiting feature, once known to the trader, disclosed rather than left to a
      generic durability statement (EU: UCPD Annex I point 23f)

## Checkout and the order button

- [ ] Characteristics, price, durability label, duration and termination conditions, and minimum
      obligation period shown again directly before the order button; showing them earlier on the
      page is not enough (EU: CRD Art. 8(2); points (a), (e), (la), (o), (p) of Art. 6(1))
- [ ] Order button for an electronic payment obligation carries PW-EU-1 or an unambiguous
      equivalent naming the payment obligation itself; a soft label such as "register" or "confirm"
      does not qualify (EU: CRD Art. 8(2); CJEU C-249/21 Fuhrmann-2)
- [ ] Full Art. 6(1) information reachable on a smartphone checkout, since expandable content and
      additional pages are always available on the device and the limited-space exception does not
      apply on that ground alone (EU: CRD Art. 8(4); CJEU C-430/17 Walbusch Walter Busch)
- [ ] Any delivery restrictions and the accepted means of payment stated at the latest at the start
      of the ordering process (EU: CRD Art. 8(1), (3))
- [ ] Technical steps to conclude the contract, whether the contract will be filed and made
      accessible, the means to identify and correct input errors, and the languages offered, given
      clearly before the order is placed (EU: ECD Art. 10(1)(a) to (d))
- [ ] A working, not merely disclosed, mechanism for the consumer to spot and fix input errors
      before placing the order (EU: ECD Art. 10(1)(c), Art. 11(2))
- [ ] No extra paid option pre-ticked by default; express consent sought before the consumer is
      bound for any extra payment beyond the agreed remuneration (EU: CRD Art. 22)
- [ ] No payment-method fee exceeds the trader's own cost of accepting that method (EU: CRD
      Art. 19)
- [ ] No contractual term, drip-priced add-on or payment-flow design makes it harder for the
      consumer to terminate the sign-up than to complete it (EU: UCTD Annex point (j); UCPD Art. 9)
- [ ] A free-trial or subscription flow discloses, in the same place as the headline offer: that
      signing up creates a recurring payment obligation, both the one-off and recurring charge
      amounts, the trader's identity and address, and the trigger for the paid period to start (EU:
      UCPD practice on subscription disclosure; CRD Art. 6(1), points a to v)
- [ ] Codes of conduct the provider subscribes to named, with how to consult them electronically
      (EU: ECD Art. 10(2); CRD Art. 6(1), points a to v)
- [ ] A sales call opens with the trader's identity, the identity of anyone it acts for, and the
      call's commercial purpose, disclosed before anything else is said (EU: CRD Art. 8(5))

## Confirmation after the order

- [ ] Contract confirmed on a durable medium within a reasonable time, at the latest by delivery
      or before service starts, repeating any Art. 6(1) item not already durably given before
      conclusion (EU: CRD Art. 8(7))
- [ ] The confirmation channel is a genuine durable medium: a bare hyperlink does not qualify, and
      an account page qualifies only if the trader cannot unilaterally alter its content and
      actively notifies the consumer when new information is posted (EU: CRD Art. 2(10); CJEU
      C-49/11 Content Services; CJEU C-375/15 BAWAG)
- [ ] Order placed through technological means: receipt acknowledged without undue delay by
      electronic means, with the order and acknowledgement treated as received once accessible to
      the addressed party, not once sent (EU: ECD Art. 11(1))
- [ ] Contract terms delivered to the consumer are storable and reproducible, not shown once and
      gone, in every channel including one that runs exclusively by email (EU: ECD Art. 10(3),
      Art. 10(4))
- [ ] A commercial guarantee, where offered, is given as a durable-medium statement at the latest
      at delivery, stating that statutory remedies are unaffected, the guarantor's name and
      address, the procedure to invoke it, the goods covered and the terms (EU: SGD Art. 17(2))
- [ ] Absent a different agreement, goods are delivered within 30 days of conclusion, with the
      consumer first granting a reasonable additional period before terminating for late delivery,
      unless the deadline was essential or the trader already refused to deliver; risk of loss or
      damage passes to the consumer on physical possession, except where the consumer independently
      chose a carrier the trader did not offer, in which case risk passes on handover to that
      carrier (EU: CRD Art. 18(1) to (4), Art. 20)

## During the contract: conformity and updates

- [ ] Goods, digital content and digital services meet both the subjective (what the contract
      promised) and the objective (what is normal for that type) conformity requirements; a clause
      naming only the subjective layer does not displace the objective floor (EU: SGD Art. 5 to 7;
      DCD Art. 6 to 8)
- [ ] A deviation from objective conformity is disclosed as a named, specific item before
      conclusion, with its own express, separate acceptance distinct from general terms acceptance;
      a general sold-as-seen clause does not meet this (EU: SGD Art. 7(5); DCD Art. 8(5))
- [ ] Updates, including security updates, needed to keep goods with digital elements, digital
      content or digital services in conformity are supplied for the contract's whole term on
      continuous supply, or for a reasonable expected period otherwise; where the consumer can
      decline an update, the consequence of declining is disclosed first (EU: SGD Art. 7(3), (4);
      DCD Art. 8(2), (3))
- [ ] Continuous-supply interruptions are logged: an interruption of more than negligible length,
      or one that recurs, is itself a lack of conformity, not a total outage alone (EU: DCD Art. 8(4)
      and Recital 51)
- [ ] Records show what pre-contractual information was shown to a given consumer and when, since
      the trader carries the burden of proof throughout (EU: CRD Art. 6(9); UCTD Guidance section
      3.3.1; DCD Art. 12)
- [ ] No clause gives the trader the sole right to decide whether goods or services conform, or the
      exclusive right to interpret a contract term (EU: UCTD Annex point (m))
- [ ] No clause lets the trader honour commitments made by its agents only subject to a particular
      formality, or obliges the consumer to perform while the trader does not (EU: UCTD Annex
      points (n), (o))
- [ ] Liability runs for two years from delivery (goods, including goods with digital elements) or
      from supply, or throughout the supply period on continuous supply (digital content or digital
      service); within that period, the trader by default carries the burden of proving conformity,
      shifting to the consumer only where the trader has disclosed a pre-contractual incompatibility
      check or a consumer cooperation duty and relies on the consumer's failure under it (EU: SGD
      Art. 10(1), 11(1) to (3); DCD Art. 11(2), (3), 12(1) to (5))

## Price changes and other material changes during the contract

- [ ] A unilateral price or term change states, in the contract itself, a valid reason and a
      method, and gives the consumer a matching termination right; a clause missing any one of the
      three is exposed (EU: UCTD Annex points (i), (j), (l); CJEU C-472/10 Invitel)
- [ ] A modification beyond what conformity requires carries no extra cost to the consumer, is
      disclosed in clear and comprehensible language, and, where the negative effect on access or
      use is more than minor, is announced on a durable medium with reasonable advance notice of its
      features, timing and the consumer's free termination right within 30 days, unless a genuine
      no-cost unmodified option is also offered (EU: DCD Art. 19)
- [ ] No clause lets the trader dissolve the contract at its own discretion without giving the
      consumer an equivalent right, or terminate an indefinite-duration contract without reasonable
      notice except for serious grounds (EU: UCTD Annex points (f), (g))
- [ ] An automatic renewal clause on a fixed-term contract sets a reasonable, not unreasonably
      early, deadline for the consumer's objection (EU: UCTD Annex point (h))

## Cancellation and termination

- [ ] Cancellation, downgrade and provider-switching carry no more steps, verification or delay
      than the sign-up flow for the same relationship (EU: UCPD Art. 9(d) practice)
- [ ] A cancellation flow does not use loss-framed reminders or guilt-inducing messaging without a
      reasoned, substantive change in terms, and adds no onerous or disproportionate barrier a
      consumer must clear only to leave (EU: UCPD Art. 8, 9(d))
- [ ] On termination for lack of conformity, the consumer is not charged for return shipping,
      removal or reinstallation costs tied to repair or replacement (EU: SGD Art. 14(2), (3),
      16(3)(a); DCD Art. 16 to 18)
- [ ] A reimbursement owed on termination is made within 14 days of the trader learning of the
      consumer's decision, by the same payment means the consumer used unless they expressly agree
      otherwise, with no fee for the reimbursement itself (EU: SGD Art. 18(1), (2); DCD Art. 18)
- [ ] On termination of a digital content or digital service contract, the trader stops using
      consumer-provided content outside the narrow listed exceptions and, where required, makes it
      available to the consumer free of charge, within a reasonable time, in a common
      machine-readable format (EU: DCD Art. 16(3), (4))

## The EU right of withdrawal

- [ ] 14 days to withdraw, no reason needed, starting from conclusion for a service, digital
      content or continuous utility contract, or from physical possession for goods, unless an
      Art. 16 exception genuinely applies (EU: CRD Art. 9(1), (2))
- [ ] Where the Art. 6(1)(h) withdrawal notice was never given, the period extends to 12 months
      from the original period's end (EU: CRD Art. 10(1))
- [ ] A claimed Art. 16 exception is checked against the facts, not recited by label alone: the
      exception list is read narrowly against the trader in every case (EU: CRD Art. 16; CJEU
      C-208/19 NK; CJEU C-529/19 Möbel Kraft; CJEU C-681/17 slewo; CJEU C-336/03 easyCar)
- [ ] For digital content excluding withdrawal once performance starts: prior express consent,
      acknowledgment of losing the right, and confirmation obtained and recorded (EU: CRD
      Art. 16(m))
- [ ] For a distance contract concluded via an online interface: a persistent withdrawal function
      carrying PW-EU-2, prominently displayed throughout the withdrawal period, plus a second,
      separate confirmation function carrying PW-EU-3 (EU: CRD Art. 11a(1), (3))
- [ ] On activation, a durable-medium acknowledgment of receipt sent without undue delay, stating
      the declaration's content, date and time (EU: CRD Art. 11a(4))
- [ ] On withdrawal, all payments including delivery cost reimbursed within 14 days using the
      original payment means, with no fees, withholding only the cheapest-standard-delivery excess
      (EU: CRD Art. 13(1), (2))
- [ ] Goods returned within 14 days of notifying withdrawal unless the trader offered to collect;
      diminished-value liability limited to handling beyond what establishes the goods' nature and
      function, with none at all if the Art. 6(1)(h) notice was never given (EU: CRD Art. 14(1),
      (2); CJEU C-489/07 Messner)
- [ ] No clause claims a withdrawal exception beyond the closed Art. 16 list, and no formal
      requirement is imposed on the Annex I(B) model form beyond what the Annex itself specifies
      (EU: CRD Art. 16; Art. 11(1))

## Repair

- [ ] Where a Repair Information Form is offered, it is free except for a pre-disclosed diagnostic
      fee, given on a durable medium before the consumer is bound, and states all eleven required
      items including price (or how it will be calculated), time needed, and validity period (EU:
      Repair Directive Art. 4(1) to (4))
- [ ] The form's conditions, including price, are not altered for 30 days from issue unless a
      longer period is agreed (EU: Repair Directive Art. 4(5))
- [ ] For goods covered by repairability requirements: the manufacturer's repair duty is not
      refused solely because of cost or because another repairer already worked on the good (EU:
      Repair Directive Art. 5(1), (7))
- [ ] No contractual clause, hardware or software technique impedes an independent repairer's use
      of original, second-hand, compatible or 3D-printed spare parts without a legitimate,
      objective justification (EU: Repair Directive Art. 5(6))
- [ ] Where the manufacturer is established outside the Union, the repair document names whichever
      of its authorised representative, importer or distributor holds the repair obligation, in the
      fixed order the Directive sets (EU: Repair Directive Art. 5(3))
- [ ] For a contract concluded on or after 31 July 2026: choosing repair as the remedy extends the
      seller's statutory liability period once by 12 months, and the seller discloses this, together
      with the choice between repair and replacement, before providing the remedy (EU: SGD
      Art. 10(2a), 13(2a))

## Cross-border sales and non-discrimination

- [ ] The online interface serves every customer the same content and functionality regardless of
      detected nationality, residence or establishment, except where a stated legal requirement
      justifies blocking or redirecting, with the explanation given in the language of the
      interface the customer first tried to reach (EU: Geoblocking Regulation Art. 3)
- [ ] Price, payment terms and delivery terms in the three covered situations (delivery or
      collection the trader already offers, electronically supplied services, and services at a
      physical location) do not vary by the customer's nationality, residence or establishment, or
      a proxy for them such as IP address or card-issuing country (EU: Geoblocking Regulation
      Art. 4(1), 5(1))
- [ ] A reseller, distribution or franchise agreement contains no clause requiring a trader to act
      against the geo-blocking prohibitions for passive sales; any such clause is automatically void
      (EU: Geoblocking Regulation Art. 6(2))

## Governing law and jurisdiction

- [ ] A governing-law clause naming the trader's home member state discloses, in plain and
      intelligible language, that the consumer still keeps the mandatory consumer-protection rules
      of their own habitual-residence law (EU: Rome I Art. 6(1), (2); Case C-191/15 VKI v Amazon)
- [ ] No clause fixes the consumer's forum to a single named court in place of the two fora
      Brussels Ia already gives (the trader's domicile or the consumer's own domicile), except
      through the three narrow routes the Regulation allows (EU: Brussels Ia Art. 18(1), (2),
      Art. 19(1) to (3))
- [ ] A jurisdiction clause states expressly whether the named court's jurisdiction is exclusive or
      non-exclusive (EU: Brussels Ia Art. 25(1))
- [ ] Where terms are presented by click-wrap, the acceptance mechanism lets the consumer print or
      save the text of the terms before the contract is concluded (EU: Brussels Ia Art. 25(2); Case
      C-322/14 El Majdoub)

## Drafting and transparency, every clause

- [ ] Every written term is in plain, intelligible language; a term is substantively transparent
      only where the consumer can use clear, intelligible criteria to estimate its real economic
      consequences, not merely where every sentence is grammatically correct (EU: UCTD Art. 5;
      Guidance section 3.3.1)
- [ ] No term, individually negotiated or not, contradicts a mandatory statutory rule; where one
      does, the mandatory rule prevails for that question only (EU: UCTD Art. 1(2); Guidance section
      1.2.3)
- [ ] A term defining main subject matter or price adequacy is excluded from the unfairness test
      only where it is both correctly classified as such and genuinely transparent; a
      price-variation mechanism is never covered by this exclusion, however transparent (EU: UCTD
      Art. 4(2); Guidance sections 3.2, 3.2.2)
- [ ] No clause purports to waive, in advance, the consumer's protection under Art. 6(1) UCTD, and
      no settlement or novation tied to a term already found unfair asks the consumer to give up the
      resulting restitution claim (EU: UCTD Guidance sections 4.1, 4.3.1)
- [ ] No clause removes, limits or contracts around the consumer's entitlement to have a court
      assess a term's fairness of its own motion (EU: UCTD Guidance sections 5.2.2, 5.3.1)
- [ ] No arbitration, ADR or forum clause excludes or hinders the consumer's access to courts, or
      routes disputes to a forum materially distant from the consumer's residence (EU: UCTD Annex
      point (q); Guidance sections 5.4.2, 5.7)
- [ ] Every sanction clause (default interest, acceleration, a breach penalty) is checked together
      with every other sanction clause in the document, not one at a time, for cumulative
      proportionality (EU: UCTD Guidance section 3.4.3)
- [ ] Marketing and terms copy is reviewed for the impression it creates as a whole, not sentence by
      sentence; a correction buried below the fold or behind a link does not cure a misleading
      headline impression (EU: UCPD Art. 6(1); practice on overall presentation)
- [ ] A multi-language market has every required disclosure, including the standard terms
      themselves, translated into each targeted member state's language, not merely the marketing copy
      (EU: UCPD Art. 7)
- [ ] No clause or process step threatens a consequence the trader cannot legally impose, such as
      an unsupported debt-collection or blacklisting threat (EU: UCPD Art. 9(e))
- [ ] A consumer harmed by an unfair commercial practice keeps a right to proportionate and
      effective remedies: compensation and, where relevant, price reduction or contract
      termination (EU: UCPD Art. 11a)

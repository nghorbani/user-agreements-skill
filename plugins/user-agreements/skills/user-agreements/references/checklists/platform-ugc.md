# Platform and user-generated content checklist

Law as of: 2026-10-20

For a service that hosts, stores or disseminates content its users supply: a social platform, a
marketplace, a forum, a review site, a file-sharing or video-hosting service, or any site with a
comment, listing or upload feature. Work the stages in order. Classify the service first (mere
conduit, caching, hosting, online platform, marketplace, online search engine; EU/US; platform
size) since several duties apply only once that classification is made.

## Before drafting: classify the service

- [ ] Service classified as a mere conduit, caching service, or hosting service under the DSA, since the due diligence duties stack on top of each other by category (EU: Art. 3(g); Art. 11 to 15 for every intermediary, Art. 16 to 18 added for hosting)
- [ ] Hosting service checked against the online-platform definition (stores and disseminates recipient-provided information to the public), with the minor-ancillary-feature and inseparable-minor-functionality carve-outs considered before assuming the heavier Art. 20 to 28 duty set applies (EU: Art. 3(i))
- [ ] If consumers can conclude distance contracts with traders through the service: the Art. 29 to 32 marketplace duties flagged as additional, not as a replacement for the online-platform duties (EU: Art. 19(1); Art. 29(1))
- [ ] Micro or small enterprise status under Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC checked, including whether that status was lost in the last 12 months, since it exempts the online-platform and marketplace Sections unless the provider is a designated very large online platform (EU: Art. 19(1), (2); Art. 29(1), (2))
- [ ] Whether the provider is, or is approaching designation as, a very large online platform or very large online search engine under Art. 33 flagged as an open point: that status is outside this file's Art. 1 to 32 scope but triggers the heavier terms-summary, translation and reporting duties named below (EU: Art. 14(5), (6); Art. 24(2) to (4))
- [ ] US service classified by § 512 category for any content it stores, transmits, caches or links to (transitory communication, system caching, user-stored material, information location tool), since each category has its own separate conditions (US: 17 U.S.C. § 512(a) to (d), (n))
- [ ] Section 230 immunity checked against whether the service's own staff or systems originate, substantially edit or curate the specific content at issue, since that content falls outside the publisher/speaker shield (US: 47 U.S.C. § 230(c)(1), (f)(3))
- [ ] Whether the service, or a specific feature of it, is directed to children, or whether the operator has actual knowledge of collecting personal information from a child, checked per portion of the product rather than as one whole-product flag (US: 16 C.F.R. § 312.2 "Website or online service directed to children"; § 312.3)

## Terms and conditions content

- [ ] Any restriction on using the service for recipient-provided content, the content-moderation policies, procedures, measures and tools (including whether algorithmic decision-making or human review applies), and the internal complaint system's rules of procedure stated in clear, plain, intelligible, user-friendly, unambiguous language, publicly available in an easily accessible, machine-readable format (EU: Art. 14(1))
- [ ] Recipients notified of any significant change to the terms and conditions (EU: Art. 14(2))
- [ ] Where the service is primarily directed at, or predominantly used by, minors: the conditions and restrictions explained in a way minors can understand (EU: Art. 14(3))
- [ ] Misuse-suspension policy set out clearly and in detail, with concrete examples of the facts and circumstances considered and the suspension's stated duration, not a generic abusive-account statement (EU: Art. 23(4))
- [ ] Main parameters of any recommender system, and any options a recipient has to modify or influence them, stated in plain, intelligible language, with the criteria most significant in determining the suggested information and the reasons for their relative importance (EU: Art. 27(1), (2))
- [ ] Where more than one ordering option exists, a functionality offered, directly and easily accessible from the interface section where information is prioritised, letting the recipient select and change their preferred option at any time (EU: Art. 27(3))
- [ ] For a marketplace: trader's name, trade-register details and self-certification displayed clearly, accessibly and comprehensibly, at least on the interface page where the product or service information is presented (EU: Art. 30(7))
- [ ] A repeat-infringer termination policy adopted, reasonably implemented and communicated to subscribers and account holders, as a condition that applies across every US copyright safe harbor (US: 17 U.S.C. § 512(i)(1), (i)(2))
- [ ] A designated agent for copyright notices named with current name, address, phone number and email address, published on the public part of the service and separately filed with the Copyright Office; both steps are required for the hosting and linking safe harbors (US: 17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(2)(A), (B))
- [ ] Section 230 clause states only that the service is not treated as the publisher or speaker of another's content and that moderating it does not change that; it does not promise full or guaranteed review of user content (US: 47 U.S.C. § 230(c)(1); § 230(f)(3))
- [ ] Parental-control notice delivered to the customer when entering the agreement for an interactive computer service, identifying or linking to current providers of parental control protections (US: 47 U.S.C. § 230(d))
- [ ] For a service directed to children, or with actual knowledge of collecting from a child: direct notice to the parent precedes any collection, use or disclosure of the child's personal information, with content matching the purpose (general consent-seeking, voluntary participation, repeated contact, or child safety), each variant linking to the posted online notice (US: 16 C.F.R. § 312.4(c)(1) to (4))
- [ ] Posted online notice sits on the home or landing page and at every point personal information is collected from children, listing every operator collecting or maintaining it, what is collected and why, third-party recipients by name or category and the purpose of each disclosure, and the written retention policy (US: 16 C.F.R. § 312.4(d))

## Content intake and the notice-and-action flow

- [ ] Every authority order under Art. 9 (act against illegal content) or Art. 10 (provide information) checked, before being acted on, for its required elements: the legal basis for the order, a statement of reasons, precise identification of the content or information, redress information, and, for an Art. 9 order, its territorial scope; the affected recipient notified of the order and that same information no later than when effect is given (EU: Art. 9(2), (5); Art. 10(2), (5))
- [ ] On becoming aware of information giving rise to a suspicion that a criminal offence involving a threat to a person's life or safety has taken place, is taking place or is likely to take place, the law enforcement or judicial authorities of the Member State concerned promptly informed, or Europol where the Member State cannot be identified with reasonable certainty (EU: Art. 18(1), (2))
- [ ] A notice mechanism built for any individual or entity to flag specific items of allegedly illegal content: easy to access, user-friendly, submittable only by electronic means (EU: Art. 16(1))
- [ ] Notice intake requires: a substantiated explanation of why the content is alleged illegal; a clear indication of its exact electronic location; the notifier's name and email address, except for the Art. 3 to 7 Directive 2011/93/EU offence categories; and a bona fide statement that the information and allegations are accurate and complete (EU: Art. 16(2)(a) to (d))
- [ ] A notice meeting all four elements is treated as giving actual knowledge or awareness for the Art. 6 hosting exemption, where it lets a diligent provider identify the illegality without a detailed legal examination (EU: Art. 16(3))
- [ ] Confirmation of receipt sent without undue delay where the notice carries electronic contact information, and the notifier told, also without undue delay, of the decision taken, with redress information (EU: Art. 16(4), (5))
- [ ] Every notice processed in a timely, diligent, non-arbitrary, objective manner, with any use of automated means disclosed in the decision notification (EU: Art. 16(6))
- [ ] A notice submitted by an entity holding trusted-flagger status, acting within its designated area of expertise, is routed to priority, undue-delay processing automatically (EU: Art. 22(1))
- [ ] A pattern of low-quality notices from a trusted flagger is reported, with explanation and supporting documents, to the awarding Digital Services Coordinator rather than handled by unilateral de-prioritisation (EU: Art. 22(6), (7))
- [ ] US copyright notice of claimed infringement accepted where it substantially contains: a signature of a person authorised to act for the rights owner; identification of the work (or a representative list for multiple works at one site); identification of the allegedly infringing material with information reasonably sufficient to locate it; the complaining party's contact information; a good-faith-belief statement; and a statement under penalty of perjury that the information is accurate and the complaining party is authorised to act (US: 17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3)(A)(i) to (vi))
- [ ] A notice missing some elements but substantially containing identification of the material and contact information still counts toward actual knowledge once the service promptly tries to reach the notifier or takes other reasonable steps (US: 17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3)(B)(ii))
- [ ] Takedown acted on expeditiously once actual knowledge exists or facts make infringement apparent; monetisation and promotion mechanics kept platform-wide rather than tied to specific uploads, so no financial benefit is directly attributable to the infringing activity (US: 17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(1)(A), (B))
- [ ] No clause conditions a § 512 safe harbor on monitoring the service or affirmatively seeking facts of infringement, except to the extent a standard technical measure under § 512(i) requires it, and none conditions it on access, removal or disabling that is otherwise unlawful (US: 17 U.S.C. § 512(m)(1), (2))

## Decisions, statements of reasons and suspensions

- [ ] A clear and specific statement of reasons given to any affected recipient for every content or account restriction (visibility restriction, payment restriction or suspension, service suspension or termination, account suspension or termination), whether it started from a notice or an own-initiative investigation, except the deceptive-high-volume-commercial-content and Art. 9-order carve-outs (EU: Art. 17(1), (2), (5))
- [ ] The statement covers: what the decision entails, its territorial scope and duration; the facts and circumstances relied on, including whether based on a notice (with the notifier's identity only where strictly necessary) or an own-initiative investigation; any use of automated means; the legal or contractual ground and why the content is illegal or incompatible on that ground; and redress information covering internal complaints, out-of-court settlement and judicial redress (EU: Art. 17(3)(a) to (f))
- [ ] Statement written clear, easily comprehensible, and as precise and specific as reasonably possible, sufficient for the recipient to actually use the stated redress routes (EU: Art. 17(4))
- [ ] A misuse suspension of service to a recipient, or of processing that recipient's or complainant's notices, preceded by a prior warning and based on a case-by-case assessment against the absolute number, relative proportion, gravity and (where identifiable) intention behind the conduct (EU: Art. 23(1) to (3))
- [ ] Counter notification accepted where it substantially contains: the subscriber's signature; identification of the removed material and where it appeared before removal; a good-faith-belief statement under penalty of perjury that removal was a mistake or misidentification; and the subscriber's name, address, phone number, with consent to federal jurisdiction and acceptance of service of process (US: 17 U.S.C. § 512(g)(3)(A) to (D))
- [ ] Subscriber notified of removal, the counter notice forwarded to the original notifier with a statement that restoration follows in 10 business days, and the material restored between 10 and 14 business days after the counter notice, absent notice that the original notifier filed suit (US: 17 U.S.C. § 512(g)(2)(A) to (C)); no clause promises faster restoration or lets the service skip the forwarding step
- [ ] A service provider who knowingly and materially misrepresents that material is infringing, or that a takedown was a mistake or misidentification, is liable for the resulting damages, costs and fees; no clause states otherwise (US: 17 U.S.C. § 512(f))

## Internal complaints and out-of-court settlement

- [ ] An effective internal complaint-handling system in place, accessible electronically and free of charge, for at least six months from the notification, against a decision on a notice, a content removal or visibility restriction, a service suspension or termination, an account suspension or termination, or a monetisation restriction (EU: Art. 20(1), (2))
- [ ] System handles every complaint in a timely, non-discriminatory, diligent, non-arbitrary manner, reversing the original decision without undue delay where the complaint shows sufficient grounds (EU: Art. 20(4))
- [ ] Complainant informed without undue delay of the reasoned decision and of the out-of-court settlement possibility and other redress options (EU: Art. 20(5))
- [ ] Every complaint decided under the supervision of appropriately qualified staff, never solely by automated means (EU: Art. 20(6))
- [ ] Out-of-court settlement route signposted: a recipient addressed by an Art. 20(1) decision, including an unresolved complaint, may select any certified body; the recipient's unaffected right to go to court at any stage is preserved (EU: Art. 21(1))
- [ ] Fee rule stated accurately: where the body decides for the recipient, the provider pays all the body's fees and the recipient's other reasonable expenses; where it decides for the provider, the recipient owes nothing unless found to have acted manifestly in bad faith (EU: Art. 21(5))

## Trader onboarding and marketplace duties (where consumers conclude contracts with traders)

- [ ] Before letting a trader use the platform: name, address, phone number, email address, an identification document or electronic identification, payment account details, trade register and registration number where applicable, and a self-certification to offer only compliant products or services obtained (EU: Art. 30(1)(a) to (e))
- [ ] On receipt, best efforts made, through a freely accessible official database or interface or by requesting supporting documents, to assess whether the information is reliable and complete; the trader itself stays liable for its own information's accuracy (EU: Art. 30(2))
- [ ] A trader asked to remedy inaccurate, incomplete or outdated information without delay, and swiftly suspended until it complies (EU: Art. 30(3))
- [ ] Information retained securely for six months after the relationship ends, then deleted, and disclosed to a third party only where applicable law requires it (EU: Art. 30(5), (6))
- [ ] Online interface designed so a trader can supply its own economic-operator and product-safety information under applicable Union law, with best efforts to assess that information before onboarding and reasonable, recurring, random checks against official illegal-product databases afterward (EU: Art. 31(1) to (3))
- [ ] Where aware that an illegal product or service was offered through the platform: every consumer who purchased it in the preceding six months, whose contact details are held, informed of the illegality, the trader's identity and redress; where contact details are not held, the same information made public and easily accessible on the interface (EU: Art. 32(1), (2))

## Business users posting or trading through the platform (P2B)

Where recipients include business users who offer goods or services through the platform, the P2B Regulation adds its own duties toward those business users, separate from the DSA duties above that run toward the consumers who buy from them (EU: P2B Art. 1(2)).

- [ ] Terms and conditions state the grounds for a decision to restrict, suspend or terminate, in whole or in part, the service to a business user; where restricting or suspending for individual goods or services, the business user is given a statement of reasons on a durable medium no later than when it takes effect, and where terminating the whole of the service, a statement of reasons on a durable medium at least 30 days before termination takes effect, subject to the three narrow Art. 4(4) exceptions (which still require a statement of reasons without undue delay) (EU: P2B Art. 3(1)(c); Art. 4(1), (2), (4), (5))
- [ ] Main parameters determining a business user's ranking, and the reasons for their relative importance against other parameters, set out in the terms and conditions (or, for a search engine, in an easily and publicly available description), distinct from and in addition to the Art. 27 recommender-system disclosure this file requires toward consumers (EU: P2B Art. 5(1), (2))
- [ ] An internal complaint-handling system operating for business users, easily accessible and free of charge, handling complaints about alleged non-compliance with the Regulation, a technological issue, or a provider measure or behaviour affecting them, distinct from and in addition to the Art. 20 internal complaint system this file requires for recipients and consumers (EU: P2B Art. 11(1), (3))

## Advertising, interface design and recommender systems

- [ ] Online interface not designed, organised or operated in a way that deceives or manipulates recipients, or materially distorts or impairs a free, informed decision, except to the extent already covered by Directive 2005/29/EC or Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (EU: Art. 25(1), (2))
- [ ] Each advertisement labelled, clearly, concisely, unambiguously and in real time: that it is an advertisement; who it is presented on behalf of; who paid for it if different; and the main targeting parameters with how to change them (EU: Art. 26(1)(a) to (d))
- [ ] A functionality lets users declare that their own content is or contains commercial communications, and other users then see that marking clearly and in real time (EU: Art. 26(2))
- [ ] No advertisement presented based on profiling that uses special categories of personal data (EU: Art. 26(3))
- [ ] No advertisement presented based on profiling using a recipient's personal data where the provider is aware, with reasonable certainty, that the recipient is a minor, without an obligation to collect additional data to find out (EU: Art. 28(2), (3))
- [ ] Appropriate, proportionate measures in place for a high level of privacy, safety and security for minors on a service accessible to them (EU: Art. 28(1))
- [ ] Advertising tooling keeps targeted or behavioural advertising to children off by default, enabled only after the parent's separate, affirmative consent, never as a condition of access (US: 16 C.F.R. § 312.5(a)(2); 90 FR 16918)
- [ ] A child's participation in a game, prize or activity never conditioned on disclosing more personal information than reasonably necessary for that activity, even where consent for the broader collection exists (US: 16 C.F.R. § 312.7)

## Transparency reporting and records

- [ ] Annual content-moderation report published, machine-readable and easily accessible, covering authority-order counts and response times, notice counts and the action taken, own-initiative moderation data (including automated-tool use), internal complaint data, and automated-means descriptions; this duty does not apply to a micro or small enterprise provider that is not a very large online platform (EU: Art. 15(1), (2))
- [ ] For an online platform: the report adds out-of-court dispute data and misuse-suspension counts split between illegal content and unfounded notices or complaints (EU: Art. 24(1)(a), (b))
- [ ] For an online platform or search engine: average monthly active recipients in the Union published at least every six months in a public section of the interface (EU: Art. 24(2))
- [ ] A written data retention policy for children's personal information states the purposes, the business need for each category, and a deletion timeframe (which may be tied to a defined triggering event if a business need supports it and the trigger is disclosed); no clause describes indefinite retention as compliant (US: 16 C.F.R. § 312.10)
- [ ] Parental consent method matches whether the service discloses children's data to third parties: email-plus or text-plus only where it does not disclose; a heavier verification method otherwise (US: 16 C.F.R. § 312.5(b)(2))
- [ ] A written information security program maintained, sized to the sensitivity of children's personal information and to the operator's size, complexity and activities (US: 16 C.F.R. § 312.8(b))

## What the documents must not say

- [ ] No clause asserts unconditional non-liability for user content: the mere-conduit, caching and hosting exemptions each apply only while their conditions hold, and the hosting exemption fails where the recipient acts under the provider's authority or control (EU: Art. 4(1), 5(1), 6(1), (2))
- [ ] No clause suggests the DSA itself requires blanket pre-screening or general monitoring of content (EU: Art. 8)
- [ ] No internal complaint clause shortens the six-month minimum access window, treats reversal on sufficient grounds as discretionary, or routes the decision through automated means alone (EU: Art. 20(1), (2), (4), (6))
- [ ] No clause claims the Section 230 shield for content the service itself created or developed, in whole or in part (US: 47 U.S.C. § 230(f)(3))
- [ ] No clause states or implies that Section 230 limits federal criminal law, intellectual property law, or the Electronic Communications Privacy Act (US: 47 U.S.C. § 230(e)(1), (2), (4))
- [ ] No clause claims the Section 230(c)(1) or (c)(2)(B) immunity as a defense to a civil sex-trafficking claim tied to 18 U.S.C. § 1591, or to a related state criminal prosecution (US: 47 U.S.C. § 230(e)(5)(A) to (C))
- [ ] No clause claims the § 512(c) safe harbor where the designated agent was only published on the service, or only filed with the Copyright Office, and not both (US: 17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(2))
- [ ] No clause states that qualifying, or failing to qualify, for one § 512 safe harbor affects qualification for another; each is judged solely on its own conditions (US: 17 U.S.C. § 512(n))

## Open points to raise

- Whether the service, or a specific feature, is an online platform under Art. 3(i) at all, given the minor-ancillary-feature carve-out, since that classification decides whether the whole Art. 20 to 28 Section applies.
- Whether the provider is designated, or is approaching designation, as a very large online platform or search engine under Art. 33, since several duties named above (Art. 14(5), (6); 19(2); 24(2) to (4); 29(2)) turn on that status, which sits outside this file's own Art. 1 to 32 scope.
- Whether a specific review, editing or curation workflow crosses from carrying another party's content into creating or developing it, taking that content outside the Section 230(c)(1) shield; this is fact-specific and not settled by clause wording alone.
- Whether a specific revenue-share, bounty or premium-placement design counts as a financial benefit directly attributable to infringing activity under § 512(c)(1)(B) and § 512(d)(2).
- Whether a specific product's content, audience evidence and advertising mix cross the COPPA "directed to children" multi-factor threshold; this is fact-specific and should be documented per product.
- What the provider's internal threshold is for a significant change to the terms and conditions that triggers the Art. 14(2) notification duty, since the DSA does not define the term.

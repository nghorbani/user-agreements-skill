# EU B2B checklist

Law as of: 2026-10-20

For a provider dealing with business users, business customers or corporate website users rather
than consumers: an online intermediation platform or search engine with business users, a supplier
or recipient of data under the Data Act, a controller or processor under the GDPR, and the
governing-law and jurisdiction clauses of a business-facing contract. Covers the directive and
regulation floor only: the P2B Regulation, the Data Act, the GDPR's processor and transfer rules,
the e-Commerce Directive, and Rome I and Brussels Ia. National transposition, in particular
Germany's, decides points these instruments leave to member state law and is checked separately
(see the de-agb checklist and the references/de files for Germany).

## Before drafting: classify the relationship

- [ ] Provider classified as a provider of online intermediation services or of an online search
      engine, and confirmed it is established or resident in the Union and offers goods or
      services to Union consumers through that service, since the P2B Regulation turns on this
      regardless of the provider's own establishment or what law would otherwise apply (EU: P2B
      Art. 1(2))
- [ ] Each distinct intermediation service or search engine identified separately, including by
      device, embedded storefront, domain name, syndicated search feed or integrated third-party
      offer, since a single combined ranking explanation across several distinct services is
      normally not meaningful to any one of their users (EU: P2B Art. 5(1), (2); ranking guidance
      para. 26 to 31)
- [ ] Whether the provider is a small enterprise under Recommendation 2003/361/EC checked, since
      that status exempts only the Art. 11 internal complaint-handling duty and no other P2B
      article (EU: P2B Art. 11(5))
- [ ] Each party to a data-related contract classified as manufacturer, related-service provider,
      user, data holder or data recipient under the Data Act's own definitions, since the duties
      below attach to those roles, not to a label the contract gives them (EU: Data Act Art. 2(5),
      (6), (12), (13), (14))
- [ ] Whether a microenterprise or small enterprise exemption applies to the Data Act's Chapter II
      duties for data generated by the enterprise's own connected products or related services
      checked, including the disqualifying-partner and medium-sized-enterprise carve-outs (EU: Data
      Act Art. 7(1))
- [ ] Each party to a data-processing engagement classified as controller, joint controller or
      processor by its actual decisive influence over why and how data are processed, not by the
      label the contract gives it; a standardised, take-it-or-leave-it service contract does not by
      itself turn a customer into anything but controller for its own purposes (EU: GDPR Art. 4(7),
      (8); EDPB Guidelines 07/2020 para. 28 to 30, 39, 40, 76 to 78, 80)
- [ ] Whether two or more parties jointly decide both the purpose and the essential means of the
      same processing checked on the facts, since a shared benefit from the processing is only an
      indication of a joint purpose and never decisive alone (EU: GDPR Art. 26(1); EDPB Guidelines
      07/2020 para. 54, 55, 59, 60, 62, 68)
- [ ] Whether any planned cross-border transfer of personal data has a destination country, and if
      so which transfer tier applies to it (adequacy, an Art. 46 safeguard, or a specific Art. 49(1)
      derogation), identified per transfer rather than asserted generically (EU: GDPR Art. 44, 45,
      46, 49)
- [ ] Whether the contract will be concluded exclusively by email or an equivalent individual
      communication checked, since that narrows which e-Commerce Directive pre-order and
      order-placement duties still apply (EU: ECD Art. 10(4), Art. 11(3))
- [ ] Member state governing law and forum identified for the contract, since the Brussels Ia
      jurisdiction-agreement rules and Rome I's non-derogable-rule floor both depend on it (EU: Rome
      I Art. 3(3), (4); Brussels Ia Art. 25(1))

## Platform terms and conditions (online intermediation services and search engines)

- [ ] Terms and conditions drafted in plain and intelligible language and made easily available to
      business users at every stage of the relationship, including pre-contractually (EU: P2B
      Art. 3(1)(a), (b))
- [ ] Grounds for suspending, terminating or otherwise restricting a business user's access stated
      in the terms; a clause stating only that access may be restricted at the provider's
      discretion, without those grounds, is null and void (EU: P2B Art. 3(1)(c), 3(3))
- [ ] Additional distribution channels and affiliate programmes through which the provider might
      market the business user's goods or services disclosed (EU: P2B Art. 3(1)(d))
- [ ] General information on how the terms affect ownership and control of the business user's
      intellectual property rights given (EU: P2B Art. 3(1)(e))
- [ ] The identity of the business user supplying the goods or services kept clearly visible on the
      service (EU: P2B Art. 3(5))
- [ ] Terms-change notice given on a durable medium, with a reasonable period proportionate to the
      change and at least 15 days, longer where the business user needs it for technical or
      commercial adaptation; a change to a main ranking parameter is never an editorial change
      eligible for a shorter path (EU: P2B Art. 3(2); ranking guidance para. 128)
- [ ] Business user given a right to terminate before the notice period expires, taking effect
      within 15 days of the notice unless a shorter period already applies, and a working way to
      waive that period by written statement or clear affirmative action (EU: P2B Art. 3(2), third
      and fourth subparagraph)
- [ ] The two narrow exceptions to the 15-day minimum (a legal or regulatory obligation requiring
      an incompatible timeline, or an unforeseen and imminent cybersecurity danger) checked against
      the facts before relying on either (EU: P2B Art. 3(4)(a), (b))

## Restriction, suspension and termination of a business user

- [ ] Partial restriction or suspension of a business user's service for specific goods or services
      accompanied by a statement of reasons on a durable medium, no later than when it takes effect
      (EU: P2B Art. 4(1))
- [ ] Whole-service termination accompanied by a statement of reasons on a durable medium at least
      30 days before it takes effect, unless one of the three Art. 4(4) exceptions applies, in which
      case the statement is still given without undue delay (EU: P2B Art. 4(2), (4))
- [ ] Every statement of reasons references the specific facts or circumstances, including
      third-party notification contents, and the applicable Art. 3(1)(c) ground, unless a legal
      non-disclosure obligation applies or the business user has repeatedly infringed the terms,
      resulting in whole-service termination (EU: P2B Art. 4(5))
- [ ] Business user given access to the Art. 11 internal complaint-handling process to clarify the
      facts in every restriction, suspension or termination case (EU: P2B Art. 4(3), first sentence)
- [ ] A revoked restriction, suspension or termination reinstates the business user without undue
      delay, including restoring any data access lost because of the measure (EU: P2B Art. 4(3),
      second sentence)

## Ranking transparency

- [ ] Main parameters determining ranking, and the reasons for their relative importance against
      other parameters, set out in the terms and conditions (online intermediation services) or in
      an easily and publicly available, plain-language description kept up to date (search engines)
      (EU: P2B Art. 5(1), (2))
- [ ] Main parameters identified by what actually determines ranking in the mechanism in question,
      not by a generic reference to an algorithm or to artificial intelligence, and described with
      at least a second layer of explanatory detail beyond a bare label such as "quality" or "search
      history" (EU: P2B Art. 5(1), (2); ranking guidance para. 39, 47, 96 to 99)
- [ ] Where personalisation, consumer search behaviour, default settings or filters, cross-platform
      presence, external ratings or awards, third-party notifications, randomisation, housekeeping
      demotion, the provider's own ancillary services, machine learning, website-trust signals,
      fraud-prevention mechanisms, user reviews, or measures against illegal content function as a
      main parameter, each is described to the same level of detail as any other main parameter
      (EU: P2B Art. 5(1); ranking guidance para. 48 to 75)
- [ ] Where ranking can be influenced by direct or indirect remuneration from business users or
      corporate website users, that possibility and its effects on ranking are described to the same
      standard, and cross-referenced against the Art. 7(3)(c), (d) differentiated-treatment
      description (EU: P2B Art. 5(3); ranking guidance para. 88 to 92, 102)
- [ ] The ranking-parameter description meets the Art. 5(5) adequacy standard: it lets business
      users or corporate website users understand whether, how and to what extent ranking accounts
      for the characteristics of the goods or services offered to consumers, those characteristics'
      relevance to consumers, and, for a search engine, the corporate website user's own website
      design characteristics (EU: P2B Art. 5(5))
- [ ] A search engine that altered ranking or delisted a website following a third-party
      notification offers the corporate website user the chance to inspect that notification's
      contents (EU: P2B Art. 5(4))
- [ ] Ranking information withheld only where disclosure would, with reasonable certainty, enable
      consumer deception or manipulation of search results, or where it is a genuine trade secret
      meeting all three Trade Secrets Directive elements; commercial sensitivity alone, or never
      having disclosed before, is not a ground to withhold (EU: P2B Art. 5(6); ranking guidance
      para. 77, 78, 81, 82)
- [ ] A fraud-prevention disclosure states the mechanism's existence and a high-level account of its
      effect on ranking, withholding only the operational detail that would undermine its purpose
      (EU: P2B Art. 5(4); ranking guidance para. 72, 73)
- [ ] The ranking-parameter description kept up to date, with the frequency and effect of changes
      and of experiments such as A/B testing or machine learning assessed regularly (EU: P2B
      Art. 5(2); ranking guidance para. 120, 121)

## Ancillary goods, self-preferencing and parity

- [ ] Where ancillary goods or services, including financial products, are offered to consumers
      through the service by the provider or a third party, the terms describe their type and
      whether and how the business user may also offer its own (EU: P2B Art. 6)
- [ ] Differentiated treatment between the provider (or a business user it controls) and other
      business users described in the terms, with the main economic, commercial or legal
      considerations, covering each of data access, ranking or settings, remuneration, and access to
      connected services or technical interfaces; a single general sentence announcing that the
      provider may treat its own goods differently, without naming the considerations across all
      four areas, does not satisfy this (EU: P2B Art. 7(1) to (3))
- [ ] A clause restricting a business user from offering the same goods elsewhere under different
      conditions states the grounds, including the main economic, commercial or legal
      considerations, in the terms and makes them public; a parity clause with no stated grounds is
      exposed, and even a properly grounded clause can still be unlawful under another instrument
      (EU: P2B Art. 10(1), (2))
- [ ] No retroactive change to the terms and conditions except where a legal obligation requires it
      or the change benefits business users (EU: P2B Art. 8(a))
- [ ] Conditions for a business user to terminate the relationship stated in the terms (EU: P2B
      Art. 8(b))
- [ ] Technical and contractual data access the business user keeps, or loses, after the contract
      expires described in the terms (EU: P2B Art. 8(c))

## Data access (P2B)

- [ ] Terms describe, as four separate strands rather than one undifferentiated sentence: whether
      the provider itself has access to business-user or consumer data and to which categories;
      whether a business user has access to its own provided or generated data; whether a business
      user has access to aggregated cross-user data; and whether data is given to third parties,
      with the purpose and an opt-out where the sharing is not necessary for the service's proper
      functioning (EU: P2B Art. 9(2)(a) to (d))
- [ ] This data-access description checked against, not substituted for, whatever the GDPR,
      Directive (EU) 2016/680 or the ePrivacy Directive separately require (EU: P2B Art. 9(3))

## Internal complaint-handling system

- [ ] An internal system operating for business users to complain about alleged non-compliance with
      the Regulation, a technological issue, or a provider measure or behaviour that affects them,
      easily accessible, free of charge, handled within a reasonable time, applying transparency and
      equal treatment, and not folded into a generic support contact line (EU: P2B Art. 11(1), (3))
- [ ] Complaints duly considered with adequate follow-up, processed swiftly given their importance
      and complexity, and their outcome communicated individually in plain and intelligible language
      (EU: P2B Art. 11(2))
- [ ] Information on the system's functioning and effectiveness (total complaints, main types,
      average processing time, aggregated outcomes) published and verified at least annually, unless
      the provider is a small enterprise under Recommendation 2003/361/EC (EU: P2B Art. 11(4), (5))

## Data Act: pre-contract disclosures for connected products and related services

- [ ] Before a contract for a connected product's purchase, rent or lease, the type, format and
      estimated volume of data the product can generate, whether it generates data continuously and
      in real time, how long and where it stores data, and how the user can access, retrieve or
      erase it disclosed clearly and comprehensibly (EU: Data Act Art. 3(2)(a) to (d))
- [ ] Before a related-service contract, the nine separate Art. 3(3) items disclosed: expected
      product data volume, access and retention arrangements; related-service data volume and the
      same detail; whether the provider itself will use readily available data and for what
      purposes, and whether third parties may; the provider's identity and contact point; how the
      user requests and ends third-party sharing; the user's right to complain to the Art. 37
      competent authority; whether the provider holds trade secrets in the data and if not who does;
      and the contract's duration and termination (EU: Data Act Art. 3(3)(a) to (i))

## Data Act: access and sharing commitments

- [ ] Product and related service designed so product data and related-service data, with
      interpreting metadata, are by default easily, securely, free of charge, in a structured,
      machine-readable format, directly accessible to the user where relevant and technically
      feasible (EU: Data Act Art. 3(1))
- [ ] Where direct access is not built in, readily available data made accessible to the user
      without undue delay, free of charge, at the data holder's own quality, on simple electronic
      request where feasible (EU: Data Act Art. 4(1))
- [ ] On the user's request, the same data made available to a named third party, to the same
      standard, without undue delay and free of charge to the user, except readily available data
      from pre-market testing of a connected product unless the third party's use is contractually
      permitted (EU: Data Act Art. 5(1), (2))
- [ ] Access and sharing choices verified against no more information than necessary, with no log
      data kept beyond what the request's execution and the data infrastructure's security and
      maintenance require (EU: Data Act Art. 4(5), 5(4))
- [ ] Interface offering access or sharing choices presents them neutrally, with no design that
      subverts or impairs the user's decision-making (EU: Data Act Art. 4(4))
- [ ] A contractual right to restrict or prohibit data access where it would undermine a security
      requirement and cause a serious health, safety or security risk reserved only with notice to
      the Art. 37 competent authority where sharing is refused on this ground, and with the user's
      right to challenge the restriction before that authority, a dispute settlement body, or a
      court (EU: Data Act Art. 4(2), (3))

## Data Act: trade secrets in shared data

- [ ] Trade-secret-protected parts of shared data, including in metadata, identified, with
      proportionate technical and organisational measures agreed with the recipient before
      disclosure (EU: Data Act Art. 4(6), 5(9))
- [ ] A right to withhold or suspend trade-secret data sharing reserved only where no agreement on
      measures is reached, or the recipient fails to implement or undermines them, with the decision
      substantiated in writing to the recipient without undue delay and the Art. 37 authority
      notified (EU: Data Act Art. 4(7), 5(10))
- [ ] An exceptional refusal of a specific request, where the data holder is itself the trade
      secret holder and is highly likely to suffer serious economic damage despite the agreed
      measures, substantiated in writing on objective grounds, with the same notification (EU: Data
      Act Art. 4(8), 5(11))
- [ ] A user or third party facing withholding, suspension or refusal given the right to challenge
      it before the Art. 37 authority or a dispute settlement body, without prejudice to a court (EU:
      Data Act Art. 4(9), 5(12))

## Data Act: third-party recipient obligations and the gatekeeper exclusion

- [ ] A third-party recipient bound not to: make exercising the user's Art. 5 and 6 choices unduly
      difficult through non-neutral framing or manipulative design; use the data for profiling
      beyond what the requested service needs; pass the data to another third party without a
      further user contract and the same trade-secret measures; pass the data to a gatekeeper under
      Art. 3 of Regulation (EU) 2022/1925; use the data to develop a competing connected product or
      derive insights about the data holder's economic situation; use the data in a way that harms
      the product's or service's security; or disregard the agreed trade-secret measures (EU: Data
      Act Art. 6(1), (2)(a) to (g))
- [ ] No clause stops a consumer user making the data it received available to other parties (EU:
      Data Act Art. 6(2)(h))
- [ ] No document treats a gatekeeper under Art. 3 of Regulation (EU) 2022/1925 as an eligible Art.
      5 third party, and no document lets a gatekeeper solicit or incentivise a user to route data
      to one of its services, or receive data the user obtained under Art. 4(1) (EU: Data Act
      Art. 5(3)(a) to (c))

## Data Act: the B2B unfair-terms test

- [ ] Every unilaterally imposed term on data access, data use, or liability and remedies for breach
      or termination of a data-related obligation checked against the Art. 13(4) per-se-unfair list
      (excluding or limiting liability for intentional acts or gross negligence, excluding the other
      party's remedies or the imposing party's own liability, or giving the imposing party the sole
      right to decide conformity or interpret a term) and against the Art. 13(5) rebuttable-unfair
      list (inappropriately limiting the other party's remedies or extending its liability; accessing
      or using the other party's data to its significant detriment; stopping the other party using
      its own data or capturing its value; stopping reasonable-period termination; stopping the
      other party obtaining a copy of its own data; unreasonably short termination notice weighed
      against switching difficulty and harm; or unilateral price or condition change with no valid
      reason and no termination right) (EU: Data Act Art. 13(4), (5))
- [ ] A unilateral-change clause for an indeterminate-duration contract states a valid reason,
      requires reasonable advance notice, and gives a no-cost termination right on the change, as
      the only way to escape the Art. 13(5) presumption for that kind of clause (EU: Data Act
      Art. 13(5), second subparagraph)
- [ ] Whether a term was unilaterally imposed, meaning the other party could not influence its
      content despite trying to negotiate, assessed on the facts, with the burden on the party that
      supplied the term; a term defining the contract's main subject matter or the price-for-data
      adequacy is outside this test entirely, and the parties cannot contract out of Art. 13 itself
      (EU: Data Act Art. 13(6), (8), (9))
- [ ] A term that reflects mandatory Union law, or the law that would apply if the contract were
      silent, is not treated as unfair on that ground alone (EU: Data Act Art. 13(2))

## Data Act: cloud and data-processing-service switching (source-provider duties)

- [ ] The written switching contract, available to the customer before signing in a storable and
      reproducible form, includes the nine Art. 25(2) items: a switching or porting clause within a
      30-day transitional period from the end of the notice period, with reasonable assistance,
      continuity care and disclosed continuity risks; exit-strategy support; a termination clause on
      successful switching or at the end of the notice period if the customer chooses erasure
      instead; a maximum two-month notice period to start switching; an exhaustive portable-data
      category list; an exhaustive exempt-category list for data specific to the provider's own
      internal functioning, not impeding or delaying switching; a minimum 30-day data-retrieval
      period after the transitional period; a full-erasure guarantee after that period expires; and
      the switching charges, limited to what Art. 29 permits (EU: Data Act Art. 25(1), (2))
- [ ] These switching duties fall on the source provider the customer is switching away from, not on
      the destination provider receiving the customer (EU: Data Act Art. 24)
- [ ] Where the 30-day transitional period is technically unfeasible, the customer notified within
      14 working days of the switching request, with a justified reason and an alternative period of
      no more than seven months, maintaining service continuity throughout (EU: Data Act Art. 25(4))
- [ ] Customer given information on switching and porting procedures, formats and known
      restrictions, and a reference to an up-to-date public register of data structures, formats and
      interoperability specifications (EU: Data Act Art. 26(a), (b))
- [ ] Provider's website publishes and keeps current the jurisdiction governing the ICT
      infrastructure for each service and a description of measures against unlawful international
      governmental access to non-personal Union data, with every contract listing that website (EU:
      Data Act Art. 28(1), (2))
- [ ] Prospective customer given clear information, before contracting, on standard fees, early
      termination penalties and any reduced switching charges, with any highly complex, costly or
      near-impossible-to-switch service separately flagged (EU: Data Act Art. 29(4), (5))
- [ ] A reduced, capped switching charge only between 11 January 2024 and 12 January 2027; no
      switching charge at all from 12 January 2027, so a terms document drafted before that date
      needs a planned revision (EU: Data Act Art. 29(1) to (3))
- [ ] A custom-built-for-one-customer or evaluation-only service names exactly which Chapter VI
      obligations do not apply, before contracting (EU: Data Act Art. 31(1) to (3))
- [ ] No switching clause claims the Art. 25 to 30 duties require developing new technology,
      disclosing intellectual property or trade secrets, or compromising security; a clause can rely
      on this limit but not read it more broadly than stated (EU: Data Act Art. 30(6))

## The processor contract (data processing agreement)

- [ ] A written contract or other legal act binds every processor toward the controller, fixing the
      subject matter, duration, nature and purpose of the processing, the data type and categories
      of data subjects, and the controller's obligations and rights; a contract that only repeats
      this statutory list without saying concretely how each duty is met does not satisfy this (EU:
      GDPR Art. 28(3), chapeau, (9); EDPB Guidelines 07/2020 para. 95, 103, 112, 113)
- [ ] The contract binds the processor to: process only on documented instructions, including on
      transfers, and flag an instruction it considers unlawful; keep staff access under
      confidentiality; take the Art. 32 security measures; respect the Art. 28(2), (4)
      sub-processor conditions; assist with data subject rights requests and with Art. 32 to 36
      compliance; delete or return all data at the controller's choice after the engagement ends, or
      justify continued storage under law; and make compliance information available and permit
      audits, including inspections, at a cost that does not deter exercising this right (EU: GDPR
      Art. 28(3)(a) to (h); EDPB Guidelines 07/2020 para. 145)
- [ ] The contract states which party decides deletion versus return of data at the end of the
      engagement, the timescale and manner, and lets the controller change that choice before the
      engagement ends (EU: GDPR; EDPB Guidelines 07/2020 para. 139 to 142)
- [ ] A change the processor proposes to standard terms embedding the agreement is directly notified
      to, and approved by, the controller; publishing a change on the processor's website does not
      satisfy this (EU: EDPB Guidelines 07/2020 para. 110)
- [ ] Sub-processor engagement needs either the controller's prior specific written authorisation
      per engagement, or a general written authorisation with notice of each intended addition or
      replacement and a real chance to object; the same data protection obligations imposed on the
      sub-processor by a further contract, with the first processor staying fully liable to the
      controller for the sub-processor's performance (EU: GDPR Art. 28(2), (4); EDPB Guidelines
      07/2020 para. 155, 157, 158, 160)
- [ ] On a personal data breach, the processor notifies the controller without undue delay after
      becoming aware of it, not on a fixed schedule, since the controller's own 72-hour clock to the
      supervisory authority runs from the controller becoming aware (EU: GDPR Art. 33(1), (2))
- [ ] The processor assists the controller with a data protection impact assessment and, where high
      residual risk remains, with the Art. 36 prior consultation (EU: GDPR Art. 28(3)(f), 35(1))
- [ ] Either the Commission's Art. 28(7) controller-to-processor standard contractual clauses, or an
      individually negotiated agreement, used to meet Art. 28(3) and (4), with neither option
      preferred by the Regulation; the clauses, where embedded in a broader contract such as a
      service-level agreement, clearly identified as such in a dedicated annex or schedule (EU: GDPR
      Art. 28(6); EDPB Guidelines 07/2020 para. 104 to 106, 103)
- [ ] A document does not describe a genuinely joint controller arrangement as a plain
      controller-processor relationship, and does not label a provider a processor while in
      substance letting it use the data for purposes of its own beyond the engaged service (EU:
      GDPR Art. 4(7), (8), 26(1))

## Joint controller arrangements

- [ ] Joint controllers determine and agree their respective GDPR compliance responsibilities in a
      transparent arrangement, in particular for data subject rights and the Art. 13, 14 information
      duties, with its essence made available to data subjects (EU: GDPR Art. 26(1), (2))
- [ ] No arrangement states or implies that data subjects may contact only a designated contact
      point; a data subject may exercise GDPR rights against any joint controller whatever the
      arrangement's internal terms say (EU: GDPR Art. 26(3))
- [ ] Each joint controller keeps its own Art. 30 record and, where Art. 37(1) applies, its own DPO
      designation, regardless of how responsibilities are otherwise split (EU: GDPR; EDPB Guidelines
      07/2020 para. 169, 170)

## International transfers of personal data

- [ ] For every cross-border transfer, the tier relied on identified and documented per transfer:
      Tier 1, an Art. 45 adequacy decision; Tier 2, an Art. 46 safeguard (standard contractual
      clauses, binding corporate rules, an approved code of conduct or certification, or, with prior
      authorisation, ad hoc clauses); or Tier 3, a specific Art. 49(1) derogation, used only where
      the first two tiers are both unavailable (EU: GDPR Art. 44, 45, 46, 49)
- [ ] A document names the concrete mechanism relied on for a transfer rather than reciting a
      generic reference to applicable law or restating the GDPR's own wording as if that were itself
      the safeguard (EU: GDPR Art. 44 to 50)
- [ ] Where the Commission's Art. 46(2)(c) transfer clauses are used, the applicable Module
      identified (One, controller to controller; Two, controller to processor; Three, processor to
      processor; Four, processor to controller) and Annexes I, II and, where relevant, III completed
      (EU: GDPR; Decision (EU) 2021/914 Clause 1(b), 8)
- [ ] Before relying on the transfer clauses, a Clause 14 warranty given and documented on the
      destination country's laws and practices, including public-authority access measures, taking
      account of the transfer's specific circumstances and any supplementary safeguards; no clause
      promises the importer will never comply with a lawful government request, since Clause 15.2
      requires legality review and minimisation instead (EU: Decision (EU) 2021/914 Clause 14(a),
      (b), 15.2)
- [ ] Neither Commission clause set (the Art. 28(7) processor clauses or the Art. 46(2)(c) transfer
      clauses) substantively rewritten beyond selecting the applicable option or Module and
      completing the Annexes (EU: Decision (EU) 2021/915 Clause 2(a); Decision (EU) 2021/914 Clause
      2(a))
- [ ] Where the user is not the data subject whose personal data is requested, no document commits
      to sharing that data with the user or a third party absent a valid GDPR legal basis, and no
      failure to agree transmission arrangements is allowed to hinder a data subject's portability
      right (EU: Data Act Art. 4(12), 5(7), (8), (13); GDPR Art. 20)

## Provider identification and pre-order information (e-Commerce Directive)

- [ ] Provider's name, geographic establishment address, an email address for rapid direct contact,
      trade register entry and number where registered, supervisory authority particulars where an
      authorisation scheme applies, regulated-profession details where relevant, and VAT number
      where applicable, rendered on a standing, permanently accessible page, not only during
      onboarding (EU: ECD Art. 5(1)(a) to (g))
- [ ] Every price statement states clearly and unambiguously whether tax and delivery costs are
      included, attached to the price statement itself rather than cured by a breakdown elsewhere
      (EU: ECD Art. 5(2))
- [ ] Commercial communications clearly identifiable as such, and the person they are made on behalf
      of clearly identifiable (EU: ECD Art. 6(a), (b))
- [ ] Where the provider's home member state permits promotional offers or competitions, they are
      clearly identifiable as such, with easily accessible, clear and unambiguous participation
      conditions (EU: ECD Art. 6(c), (d))
- [ ] Before the order, the technical steps to conclude the contract, whether the contract will be
      filed and made accessible, the means to identify and correct input errors, and the languages
      offered, given clearly, comprehensibly and unambiguously, unless the contract is concluded
      exclusively by email or an equivalent individual communication (EU: ECD Art. 10(1)(a) to (d),
      (4))
- [ ] Codes of conduct the provider subscribes to named, with how to consult them electronically,
      unless the contract is concluded exclusively by email (EU: ECD Art. 10(2), (4))
- [ ] Contract terms given to the recipient made storable and reproducible rather than displayed
      once, in every channel including one that runs exclusively by email (EU: ECD Art. 10(3), (4))
- [ ] An order placed through technological means acknowledged without undue delay by electronic
      means, with the order and acknowledgement treated as received once accessible to the addressed
      party, not once sent (EU: ECD Art. 11(1))
- [ ] A working, not merely disclosed, mechanism for the recipient to spot and fix input errors
      before placing the order, unless the contract is concluded exclusively by email (EU: ECD
      Art. 11(2), (3))
- [ ] Whether the business counterparty is, by actual agreement rather than a one-sided label, not a
      consumer, checked before relying on any Art. 10 or 11 business-to-business carve-out (EU: ECD
      Art. 10(1), (2), 11(1), (2))

## Governing law and jurisdiction

- [ ] Governing law and jurisdiction treated as two separate questions under two separate
      instruments: Rome I for which country's substantive law applies, Brussels Ia for which
      country's courts may hear the dispute (EU: Rome I; Brussels Ia)
- [ ] Where every other element of the situation sits in one country, or one or more member states,
      a chosen governing law checked against that country's or Union law's non-derogable rules,
      which the choice cannot displace (EU: Rome I Art. 3(3), (4))
- [ ] Absent a valid choice, the applicable law identified by contract type under Art. 4 (seller's,
      provider's, franchisee's or distributor's habitual residence, or the property's or auction's
      location, as the type requires), falling back to the characteristic-obligation residence, then
      a manifestly closer connection, then the closest connection overall; carriage of goods and
      passengers follow their own Art. 5 rules instead (EU: Rome I Art. 4(1) to (4), 5)
- [ ] A jurisdiction clause in writing or evidenced in writing, in a form according with the
      parties' established practice, or, in international trade, in a form according with a widely
      known and regularly observed usage in that trade; an electronic communication providing a
      durable record is equivalent to writing, and a click-wrap mechanism that lets the party print
      and save the terms before the contract is concluded satisfies this (EU: Brussels Ia Art. 25(1)
      (a) to (c), (2); Case C-322/14 El Majdoub)
- [ ] A jurisdiction clause rests on a genuine consensus between the parties, clearly and precisely
      shown, as a question logically prior to and separate from the form question above (EU:
      Brussels Ia Art. 25(1); El Majdoub paragraphs 29, 30)
- [ ] The clause states expressly whether the named court's jurisdiction is exclusive or
      non-exclusive, since Art. 25(1) makes it exclusive by default only absent a contrary agreement
      (EU: Brussels Ia Art. 25(1))
- [ ] A jurisdiction clause treated as independent of the rest of the contract, so a challenge to the
      contract's own validity is not on its own a ground to contest the jurisdiction clause (EU:
      Brussels Ia Art. 25(5))
- [ ] The chosen governing law checked against the overriding mandatory provisions of the forum
      actually hearing the dispute, which the choice can never displace, and, where relevant, against
      the overriding mandatory provisions of the place of performance that would render performance
      unlawful (EU: Rome I Art. 9(1) to (3))

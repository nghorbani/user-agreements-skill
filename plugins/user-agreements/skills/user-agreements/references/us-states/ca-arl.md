# California Automatic Renewal Law

Citation: Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code §§ 17600 to 17606, as amended by Stats. 2024, Ch. 515 (AB 2863)
Version: Article 9 as amended by AB 2863, in force since 1 July 2025
Official text: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&division=7.&title=&part=3.&chapter=1.&article=9.
Law as of: 2026-10-20

## When it applies

- Applies to any business that makes an automatic renewal offer or a continuous service offer to a consumer in California, where "consumer" means an individual acquiring goods, services, money, or credit by purchase or lease for personal, family, or household use (§ 17601(a)(4)). A business-to-business subscription falls outside the article.
- "Automatic renewal" covers a free-to-pay conversion or a paid plan that renews automatically at the end of a definite term; "continuous service" covers a plan with a free-to-pay conversion, or one that continues until the consumer cancels (§ 17601(a)(1), (a)(5)). Draft to the broader label whenever the product auto-charges after a free or discounted start.
- The amended definitions and duties bind a contract entered into, amended, or extended on or after 1 July 2025 (§ 17601(b), § 17602(j)). An older contract that has not been amended or extended since that date is not yet covered by this version of the text; flag it as an open point rather than drafting against it.
- Six categories of business are exempt from the whole article regardless of contract date: franchise-of-a-political-subdivision or CPUC-licensed services; CPUC, FCC, or Federal Energy Regulatory Commission regulated affiliates; entities regulated by the Department of Insurance; alarm company operators under § 7590.2; banks, bank holding companies, credit unions, or other state- or federally-licensed financial institutions; and service contract sellers and administrators regulated under Article 4.5 of Chapter 20, Division 3 (§ 17605(a) to (f)). Confirm the seller's regulatory status before drafting any of the clauses below for it.

## What the documents must say

- State in the offer screen, before the agreement is fulfilled and in visual proximity (temporal proximity for a spoken offer) to the consent request: that the plan continues until the consumer cancels, the cancellation policy, the recurring charge and whether and how it may change, the renewal term length (or that service is continuous, unless the consumer chose the term), and any minimum purchase obligation (§ 17601(a)(2)(A) to (E), § 17602(a)(1)).
- If the offer includes a free gift or trial, add a clear and conspicuous statement of the price to be charged once the trial ends, or of how the price will change at that point (§ 17602(a)(1)).
- After consent, send a retainable acknowledgment containing the same offer terms, the cancellation policy, and how-to-cancel instructions. Where a free gift or trial is involved, the acknowledgment must also explain how to cancel and must let the consumer cancel before being charged for the goods or services (§ 17602(a)(3)).
- Where a pre-billing notice is owed (see Dates), state all six of: that the plan auto-renews unless cancelled; the length and any additional terms of the renewal period; the cost or cost range and, where relevant, the charge frequency; one or more cancellation methods; for an electronic notice, a link or another reasonably accessible electronic route into the cancellation process; and the business's contact information (§ 17602(a)(8)(A) to (F)).
- Where an annual reminder is owed (see Dates), disclose the product or service the renewal covers, the charge frequency and amount, and the means to cancel (§ 17602(h)(2)(A) to (C)).
- Where a material change, including a fee change, is made to accepted terms, give clear and conspicuous, retainable notice of the change together with how-to-cancel information before the change takes effect (§ 17602(g)(1), (g)(2)).
- Describe, in the acknowledgment, the cancellation mechanism offered: a toll-free number, an email address, a postal address if the business bills the consumer directly, or another cost-effective, timely, and easy-to-use method (§ 17602(c)(1)).

## What the documents must not say

- No information in the contract may interfere with, detract from, contradict, or otherwise undermine the consumer's ability to give affirmative consent to the automatic renewal or continuous service (§ 17602(a)(5)).
- Do not misrepresent, expressly or by implication, a material fact about the transaction, including whether the plan auto-renews or anything material about the underlying good or service (§ 17602(a)(7)).
- Do not condition a consumer's ability to cancel on anything beyond what the statute allows: a business may show a discount, a retention benefit, or cancellation-effect information only together with the required cancellation trigger (see Prescribed wording), never instead of it or ahead of disclosing it (§ 17602(e)(1), (e)(2)).
- If online acceptance is offered, do not require any further step, beyond the account authentication allowed under § 17602(d)(3), that obstructs or delays immediate online termination (§ 17602(d)(1)).
- Do not describe the online-termination duty as covering the whole contract: it reaches only the automatic renewal and continuous service terms, with the rest of the agreement governed by other applicable law (§ 17602(d)(2)).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- The checkout captures a distinct affirmative consent action before the first charge, including for a promotional or discounted initial price, and keeps a verifiable record of it for at least three years, or one year after the contract ends, whichever is longer (§ 17602(a)(2), (a)(4), (a)(6)).
- The system computes and sends the pre-billing notice automatically within its window (3 to 21 days, or 15 to 45 days, see Dates), picking only one notice where both windows would otherwise apply, and skips the 31-day notice only where the consumer did not contract electronically and the business holds no electronic contact method for them (§ 17602(b)(1)(B)).
- An account area, profile, or device or user settings screen carries a prominent direct cancellation link or button, or the business offers an immediately usable termination email requiring no further information, so that online cancellation is at-will and immediate; authentication may gate it only where the consumer already has an account, and an offline route stays open for anyone unwilling or unable to authenticate (§ 17602(d)(1), (d)(3)).
- A phone cancellation channel, if offered, is staffed to answer promptly in business hours, and a cancellation voicemail is actioned, or returned, within one business day (§ 17602(c)(2)(A), (c)(2)(B)).
- Cancellation stays available in the medium the consumer used to activate the plan, or the medium they normally use with the business; where activation was by phone, a retainable cancellation phone number is kept available and shown on the company website (§ 17602(f)).
- The system sends a yearly reminder for any annual plan, and a material-change or fee-change notice ahead of the applicable window, through the activation medium or the consumer's accustomed medium (§ 17602(g), (h)).
- Unconsented shipments are tracked and written off: the recipient owes nothing back, including no shipping cost, so no collection or invoicing process should run against them (§ 17603).

## Prescribed wording

- PW-US-CA-1: see `references/prescribed-wording.md`. Quote: "cancel" (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17602(e)(1)). Where it goes: the fixed trigger phrase a phone agent must disclose to the consumer, before presenting any retention offer or cancellation-effect information, as the way to complete cancellation at any time; once the consumer says it (or words to that effect), the agent must process the cancellation promptly.
- PW-US-CA-2: see `references/prescribed-wording.md`. Quote: "click to cancel" (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17602(e)(2)). Where it goes: the label on the online cancellation link or button that must be shown prominently and continuously, in proximity to any retention offer or cancellation-effect information displayed during an online cancellation attempt; using it must promptly trigger cancellation.

## Dates

- In force since 1 July 2025: the AB 2863 amendments to §§ 17601 and 17602 apply to a contract entered into, amended, or extended on or after that date (§ 17601(b), § 17602(j)).
- Timing windows: pre-billing notice for a free gift, trial, or promo price over 31 days, 3 to 21 days before it ends (§ 17602(b)(1)(A)); a free promotional item or gift given by the business that differs from the subscribed product itself does not count as the triggering free gift for this notice (§ 17602(b)(1)(C)); pre-billing notice for a one-year-plus auto-renewing term, 15 to 45 days before renewal, which controls alone if both windows would apply (§ 17602(b)(2)); fee change notice, 7 to 30 days before the change (§ 17602(g)(2)); consent-verification record, kept 3 years or 1 year post-termination, whichever is longer (§ 17602(a)(6)).
- Article-wide timing rule: every duty in § 17602 applies only before the initial order completes, except the acknowledgment (a)(3), the pre-billing notice (a)(8), and the subdivision (b) notice, which may follow; the material-change duty must precede the change; the annual reminder runs every year (§ 17602(i)(1) to (4)).

## Open points to raise

- Whether the product's renewal and pricing design, including a usage-based or freemium-to-paid step, falls within "automatic renewal," "continuous service," or "free-to-pay conversion" as defined (§ 17601(a)(1), (a)(5), (a)(6)).
- Whether the seller or an affiliate falls into one of the six exempt categories, which would remove the article's duties entirely rather than just change their content (§ 17605).
- Who sends the phone, online, and website-displayed cancellation channels, and who keeps the consent-verification record current, where billing runs through an app store rather than the seller's own checkout; whether the app store's own cancellation path can stand in for the "same medium as activation" requirement (§ 17602(f), (a)(6)).
- Whether the product's price-change or tier-change mechanics count as a "material change" beyond a fee change, which would need the subdivision (g)(1) notice on a timeline counsel has to set, since the statute fixes no numeric window for that notice (§ 17602(g)(1)).
- Whether an existing contract predating 1 July 2025 has since been amended or extended in a way that pulls it under the amended text, since an unamended older contract may still sit under the prior version of §§ 17601 to 17602 (§ 17601(b), § 17602(j)).

# Texas Data Privacy and Security Act

Citation: Tex. Bus. and Com. Code ch. 541
Version: as published on the Texas statute site, current through the 89th Legislature, 2nd Called Session, 2025
Official text: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/BC/htm/BC.541.htm
Law as of: 2026-10-20

## When it applies

- Applies only to a person that conducts business in Texas or produces a product or service Texas residents consume, that processes personal data or sells personal data, and that is not a small business as the United States Small Business Administration defines that term, except that a small business still owes the sensitive-data-sale consent duty below (Sec. 541.002(a)). The chapter sets no consumer-count or revenue threshold; the SBA small-business status is the only size test.
- "Consumer" means an individual who is a Texas resident acting only in an individual or household context; it excludes an individual acting in a commercial or employment context (Sec. 541.001(7)).
- "Controller" determines, alone or jointly with others, the purpose and means of processing personal data; "processor" processes personal data on a controller's behalf; "third party" is any person other than the consumer, the controller, the processor, or an affiliate of the controller or processor (Sec. 541.001(8), (23), (32)).
- The chapter does not apply at all to a state agency or political subdivision of Texas, a financial institution or data covered by Title V of the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, a HIPAA covered entity or business associate governed by the federal privacy, security, and breach-notification rules, a nonprofit organization, an institution of higher education, or an electric utility, power generation company, or retail electric provider as defined in the Utilities Code (Sec. 541.002(b)). The nonprofit and higher-education exemptions apply to the whole entity rather than to specific activities.
- A separate 17-item information-level exemption list covers categories of data regardless of who holds them, including HIPAA protected health information and health records, patient-identifying information under 42 U.S.C. Section 290dd-2, human-subjects-research information under named federal regimes, Fair Credit Reporting Act consumer-report data, Driver's Privacy Protection Act data, Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act data, Farm Credit Act data, employment- and contractor-role data limited to that role's context, emergency contact information, and related benefits-administration data (Sec. 541.003(1) to (17)).
- The chapter does not apply to processing personal data in the course of a purely personal or household activity (Sec. 541.004).
- A controller or processor that complies with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act's verifiable-parental-consent requirements for data collected online is treated as complying with any chapter requirement to obtain parental consent (Sec. 541.005).
- "Sensitive data" is a category of personal data that includes data revealing racial or ethnic origin, religious belief, a mental or physical health diagnosis, sexuality, or citizenship or immigration status; genetic or biometric data processed to uniquely identify an individual; personal data collected from a known child; and precise geolocation data (Sec. 541.001(29)).
- "Precise geolocation data" means information derived from technology, including GPS-level coordinates or other mechanisms, that directly identifies an individual's specific location with precision and accuracy within a radius of 1,750 feet; it excludes the content of communications and data from or connected to advanced utility metering infrastructure or utility equipment (Sec. 541.001(21)).
- "Biometric data" means data generated by automatic measurement of an individual's biological characteristics, including a fingerprint, voiceprint, eye retina or iris, or other unique biological pattern used to identify a specific individual; it excludes a photograph or video or audio recording or data generated from one, and HIPAA-covered treatment, payment, or operations information (Sec. 541.001(3)).
- "Known child" means a child in circumstances where the controller has actual knowledge of, or wilfully disregards, the child's age; "child" means an individual under 13 (Sec. 541.001(5), (17)).
- "Sale of personal data" means sharing, disclosing, or transferring personal data for monetary or other valuable consideration by the controller to a third party; it excludes disclosure to a processor acting for the controller, disclosure to a third party to provide a product or service the consumer requested, disclosure or transfer to the controller's own affiliate, disclosure of information the consumer intentionally made available to the general public through a mass media channel without restricting it to a specific audience, and disclosure or transfer to a third party as a merger or acquisition asset (Sec. 541.001(28)).
- "Targeted advertising" means displaying an advertisement selected based on personal data obtained from a consumer's activities over time and across nonaffiliated websites or applications, to predict the consumer's preferences; it excludes advertising based on activity within the controller's own sites, advertising based on the context of a current search or visit, advertising responding to the consumer's own request for information, and processing solely to measure advertising performance, reach, or frequency (Sec. 541.001(31)).
- "Consent" means a clear affirmative act signifying a consumer's freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous agreement to process personal data, including a written statement by any means including electronic, or any other unambiguous affirmative action (Sec. 541.001(6)).
- A person processing personal data under one of the exemptions in Subchapter E must keep that processing necessary, proportionate, and limited to the purpose claimed, and bears the burden of showing it qualifies for the exemption and meets those limits (Sec. 541.204(a), (c)).

## What the documents must say

- The controller limits the collection of personal data to what is adequate, relevant, and reasonably necessary for the purposes disclosed to the consumer, and establishes, implements, and maintains reasonable administrative, technical, and physical data security practices appropriate to the volume and nature of the personal data at issue (Sec. 541.101(a)(1), (2)).
- A privacy notice, reasonably accessible and clear, stating: the categories of personal data processed, including sensitive data where applicable; the purpose for processing personal data; how consumers may exercise their consumer rights, including the appeal process; the categories of personal data shared with third parties, where applicable; the categories of third parties receiving that data, where applicable; and a description of the request-submission methods (Sec. 541.102(a)). A notice should pair each category of data with its own stated purpose rather than one general purpose covering every category.
- Where the controller sells personal data to third parties or processes personal data for targeted advertising, a clear and conspicuous disclosure of that processing and how a consumer may exercise the opt-out right (Sec. 541.103).
- Two or more secure and reliable methods for a consumer to submit a rights request, fitted to how consumers normally interact with the controller, the need for secure and reliable communication, and the controller's ability to authenticate the requester (Sec. 541.055(a)). A controller that maintains a website must provide a website submission mechanism, except that a controller operating exclusively online with a direct consumer relationship need only provide an email address for that purpose (Sec. 541.055(c), (d)).
- The five consumer rights: confirm whether the controller is processing the consumer's personal data and access it; correct inaccuracies in the consumer's personal data, with regard to the data's nature and the purposes of its processing (Sec. 541.051(b)(2)); delete personal data provided by or obtained about the consumer; obtain a portable copy of personal data the consumer previously provided, where available in digital format and technically feasible; and opt out of processing for targeted advertising, sale of personal data, or profiling toward a decision with a legal or similarly significant effect (Sec. 541.051(b)). A parent or legal guardian of a known child may exercise these rights on the child's behalf (Sec. 541.051(a)).
- A response to a rights request without undue delay and no later than the 45th day after receipt, extendable once by 45 more days when reasonably necessary given complexity or the number of requests, with notice of the extension and its reason given within the initial 45-day period (Sec. 541.052(b)). A refusal notice, within the same 45-day window, stating the justification and instructions to appeal (Sec. 541.052(c)).
- Response free of charge at least twice a year per consumer; a manifestly unfounded, excessive, or repetitive request may carry a reasonable fee or be declined, with the controller bearing the burden of showing it meets that description (Sec. 541.052(d)).
- Where the controller obtained a consumer's personal data from a source other than the consumer, it may satisfy a deletion request under Sec. 541.051(b)(3) either by retaining a record of the deletion request and the minimum data necessary to keep the consumer's personal data deleted from its records, without using the retained data for any other purpose, or by opting the consumer out of processing that personal data for any purpose the chapter does not exempt (Sec. 541.052(f)(1), (2)).
- An appeal process, conspicuously available and similar in process to the rights-request submission process, with a written decision and reasons no later than the 60th day after the appeal is received; on denial, the controller gives the consumer the attorney general's online complaint mechanism (Sec. 541.053).
- Support for a consumer's authorized agent to opt out of targeted advertising and sale on the consumer's behalf, using technology such as a website link, browser setting or extension, or global device setting; the controller honors the request if it can verify, with commercially reasonable effort, the consumer's identity and the agent's authority (Sec. 541.055(e)).
- Where the controller sells sensitive personal data, the notice "NOTICE: We may sell your sensitive personal data." posted in the same location and manner as the privacy notice (Sec. 541.102(b)).
- Where the controller sells biometric personal data, the notice "NOTICE: We may sell your biometric personal data." posted in the same location and manner as the privacy notice (Sec. 541.102(c)).
- Consent before processing a consumer's sensitive data, or, for a known child's sensitive data, processing under the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act's verifiable-parental-consent framework instead (Sec. 541.101(b)(4), Sec. 541.005).
- A small business relying on the SBA exception from Sec. 541.002(a)(3) still needs the consumer's prior consent before selling sensitive personal data; this duty is not displaced by its otherwise-exempt status (Sec. 541.107(a)).
- A processing contract between a controller and a processor stating clear processing instructions, the nature and purpose of processing, the type of data processed, the duration of processing, and the rights and obligations of both parties, plus processor commitments to: keep every person processing the data under a confidentiality duty; delete or return all personal data at the controller's direction once the service ends, unless law requires retention; make compliance information available to the controller on reasonable request; allow and cooperate with reasonable assessments by the controller or its designated assessor, or instead arrange a qualified independent assessor's report on request; and bind any subcontractor by written contract to the processor's own requirements (Sec. 541.104(b), (c)).
- A data protection assessment, conducted and documented, for processing for targeted advertising, sale of personal data, profiling presenting a reasonably foreseeable risk of unfair or deceptive treatment, unlawful disparate impact, financial, physical, or reputational injury, an offensive intrusion on solitude or private affairs, or other substantial injury, processing of sensitive data, and any processing presenting a heightened risk of harm, weighing the processing's benefits to the controller, the consumer, other stakeholders, and the public against the risk to consumer rights as mitigated by safeguards, and factoring in the use of deidentified data, consumer expectations, the processing context, and the controller-consumer relationship (Sec. 541.105(a), (b)).
- A deidentified-data governance statement covering all three duties together: reasonable measures that the data cannot be associated with an individual, a public commitment not to attempt reidentification, and a contractual obligation on any recipient of the data to comply with the chapter (Sec. 541.106(a)).

## What the documents must not say

- No clause letting the controller process personal data for a purpose that is neither reasonably necessary to nor compatible with the purpose disclosed to the consumer, unless the chapter otherwise allows it or the controller obtains the consumer's consent (Sec. 541.101(b)(1)).
- No clause letting the controller process personal data in violation of state or federal laws prohibiting unlawful discrimination against consumers (Sec. 541.101(b)(2)).
- No clause requiring a consumer to create a new account to exercise a right under this chapter; the controller may require use of an existing account instead (Sec. 541.055(b)).
- No clause waiving or limiting, in any way, a consumer right under Sec. 541.051, 541.052, or 541.053; any such provision is contrary to public policy, void, and unenforceable (Sec. 541.054).
- No opt-out mechanism built on a default opted-out or opted-in setting; the technology must instead require the consumer to make an affirmative, freely given, unambiguous choice, must not unfairly disadvantage another controller, and must be consumer-friendly and easy for the average consumer to use (Sec. 541.055(f)).
- No consent built on acceptance of a general or broad terms-of-use document that bundles personal-data-processing descriptions with unrelated information, on hovering over, muting, pausing, or closing a piece of content, or on agreement obtained through a dark pattern; none of these counts as consent under the chapter's definition (Sec. 541.001(6)(A) to (C)).
- No clause discriminating against a consumer for exercising a chapter right, such as denying goods or services, charging a different price or rate, or providing a different quality level because of that exercise; this does not bar a different price, rate, quality, or selection, including a no-fee offer, tied to the consumer's voluntary participation in a bona fide loyalty, rewards, premium-features, discount, or club-card program, or following the consumer's own opt-out (Sec. 541.101(b)(3), (c)).
- No charging for a rights-request response more than twice a year per consumer, except where the controller can show the request is manifestly unfounded, excessive, or repetitive (Sec. 541.052(d)).
- No paraphrase of the sensitive-data-sale or biometric-data-sale notice in place of the exact statutory sentence; a looser description such as sharing sensitive information with partners does not meet Sec. 541.102(b) or (c) even where it conveys the same substance.
- No clause representing that a consumer may bring a private claim against the controller for a chapter violation; no private right of action exists under this chapter or any other law for such a violation (Sec. 541.156).
- No clause suggesting a municipal or county ordinance imposes additional data-processing duties beyond this chapter; the chapter supersedes and preempts any such local regulation (Sec. 541.205).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- The privacy, account, and checkout pages name the specific rights-request submission channel or channels, support authentication without forcing a new account, and route a request into the 45-day (plus a possible single 45-day extension) response pipeline with a documented appeal path reaching a decision by day 60 of the appeal (Sec. 541.051 to 541.053, 541.055).
- Where sensitive or biometric personal data is sold, the controller's systems post the matching prescribed notice in the same place and format as the privacy notice, not merely referenced from it (Sec. 541.102(b), (c)).
- Where the controller sells personal data or processes it for targeted advertising, an opt-out mechanism exists that recognizes an authorized agent's verified request and does not rely on a default setting (Sec. 541.055(e), (f), Sec. 541.103).
- A documented data protection assessment exists before or during any processing for targeted advertising, sale, risky profiling, sensitive-data processing, or other heightened-risk processing, kept ready to produce to the attorney general on a civil investigative demand, and treated as confidential (Sec. 541.105).
- A deidentified-data program, where relied on, keeps all three required elements (technical measures, a public non-reidentification commitment, and contractual recipient obligations) rather than only one or two (Sec. 541.106(a)).
- A vendor or processor contract exists for every processor relationship, with the six required terms and either controller-led audit cooperation or an independent assessor's report (Sec. 541.104(b), (c)).
- An internal compliance process can produce the attorney general's required 30-day cure within that window, with the four written cure-statement elements, since a timely and complete cure forecloses a civil-penalty action of up to $7,500 for each violation (Sec. 541.154, Sec. 541.155(a)).

## Prescribed wording

- Sale of sensitive personal data notice, verbatim (Sec. 541.102(b)):

  > NOTICE: We may sell your sensitive personal data.

  Posted in the same location and in the same manner as the privacy notice.

- Sale of biometric personal data notice, verbatim (Sec. 541.102(c)):

  > NOTICE: We may sell your biometric personal data.

  Posted in the same location and in the same manner as the privacy notice. Both notices may run together if the controller sells both categories.

## Dates

- July 1, 2024: Chapter 541 as a whole takes effect (Acts 2023, 88th Leg., R.S., Ch. 995, H.B. 4).
- January 1, 2026: Sec. 541.104's processor-duties subsection is amended to add references to the security of personal data collected, stored, or processed by an artificial intelligence system as defined by Sec. 551.001, and to Chapter 521 breach notification (Acts 2025, 89th Leg., R.S., Ch. 1174, H.B. 149, Sec. 3). No other section of the chapter carries an amendment in the statute-site text read 2026-10-21.

## Open points to raise

- Whether a specific data flow falls within one of the five exclusions from "sale of personal data" (Sec. 541.001(28)(A) to (E)) on the facts, since labeling a disclosure as a mere "service" does not change which exclusion, if any, actually applies.
- Whether the controller's processing of biometric data brings it within the chapter's sensitive-data consent gate (Sec. 541.101(b)(4)) only where the biometric data is processed to uniquely identify an individual, as the definition requires, or whether a specific product's use of biometric data meets that identification purpose at all.
- Whether the controller meets the Small Business Administration's small-business definition for purposes of Sec. 541.002(a)(3), a fact question this file does not resolve for any specific entity.
- Whether a specific processing activity needs a data protection assessment under the "heightened risk of harm" catch-all at Sec. 541.105(a)(5), a standard the statute does not itself define further.

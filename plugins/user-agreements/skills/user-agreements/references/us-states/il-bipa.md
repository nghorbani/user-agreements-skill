# Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act

Citation: 740 ILCS 14/1 to 14/99
Version: 740 ILCS 14, with the P.A. 103-769 amendments, effective August 2, 2024
Official text: https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=3004&ChapterID=57
Law as of: 2026-10-20

## When it applies

- Applies to any "private entity" that collects, captures, purchases, receives through trade, or otherwise obtains, or that possesses, a "biometric identifier" or "biometric information" (740 ILCS 14/15). "Private entity" means any individual, partnership, corporation, limited liability company, association, or other group, however organized; it excludes a State or local government agency, and excludes any court of Illinois, a clerk of the court, or a judge or justice (740 ILCS 14/10).
- "Biometric identifier" means a retina or iris scan, fingerprint, voiceprint, or scan of hand or face geometry. It does not include writing samples, written signatures, photographs, human biological samples used for valid scientific testing or screening, demographic data, tattoo descriptions, or physical descriptions such as height, weight, hair color, or eye color. It also excludes donated organs, tissues, or parts as defined in the Illinois Anatomical Gift Act, blood or serum stored on behalf of transplant recipients or potential recipients and obtained or stored by a federally designated organ procurement agency, biological materials regulated under the Genetic Information Privacy Act, information captured from a patient in a health care setting or collected, used, or stored for health care treatment, payment, or operations under HIPAA, and an X-ray or other diagnostic image or film of the human anatomy used to diagnose, prognose, or treat an illness or other medical condition, or to further validate scientific testing or screening (740 ILCS 14/10).
- "Biometric information" means any information, regardless of how it is captured, converted, stored, or shared, based on an individual's biometric identifier and used to identify an individual. It does not include information derived from an item or procedure excluded from the biometric identifier definition (740 ILCS 14/10).
- "Confidential and sensitive information" means personal information that can be used to uniquely identify an individual or an individual's account or property, including a genetic marker, genetic testing information, a unique identifier number to locate an account or property, an account number, a PIN, a pass code, a driver's license number, or a social security number (740 ILCS 14/10).
- "Written release" means informed written consent, an electronic signature, or, in the employment context, a release executed by an employee as a condition of employment (740 ILCS 14/10).
- "Electronic signature" means an electronic sound, symbol, or process attached to or logically associated with a record and executed or adopted by a person with the intent to sign the record (740 ILCS 14/10).
- A financial institution or an affiliate of a financial institution subject to Title V of the federal Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act of 1999 falls outside the Act entirely (740 ILCS 14/25(c)).
- A contractor, subcontractor, or agent of a State agency or local unit of government, when working for that agency or unit of government, falls outside the Act entirely (740 ILCS 14/25(e)).

## What the documents must say

- Before any collection, capture, purchase, or receipt through trade of a person's biometric identifier or biometric information, a private entity must first: inform the subject or the subject's legally authorized representative in writing that a biometric identifier or biometric information is being collected or stored; inform the subject or representative in writing of the specific purpose and length of term for which it is being collected, stored, and used; and obtain a written release executed by the subject or representative (740 ILCS 14/15(b)(1) to (3)). All three steps come before collection, not after.
- The purpose and retention-term notice must be specific; a generic statement that data is collected as needed, or as permitted by law, does not supply the specific purpose and length of term Sec. 15(b)(2) requires.
- A private entity in possession of biometric identifiers or biometric information must develop a written policy, made available to the public, establishing a retention schedule and guidelines for permanently destroying the data (740 ILCS 14/15(a)).
- A private entity in possession of the data must store, transmit, and protect it from disclosure using the reasonable standard of care within the entity's own industry, and in a manner that is the same as or more protective than how the entity stores, transmits, and protects its other confidential and sensitive information (740 ILCS 14/15(e)(1), (2)).

## What the documents must not say

- No clause that sells, leases, trades, or otherwise profits from a person's biometric identifier or biometric information; Sec. 15(c) bars this outright, with no stated exception (740 ILCS 14/15(c)).
- No disclosure, redisclosure, or dissemination clause reaching beyond the four grounds Sec. 15(d) permits: the subject's or representative's consent to the disclosure; completing a financial transaction the subject or representative requested or authorized; a requirement of State or federal law or municipal ordinance; or a valid warrant or subpoena issued by a court of competent jurisdiction (740 ILCS 14/15(d)(1) to (4)). A broader clause, for example covering disclosure to partners for general business purposes, exceeds what Sec. 15(d) allows unless it in fact reduces to one of the four grounds.
- No clause that treats a general terms-of-service acceptance, continued use, or inferred assent as the "written release" Sec. 15(b)(3) requires; the release must be the subject's informed written consent, electronic signature, or, for an employee, a release signed as a condition of employment, executed with respect to the biometric collection itself (740 ILCS 14/10, 14/15(b)(3)).
- No retention clause that keeps biometric identifiers or biometric information past the earlier of the initial collection purpose being satisfied, or three years after the individual's last interaction with the private entity, except where held under a valid warrant or subpoena from a court of competent jurisdiction (740 ILCS 14/15(a)).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- A pre-collection notice and release flow for any Illinois resident's retina or iris scan, fingerprint, voiceprint, or hand or face geometry scan: written notice of collection or storage, written notice of the specific purpose and retention length, and a written release, each completed before the data is collected, captured, purchased, or received through trade (740 ILCS 14/15(b)).
- A published, public written policy setting a retention schedule and destruction guidelines, and a destruction process that runs at the earlier of purpose-satisfaction or three years after the individual's last interaction, absent a valid warrant or subpoena (740 ILCS 14/15(a)).
- No data pipeline that sells, leases, trades, or profits from biometric data, and no disclosure path to a third party outside the four permitted grounds (740 ILCS 14/15(c), (d)).
- Storage and transmission controls for biometric data that meet the entity's own industry's reasonable standard of care, and that are at least as protective as the entity's handling of its other confidential and sensitive information (740 ILCS 14/15(e)).
- Any person aggrieved by a Sec. 15 violation has a right of action directly, in an Illinois circuit court or as a supplemental claim in federal district court, with no notice-and-cure step before it accrues (740 ILCS 14/20(a)). A negligent violation carries liquidated damages of $1,000 or actual damages, whichever is greater, per violation; an intentional or reckless violation carries $5,000 or actual damages, whichever is greater, per violation; both carry reasonable attorneys' fees, costs, expert witness fees, other litigation expenses, and other relief including an injunction (740 ILCS 14/20(a)(1) to (4)). Repeated collection of the same data from the same person using the same method under Sec. 15(b) is a single recoverable violation (740 ILCS 14/20(b)). Repeated disclosure of the same data from the same person to the same recipient using the same method under Sec. 15(d) is a single recoverable violation (740 ILCS 14/20(c)). A Sec. 15 gap in a product's biometric collection or disclosure flow is live exposure the moment it occurs, not a defect that can be assumed fixable before suit.

## Prescribed wording

None. Sec. 15 prescribes required content, specificity, and timing for the pre-collection notices and the release, not exact phrasing.

## Dates

- October 3, 2008: the original Act, P.A. 95-994, takes effect, including Sec. 1, 5, 15, and 25. Sec. 20(a), the right of action itself, originates in this same 2008 Act, though the source's source-note for Sec. 20 as a whole (subsections (a), (b), and (c) together) is dated 2024, not 2008.
- August 2, 2024: P.A. 103-769 takes effect, amending the Sec. 10 definitions and, within Sec. 20, adding the repeat-instance aggregation rules at Sec. 20(b) and (c).
- Sec. 30 was repealed internally, effective January 1, 2009.

## Open points to raise

- Whether a specific data flow is a "private entity" activity at all, since a State or local government agency, an Illinois court, a clerk of the court, or a judge or justice falls outside the Act regardless of what data is involved (740 ILCS 14/10).
- Whether the entity or an affiliate is subject to Title V of the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, or is a contractor, subcontractor, or agent working for a State agency or local unit of government, either of which removes the entity from the Act entirely for that activity (740 ILCS 14/25(c), (e)).
- Whether a specific retention schedule and destruction guideline, drafted internally, is in fact made available to the public as Sec. 15(a) requires, a fact question this file cannot settle from the statute alone.
- Whether a release obtained through a bundled consent flow in fact meets the written-release standard on its specific facts, since the Act gives no safe harbor for a release bundled with unrelated terms and this file states the standard, not a ruling on any particular flow.

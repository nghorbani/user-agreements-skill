# Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act

Citation: Va. Code § 59.1-575 to § 59.1-585
Version: current, full chapter text as consolidated at law.lis.virginia.gov
Official text: https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodefull/title59.1/chapter53/
Law as of: 2026-10-20

## When it applies

- Applies to a person that conducts business in Virginia, or produces products or services targeted to Virginia residents, and that during a calendar year either (i) controls or processes the personal data of at least 100,000 consumers, or (ii) controls or processes the personal data of at least 25,000 consumers and derives over 50 percent of gross revenue from the sale of personal data (§ 59.1-576(A)).
- "Consumer" means a natural person who is a Virginia resident acting only in an individual or household context; it excludes a person acting in a commercial or employment context (§ 59.1-575).
- Five categories of entity are exempt regardless of size: a Commonwealth or political-subdivision body, authority, board, bureau, commission, district or agency; a financial institution or data covered by Title V of the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act; a HIPAA covered entity or business associate; a nonprofit organization; and an institution of higher education (§ 59.1-576(B)).
- Fourteen categories of information are exempt regardless of who holds them, including protected health information and health records under HIPAA and Title 32.1, patient identifying information under 42 U.S.C. § 290dd-2, human-subjects research data under the listed federal frameworks, consumer-report data regulated under the federal Fair Credit Reporting Act, driver data under the Driver's Privacy Protection Act, education records under FERPA, Farm Credit Act data, and employment-context or emergency-contact data tied to an individual's role as an applicant, employee, agent or contractor (§ 59.1-576(C)).
- A controller or processor that meets the verifiable parental consent requirements of the federal Children's Online Privacy Protection Act is deemed to meet this chapter's parental consent duties (§ 59.1-576(D)).
- "Sensitive data" covers data revealing racial or ethnic origin, religious beliefs, a mental or physical health diagnosis, sexual orientation, or citizenship or immigration status; genetic or biometric data processed to uniquely identify a person; personal data collected from a known child; and precise geolocation data (§ 59.1-575).
- "Precise geolocation data" means information derived from technology, including GPS latitude and longitude or other mechanisms, that directly identifies a natural person's specific location within a radius of 1,750 feet; it excludes communications content and data from advanced utility metering infrastructure (§ 59.1-575).
- "Sale of personal data" means its exchange for monetary consideration to a third party; it excludes disclosure to a processor, disclosure to fulfill a consumer-requested product or service, disclosure to an affiliate, disclosure of information the consumer intentionally made available to the general public via mass media without restricting it to a specific audience, and transfer as a business asset in a merger, acquisition, bankruptcy or similar transaction (§ 59.1-575).
- "Targeted advertising" means selecting an advertisement for a consumer based on personal data from that consumer's activity over time and across nonaffiliated sites or applications to predict preferences or interests; it excludes advertising based on a controller's own site or application, on the context of a current visit or search, on a consumer's own request for information or feedback, and data processed solely to measure or report advertising performance, reach or frequency (§ 59.1-575).
- A "social media platform" is a public or semipublic internet service with Virginia users that lets users interact socially, build a profile, populate a list of connections, and create or post content viewable by other users; a service that is only email or direct messaging does not qualify on that function alone, and a service that is primarily news, sports, entertainment, ecommerce, interactive gaming, or provider-selected content, where any chat or comment function is incidental to that content, does not qualify on that function alone (§ 59.1-575).

## What the documents must say

- Disclose in a privacy notice: the categories of personal data processed, the purpose of processing, how a consumer may exercise and appeal rights under § 59.1-577, the categories of personal data shared with third parties (if any), and the categories of those third parties (if any) (§ 59.1-578(C)).
- If the controller sells personal data to third parties or processes personal data for targeted advertising, add a clear and conspicuous disclosure of that processing and of how a consumer can opt out of it (§ 59.1-578(D)).
- Describe in the privacy notice one or more secure and reliable means to submit a rights request, suited to how consumers normally interact with the controller and to the need to authenticate the request (§ 59.1-578(E)).
- State the appeal process for a declined request: conspicuously available, similar in form to the request channel, and naming the 60-day response window (§ 59.1-577(C)).

## What the documents must not say

- No clause may waive or limit a consumer's rights under § 59.1-577; any such clause is void and unenforceable as against public policy (§ 59.1-578(B)).
- Do not require a consumer to open a new account to exercise rights under § 59.1-577; the controller may require use of an existing account (§ 59.1-578(E)).
- Do not condition a minor's full one-hour daily use of a social media platform on a price increase or a withheld, degraded or lower-quality service; a platform may still decline to offer a feature that needs a known minor's personal data, or may offer a known minor a different price, rate, level, quality or selection, including a free offering, where that is reasonably related to exercising rights under or complying with this chapter (§ 59.1-577.1(E)).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- Honor, on an authenticated request, the right to confirm processing and access data, correct inaccuracies, delete data provided by or obtained about the consumer, receive a portable copy of data the consumer provided (for processing carried out by automated means), and opt out of targeted advertising, sale, or profiling in furtherance of decisions with legal or similarly significant effects (§ 59.1-577(A)).
- A known child's parent or legal guardian may invoke these rights on the child's behalf for the child's personal data (§ 59.1-577(A)).
- Respond without undue delay and in all cases within 45 days of receipt; one 45-day extension is available when reasonably necessary given complexity or request volume, if the controller tells the consumer of the extension and its reason within the initial 45 days (§ 59.1-577(B)(1)).
- If declining a request, tell the consumer within the same 45-day window, with the reason and appeal instructions (§ 59.1-577(B)(2)).
- Fulfill requests free of charge up to twice a year per consumer; a manifestly unfounded, excessive or repetitive request may carry a reasonable administrative fee or be declined, with the controller bearing the burden of showing that character (§ 59.1-577(B)(3)).
- Where the controller cannot authenticate a request by commercially reasonable efforts, it need not act on it and may ask the consumer for more information to authenticate (§ 59.1-577(B)(4)).
- For a deletion request concerning data the controller obtained from a source other than the consumer, compliance is satisfied by either retaining a deletion record and the minimum data needed to keep the data deleted from company records, without using that retained data for any other purpose, or by opting the consumer out of processing that data for any purpose except one this chapter exempts (§ 59.1-577(B)(5)).
- Run an appeal process for declined requests: conspicuous, similar to the request channel, decided in writing within 60 days with reasons, and, on a denied appeal, an online or other route to reach the Attorney General with a complaint (§ 59.1-577(C)).
- Limit data collection to what is adequate, relevant and reasonably necessary for the disclosed purposes; do not process for a purpose neither reasonably necessary to nor compatible with the disclosed purpose without the consumer's consent (§ 59.1-578(A)(1), (2)).
- Maintain reasonable administrative, technical and physical security practices scaled to the volume and nature of the personal data (§ 59.1-578(A)(3)).
- Do not process in violation of discrimination law, and do not discriminate against a consumer for exercising chapter rights, including by denying goods or services or charging or degrading quality differently; this does not require offering a product that needs data the controller does not collect, and does not bar a different price, rate, level, quality or selection, including a free offering, tied to an opt-out exercised under § 59.1-577 or to voluntary participation in a bona fide loyalty, rewards, premium-features, discount or club-card program (§ 59.1-578(A)(4)).
- Do not process sensitive data without the consumer's consent; for sensitive data from a known child, process in line with the federal Children's Online Privacy Protection Act instead (§ 59.1-578(A)(5)).
- Do not sell or offer for sale precise geolocation data (§ 59.1-578(A)(6)).
- For a known child's data, run a double gate: an activity limit (no targeted advertising, sale, or significant-effect profiling unless reasonably necessary to provide the service; no processing beyond the disclosed or compatible purpose; no retention beyond what the service needs; geolocation collection only where reasonably necessary, time-boxed to that need, and signaled to the child for the collection's duration) plus COPPA-compliant verifiable parental consent before any of it runs (§ 59.1-578(F)).
- For this section, a minor is a natural person younger than 16, defined for this section only (§ 59.1-577.1(A)). On a social media platform, use a commercially reasonable method, such as a neutral age screen, to tell whether a user is a minor, and cap a minor's use at one hour per day per service, adjustable only by verifiable parental consent to raise or lower the limit; treat a user as a minor whenever the user's device signals that status, including through a browser plug-in, privacy setting or device setting; use age-determination data only for age determination and age-appropriate experiences; and grant a consenting parent no additional access to or control over the minor's data or account beyond the consent itself (§ 59.1-577.1(B) to (D)).
- Put a binding, written contract behind every processor relationship, covering confidentiality duties for personnel, end-of-service deletion or return of data (unless law requires retention), compliance information on the controller's reasonable request, cooperation with controller or independent assessments (with a report provided on request), and a written flow-down contract for any subcontractor (§ 59.1-579(B)). A processor follows the controller's instructions and helps the controller meet its own duties, including rights-request response, security and breach-notice obligations, and data protection assessments (§ 59.1-579(A)). Neither party's liability for its own role is reduced by this division of duties, and the controller-or-processor determination is fact-based, not fixed by contract label (§ 59.1-579(C), (D)).
- Conduct and document a data protection assessment, weighing benefits to the controller, consumer, other stakeholders and the public against the risk to consumers as mitigated by safeguards, for: processing for targeted advertising; sale of personal data; profiling that carries a reasonably foreseeable risk of unfair or deceptive treatment, unlawful disparate impact, financial, physical or reputational injury, an offensive intrusion on seclusion or private affairs, or other substantial injury; processing of sensitive data; and any processing activity carrying a heightened risk of harm (§ 59.1-580(A), (C)). An online service, product or feature directed to consumers the controller actually knows are children needs its own assessment covering the feature's purpose, the categories of a known child's data it processes, and the purposes of that processing (§ 59.1-580(B)). One assessment may cover a comparable set of similar processing activities, and an assessment made to comply with another law may serve here if its scope and effect are reasonably comparable (§ 59.1-580(E), (F)). This duty reaches only processing activities created or generated after 2023-01-01, and is not retroactive (§ 59.1-580(G)).
- Maintain an affirmative de-identification program for any de-identified data in the controller's possession: reasonable measures that the data cannot be linked to a natural person, a public commitment not to attempt re-identification, and a contractual flow-down binding recipients to this chapter (§ 59.1-581(A)). Exercise reasonable oversight of contractual commitments attached to disclosed pseudonymous or de-identified data, and address breaches of those commitments (§ 59.1-581(E)).
- For pseudonymous data, the access, correction, deletion and portability rights under § 59.1-577(A)(1) to (4) and the § 59.1-578 controller duties do not apply where the controller can show that the information needed to identify the consumer is kept separately and under effective technical and organizational controls that prevent the controller from accessing it (§ 59.1-581(D)).
- A controller or processor need not re-identify de-identified or pseudonymous data, or keep, collect, obtain, retain or access data or technology, solely to be able to match an authenticated request to personal data (§ 59.1-581(B)); nor must it honor an authenticated rights request where it cannot reasonably associate the request with the data, or doing so would be unreasonably burdensome, and it does not use the data to recognize or respond to that specific consumer or link the data to other data about that consumer, and it does not sell or otherwise voluntarily disclose the data to a third party other than a processor (§ 59.1-581(C)).

## Prescribed wording

No phrase in this chapter is prescribed in fixed, quotable words for a consumer-facing notice or screen; every duty above is a substantive disclosure, assessment or process requirement, not a quoted label.

## Dates

- The data protection assessment duty under § 59.1-580 applies only to processing activities created or generated after January 1, 2023, and is not retroactive (§ 59.1-580(G)).
- A rights-request response is due within 45 days of receipt, extendable once by 45 more days with timely notice to the consumer within the initial period (§ 59.1-577(B)(1)).
- An appeal of a declined request is answered within 60 days of receipt (§ 59.1-577(C)).
- Before any enforcement action, the Attorney General gives 30 days' written notice of the alleged violation; a timely cure plus a written statement that no further violation will occur blocks the action (§ 59.1-584(B)).
- § 59.1-585 was repealed by Acts 2022, cc. 451 and 452, and carries no current text.

## Open points to raise

- Whether the business, or an affiliate, falls within one of the five entity exemptions or the fourteen data exemptions, since that removes the chapter's duties for the affected data rather than changing their content (§ 59.1-576(B), (C)).
- Whether an online service, product or feature is "directed to" or processes data from consumers the controller has "actual knowledge" are children, which triggers both the known-child activity limits under § 59.1-578(F) and the separate assessment duty under § 59.1-580(B); this chapter does not define the knowledge standard itself.
- Whether the product meets the definition of a "social media platform" under § 59.1-575, given the carve-outs for messaging-only services and for primarily provider-selected content with incidental interactivity, since that decides whether the minor time-limit duty under § 59.1-577.1 applies at all.
- Whether any processing qualifies for an exemption under § 59.1-582 (legal compliance, law enforcement cooperation, contract performance, safety, security and fraud response, research, internal product operations); the controller bears the burden of showing both that the exemption fits and that the processing stays necessity-, proportionality- and minimization-limited under § 59.1-582(F), (G).

# Connecticut Data Privacy Act

Citation: Conn. Gen. Stat. chapter 743jj, Sec. 42-515 to 42-526, 42-528, 42-529 to 42-529e
Version: current, revised to January 1, 2026, reflecting 2025 session amendments
Official text: https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_743jj.htm
Law as of: 2026-10-20

## When it applies

- The general chapter (Sec. 42-515 to 42-525) applies to a person that conducts business in Connecticut, or that produces products or services targeted to Connecticut residents, and that during the preceding calendar year controlled or processed the personal data of at least 100,000 consumers (excluding data controlled or processed solely to complete a payment transaction), or controlled or processed the personal data of at least 25,000 consumers and derived more than 25 percent of gross revenue from the sale of personal data (Sec. 42-516).
- The consumer health data rules apply to any person conducting business in Connecticut or targeting Connecticut residents, with no volume threshold (Sec. 42-526(a)(2)).
- The social media minors' rights (Sec. 42-528) and the online-services minors regime (Sec. 42-529a to 42-529e) carry no volume threshold either: they reach any controller a minor uses, as long as the controller has actual knowledge of the minor, or wilfully disregards that fact.
- "Consumer" is an individual resident of Connecticut; it excludes a person acting in a commercial or employment context, or as an employee, owner, director, officer, or contractor acting solely within that role (Sec. 42-515(8)).
- "Controller" determines, alone or jointly, the purpose and means of processing personal data; "processor" processes personal data on a controller's behalf (Sec. 42-515(11), (29)). Whether a party is a controller or a processor for a given activity is fact based: a party not limited by, or that fails to follow, a controller's instructions is itself a controller for that processing, and a processor that starts determining purposes and means becomes a controller (Sec. 42-521(d)).
- The general chapter (Sec. 42-515 to 42-525) and the consumer health data rules (through Sec. 42-526) do not apply to a state or local government body, a contractor processing consumer health data for one, a nonprofit organization, an institution of higher education, a national securities association registered under 15 U.S.C. § 78o-3, a financial institution or data subject to Title V of the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, a HIPAA covered entity or business associate, a tribal nation government organization, or an air carrier regulated under the Federal Aviation Act and the Airline Deregulation Act (Sec. 42-517(a); the consumer health data exemption list in Sec. 42-526(b) omits the nonprofit carve-out).
- Both chapters also exempt named categories of information: protected health information and other HIPAA-governed data, several categories of human-subjects research data, patient safety work product, de-identified health data, consumer-report data regulated under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, and data governed by the Driver's Privacy Protection Act, the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act, or the Farm Credit Act, each only to the extent that other law's conditions are met (Sec. 42-517(b); Sec. 42-529d(b) restates the same list for the online-services minors regime).
- The online-services minors regime (Sec. 42-529 to 42-529e) carries its own, largely parallel exemption list: state and local bodies, Section 501(c)(3), (4), (6), or (12) nonprofits, institutions of higher education, registered national securities associations, Gramm-Leach-Bliley financial institutions, HIPAA covered entities and business associates, tribal nation government organizations, and air carriers (Sec. 42-529d(a)).
- None of these duties restrict a controller's, processor's, or consumer health data controller's ability to comply with legal process, cooperate with law enforcement on a good-faith suspected violation, pursue or defend legal claims, provide a consumer-requested product or service, perform a contract with the consumer, take precontractual steps the consumer requested, protect someone's life or physical safety, respond to security incidents or fraud, conduct approved human-subjects research, assist another controller or processor with its own duties under the chapter, or process for public health purposes under confidentiality safeguards (Sec. 42-524(a); Sec. 42-529d(c) restates this for the minors regime). They likewise do not restrict internal-use collection to develop or repair products, run a recall, fix technical errors, or run internal operations aligned with the consumer's existing relationship or a requested product or service (Sec. 42-524(b); Sec. 42-529d(d)). None of these duties override an evidentiary privilege, reach a person's purely personal or household processing, or impose an obligation that burdens First Amendment speech or press rights (Sec. 42-524(c), (e); Sec. 42-529d(e), (f)).
- Processing under one of these exemptions must stay reasonably necessary and proportionate to, and limited to what the exempt purpose requires; a controller invoking an exemption carries the burden of showing both that it qualifies and that it stayed within that limit (Sec. 42-524(f), (g); Sec. 42-529d(g), (h)). A controller disclosing personal data to a processor or third-party controller in compliance with the chapter is not liable for that recipient's later violation, unless the disclosing party had actual knowledge the recipient would violate the chapter, and the same protection runs the other way for a recipient that received the data in compliance (Sec. 42-524(d)).
- "Sensitive data" covers data revealing racial or ethnic origin, religious belief, a mental or physical health condition or diagnosis, sex life, sexual orientation, or citizenship or immigration status; consumer health data; genetic or biometric data processed to uniquely identify an individual; data collected from a known child; data concerning a consumer's status as a crime victim; and precise geolocation data (Sec. 42-515(38)).
- "Precise geolocation data" is information, including GPS-derived coordinates, that directly identifies an individual's specific location within a radius of 1,750 feet; it excludes the content of communications and data from advanced utility metering infrastructure (Sec. 42-515(27)).
- "Sale of personal data" is the exchange of personal data for monetary or other valuable consideration to a third party; it excludes disclosure to a processor, disclosure to provide a consumer-requested product or service, disclosure to the controller's affiliate, consumer-directed disclosure, disclosure of data the consumer intentionally made available to the general public through mass media without restricting the audience, and a transfer of data as a business asset in a merger, acquisition, or bankruptcy (Sec. 42-515(37)).
- "Consent" is a clear affirmative act showing a consumer's freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous agreement to the processing, which can be a written statement, including by electronic means, or another unambiguous affirmative action. It does not include acceptance of broad terms of use bundling a processing description with unrelated material, hovering over, muting, pausing, or closing content, or agreement obtained through a dark pattern (Sec. 42-515(7)).
- "Social media platform" is a public or semi-public internet-based service or application that a Connecticut consumer uses, that is primarily intended to let users socially interact within it, and that lets a user build a public or semi-public profile, populate a public list of other users the user shares a social connection with, and create or post content other users can view. It excludes a service or application that exclusively provides electronic mail or direct messaging, that primarily consists of provider-preselected news, sports, entertainment, interactive video games, or e-commerce content where any chat, comments, or interactive functionality is incidental to, directly related to, or dependent on that content, or that is used under an educational entity's direction, such as a learning management system or a student engagement program (Sec. 42-528(a)(5)).

## What the documents must say

- A reasonably accessible, clear, and meaningful privacy notice stating the categories of personal data processed, the purpose of processing, how a consumer exercises the rights below (including the appeal process), the categories of personal data shared with third parties, the categories of those third parties, and an active email address or other online contact mechanism (Sec. 42-520(c)). If the controller sells personal data or processes it for targeted advertising, the notice clearly and conspicuously discloses that and the opt-out method (Sec. 42-520(d)).
- A named, secure, and reliable request channel, fitted to how consumers normally interact with the controller, for the five consumer rights: confirming whether the controller processes the consumer's data and accessing it (unless that would reveal a trade secret); correcting inaccuracies; deleting data the consumer provided or that was obtained about the consumer; obtaining a portable copy in a readily usable format, where processing is automated (again without revealing a trade secret); and opting out of processing for targeted advertising, sale, or profiling toward a solely automated decision with a legal or similarly significant effect (Sec. 42-518(a), (b), 42-520(e)(1)). The controller must not require a new account to exercise any right, though it may require use of an existing one (Sec. 42-520(e)(1)).
- A response to any such request without undue delay, and no later than 45 days after receipt, extendable by 45 more days where reasonably necessary given the complexity or number of requests, with notice of the extension and its reason given within the initial period (Sec. 42-518(c)(1)). Where the controller declines to act, notice of the reason and of the appeal instructions within the same 45-day window (Sec. 42-518(c)(2)). Responses are free once per consumer per 12 months; a manifestly unfounded, excessive, or repetitive request may carry a reasonable fee, or be declined, with the controller bearing the burden of showing that character (Sec. 42-518(c)(3)).
- An appeal process, conspicuously available and similar in form to the request process, with a written decision and reasons within 60 days of the appeal; where the appeal is denied, an online mechanism or other method for the consumer to contact the Attorney General (Sec. 42-518(d)).
- Where the controller sells personal data or processes it for targeted advertising, a clear and conspicuous website link to an opt-out page (Sec. 42-520(e)(1)(A)(i)). From January 1, 2025, the controller must also honor an opt-out preference signal that does not unfairly disadvantage another controller, does not rely on a default setting, is consumer-friendly and easy to use, is as consistent as possible with similar signals required elsewhere, and lets the controller verify the consumer's residency and the request's legitimacy (Sec. 42-520(e)(1)(A)(ii)).
- Recognition of an authorized agent's opt-out request, where the controller can verify, with commercially reasonable effort, both the consumer's identity and the agent's authority to act for the consumer (Sec. 42-519).
- Consent before processing sensitive data, except that sensitive data collected from a known child may instead be processed under COPPA (Sec. 42-520(a)(4)).
- Consent before processing the data of a consumer the controller actually knows, or wilfully disregards, to be at least 13 but under 16 years old, for targeted advertising or sale (Sec. 42-520(a)(7)).
- An effective revocation mechanism for consent, at least as easy to use as the consent mechanism itself, with processing stopped as soon as practicable and no later than 15 days after the revocation request (Sec. 42-520(a)(6)).
- Confirmation that every employee or contractor with access to consumer health data is bound by a contractual or statutory confidentiality duty, and that every processor with such access complies with the processor-contract duties of Sec. 42-521 (Sec. 42-526(a)(1)(A), (B)).
- Consent before selling, or offering to sell, consumer health data (Sec. 42-526(a)(1)(D)).
- For a social media platform (defined under When it applies, above): a secure and reliable request channel, described in the privacy notice, for a minor, or for the parent or legal guardian of a minor under 16, to request that the platform unpublish the minor's account (within 15 business days) or delete it (within 45 business days, extendable by 45 more business days with notice of the extension and its reason given within the initial period); deletion also stops processing the minor's personal data except where preservation is otherwise required or permitted by law (Sec. 42-528(b)(1), (2), (3)).
- For an online service, product, or feature a controller knows, or wilfully disregards, is used by a minor: a consent gate (the minor's own consent, or, if under 13, a parent's or guardian's, satisfied by COPPA-compliant verifiable parental consent) before processing the minor's data for targeted advertising, sale, or profiling toward a decision with the Sec. 42-515(15) legal or similarly significant effects, before processing for an undisclosed or incompatible purpose, or before processing longer than reasonably necessary to provide the service (Sec. 42-529a(b)(1), (3)).
- For the same services, a visible signal to the minor for the full duration that precise geolocation data is being collected, where that collection is reasonably necessary to provide the service and limited to the time the service needs it (Sec. 42-529a(b)(2)).
- Readily accessible, easy-to-use safeguards limiting an unconnected adult's ability to send a minor unsolicited direct messages, on any such service's direct-messaging feature (Sec. 42-529a(c)(1)(B)).
- A written contract with every processor fixing the processing instructions, the nature and purpose of processing, the data type, the processing duration, and each party's rights and obligations; requiring a confidentiality duty on everyone who processes the data; an end-of-service deletion or return of the data unless retention is legally required; the processor making compliance information available on reasonable request; a chance to object before the processor engages a subcontractor, with the subcontractor bound to the processor's own obligations; and cooperation with a reasonable assessment, whether controller-led or by an independent assessor the processor arranges, with a report on request (Sec. 42-521(b); Sec. 42-529c(b) applies the same duties to the minors regime).

## What the documents must not say

- No clause suggesting a consumer has a private right of action under the chapter: no private right of action exists under any of its four regimes, or under any other law on this account (Sec. 42-525(d), 42-528(d), 42-529e(a)).
- No consent mechanism built on a bundled terms-of-service-and-privacy-policy checkbox covering unrelated processing; hovering over, muting, pausing, or closing content as if it were agreement; or agreement obtained through a dark pattern; none of these meets the Sec. 42-515(7) definition of consent.
- No requirement to create a new account to exercise any of the five consumer rights; an existing account may still be required (Sec. 42-520(e)(1)).
- No discrimination against a consumer for exercising a chapter right, including denying goods or services, charging a different price, or providing a different quality level because of that exercise; this does not bar a different price or quality tied to voluntary participation in a bona fide loyalty, rewards, premium-features, discount, or club-card program (Sec. 42-520(a), (b)).
- No geofence within 1,750 feet of a mental health facility or a reproductive or sexual health facility used to identify, track, collect data from, or send a notification to a consumer about that consumer's consumer health data; this prohibition applies without regard to the controller's size or processing volume, but subject to the same exempt-category and construction exemptions as the rest of Sec. 42-526(a)(1) (Sec. 42-526(a)(1), (2), (b); Sec. 42-517(b), (c); Sec. 42-524).
- No sale, or offer to sell, consumer health data without the consumer's consent (Sec. 42-526(a)(1)(D)).
- No processing of a minor's data for targeted advertising, sale, or profiling toward a Sec. 42-515(15) significant-effect decision, and no use of a system design feature to significantly increase, sustain, or extend a minor's use of the service, without the minor's (or, under 13, the parent's or guardian's) consent; this restriction does not apply to a service used under an educational entity's direction, such as a learning management system or a student engagement program (Sec. 42-529a(b)(1), (3)).
- No consent mechanism reachable by a minor that is designed or manipulated to substantially subvert or impair a minor's autonomy, decision-making, or choice (Sec. 42-529a(c)(1)(A)).
- No direct-messaging feature reachable by minors without the safeguards described above; this requirement does not apply where the service's predominant or exclusive function is electronic mail, or private direct messaging shared only between sender and recipient and not posted publicly (Sec. 42-529a(c)(2)).
- No local-government or municipal privacy ordinance compliance clause purporting to add duties beyond the chapter for processing the chapter covers; the Attorney General has exclusive enforcement authority over the general chapter, the consumer health data rules, and both minors regimes (Sec. 42-525(a); Sec. 42-528(d); Sec. 42-529e(a)).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- The privacy, account, and rights-request pages name the specific request channel, support authentication without a new account, and route a request into the 45-day (plus a possible 45-day extension) pipeline with a documented appeal path (Sec. 42-518, 42-520(e)(1)).
- The privacy notice, or a location outside it, carries a clear and conspicuous opt-out link for targeted advertising and sale; from January 1, 2025, the controller's systems also recognize and honor an opt-out preference signal meeting the five Sec. 42-520(e)(1)(A)(ii) conditions (Sec. 42-520(e)(1)(A)).
- Where an opt-out preference signal conflicts with the consumer's existing controller-specific privacy setting or voluntary participation in a loyalty, rewards, premium-features, discounts, or club-card program, the controller's systems still honor the opt-out signal; the controller may notify the consumer of the conflict and offer the choice to confirm the setting or program participation, but compliance with the signal does not wait on that confirmation (Sec. 42-520(e)(1)(B)).
- Where a controller responds to an opt-out request by telling the consumer a fee applies to the product or service, the response presents the terms of any financial incentive offered under Sec. 42-520(b) for the retention, use, sale, or sharing of the consumer's personal data (Sec. 42-520(e)(2)).
- A documented data protection assessment exists, before or during the processing, for any activity presenting a heightened risk of harm: targeted advertising, sale, profiling with a listed risk, or sensitive-data processing, for activity after July 1, 2023, weighing benefits against the mitigated risks; it is produced to the Attorney General on request and otherwise kept confidential (Sec. 42-522).
- Where a controller holds de-identified data, it takes reasonable measures to ensure the data cannot be associated with an individual, publicly commits to processing it only in de-identified form without attempting re-identification, and contractually binds every recipient of that data to the same two duties; the controller's processor contracts carry this pass-through (Sec. 42-523(a)). Properly segregated pseudonymous data, where the controller can show the identifying information is kept separate and subject to effective technical and organizational controls that prevent the controller from accessing it, falls outside the confirm-and-access, correction, deletion, and portability rights, though the opt-out right still applies (Sec. 42-523(d)).
- Any consumer health data flow confirms confidentiality duties on employees and contractors with access, routes processor access through a compliant contract, and gates a sale of that data behind the consumer's consent, regardless of the controller's size or volume (Sec. 42-526(a)(1)).
- Any online service, product, or feature reaching known or willfully-disregarded minors carries its own data protection assessment, consistent with the general Sec. 42-522 assessment, addressing the service's purpose, the minors' data categories processed, the processing purposes, and any foreseeable heightened risk of harm; where the assessment finds that risk, a mitigation or elimination plan is established and implemented, and the documentation is kept for three years after the processing ends or for as long as the service is offered, whichever is longer (Sec. 42-529b).
- A social media platform's account-management flow supports a minor's (or qualifying parent's or guardian's) unpublish and delete requests within the business-day deadlines above; offering a self-service unpublish or delete mechanism directly to the minor or guardian satisfies this duty without a separate request-and-response workflow (Sec. 42-528(b)(3)).

## Prescribed wording

None. The chapter requires specific content and deadlines but does not prescribe exact link text or notice wording for Connecticut.

## Dates

- July 1, 2023: the general chapter (Sec. 42-515 to 42-525) takes effect, including the original consumer health data provisions within Sec. 42-515 to 42-524 (Sec. 42-525, history note).
- October 1, 2023: the consumer health data rules at Sec. 42-526 take effect, along with the P.A. 23-56 restructuring of Sec. 42-515, 42-517, 42-520, and 42-524 (Sec. 42-526, history note; Sec. 42-524, history note).
- July 1, 2024: the social media platforms and minors provisions, Sec. 42-528, take effect (Sec. 42-528, history note).
- October 1, 2024: the online services, products, or features minors regime, Sec. 42-529 to 42-529e, takes effect (Sec. 42-529, history note).
- January 1, 2025: the opt-out preference signal duty begins (Sec. 42-520(e)(1)(A)(ii)); the general chapter's and the consumer health data rules' mandatory 60-day cure-notice period ends, and a discretionary, factors-weighed cure period begins (Sec. 42-525(b), (c)).
- February 1, 2026: the Attorney General's report on the Sec. 42-529e mandatory cure-notice track is due to the General Assembly's joint standing committee (Sec. 42-529e(b)(2)(C)).
- January 1, 2026: the Sec. 42-529 to 42-529d discretionary, factors-weighed cure period begins, replacing the mandatory 30-day cure-response track that ran from October 1, 2024 (Sec. 42-529e(c)).

## Open points to raise

- Whether a particular data flow counts as a "sale" under Sec. 42-515(37) on its facts, since labeling a disclosure a mere "service" does not change which duties attach to it.
- Whether the controller actually knows, or wilfully disregards, that a consumer using an online service, product, or feature is a minor; the chapter ties several duties to that state of knowledge without defining a bright line for any specific product.
- Whether an existing Connecticut-facing clause was drafted against the superseded effective dates in P.A. 23-56 before P.A. 23-204 moved them to October 1, 2023, since the statute's own history notes record that change.

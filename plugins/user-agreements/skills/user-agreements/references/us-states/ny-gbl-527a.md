# New York General Business Law 527 and 527-a, automatic renewal

Citation: N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law §§ 527, 527-a, enacted by L. 2020, ch. 267, as amended by L. 2025, ch. 58, Part W
Version: Part W, signed 2025-05-09, in force from 2025-11-05
Official text: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/527-A
Law as of: 2026-10-20

## When it applies

- Applies to any business making an automatic renewal offer or a continuous service offer to a consumer in New York. "Consumer" means an individual who seeks or acquires, by purchase or lease, any goods, services, money, or credit for personal, family, or household purposes (§ 527(3)). A business-to-business subscription falls outside the article.
- "Continuous service" means a plan or arrangement in which a subscription or purchasing agreement continues until the consumer cancels (§ 527(4)).
- An automatic renewal is a plan or arrangement under which a paid subscription or purchasing agreement renews automatically at the end of a definite term for a subsequent term (§ 527(1), unchanged by the 2025 amendment). A plan that renews for a fixed next term is an automatic renewal; a plan with no definite term that runs until cancelled is a continuous service. Both are covered.
- "Clear and conspicuous" means in larger type than the surrounding text, or in contrasting type, font, or color to it, or set off from it by symbols or other marks, in a way that clearly calls attention to the language; for an audio disclosure, a volume and cadence sufficient to be readily audible and understandable (§ 527(2)).
- Five categories of business are exempt from the whole article: a business or affiliate operating under a franchise issued by a political subdivision of the state; an entity, or its subsidiary or affiliate, regulated by the Department of Financial Services; security system alarm operators; banks, bank holding companies, their subsidiaries or affiliates, credit unions, or other state- or federally-licensed financial institutions; and sellers and administrators of a service contract as defined in Insurance Law § 7902 (§ 527-a(4)(a) to (e)). Confirm the seller's regulatory status before drafting any of the clauses below for it.

## What the documents must say

- Disclose the offer's material terms clearly and conspicuously before consent to the offer or billing information is requested, in visual proximity (temporal proximity for a spoken offer) to the request for consent. The terms include, without limitation, a description of the product or service subject to renewal, the costs that will be charged, the frequency of charges, the deadline by date or frequency by which the consumer must act to prevent or stop further charges, and the cancellation mechanisms (§ 527-a(1)(a)).
- If the offer includes a free gift or trial, or a temporary price, add a clear and conspicuous explanation of how and when the price will change and the price or prices that will subsequently be charged (§ 527-a(1)(a)).
- After affirmative consent, send a notice promptly, in a form the consumer can retain. The notice must cover: the terms of the agreement; the amount of costs that will be charged; the frequency of charges; the deadline by date or frequency by which the consumer must act to prevent or stop further charges; and the cancellation mechanisms (§ 527-a(1)(c)(i) to (v)).
- Describe the cancellation mechanism so it is at least as easy to use as the mechanism the consumer used to give consent, and reachable through that same medium, plus every other medium through which the business lets a consumer give affirmative consent (§ 527-a(1)(d), (d-1)).
- Where consent was given in person, and an in-person cancellation method similar to the consent method is not practical, offer cancellation through an online mechanism or over a telephone number at least (§ 527-a(1)(d-1)).
- For a renewal reminder (see Dates), disclose, through the channel the consumer selected, instructions on how to cancel the renewal charge (§ 527-a(1)(f), (h)).
- For a material-change notice, including a price increase, give clear and conspicuous notice before the change takes effect, in the same manner as the renewal reminder under paragraph f (§ 527-a(1)(g)).

## What the documents must not say

- Do not present a renewal or continuous-service offer without the required disclosures appearing before consent or billing information is requested; the disclosures cannot be deferred to a later screen or a later document (§ 527-a(1)(a)).
- Do not describe a retention offer, discount, or cancellation-effect information as a condition the consumer must sit through before cancelling; the business may present one only after a cancellation request, and never in a way that imposes unreasonable or unlawful conditions on, refuses to acknowledge, obstructs, or unreasonably delays the cancellation (§ 527-a(1)(e)(ii)).
- Unreasonable or unlawful conditions include hanging up on a consumer who calls to cancel, giving false information about how to cancel, misrepresenting the consequences or costs of cancellation, or misrepresenting the reasons for a delay in processing a cancellation request (§ 527-a(1)(e)(i)).
- Do not charge, or re-charge after a price increase, without the consent or cancellation-window route the statute requires (see Product behaviour it assumes); a business need not ask for repeat consent to the same increase more than once before charging it (§ 527-a(1)(b-1)).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- The checkout captures affirmative consent before the initial charge for an automatic renewal or continuous service, including one offered at a promotional or discounted price for a limited period (§ 527-a(1)(b)).
- On a price increase, or a price higher than what was disclosed at the offer stage, the system either captures the consumer's affirmative consent to the new price before charging it, or lets the consumer cancel at any time within at least fourteen days after the charge and issues a pro rata refund for the remaining term as of the cancellation date. One captured consent to a given increase covers every later charge at that price; a further increase needs its own consent or cancellation window (§ 527-a(1)(b-1)).
- The post-consent notice is generated and sent promptly after consent, in a form the consumer can keep, such as a confirmation email or a saved PDF (§ 527-a(1)(c)).
- The cancellation flow matches the consent flow: if consent was captured online, cancellation is available online; if by phone, by phone; and so on for every medium the business uses to take consent, with no extra step added for the cancelling medium that the consenting medium did not require (§ 527-a(1)(d), (d-1)).
- For a plan with an initial paid term of one year or longer that renews for a paid term of six months or longer, the system sends a renewal reminder 15 to 45 days before the cancellation deadline, through whatever channel (text, email, app notification, or other) the consumer selected, with cancellation instructions (§ 527-a(1)(f)).
- For an offer with a free gift or trial lasting more than one month followed by a renewal charge, the system sends a reminder 3 to 21 days before the cancellation deadline for the first chargeable period, through the consumer's selected channel, with cancellation instructions (§ 527-a(1)(h)).
- For a material change to the accepted terms, including a price increase, the system sends clear and conspicuous notice 5 business days to 30 days before the change takes effect, through the same channel rule as the renewal reminder (§ 527-a(1)(g)).
- Any goods, wares, merchandise, or products shipped under a continuous service agreement or automatic renewal without first obtaining affirmative consent are treated internally as an unconditional gift: no invoice, collection, or return-shipping charge runs against the recipient, who owes the business nothing for them (§ 527-a(2)).

## Prescribed wording

No phrase is prescribed in fixed words for a consumer-facing screen or notice; every duty above is stated as a substantive disclosure or notice requirement, not a quoted label. Where a drafting choice needs a fixed phrase for another US jurisdiction's trigger word, such as California's cancel or click to cancel labels, see the matching entry in `references/prescribed-wording.md`; none of those entries rests on this file's sources.

## Dates

- Part W of L. 2025, ch. 58 amended § 527(2) to (4) and restated § 527-a in full, taking effect on the one hundred eightieth day after it became law; in force from 2025-11-05 (L. 2025, ch. 58, Part W, § 3).
- § 527(1), the automatic renewal definition, was added by L. 2020, ch. 267 and was not touched by the 2025 amendment.
- The renewal reminder for a plan with an initial paid term of one year or more, renewing for six months or more: 15 to 45 days before the cancellation deadline (§ 527-a(1)(f)).
- The material-change notice, including a price increase: 5 business days to 30 days before the change takes effect (§ 527-a(1)(g)).
- The free-gift-or-trial renewal reminder, where the gift or trial runs more than a month: 3 to 21 days before the cancellation deadline for the first chargeable period (§ 527-a(1)(h)).
- The price-increase cancellation window, where the business does not obtain affirmative consent to the increase first: at least 14 days after the charge (§ 527-a(1)(b-1)).
- Attorney General injunction proceedings require at least 5 days' notice to the defendant (§ 527-a(3)).
- A court that finds a violation may impose a civil penalty of up to $100 for a single violation and up to $500 for multiple violations resulting from a single act or incident; a knowing violation raises the ceiling to $500 for a single violation and $1,000 for multiple violations resulting from a single act or incident (§ 527-a(3)).
- A business is not deemed to have violated the article if it shows, by a preponderance of the evidence, that the violation was unintentional and resulted from a bona fide error made notwithstanding the maintenance of procedures reasonably adopted to avoid that error (§ 527-a(3)).

## Open points to raise

- Whether the product's billing and churn flows (free-to-paid conversion, usage-based upgrades) fall within automatic renewal or within continuous service (§ 527(1), (4)), where a free trial or usage-based charge does not fit either definition cleanly.
- Who within the business is positioned to honor the same-medium-as-consent cancellation duty where billing runs through a third-party platform, for example an app store, rather than the business's own checkout, and whether that platform's cancellation path can stand in for it (§ 527-a(1)(d)).
- Whether the business or an affiliate falls into one of the five exempt categories, which would remove the article's duties entirely rather than change their content (§ 527-a(4)).
- Whether the general consumer-protection enforcement history under the 2020-to-2023 version of this article (before the 2025 restatement) bears on an existing contract still running under older terms; this file speaks only to the law in force from 2025-11-05.

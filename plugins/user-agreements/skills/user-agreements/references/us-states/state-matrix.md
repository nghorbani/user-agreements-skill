# US state privacy law matrix

Official text: each row gives the official or official-adjacent citation for its own statute; see each state's file in this folder for the full URL where one is merged separately.
Law as of: 2026-10-20

Columns: state, statute and citation, in force from, applicability thresholds, consumer rights, sensitive data (opt-in or opt-out), universal opt-out signal, cure period, notable duties, check before relying (date of the text read).

## California

- Statute and citation: California Consumer Privacy Act as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act, Cal. Civ. Code 1798.100 et seq.
- In force from: 2020-01-01 (CCPA), CPRA amendments in force 2023-01-01.
- Applicability thresholds: a for-profit business doing business in California that controls the means and purposes of processing and meets one of: over $25,000,000 in prior-year gross revenue (adjusted for inflation every odd-numbered year from 2025); buys, sells, or shares the personal information of 100,000 or more consumers or households; or derives 50 percent or more of annual revenue from selling or sharing personal information (Cal. Civ. Code 1798.140(d)(1)).
- Consumer rights: know, delete, correct, portability, opt out of sale or sharing, limit use of sensitive personal information, non-discrimination.
- Sensitive data: opt-out model; a consumer can limit the use of sensitive personal information to what is necessary for specified purposes, rather than a general opt-in consent gate (Cal. Civ. Code 1798.121).
- Universal opt-out signal: required; businesses must honor a compliant opt-out preference signal sent in a commonly recognized format.
- Cure period: none currently; the general cure period for civil enforcement by the Attorney General expired January 1, 2023, and is not restored in the text read.
- Notable duties: risk assessments and cybersecurity audits for high-risk processing (phased in by revenue tier through 2029); automated decision-making technology rules; service-provider and contractor contract terms.
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20 (see references/us-states/ca-ccpa.md for the full file).

## Virginia

- Statute and citation: Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act, Va. Code 59.1-575 to 59.1-585.
- In force from: 2023-01-01.
- Applicability thresholds: conducts business in Virginia or targets Virginia residents and, in a calendar year, controls or processes the personal data of at least 100,000 consumers, or controls or processes the personal data of at least 25,000 consumers and derives over 50 percent of gross revenue from the sale of personal data (Va. Code 59.1-576(A)).
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, correct, delete, portability, opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and profiling for decisions with legal or similarly significant effects.
- Sensitive data: opt-in; consent required before processing (Va. Code 59.1-578(A)(5)).
- Universal opt-out signal: not required by statute.
- Cure period: 30 days' written notice before an enforcement action; curing the violation and giving a written statement that no further violation will occur blocks the action (Va. Code 59.1-584(B)).
- Notable duties: minors under 16 get a double gate on social media platforms (one-hour daily use cap plus parental-consent override) and a general known-child activity limit; data protection assessments for targeted advertising, sale, risky profiling, sensitive data, and known-child processing.
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20 (see references/us-states/va-vcdpa.md for the full file).

## Colorado

- Statute and citation: Colorado Privacy Act, C.R.S. 6-1-1301 to 6-1-1314, as amended by HB 24-1058, HB 24-1130, SB 24-041, and SB 25-276.
- In force from: 2023-07-01 (general part); biometric regime 2025-07-01; minors sections 2025-10-01.
- Applicability thresholds: conducts business in or targets Colorado residents and either controls or processes 100,000 or more consumers' personal data in a calendar year, or derives revenue or a price discount from selling personal data and controls or processes 25,000 or more consumers' personal data (C.R.S. 6-1-1304(1)(a)(I)). A controller that controls or processes any amount of biometric identifiers or biometric data is covered for that processing regardless of volume (6-1-1304(1)(a)(II)). From 2025-10-01 the minors sections apply with no volume threshold at all (6-1-1304(1)(b)).
- Consumer rights: opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and significant-effect profiling; access; correction; deletion; portability.
- Sensitive data: opt-in; consent required before processing, and, from the amendment approved 2025-05-23, before selling sensitive data too (C.R.S. 6-1-1308(7)).
- Universal opt-out signal: required from 2024-07-01, to the Attorney General's technical specifications (C.R.S. 6-1-1306(1)(a)(IV)(B)).
- Cure period: the general 60-day cure-notice requirement was repealed 2025-01-01; the minors-sections cure-notice requirement is repealed 2026-12-31 (C.R.S. 6-1-1311(1)(d)(I), (II)).
- Notable duties: biometric-identifier regime from 2025-07-01 (pre-collection disclosure, written retention policy, absolute ban on sale/lease/trade); minors data protection assessments from 2025-10-01; precise geolocation data radius changed from 1,750 feet to 1,850 feet effective 2025-05-23.
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20 (see references/us-states/co-cpa.md for the full file).

## Connecticut

- Statute and citation: Connecticut Data Privacy Act, Conn. Gen. Stat. ch. 743jj, Sec. 42-515 to 42-526, 42-528, 42-529 to 42-529e.
- In force from: 2023-07-01 (general chapter); consumer health data rules 2023-10-01; social media minors (Sec. 42-528) 2024-07-01; online-services minors regime 2024-10-01.
- Applicability thresholds: conducts business in or targets Connecticut residents and, in the preceding calendar year, controlled or processed the personal data of at least 100,000 consumers, or controlled or processed the personal data of at least 25,000 consumers and derived more than 25 percent of gross revenue from the sale of personal data (Sec. 42-516). The consumer health data rules and the minors regimes carry no volume threshold.
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, correct, delete, portability, opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and significant-effect profiling.
- Sensitive data: opt-in; consent required, except sensitive data from a known child may instead be processed under COPPA (Sec. 42-520(a)(4)). Consumer health data: consent also required before sale (Sec. 42-526(a)(1)(D)).
- Universal opt-out signal: required from 2025-01-01 (Sec. 42-520(e)(1)(A)(ii)).
- Cure period: the general chapter's and consumer health data rules' mandatory 60-day cure notice ended 2025-01-01, replaced by a discretionary, factors-weighed cure track (Sec. 42-525(b), (c)); the minors regime's mandatory 30-day cure-notice track that ran from 2024-10-01 was replaced by a discretionary track from 2026-01-01 (Sec. 42-529e(c)).
- Notable duties: no geofencing within 1,750 feet of a mental health or reproductive/sexual health facility to identify, track, or notify a consumer, regardless of controller size (Sec. 42-526(a)(1)); social media minors get account unpublish (15 business days) and delete (45 business days) rights.
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20 (see references/us-states/ct-ctdpa.md for the full file).

## Texas

- Statute and citation: Texas Data Privacy and Security Act, Tex. Bus. and Com. Code ch. 541.
- In force from: 2024-07-01.
- Applicability thresholds: conducts business in Texas or produces a product or service Texas residents consume, processes or sells personal data, and is not an SBA-defined small business, except that a small business still owes the sensitive-data-sale consent duty (Sec. 541.002(a)). No consumer-count or revenue threshold otherwise.
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, correct, delete, portability, opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and significant-effect profiling.
- Sensitive data: opt-in; consent required, or COPPA-compliant parental consent for a known child's sensitive data (Sec. 541.101(b)(4), Sec. 541.005). A small business relying on the SBA exception still needs consent before selling sensitive personal data (Sec. 541.107(a)).
- Universal opt-out signal: the opt-out mechanism may not rely on a default setting and must be consumer-friendly, but the text read does not impose a specific signal-recognition technical standard the way Colorado and Connecticut do (Sec. 541.055(f)).
- Cure period: 30 days before an Attorney General enforcement action; a timely and complete cure forecloses a civil penalty of up to $7,500 per violation (Sec. 541.154, Sec. 541.155(a)).
- Notable duties: prescribed point-of-sale notices for sensitive personal data ("NOTICE: We may sell your sensitive personal data.") and biometric personal data ("NOTICE: We may sell your biometric personal data."), each posted in the same place and manner as the privacy notice (Sec. 541.102(b), (c)).
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20 (see references/us-states/tx-tdpsa.md for the full file).

## Utah

- Statute and citation: Utah Consumer Privacy Act, Utah Code 13-61-101 to 13-61-404 (Part 5, Motor Vehicle Data Privacy, effective 2027-01-01).
- In force from: 2023-12-31.
- Applicability thresholds: conducts business in Utah or targets Utah residents, has annual revenue of $25,000,000 or more, and during a calendar year controls or processes personal data of 100,000 or more consumers, or derives over 50 percent of gross revenue from the sale of personal data and controls or processes personal data of 25,000 or more consumers (13-61-102(1)). From 2027-01-01 the chapter also reaches a motor vehicle manufacturer that manufactures vehicles sold or leased in Utah and collects, transmits, or stores personal data through a vehicle data collection system, regardless of the revenue and consumer-count thresholds (13-61-102(1)(b)).
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, correct, delete, portability, opt out of targeted advertising and sale (13-61-201).
- Sensitive data: opt-out model; the controller must present clear notice and an opportunity to opt out before processing sensitive data, except processing of a known child's data follows COPPA instead (13-61-302(3)). This is weaker than the opt-in consent gate most other states in this matrix use.
- Universal opt-out signal: not required by statute.
- Cure period: 30 days' written notice before an Attorney General enforcement action; curing the violation within the 30-day period and giving the attorney general an express written statement that the violation has been cured and will not recur blocks the action (13-61-402(3)).
- Notable duties: no private right of action (13-61-305); from 2027-01-01, in-vehicle privacy controls letting a consumer view data categories collected, view third-party sharing categories, opt out of sale or targeted advertising, and delete readily accessible data, for model year 2030 or later vehicles (13-61-502).
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20.

## Iowa

- Statute and citation: Iowa Consumer Data Protection Act, Iowa Code ch. 715D.
- In force from: 2023 enactment; see 715D.1 et seq.
- Applicability thresholds: conducts business in Iowa or targets Iowa residents and, during a calendar year, controls or processes personal data of at least 100,000 consumers, or controls or processes personal data of at least 25,000 consumers and derives over 50 percent of gross revenue from the sale of personal data (715D.2(1)).
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, delete, portability (for data covered by security-breach law), opt out of sale (715D.3(1)).
- Sensitive data: opt-out model; the controller must present clear notice and an opportunity to opt out before processing, except a known child's sensitive data follows COPPA (715D.4(2)). Iowa does not give consumers a correction right or an opt-out right for targeted advertising or significant-effect profiling, unlike the Virginia-model states.
- Universal opt-out signal: not required by statute.
- Cure period: 90 days' written notice before an Attorney General action; curing within the period and giving a written statement blocks the action (715D.8(2)).
- Notable duties: no private right of action (715D.8(4)); appeal process for a declined request within 60 days (715D.3(3)).
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20.

## Delaware

- Statute and citation: Delaware Personal Data Privacy Act, 6 Del. C. ch. 12D.
- In force from: 2025-01-01, with a definitions revision effective 2027-01-01.
- Applicability thresholds: does business in Delaware or targets Delaware residents and, during the preceding calendar year, controlled or processed the personal data of not less than 35,000 consumers, excluding data controlled or processed solely to complete a payment transaction, or controlled or processed the personal data of not less than 10,000 consumers and derived more than 20 percent of gross revenue from the sale of personal data (12D-103(a)). This threshold is markedly lower than the Virginia-model 100,000/25,000 pair.
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, correct, delete, portability, opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and significant-effect profiling (12D-104(a)).
- Sensitive data: opt-in; consent required. The sensitive-data definition is broader than Virginia's: it adds financial account and government-ID numbers as of the version effective 2027-01-01, plus neural data generated by measuring central-nervous-system activity.
- Universal opt-out signal: a consumer may designate an authorized agent to opt out of sale, targeted advertising or significant-effect profiling by way of a platform, technology or mechanism, including a browser setting, browser extension or global device setting, and the controller must comply with an opt-out request from such an agent if it can verify, with commercially reasonable effort, the consumer's identity and the agent's authority to act (12D-105(a), (b)).
- Cure period: 60 days' notice before an enforcement action, but only from 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31; from 2026-01-01 a cure opportunity is discretionary, weighed against the number of violations, the controller's or processor's size and complexity, the nature and extent of its processing, the likelihood of injury to the public, safety of persons or property, whether the violation was likely caused by human or technical error, and past violations of this or similar laws (12D-111(b), (c)).
- Notable duties: a controller may not process a consumer's personal data for targeted advertising, sale, or significant-effect profiling without consent where it knows or willfully disregards that the consumer is at least 13 but younger than 18 (12D-104, consent-age provision), a wider band than the 13-to-16 age gate most other matrix states use; a "report" is broadly defined to include automated decisions or summaries based on personal data or profiling (12D-102(30), 2027-01-01 version); a controller disclosing to a third party a report used in connection with a decision that produces legal or similarly significant effects must contractually require the third party to notify the consumer of an adverse action based wholly or partly on the report, describe the personal data relied on, and, where technically feasible, offer the consumer a human review of the adverse action unless that review is not in the consumer's best interest (12D-106(f)(1)); the controller itself must, on request, give the consumer the personal data it holds, the source of data used in profiling, and the identity of every third party that obtained a report about the consumer in the preceding 24 months, within 30 days, and must give the consumer a chance to correct inaccurate personal data (12D-106(f)(2), (3)); nonprofit organizations are not categorically exempt the way they are in Virginia, Colorado, and most other matrix states.
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20.

## Nebraska

- Statute and citation: Nebraska Data Privacy Act (LB1074, 2024), Sections 1 to 30 of the enacted bill.
- In force from: 2024 enactment (no small-business revenue floor specified beyond the SBA test described below).
- Applicability thresholds: conducts business in Nebraska or produces a product or service Nebraska residents consume, processes or engages in the sale of personal data, and is not a small business as determined under the federal Small Business Act, except that the sensitive-data-sale consent duty (Sec. 18) reaches a person who would otherwise be exempt as a small business (Sec. 3(1)). No consumer-count or revenue threshold otherwise, matching the Texas model rather than the Virginia model.
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, correct, delete, portability, opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and significant-effect profiling.
- Sensitive data: opt-in; consent required, or COPPA compliance for a known child (Sec. 12(2)(d)). A person who would otherwise be exempt as a small business may not sell sensitive data without the consumer's prior consent (Sec. 18).
- Universal opt-out signal: not required by statute.
- Cure period: 30 days' written notice before an Attorney General action; curing and giving a written statement blocks the action (Sec. 22).
- Notable duties: no private right of action (Sec. 25); the attorney general posts controller, processor, and consumer-rights information plus an online complaint mechanism (Sec. 20).
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20.

## New Jersey

- Statute and citation: New Jersey Data Privacy Act, P.L. 2023 c. 266, N.J.S.A. 56:8-166.4 to 56:8-166.26.
- In force from: 365 days after enactment (approved 2024-01-16).
- Applicability thresholds: controllers conducting business in New Jersey or targeting New Jersey residents and, during a calendar year, controlling or processing the personal data of at least 100,000 consumers, excluding data processed solely to complete a payment transaction, or controlling or processing the personal data of at least 25,000 consumers while deriving revenue or a price discount from the sale of personal data (C.56:8-166.5).
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, correct, delete, portability, opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and profiling for decisions with legal or similarly significant effects (C.56:8-166.10).
- Sensitive data: opt-in; consent required, or COPPA compliance for a known child (C.56:8-166.12(a)(4)). Consent also required before processing personal data for targeted advertising, sale, or significant-effect profiling where the controller has actual knowledge or willfully disregards that the consumer is 13 to under 17 years old (C.56:8-166.12(a)(7)).
- Universal opt-out signal: required from six months after the effective date, to Division of Consumer Affairs technical specifications (C.56:8-166.11(b)).
- Cure period: a 30-day cure right applied only until the 18th month after the effective date, after which the Division need not give notice before an enforcement action (C.56:8-166.17(b)).
- Notable duties: data protection assessments for processing that presents a heightened risk of harm, with a broader definitions list than most states, including decisions affecting housing, education, criminal justice, and access to essential goods or services; assessments are confidential and exempt from the state's open-records law (C.56:8-166.12(a)(9), (b)).
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20.

## Montana

- Statute and citation: Montana Consumer Data Privacy Act, Mont. Code Ann. 30-14-2801 to 30-14-2820.
- In force from: 2024 (2023 enactment), with minors provisions (30-14-2811, 30-14-2818, 30-14-2819) added by 2025 amendment.
- Applicability thresholds: conducts business in Montana or targets Montana residents and controls or processes the personal data of not less than 25,000 consumers, excluding payment-transaction-only data, or controls or processes the personal data of not less than 15,000 consumers and derives more than 25 percent of gross revenue from the sale of personal data (30-14-2803(1)). The minors sections (2811, 2818, 2819) apply to any person conducting business in or targeting Montana residents, with no volume threshold (30-14-2803(2)).
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, correct, delete, portability, opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and significant-effect profiling.
- Sensitive data: opt-in; consent required, or COPPA compliance for a known child (30-14-2812(2)(b)). Consent also required for targeted advertising or sale where the controller knows or willfully disregards that the consumer is 13 to under 16 (30-14-2812(2)(d)).
- Universal opt-out signal: required since 2025-01-01 (30-14-2809(3)(b)).
- Cure period: enforcement proceeds under Title 30, chapter 14, parts 1 and 2; a person who violates the part following the 30-day period described in 30-14-2817(3) is liable for a civil penalty (30-14-2820(2)), implying a 30-day cure window tied to the Attorney General's civil investigative demand process.
- Notable duties: a controller may not disclose a consumer's social security number, government ID number, financial account number, health insurance or medical ID number, account password or security answers, or biometric data in response to an access request, but must tell the consumer with sufficient particularity that it has collected that information (30-14-2808(6)); minors-specific duty of care and rebuttable presumption of reasonable care for an online service a controller knows is used by a minor (30-14-2811); minors data protection assessments from 2025-10-01 (30-14-2819).
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20.

## Oregon

- Statute and citation: Oregon Consumer Privacy Act, Or. Rev. Stat. 646A.570 to 646A.589.
- In force from: 2024-07-01 (2023 enactment).
- Applicability thresholds: conducts business in Oregon or provides products or services to Oregon residents and, during a calendar year, controls or processes the personal data of 100,000 or more consumers, excluding payment-transaction-only data, or controls or processes the personal data of 25,000 or more consumers while deriving 25 percent or more of annual gross revenue from selling personal data (646A.572(1)(a)). A motor vehicle manufacturer and its affiliates that control or process any personal data obtained from a consumer's use of a motor vehicle are covered regardless of those thresholds (646A.572(1)(b)).
- Consumer rights: confirm and access (plus, at the controller's option, a list of specific third parties data was disclosed to), correct, delete, opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and significant-effect profiling.
- Sensitive data: opt-in; consent required, or COPPA compliance for a known child (646A.578(2)(b)). Consent also required for targeted advertising or significant-effect profiling where the controller knows or willfully disregards that the consumer is under 16 (646A.578(2)(c)), and sale of personal data is barred outright where the controller knows or willfully disregards that the consumer is under 16, or where the data is precise geolocation data (646A.578(2)(d)).
- Universal opt-out signal: required (646A.578(5)(c)).
- Cure period: none generally; the general cure-notice requirement was repealed, and a discretionary cure right survives only for noncommercial educational broadcast stations meeting specific federal-funding conditions, itself repealed 2026-07-01 (2025 c.417 sections 4 to 6).
- Notable duties: no private right of action (646A.589(7)); a five-year statute of limitations on Attorney General enforcement (646A.589(5)); public corporations including Oregon Health and Science University and the Oregon State Bar are expressly exempt, alongside the usual nonprofit and higher-education carve-outs.
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20.

## Rhode Island

- Statute and citation: Rhode Island Data Transparency and Privacy Protection Act, R.I. Gen. Laws 6-48.1-1 to 6-48.1-10.
- In force from: 2026-01-01.
- Applicability thresholds: two different thresholds apply to different sections. Section 6-48.1-3 (information sharing practices) reaches any commercial website or internet service provider conducting business in or with customers in Rhode Island, with no volume threshold. Sections 6-48.1-4, 6-48.1-5, and 6-48.1-6 (processing limits, customer rights, exercising rights) reach only for-profit entities that, during the preceding calendar year, controlled or processed the personal data of not less than 35,000 customers, excluding payment-transaction-only data, or controlled or processed the personal data of not less than 10,000 customers and derived more than 20 percent of gross revenue from the sale of personal data.
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, correct, delete, portability, opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and profiling for decisions with legal or similarly significant effects (6-48.1-5(e)).
- Sensitive data: opt-in; consent required, or COPPA compliance for a known child (6-48.1-4(c)).
- Universal opt-out signal: not required by statute; 6-48.1-7, the controller and processor responsibilities section, carries a data protection assessment duty but no signal-recognition standard, and the authorized-agent mechanism in 6-48.1-6(7) does not describe one either.
- Cure period: none; 6-48.1-7 (controller and processor responsibilities) carries no cure-notice provision, and no other declared section states one.
- Notable duties: a controller shall conduct and document a data protection assessment for processing that presents a heightened risk of harm (targeted advertising, sale of personal data, risky profiling, sensitive data), applicable only to processing activities created or generated after 2026-01-01 and not retroactive (6-48.1-7(e), (i)); a violation is a deceptive trade practice, and intentional disclosure to a shell entity formed to circumvent the chapter, or any other violative disclosure, carries a separate fine of $100 to $500 per disclosure (6-48.1-8(a)); no private right of action (6-48.1-8(c)).
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20.

## Maryland

- Statute and citation: Maryland Online Data Privacy Act, Md. Code, Com. Law 14-4701 to 14-4714, as amended by Chapter 874 of 2026 (HB 711).
- In force from: 2025-10-01.
- Applicability thresholds: conducts business in Maryland or targets Maryland residents and, during the preceding calendar year, controlled or processed the personal data of at least 35,000 consumers, excluding payment-transaction-only data, or controlled or processed the personal data of at least 10,000 consumers and derived more than 20 percent of gross revenue from the sale of personal data.
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, correct, delete, portability, opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and significant-effect profiling (Com. Law 14-4705).
- Sensitive data: a hybrid rule stricter than the opt-in model most other states use: a controller may not collect, process, or share sensitive data at all except where strictly necessary to provide or maintain a product or service the consumer requested, and may not sell sensitive data under any circumstance (14-4707(a)(1), (2), as amended).
- Universal opt-out signal: required from 2025-10-01, and a controller that recognizes signals approved by other states is considered compliant (14-4707(G)(2)).
- Cure period: discretionary, for an alleged violation occurring on or before 2027-04-01: the Division may issue a notice of violation if it determines a cure is possible, giving the controller or processor at least 60 days to cure (14-4714). After 2027-04-01 this cure track is not available.
- Notable duties: a controller must conduct and document, on a regular basis, a data protection assessment for each processing activity that presents a heightened risk of harm to a consumer, including an assessment for each algorithm used, with heightened risk defined to include targeted advertising, sale of personal data, processing of sensitive data, and profiling that presents a reasonably foreseeable risk of unfair, abusive, or deceptive treatment, unlawful disparate impact, financial, physical or reputational injury, an offensive intrusion on solitude or seclusion, or other substantial injury to a consumer (Com. Law 14-4710(b)); a controller may not knowingly sell personal data to a federal, state, or local governmental unit that, within the preceding six months, engaged in or supported civil immigration enforcement through personnel or material resources (Com. Law 14-4707(a)(6), added by Chapter 874 of 2026); may not sell personal data where the controller knew or should have known the purchaser seeks it for immigration enforcement to such a unit; corresponding limits on compliance with immigration-related subpoenas, inquiries, and law-enforcement cooperation requests from such a unit (14-4712(a)(2)).
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20.

## Minnesota

- Statute and citation: Minnesota Consumer Data Privacy Act, Minn. Stat. ch. 325M.
- In force from: 2025-07-31, except postsecondary institutions regulated by the Office of Higher Education, which have until 2029-07-31.
- Applicability thresholds: conducts business in Minnesota or targets Minnesota residents and, during a calendar year, controls or processes personal data of 100,000 or more consumers, excluding payment-transaction-only data, or derives over 25 percent of gross revenue from the sale of personal data and processes or controls personal data of 25,000 or more consumers (325M.12, subd. 1).
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, correct, delete, portability, opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and significant-effect profiling, plus a right to question and seek review of an adverse profiling-based decision (325M.14, subd. 1(g)) and a right to a list of specific third parties the controller disclosed the consumer's data to (325M.14, subd. 1(h)).
- Sensitive data: opt-in; consent required, or parental or guardian consent under COPPA for a known child (325M.16, subd. 2(d)). Consent also required for targeted advertising or sale where the controller knows the consumer is 13 to 16 (325M.16, subd. 2(f)). A small business that is otherwise exempt may still not sell a consumer's sensitive data without prior consent (325M.17(a)).
- Universal opt-out signal: required (325M.14, subd. 3).
- Cure period: a 30-day warning-letter cure applied only until it expired 2026-01-31; after that date the Attorney General may bring an enforcement action without a prior warning letter (325M.20(a)).
- Notable duties: controllers must maintain a documented inventory of personal data as part of their data security practices (325M.16, subd. 2(c)); a named chief privacy officer or equivalent contact is part of the required privacy-policy documentation (325M.18(a)(1)); civil penalty capped at $7,500 per violation, no private right of action (325M.20(c), (d)).
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20.

## Oklahoma

- Statute and citation: Oklahoma Data Privacy Act, SB 546, 2026 Reg. Sess.
- In force from: 2027-01-01 (enacted, not yet in force as of the date read).
- Applicability thresholds: conducts business in Oklahoma or targets Oklahoma residents and controls or processes personal data of at least 100,000 consumers, or at least 25,000 consumers while deriving over 50 percent of gross revenue from the sale of personal data.
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, correct, delete, portability, opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and significant-effect profiling (Section 2 of the act, to be codified at 75A O.S. Section 301).
- Sensitive data: opt-in; consent required, or COPPA compliance for a known child (Section 19, item 4 of the act).
- Universal opt-out signal: not required by the text read.
- Cure period: 30 days; if the controller or processor cures the identified violation within the period and provides the Attorney General a written statement that the violation was cured, with supportive documentation, the Attorney General may not bring an action (Section 13).
- Notable duties: a controller must provide information in response to a consumer request free of charge up to twice annually per consumer, not once every 12 months as in the Virginia-model states (Section 3, subsection D, to be codified at 75A O.S. Section 302(D)); data protection assessment requirements apply only to processing activities commencing on or after the act's effective date, with no retroactive application (Section 10(G), to be codified at 75A O.S. Section 309(G)); a controller or processor who violates the act after the Section 13 cure period, or who breaches a written statement given to the Attorney General under that section, is liable for a civil penalty of up to $7,500 for each violation (Section 14).
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20.

## Louisiana

- Statute and citation: Louisiana Data Privacy Act, SB 386, 2026 Reg. Sess., Act No. 502, R.S. 51:1780.1 to 1780.5.
- In force from: 2027-01-01 (enacted, not yet in force as of the date read, R.S. 51:1780.5 note).
- Applicability thresholds: a different test from the Virginia model: does business in Louisiana and either has annual gross revenues in excess of $25,000,000, annually buys, receives for commercial purposes, sells, or shares for commercial purposes the personal information of 75,000 or more consumers, households, or devices, or derives 50 percent or more of annual revenues from selling consumers' personal information (R.S. 51:1780.2(A)). This mirrors the CCPA's revenue-and-volume test rather than the Virginia-model consumer-count thresholds most other matrix states use.
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, correct, delete, portability, opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and significant-effect profiling (R.S. 51:1780.3(A)).
- Sensitive data: opt-in; consent required, or COPPA-compliant processing for a known child's sensitive data (R.S. 51:1780.4).
- Universal opt-out signal: not required by the text read.
- Cure period: 30 days; the Attorney General will not initiate an investigation if the person cures the alleged violation within the period, provides a written statement that it was cured, submits supporting documentation, and changes internal policy if necessary to prevent recurrence (R.S. 51:1780.5).
- Notable duties: a controller subject to the revenue-only threshold (R.S. 51:1780.2(A)(3)) may not engage in the sale of personal data that is sensitive data without receiving prior consumer consent, stated as its own separate duty beyond the general sensitive-data consent gate (R.S. 51:1780.4(P)).
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20.

## Alabama

- Statute and citation: Alabama Personal Data Protection Act, HB 351, 2026 Reg. Sess.
- In force from: 2027-05-01 (enacted, not yet in force as of the date read).
- Applicability thresholds: conducts business in Alabama or targets Alabama residents and either controls or processes the personal data of more than 25,000 consumers, excluding payment-transaction-only data, or derives more than 25 percent of gross revenue from the sale of personal data regardless of consumer count (Section 3). This 25,000-consumer floor with no higher 100,000-consumer tier is unusually low among the matrix states.
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, correct, delete, portability, opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and significant-effect profiling (Section 5(a)).
- Sensitive data: opt-in; consent required for a consumer other than a known child, or COPPA-compliant processing for a known child's data (Section 6, item 2).
- Universal opt-out signal: required; the act requires an opt-out preference signal mechanism and addresses conflicts between a signal and a controller-specific privacy setting or loyalty-program participation the same way Colorado, Connecticut, and Montana do.
- Cure period: 45 days; if the controller corrects the noticed violation within the period and gives the Attorney General a written statement that the violations were corrected and will not recur, no action may be initiated (Section 11(b)(3)).
- Notable duties: the text read does not show a lower-tier consumer-count exemption for small volumes the way the 100,000/25,000 split states do, so even a modest-volume controller crosses the 25,000-consumer threshold.
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20.

## New Hampshire

- Statute and citation: New Hampshire consumer data privacy, N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. (RSA) 507-H:1 to 507-H:9 (this row covers 507-H:1 through 507-H:4 and 507-H:6 through 507-H:9; RSA 507-H:5 and any later section are not covered here, so check them before relying on the blank cells).
- In force from: 2025-01-01.
- Applicability thresholds: conducts business in New Hampshire or produces products or services targeted to New Hampshire residents and, during a one-year period, controlled or processed the personal data of not less than 35,000 unique consumers, excluding payment-transaction-only data, or controlled or processed the personal data of not less than 10,000 unique consumers and derived more than 25 percent of gross revenue from the sale of personal data (507-H:2, I).
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, correct, delete, portability, opt out of targeted advertising, sale (except as RSA 507-H:6 limits), and significant-effect profiling (507-H:4, I).
- Sensitive data: opt-in; consent required, or COPPA compliance for a known child (507-H:6, I(d)). Consent also required for targeted advertising or sale where the controller knows and willfully disregards that the consumer is 13 to under 16 (507-H:6, I(g)).
- Universal opt-out signal: required by 2025-01-01 (507-H:6, V(a)(1)(B)).
- Cure period: not covered here. RSA 507-H:5 and any enforcement or cure provision after 507-H:9 are outside this row; check the current RSA text before relying on a cure period. This cell stays blank.
- Notable duties: a defined term "secure and reliable means" (507-H:1, XXVII-a) sets a standard for the methods a controller must use for consumer communications, naming encryption protocols, authentication mechanisms, access controls, and redundant systems as examples.
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20; RSA 507-H:5 and any section after 507-H:9 are not covered by this row for the reason stated above.

## Vermont

- Statute and citation: Vermont Data Privacy and Online Surveillance Act, 2026 Vt. Acts & Resolves No. 145 (S.71), to be codified at 9 V.S.A. chapter 61A, sections 2415a to 2415k.
- In force from: 2028-01-01 (Sec. 4); the Governor signed the bill 2026-06-16.
- Applicability thresholds: a different test from the Virginia model: conducts business in Vermont or produces products or services targeted to Vermont residents and, during the preceding calendar year, controlled or processed the personal data of not fewer than 35,000 consumers, excluding payment-transaction-only data, controlled or processed the sensitive data of not fewer than 3,000 consumers, excluding payment-transaction-only data, or offered for sale in trade or commerce the personal data of not fewer than 3,000 consumers (9 V.S.A. 2415b(a)). The consumer health data provisions and section 2415k apply with no volume threshold to any person conducting business in or targeting Vermont residents (2415b(b)).
- Consumer rights: confirm and access, including whether personal data is processed for profiling that produces a legal or similarly significant effect; correct; delete; portability; opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and profiling for decisions with a legal or similarly significant effect; question and be informed of the reason for an adverse profiling-based decision, and, for a housing decision, correct the data and have the decision reevaluated; a list of third parties the controller sold the consumer's data to, or, if none is kept, a list of all third parties sold to (2415d(a)).
- Sensitive data: opt-in; consent required, or COPPA compliance for a known child (2415e(a)(4)(C)). Consent also required before selling sensitive data (2415e(a)(4)(B)). Vermont's sensitive-data definition reaches gender-affirming health data, reproductive or sexual health data, neural data, and status as nonbinary or transgender, broader than the Virginia-model list (2415a(b)(47)).
- Universal opt-out signal: required; the opt-out mechanism must not unfairly disadvantage another controller, must not rely on a default setting, must be consumer friendly and easy to use, must be as consistent as possible with other states' mechanisms, and must let the controller determine Vermont residency and the request's legitimacy (2415e(d)(2)(C)). A conflict between the signal and a controller-specific privacy setting or loyalty-program participation is resolved in the consumer's favor, though the controller may flag the conflict and offer the consumer a choice (2415e(d)(3)).
- Cure period: during 2028-01-01 to 2029-06-30, the Attorney General must issue a notice of violation before bringing an action if a cure is possible, and a cure within 60 days of receipt blocks the action (Sec. 3). No cure right is stated for violations after 2029-06-30.
- Notable duties: no private right of action; the General Assembly states its intent to fund a dedicated enforcement capacity at the Attorney General's office and may revisit the no-private-right-of-action choice if that funding does not follow (Sec. 2(a)); a privacy notice must disclose whether the controller collects, uses, or sells personal data to train large language models (2415e(c)(1)(H)); no geofence within 1,850 feet of a health care facility, including a mental health or reproductive or sexual health facility, to identify, track, or notify a consumer about consumer health data (2415k(3)); data protection and profiling impact assessments required for heightened-risk processing, applicable only to activities created or generated after 2028-01-01, not retroactive (2415g(a), (e)(3)).
- Check before relying: 2026-10-20 (read from the enacted Act 145 text, sections 2415a to 2415k and Secs. 2 to 4).

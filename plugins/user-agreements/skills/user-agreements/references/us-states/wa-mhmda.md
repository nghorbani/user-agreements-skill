# Washington My Health My Data Act

Citation: Wash. Rev. Code ch. 19.373
Version: current Revised Code of Washington, chaptered 2023 c 191
Official text: https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373&full=true
Law as of: 2026-10-20

## When it applies

- Applies to a "regulated entity": any legal entity that conducts business in Washington, or produces or provides products or services targeted to Washington consumers, and that alone or jointly with others determines the purpose and means of collecting, processing, sharing or selling consumer health data; it excludes government agencies, tribal nations, and contracted service providers processing on a government agency's behalf (§ 19.373.010(23)).
- A "small business" is a regulated entity that either collects, processes, sells or shares the consumer health data of fewer than 100,000 consumers in a calendar year, or derives under 50 percent of gross revenue from such data and collects, processes, sells or shares the data of fewer than 25,000 consumers; a small business gets a later compliance date for the chapter's operative sections (§ 19.373.010(28)).
- "Consumer" means a natural person who is a Washington resident, or a natural person whose consumer health data is collected in Washington, acting only in an individual or household context; it excludes a person acting in an employment context (§ 19.373.010(7)).
- "Consumer health data" is personal information linked or reasonably linkable to a consumer that identifies the consumer's past, present or future physical or mental health status, including individual health conditions, treatment, diagnosis or disease; social, psychological, behavioral or medical interventions; health-related surgeries or procedures; use or purchase of prescribed medication; bodily functions, vital signs, symptoms or measurements; gender-affirming care information; reproductive or sexual health information; biometric data; genetic data; precise location information that could reasonably indicate an attempt to acquire health services or supplies; data identifying a consumer seeking health care services; and data a regulated entity or small business derives or extrapolates from nonhealth information (including by algorithm or machine learning) to associate or identify a consumer with any of the foregoing (§ 19.373.010(8)(a), (b)). It excludes personal information used for public or peer-reviewed scientific, historical or statistical research in the public interest that follows applicable ethics and privacy law and is overseen by an institutional review board or similar body that finds reasonable safeguards against reidentification risk (§ 19.373.010(8)(c)).
- "Personal information", and so "consumer health data" as a category of personal information, excludes publicly available information and excludes deidentified data (§ 19.373.010(18)(b), (c)). Data is deidentified, and so outside the definition, only where the regulated entity or small business holding it meets all three conditions: it takes reasonable measures to ensure the data cannot be associated with a consumer; it publicly commits to processing the data only in deidentified form and not attempting to reidentify it; and it contractually obligates every recipient of the data to meet the same three conditions (§ 19.373.010(10)(a)-(c)). A representation in a privacy policy that data is anonymized or aggregated and so outside the chapter holds only if this three-part test is met.
- "Collect" means to buy, rent, access, retain, receive, acquire, infer, derive, or otherwise process consumer health data in any manner, so inferring or deriving data triggers the same collection-consent duty under § 19.373.030(1)(a) as gathering it directly (§ 19.373.010(5)).
- "Biometric data" is data generated from measuring or technologically processing an individual's physiological, biological or behavioral characteristics that identifies a consumer alone or combined with other data, including iris, retina, fingerprint, face, hand, palm or vein imagery and voice recordings from which an identifier template can be extracted, and keystroke or gait patterns or rhythms carrying identifying information (§ 19.373.010(4)).
- "Genetic data" is any data concerning a consumer's genetic characteristics regardless of format, including raw DNA sequence data, genotypic and phenotypic information from analyzing it, and self-reported health data a consumer submits that is analyzed together with the consumer's raw sequence data (§ 19.373.010(13)).
- "Gender-affirming care information" is personal information about seeking or obtaining past, present or future gender-affirming care services, including health-indicative precise location information, efforts to research or obtain such services, and data derived, extrapolated or inferred from nonhealth information (§ 19.373.010(11)).
- "Reproductive or sexual health information" is personal information about seeking or obtaining past, present or future reproductive or sexual health services, including health-indicative precise location information, efforts to research or obtain such services, and data derived, extrapolated or inferred from nonhealth information (§ 19.373.010(24)).
- "Precise location information" is information, from GPS coordinates or another mechanism, that directly identifies an individual's specific location with precision and accuracy within a radius of 1,750 feet; it excludes the content of communications and data generated by or connected to advanced utility metering infrastructure (§ 19.373.010(19)).
- "Geofence" is technology using GPS, cell tower connectivity, cellular data, radio frequency identification, WiFi data or another form of spatial or location detection to establish a virtual boundary around a specific physical location, or to locate a consumer within one, limited for this chapter to a boundary of 2,000 feet or less from the location's perimeter (§ 19.373.010(14)).
- "Sell" or "sale" is the exchange of consumer health data for monetary or other valuable consideration; it excludes an exchange to a third party as part of a merger, acquisition, bankruptcy or similar transaction where the third party assumes the business's assets and complies with the chapter, and an exchange to a processor consistent with the purpose the data was collected for and disclosed to the consumer (§ 19.373.010(26)).
- "Share" or "sharing" is releasing, disclosing, disseminating, divulging, making available, providing access to, licensing or otherwise communicating consumer health data to a third party or affiliate; it excludes disclosure to a processor consistent with the collection purpose, disclosure to a third party the consumer has a direct relationship with where the disclosure serves a product or service the consumer requested, the entity keeps control and ownership of the data, and the third party acts only on the entity's direction and within the consented purpose, and disclosure as part of a qualifying merger, acquisition or bankruptcy transfer (§ 19.373.010(27)).
- "Third party" means an entity other than a consumer, regulated entity, processor, small business, or affiliate of the regulated entity or the small business; a processor or an affiliate is excluded from the definition, which matters for the share and sale carve-outs above and for the third-party disclosure list a consumer can request under § 19.373.040(1)(a) (§ 19.373.010(29)).
- "Consent" means a clear affirmative act signifying a freely given, specific, informed, opt-in, voluntary and unambiguous agreement, which may be written and given electronically; it cannot be obtained through acceptance of a general or broad terms-of-use agreement bundled with unrelated content, through hovering over, muting, pausing or closing content, or through a deceptive design (§ 19.373.010(6)).
- "Deceptive design" is a user interface designed or manipulated with the effect of subverting or impairing a user's autonomy, decision-making or choice (§ 19.373.010(9)).
- The chapter does not apply to several categories of information tied to recognized health-regulatory frameworks: HIPAA protected health information; health care information under chapter 70.02 RCW; patient identifying information under 42 C.F.R. Part 2; identifiable private information under the federal human-subjects research rules (45 C.F.R. Part 46, the international council for harmonization's good clinical practice guidelines, or 21 C.F.R. Parts 50 and 56); information and documents created for specified quality improvement, peer review or quality assurance committees, or specified hospital and manufacturer reporting duties; information under the federal health care quality improvement act of 1986; patient safety work product under 42 C.F.R. Part 3; and data deidentified under 45 C.F.R. Part 164 that derives from any of the foregoing (§ 19.373.100(1)(a)). It also excludes information that originates from, and is intermingled to be indistinguishable with, that excluded information when held by a HIPAA covered entity or business associate, a health care facility or provider under RCW 70.02.010, or a 42 C.F.R. Part 2 program or qualified service organization (§ 19.373.100(1)(b)); information used only for public health purposes under 45 C.F.R. § 164.512 or as part of a limited data set under 45 C.F.R. § 164.514 (§ 19.373.100(1)(c)); and identifiable data under chapter 43.371 RCW or RCW 69.43.165 (§ 19.373.100(1)(d)).
- Personal information governed by and collected, used or disclosed under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, Part C of Title XI of the Social Security Act, the Fair Credit Reporting Act, FERPA, the Washington health benefit exchange statutes and 45 C.F.R. § 155.260, or insurance commissioner privacy rules under chapter 48.02 or 48.43 RCW, is exempt from the chapter (§ 19.373.100(2)).
- The chapter's duties do not restrict collecting, using or disclosing consumer health data to prevent, detect, protect against or respond to security incidents, identity theft, fraud, harassment, malicious or deceptive activity, or conduct illegal under Washington or federal law; to preserve system integrity or security; or to investigate, report or prosecute responsible parties; the entity relying on this carve-out bears the burden of showing it qualifies and complies with this provision (§ 19.373.100(3), (4)).

## What the documents must say

- Maintain a consumer health data privacy policy that clearly and conspicuously discloses the categories of consumer health data collected and the purpose of collection, including how the data is used; the categories of sources the data is collected from; the categories of consumer health data shared; a list of the categories of third parties and specific affiliates the data is shared with; and how a consumer can exercise the rights in § 19.373.040 (§ 19.373.020(1)(a)).
- Prominently publish a link to the consumer health data privacy policy on the entity's homepage (§ 19.373.020(1)(b)).
- Before collecting or sharing consumer health data under a consent basis, disclose clearly and conspicuously: the categories of data collected or shared; the purpose of the collection or sharing, including the specific ways the data will be used; the categories of entities the data is shared with; and how the consumer can withdraw consent from future collection or sharing (§ 19.373.030(1)(c)).
- Describe in the privacy policy a secure and reliable means for a consumer to submit a rights request, suited to how consumers normally interact with the entity, to the need for secure and reliable communication, and to the entity's ability to authenticate the requester (§ 19.373.040(1)(d)). "Authenticate" means using reasonable means to determine that a request to exercise a chapter right is made by, or on behalf of, the consumer entitled to exercise it, and this is the standard the entity's authentication effort is measured against throughout the chapter (§ 19.373.010(3)).

## What the documents must not say

- Do not rely on acceptance of a general or broad terms-of-use agreement, on a consumer hovering over, muting, pausing or closing content, or on a deceptive design as consent for any purpose this chapter requires consent for (§ 19.373.010(6)(b)).
- Do not collect, use or share a category of consumer health data, or use it for a purpose, that is not already disclosed in the consumer health data privacy policy, without first disclosing the additional category or purpose and getting the consumer's affirmative consent (§ 19.373.020(1)(c), (d)).
- Do not contract with a processor to process consumer health data in a way inconsistent with the entity's own consumer health data privacy policy (§ 19.373.020(1)(e)).
- Do not collect consumer health data except with consent for a specified purpose, or to the extent necessary to provide a product or service the consumer requested (§ 19.373.030(1)(a)).
- Do not share consumer health data except with consent that is separate and distinct from collection consent, or to the extent necessary to provide a product or service the consumer requested (§ 19.373.030(1)(b)).
- Do not unlawfully discriminate against a consumer for exercising a chapter right (§ 19.373.030(1)(d)).
- Do not require a consumer to create a new account to exercise a chapter right; requiring use of an existing account is permitted (§ 19.373.040(1)(d)).
- Do not sell or offer to sell consumer health data without first obtaining a valid authorization from the consumer, separate and distinct from collection or sharing consent, and keep the sale consistent with that signed authorization (§ 19.373.070(1)).
- Do not condition the provision of goods or services on the consumer signing a sale authorization (§ 19.373.070(2)(e), (3)(e)).
- Do not combine a sale authorization with other documents into a compound authorization (§ 19.373.070(3)(d)).
- Do not treat an authorization as valid once its expiration date has passed, once the consumer has revoked it, or if it is missing any of the nine required elements (§ 19.373.070(3)(a), (b), (c)).
- Do not implement a geofence (within 2,000 feet of the perimeter) around an entity providing in-person health care services to identify or track consumers seeking care, collect their consumer health data, or send them notifications, messages or advertisements related to their health data or health care services; this prohibition has no consent-based exception (§ 19.373.080).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- Publish and keep current a consumer health data privacy policy with the five required disclosures, linked prominently from the homepage; for a mobile application, treat the application's platform or download page, or a link from its configuration, about, information or settings page, as the homepage (§ 19.373.020(1)(a), (b); § 19.373.010(16)).
- Gate every new collection purpose or data category, and every new sharing purpose or data category, on an updated disclosure plus fresh affirmative consent before the collection, use or sharing happens (§ 19.373.020(1)(c), (d)).
- Build collection consent, sharing consent and sale authorization as three separate consent instruments; none may be bundled into or satisfied by another, and none may be satisfied by a general terms-of-use acceptance (§ 19.373.030(1)(b)(i); § 19.373.070(1), (3)(d); § 19.373.010(6)(b)).
- Give a consumer, on request, confirmation of whether the entity is collecting, sharing or selling the consumer's health data, access to that data, a list of all third parties and affiliates the data was shared with or sold to, and an active email address or other online mechanism to contact those third parties (§ 19.373.040(1)(a)).
- Let a consumer withdraw consent to collection and sharing of the consumer's health data at any time (§ 19.373.040(1)(b)).
- On a deletion request, delete the consumer health data from the entity's records, including every part of its network and archived or backup systems, and notify every affiliate, processor, contractor and other third party the data was shared with so each honors the same deletion request on its own records, subject to the same chapter requirements (§ 19.373.040(1)(c)(i), (ii)). Where the data sits on archived or backup systems, the deletion may be delayed for restoration, but not more than six months from authenticating the request (§ 19.373.040(1)(c)(iii)).
- Accept a rights request through a secure, reliable channel described in the privacy policy, take commercially reasonable steps to authenticate the requester, and if authentication fails, ask the consumer for the additional information reasonably necessary to authenticate instead of acting on the request (§ 19.373.040(1)(d), (e)).
- Respond to a request free of charge, up to twice a year per consumer; a manifestly unfounded, excessive or repetitive request may carry a reasonable administrative fee or be declined, with the entity bearing the burden of showing that character (§ 19.373.040(1)(f)).
- Comply with an access, withdrawal or deletion request without undue delay and in all cases within 45 days of receipt; authentication steps do not extend this deadline. One 45-day extension is available when reasonably necessary, if the entity tells the consumer of the extension and its reason within the initial 45 days (§ 19.373.040(1)(g)).
- Run an appeal process for a refused request: conspicuously available, similar in form to the request channel, decided in writing within 45 days of receipt with reasons given, and on a denied appeal, an online mechanism if available, or another method, to reach the attorney general with a complaint (§ 19.373.040(1)(h)).
- Restrict employee, processor and contractor access to consumer health data to those for whom access is necessary to further a purpose the consumer consented to, or to provide a product or service the consumer requested (§ 19.373.050(1)(a)).
- Maintain administrative, technical and physical security practices meeting at least a reasonable industry standard of care for the volume and nature of the consumer health data at issue (§ 19.373.050(1)(b)).
- Process consumer health data as a processor only under a binding contract with the regulated entity or small business that sets processing instructions and limits the processor's permitted actions, and only consistent with those instructions; assist the entity with appropriate technical and organizational measures in meeting its chapter obligations (§ 19.373.060(1)(a), (b)). A processor that departs from those instructions or acts outside the contract's scope is itself treated as a regulated entity or small business for that data, with full chapter obligations attaching (§ 19.373.060(1)(c)).
- Build a sale authorization document in plain language stating: the specific consumer health data to be sold; the name and contact information of the entity collecting and selling the data; the name and contact information of the purchaser; a description of the sale's purpose, including how the data will be gathered and how the purchaser will use it; a statement that goods or services may not be conditioned on signing; a statement of the right to revoke at any time plus how to submit a revocation; a statement that the data sold may be redisclosed by the purchaser and may lose this chapter's protection; an expiration date one year from the consumer's signature; and the consumer's signature and date (§ 19.373.070(2)). Give the consumer a copy of the signed authorization (§ 19.373.070(4)).
- Retain every valid sale authorization, both as seller and purchaser, for six years from its signature date or the date it was last in effect, whichever is later (§ 19.373.070(5)).
- Treat any chapter violation as an unfair or deceptive act in trade or commerce and an unfair method of competition for purposes of the Washington consumer protection act, chapter 19.86 RCW, so an internal compliance or dispute process should route a chapter violation through that act's remedies (§ 19.373.090).

## Prescribed wording

No phrase in this chapter is prescribed in fixed, quotable words for a consumer-facing notice or screen; the privacy policy disclosures, the consent request content, and the sale authorization content are each a list of substantive elements the drafter must cover in the entity's own words, not a quoted label.

## Dates

- The consumer health data privacy policy duty, and the bar on undisclosed new collection, use or sharing categories or purposes, apply from March 31, 2024, except that a small business has until June 30, 2024 (§ 19.373.020(1)(a), (2)).
- The collection and sharing consent duties apply from March 31, 2024, except that a small business has until June 30, 2024 (§ 19.373.030(1), (2)).
- The consumer rights and request-handling duties apply from March 31, 2024, except that a small business has until June 30, 2024 (§ 19.373.040(1), (2)).
- The data security practices duty applies from March 31, 2024, except that a small business has until June 30, 2024 (§ 19.373.050(1), (2)).
- The processor contract duty applies from March 31, 2024, except that a small business has until June 30, 2024 (§ 19.373.060(1), (2)).
- The sale authorization duty applies from March 31, 2024, except that a small business has until June 30, 2024 (§ 19.373.070(1), (6)).
- The geofence ban and the consumer protection act characterization carry no small-business delay and no stated later date (§ 19.373.080; § 19.373.090).

## Open points to raise

- Whether the business qualifies as a "small business" under § 19.373.010(28), since that shifts the compliance date for every operative section except the geofence ban and the consumer protection act section from March 31, 2024 to June 30, 2024.
- Whether any of the entity's data falls within one of the chapter's exemption categories under § 19.373.100, since that removes this chapter's duties for the affected data rather than changing their content, and the entity bears the burden on the security- and fraud-prevention carve-out specifically (§ 19.373.100(3), (4)).
- Whether a given product feature (such as a store locator, check-in prompt or location-triggered notification near a clinic, pharmacy or hospital) falls within the geofence definition's 2,000-foot boundary and one of the three banned uses in § 19.373.080, since this prohibition has no consent-based cure.
- Whether data the entity derives or infers from nonhealth information (for example, from purchase history, search terms or location patterns) falls within consumer health data under § 19.373.010(8)(b)(xiii), since the chapter reaches inferred and algorithmic associations as well as directly collected health data.

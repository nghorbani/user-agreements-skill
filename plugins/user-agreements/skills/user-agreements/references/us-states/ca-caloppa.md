# California Online Privacy Protection Act (CalOPPA)

Citation: Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code §§ 22575 to 22579
Version: Bus. and Prof. Code ch. 22 (§§ 22575 to 22579); § 22575 as amended by Stats. 2013, Ch. 390, Sec. 1; §§ 22576 to 22579 unamended since 2003
Official text: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&division=8.&title=&part=&chapter=22.&article=
Law as of: 2026-10-20

## When it applies

- Applies to an operator of a commercial website or online service that collects personally identifiable information (PII) through the internet about individual consumers who reside in California and use or visit that website or online service (§ 22575(a)).
- "Operator" means a person or entity that owns a website on the internet, or an online service, that collects and maintains PII from a California-resident consumer who uses or visits it, where the website or online service is operated for commercial purposes. A third party that operates, hosts, or manages a website or online service on the owner's behalf, or that processes information on the owner's behalf, without owning it, is not an "operator" (§ 22577(c)). A hosting provider or a data processor acting for the site owner carries no duty under this chapter; the posting duty sits with the owner.
- "Consumer" means an individual who seeks or acquires, by purchase or lease, goods, services, money, or credit for personal, family, or household purposes (§ 22577(d)).
- "Personally identifiable information" means individually identifiable information about an individual consumer that is collected online by the operator from that individual and kept by the operator in an accessible form, including a first and last name; a home or other physical address, including street name and city or town; an email address; a telephone number; a social security number; any other identifier that permits physically or online contacting a specific individual; or other information about a user that the website or online service collects online and keeps in personally identifiable form together with any of the identifiers just listed (§ 22577(a)).
- The posting duty attaches only once the website or online service actually collects PII from California residents; a site or service that collects no PII, or collects only from consumers outside California, is outside the duty as written.
- This chapter preempts the field for one narrow question: it is the Legislature's stated intent that the chapter is a matter of statewide concern, and it supersedes and preempts every rule, regulation, code, ordinance, or other law adopted by a city, county, city and county, municipality, or local agency regarding the posting of a privacy policy on an internet website. No local government in California may impose its own, different privacy-policy-posting rule on an operator (§ 22578).

## What the documents must say

- Identify the categories of PII the operator collects through the website or online service about consumers who use or visit it, and the categories of third-party persons or entities with whom the operator may share that PII (§ 22575(b)(1)).
- If the operator maintains a process for a consumer to review and request changes to their own collected PII, describe that process. This item applies only where such a process exists (§ 22575(b)(2)).
- Describe how the operator notifies consumers of material changes to its privacy policy for the website or online service (§ 22575(b)(3)).
- State the policy's effective date (§ 22575(b)(4)).
- Disclose how the operator responds to web browser "do not track" signals, or other mechanisms that give consumers a choice over the collection of their online activity over time and across third-party websites or online services, but only if the operator engages in that kind of collection. A clear and conspicuous hyperlink in the privacy policy to an online location describing the program or protocol followed, including its effects, satisfies this item in place of writing the description into the policy text itself (§ 22575(b)(5), (7)).
- Disclose whether other parties may collect PII about a consumer's online activity over time and across different websites when the consumer uses the operator's website or service (§ 22575(b)(6)).
- Conspicuously post the privacy policy, or, for an operator of an online service, make it available by one of the means at § 22577(b)(5) (§ 22575(a)). One of the following mechanisms satisfies "conspicuously post" (§ 22577(b)):
  - the actual policy on a web page that is the homepage or the first significant page after entering the website;
  - an icon on the homepage or first significant page that hyperlinks to the page carrying the policy, contains the word "privacy," and uses a color that contrasts with the page's background or is otherwise distinguishable;
  - a text link on the homepage or first significant page that hyperlinks to the page carrying the policy and either includes the word "privacy," is in capital letters at least as large as the surrounding text, or is in larger, contrasting, or otherwise set-off type than the surrounding text of the same size;
  - any other functional hyperlink displayed so that a reasonable person would notice it; or
  - for an online service, any other reasonably accessible means of making the policy available to consumers of that service.

## What the documents must not say

- Do not describe a review-and-change process the operator does not actually offer; the duty to describe one is conditional on the process existing, and a policy should say plainly that no such process exists rather than describe one that is not offered (§ 22575(b)(2)).
- Do not write a do-not-track disclosure that claims the operator does not engage in over-time, cross-site collection if it in fact does; the disclosure duty is triggered by the operator's actual practice, not by what the policy chooses to state (§ 22575(b)(5)).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- The privacy policy link or icon sits on the homepage or the first significant page after entering the site, styled to meet one of the labeling or visibility tests above; a review of the actual placement and styling against § 22577(b)(1) to (4) is a checklist item distinct from checking the policy's wording.
- The operator knows, and can state accurately, which categories of PII it collects and which categories of third parties may receive them, since the content list in § 22575(b)(1) is keyed to the operator's own actual collection and sharing practices.
- The operator can state, from its own practice, whether it maintains a consumer review-and-change process and whether it engages in over-time, cross-site collection of consumer activity, since both content items are conditional on those facts (§ 22575(b)(2), (5)).
- A process exists to update the stated effective date and the change-notification description whenever the privacy policy is materially revised (§ 22575(b)(3), (4)).
- Where a posting gap is found and notice of noncompliance is received, the operator can post the corrected policy within 30 days, since an uncured gap is what converts a posting failure into a violation (§ 22575(a)).

## Prescribed wording

None. The statute fixes specific words that must appear in certain posting mechanisms ("privacy," for the icon and one branch of the text-link test) and specific topics the policy text must cover, but it does not fix verbatim policy sentences.

## Dates

- § 22575 was added by Stats. 2003, Ch. 829, Sec. 3, effective January 1, 2004, operative July 1, 2004 under § 22579. It was amended by Stats. 2013, Ch. 390, Sec. 1, effective January 1, 2014 (§ 22575).
- §§ 22576 and 22577 were added by Stats. 2003, Ch. 829, Sec. 3, effective January 1, 2004, operative July 1, 2004 under § 22579, and carry no later amendment in the official text.
- §§ 22578 and 22579 were added by Stats. 2003, Ch. 829, Sec. 3, effective January 1, 2004. § 22579 fixes the chapter's operative date: the chapter became operative on July 1, 2004.
- A failure to comply with § 22575, or with the operator's own posted privacy policy, is a violation of this chapter only if it is knowing and willful, or negligent and material (§ 22576). A posting failure under § 22575(a) is a violation only if not cured within 30 days after notice of noncompliance (§ 22575(a)).

## Open points to raise

- Whether the operator's actual review-and-change process, if any, matches what the draft policy describes, since this content item is conditional on the process's existence and must track it accurately (§ 22575(b)(2)).
- Whether the operator in fact engages in over-time, cross-site collection of consumer online activity, which decides whether the do-not-track disclosure item applies at all, and whether to rely on the hyperlink safe harbor or draft the disclosure directly (§ 22575(b)(5), (7)).
- Where the site is run through an online service rather than a conventional website, which of the reasonably accessible means under § 22577(b)(5) the operator intends to rely on, since that choice is not fixed by the statute.

# Glossary

Law as of: 2026-10-20

Alphabetical. Each entry states the term, its meaning in one or two sentences, the jurisdiction, and the defining provision. German terms keep their German form with an English gloss.

## A

- **Affirmative consent** (US federal, CAN-SPAM): the recipient expressly consented to receive a commercial email message, either by answering a clear and conspicuous consent request or by acting on their own initiative, with notice at the time of consenting if the address may be passed to another party (15 U.S.C. § 7702(1)).
- **Algorithmic decision-making / automated decision-making** (EU, GDPR): a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, that produces legal effects or similarly significantly affects a data subject; see also profiling (Art. 22(1) GDPR).
- **Announcement** (US federal, Negative Option Rule): material a seller sends under a negative option plan that identifies the selection offered and the procedure to decline it (16 C.F.R. § 425.1(c)(6)).
- **Authorized representative / legal representative** (EU, DSA): a person a non-EU provider designates in a Member State to be addressed by authorities on compliance matters, who can be held personally liable for non-compliance (Art. 13 Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).
- **Automatic renewal** (US states, automatic renewal statutes): a paid subscription or purchasing agreement that renews automatically at the end of a definite term for a subsequent term, distinct from continuous service, which has no definite term (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17601(a)(1); N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 527(1)).
- **Average consumer** (EU, UCPD): the benchmark reader for unfair commercial practices: reasonably well informed, observant and circumspect, or the average member of a vulnerable or targeted group where the practice is aimed at one (Art. 5(2), (3) Directive 2005/29/EC).

## B

- **Bestätigungsfunktion (confirmation function)** (Germany, BGB): the second control a consumer activates to submit a withdrawal declaration online, after supplying the required identification data (§ 356a Abs. 3 BGB).
- **Biometric data** (US states, privacy statutes): data generated by measuring an individual's biological, physiological or behavioral characteristics (fingerprint, voiceprint, iris or retina scan, facial geometry) used or intended to identify a specific person; definitions and exclusions (such as photographs) vary slightly by state (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(ae); Va. Code § 59.1-575; Wash. Rev. Code § 19.373.010(4); 740 ILCS 14/10).
- **Biometric identifier** (Illinois, BIPA): a retina or iris scan, fingerprint, voiceprint, or hand or face geometry scan; excludes writing samples, photographs, and several named categories of medical or tissue-derived data (740 ILCS 14/10).
- **Business user** (EU, P2B Regulation): a business using online intermediation services to offer goods or services to consumers; the party whose relationship with the provider of those services the Regulation's fairness and transparency duties protect (Art. 1(2) Regulation (EU) 2019/1150).

## C

- **CalOPPA operator** (California, CalOPPA): a person or entity that owns a commercial website or online service collecting personally identifiable information from California residents; a third party hosting or processing on the owner's behalf is not an operator (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 22577(c)).
- **Child** (US federal, COPPA): an individual under 13 (15 U.S.C. § 6501(1)); some US state privacy statutes separately define "known child" by the same age threshold for their own consent gate (Tex. Bus. and Com. Code § 541.001(17)).
- **Clear and conspicuous** (US federal and states, multiple regimes): a disclosure standard measured by how consumers actually perceive and understand it in context, not by whether the information exists somewhere findable; several states define it with specific formatting markers (larger or contrasting type, audible volume for spoken disclosures) (FTC .com Disclosures, Section III.B; N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 527(2)).
- **Clickwrap**: an online-assent mechanism where the user must click an explicit "I agree" control after being shown the terms; routinely enforced under US case law, contrasted with browsewrap and scrollwrap (Berman v. Freedom Fin. Network, 30 F.4th 849 (9th Cir. 2022)).
- **Collect** (Washington, My Health My Data Act): to buy, rent, access, retain, receive, acquire, infer, derive, or otherwise process consumer health data in any manner, so that inferring or deriving data triggers the same consent duty as direct collection (Wash. Rev. Code § 19.373.010(5)).
- **Commercial electronic mail message** (US federal, CAN-SPAM): a message whose primary purpose is the commercial advertisement or promotion of a product or service; excludes transactional or relationship messages (15 U.S.C. § 7702(2), (17)).
- **Commercial practice** (EU, UCPD): any act, omission, course of conduct, representation or commercial communication by a trader, directly connected with promoting, selling or supplying a product to a consumer (Art. 2(d) Directive 2005/29/EC).
- **Confidential and sensitive information** (Illinois, BIPA): personal information that can uniquely identify an individual or their account or property, including a genetic marker, account number, PIN, driver's license number or Social Security number, used as the comparator for how securely biometric data must be stored (740 ILCS 14/10).
- **Connected product** (EU, Data Act): an item that obtains, generates or collects data about its use or environment and can communicate that data, provided its main function is not storing, processing or transmitting data on someone else's behalf (Art. 2(5) Regulation (EU) 2023/2854).
- **Consent** (EU, GDPR): the data subject's freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous indication of wishes, given by a statement or a clear affirmative action; never inferred from silence, pre-ticked boxes, inactivity or scrolling (Art. 4(11) GDPR). US state privacy statutes define consent similarly, and several of them expressly exclude a bundled terms-of-use acceptance, hovering, muting or a dark pattern (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(h); Colo. Rev. Stat. § 6-1-1303(5)(a) to (c); Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-515(7)); Virginia's definition states only the bare clear-affirmative-act standard without those exclusions (Va. Code § 59.1-575).
- **Consumer** (varies by instrument; see jurisdiction). EU: a natural person acting for purposes outside their trade, business, craft or profession (Art. 2(b) Directive 2011/83/EU). Germany: a natural person entering a transaction for purposes predominantly outside their trade, business or independent professional activity, the Verbraucher (§ 13 BGB). US federal and states: an individual acting in an individual or household context, excluding a commercial or employment context (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(i); Va. Code § 59.1-575).
- **Consumer health data** (Washington, My Health My Data Act): personal information linked or reasonably linkable to a consumer that identifies their past, present or future physical or mental health status, including inferred or derived associations from nonhealth data (Wash. Rev. Code § 19.373.010(8)).
- **Content moderation** (EU, DSA): the policies, procedures, measures and tools a provider uses to detect, identify and address illegal content or content incompatible with its terms, including whether algorithmic decision-making or human review applies; disclosed in the terms and reported on annually (Art. 14(1), Art. 15(1) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).
- **Continuous service** (US states, automatic renewal statutes): a plan or arrangement with a free-to-pay conversion, or one that simply continues until the consumer cancels, with no definite renewal term (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17601(a)(5); N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 527(4)).
- **Contract-complete subscriber** (US federal, Negative Option Rule): a subscriber who has purchased the minimum quantity of merchandise required under a negative option plan's membership terms; this status gates both the anytime-cancellation right and the first-shipment crediting rule (16 C.F.R. § 425.1(c)(3)).
- **Controller** (EU, GDPR; US states): the natural or legal person who, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of processing personal data (Art. 4(7) GDPR). US state statutes use the same functional, fact-based test rather than a contractual label (Va. Code § 59.1-575; Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(d)).
- **Cookie-UID** (Germany, TDDDG guidance): a cookie or similar stored item carrying a unique identification marker; the strict-necessity consent exemption cannot be claimed for one without checking whether the function actually needs individualisation (German supervisory guidance under §§ 25 to 26 TDDDG).
- **Cross-context behavioral advertising** (California, CCPA): targeting advertisements to a consumer based on their activity across businesses or distinctly branded sites or apps other than the one they intentionally interact with (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(k)).
- **Customer-provided content** (EU, DCD/digital content directives): content, other than personal data, that a consumer provides or creates while using digital content or a digital service, subject to export rights on termination except in four named cases (Art. 16(3) Directive (EU) 2019/770).

## D

- **Dark pattern / deceptive design**: a user interface designed or manipulated with the effect of subverting or impairing a user's autonomy, decision-making or choice. EU: no fixed statutory term, but EDPB guidance on deceptive design patterns treats the same concept (EDPB Guidelines 03/2022). California: "dark pattern" is a defined term under the CCPA (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(l)); other US states use "dark pattern" or "deceptive design" with closely similar wording (Wash. Rev. Code § 19.373.010(9); Tex. Bus. and Com. Code § 541.001(6)).
- **Data holder** (EU, Data Act): the person with the right or obligation to use and make available product data or related-service data, including data it retrieved or generated while providing a related service (Art. 2(13) Regulation (EU) 2023/2854).
- **Data protection assessment / data protection impact assessment**: EU GDPR requires a DPIA before processing likely to result in high risk (Art. 35 GDPR). US state privacy statutes require a comparable "data protection assessment" for heightened-risk processing such as targeted advertising, sale, profiling, or sensitive-data processing, generally not retroactive to pre-enactment activity (Va. Code § 59.1-580; Cal. Code Regs. tit. 11, § 7150; Tex. Bus. and Com. Code § 541.105).
- **Dauerhafter Datenträger (durable medium)** (Germany, BGB): a medium letting the recipient store a personally addressed declaration accessibly for a period appropriate to its purpose, and reproduce it unchanged; the EU-law equivalent is "durable medium" under the Consumer Rights Directive (§ 126b BGB; Art. 2(10) Directive 2011/83/EU).
- **Deceptive design**: see dark pattern / deceptive design.
- **Deidentified data** (US states, privacy statutes): data that cannot reasonably be linked to a consumer, where the holder takes reasonable measures against re-association, publicly commits not to re-identify it, and contractually binds recipients to the same; loosely calling data "anonymous" without meeting all three conditions keeps it within personal information (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(m); Va. Code § 59.1-575; Wash. Rev. Code § 19.373.010(10)).
- **Diensteanbieter (service provider)** (Germany, DDG): a provider of a digital service offered on a commercial, business basis, normally for payment, who owes the Impressum and labeling duties of the DDG (§ 5 Abs. 1 DDG).
- **Digital content** (EU, CRD/DCD): a single act of supply of a specific file ("online digital content"), as distinct from a digital service, which is ongoing access, storage, processing or sharing; the distinction decides which withdrawal regime applies, with genuine doubt defaulting to the service rule (Art. 16(m) Directive 2011/83/EU).
- **Distance contract (Fernabsatzvertrag)**: EU: a contract concluded under an organised distance sales or service-provision scheme, with no simultaneous physical presence of trader and consumer (Art. 2(7) Directive 2011/83/EU). Germany: the parallel concept using exclusively distance-communication means within such a scheme (§ 312c Abs. 1, 2 BGB).
- **Deployer** (EU, AI Act): a natural or legal person, public authority, agency or other body using an AI system under its own authority, except where that use is a purely personal, non-professional activity (Art. 3, point 4 Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).
- **Downstream provider** (EU, AI Act): a provider of an AI system, including a general-purpose AI system, that integrates an AI model, whether vertically integrated or supplied by another entity under contract; carries its own provider-side duties for its own system (Art. 3, point 68 Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).

## E

- **Electronic signature**: EU/Germany has no single fixed term beyond the eIDAS definitions this skill's sources do not cover separately. Illinois defines it, for BIPA purposes, as an electronic sound, symbol or process attached to or logically associated with a record and executed or adopted by a person with intent to sign it (740 ILCS 14/10). US federal ESIGN treats an electronic signature as satisfying a signature requirement without further definition of the term itself, beyond recognising it alongside electronic records (15 U.S.C. § 7001(a)).
- **Electronically supplied services** (EU, geoblocking): services delivered over the internet or an electronic network whose nature makes their supply essentially automated, needing minimal human involvement, impossible without information technology; examples include cloud services, website hosting and search engines (Art. 2(1) Regulation (EU) 2018/302).
- **Exhaustion (of the distribution right)** (EU, Software Directive): once a copy of a computer program is first sold in the Union by the rightholder, or with the rightholder's consent, the rightholder can no longer oppose further resale of that copy within the Union, whatever the supply contract calls itself (Art. 4(2) Directive 2009/24/EC; Case C-128/11, UsedSoft GmbH v Oracle International Corp., judgment of 3 July 2012).
- **Explicit consent** (EU, GDPR): a higher consent standard needing an express statement, not merely an affirmative act, required for special-category processing, third-country transfers absent safeguards, and solely automated decision-making (Art. 9(2)(a), Art. 49(1)(a), Art. 22(2)(c) GDPR).

## F

- **Form** (US federal, Negative Option Rule): any document a subscriber returns to a seller instructing it not to send a selection under a negative option plan (16 C.F.R. § 425.1(c)(7)).
- **Full warranty** (US federal, Magnuson-Moss): a written warranty meeting the federal minimum standards of 15 U.S.C. § 2304, designated "full (statement of duration) warranty"; a warranty labeled full but not actually meeting those standards is nonetheless deemed to incorporate them in any later action (15 U.S.C. § 2303(a)(1), § 2304(e)).

## G

- **Gatekeeper** (EU, Data Act, cross-reference to DMA): an undertaking designated under Art. 3 of Regulation (EU) 2022/1925; not an eligible third party under the Data Act's sharing regime and barred from several data-routing practices (Art. 5(3) Regulation (EU) 2023/2854).
- **Gender-affirming care information** (Virginia-model and Washington statutes): personal information about seeking or obtaining gender-affirming care services, including health-indicative precise location information and data derived or inferred from nonhealth information; one of the broader sensitive-data categories among US state health-privacy statutes (Wash. Rev. Code § 19.373.010(11)).
- **Geofence** (Washington, Connecticut, Vermont): technology using GPS, cell tower connectivity, WiFi data or similar means to establish a virtual boundary around a physical location, bounded at 2,000 feet (Washington) or 1,750/1,850 feet (Connecticut, Vermont) for the respective statute's prohibition on using it to identify or track consumers near a health care facility (Wash. Rev. Code § 19.373.010(14); Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-526(a)(1)).
- **Geschäftsmäßig (on a commercial, business basis)** (Germany, DDG): the threshold distinguishing a provider subject to the Impressum duty from a purely private, non-commercial offering (§ 5 Abs. 1 DDG chapeau).

## H

- **Haltbarkeitsgarantie (durability guarantee)** (Germany, BGB): a manufacturer's guarantee that an item keeps a stated condition for a stated period, giving the consumer a minimum repair claim directly against the manufacturer during that period, alongside the claim against the seller (§ 479 Abs. 3 BGB).
- **Hosting service** (EU, DSA): an intermediary service consisting of the storage of information provided by, and at the request of, a recipient of the service; a hosting service that also stores and disseminates that information to the public at the recipient's request is an online platform, unless the dissemination is a minor, purely ancillary or non-circumventing feature of another service (Art. 3(g), sub-point iii, and Art. 3(i) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).

## I

- **Impressum (legal notice)** (Germany, DDG): the closed, conditional list of identity and contact information a commercial digital-service provider must keep permanently available, easily recognizable and directly accessible (§ 5 DDG).
- **Information content provider** (US federal, Section 230): a person or entity responsible, in whole or in part, for creating or developing the information at issue; a platform that itself creates the specific content is an information content provider for that content and loses the publisher/speaker immunity for it (47 U.S.C. § 230(f)(3)).
- **Initial merchant** (US federal, ROSCA): the person who obtained the consumer's billing information directly from the consumer through a consumer-initiated internet transaction, as distinguished from a post-transaction third party seller (15 U.S.C. § 8402(d)(1)).
- **Interactive computer service** (US federal, Section 230): any information service, system or access software provider that provides or enables computer access by multiple users to a computer server, including internet access providers and library or educational-institution systems (47 U.S.C. § 230(f)(2)).
- **Invitation to purchase** (EU, UCPD): copy that names a product and its price, triggering the Art. 7(4) mandatory-information list once the information is not already apparent from context (Art. 7(4) Directive 2005/29/EC).

## J

- **Jetzt kündigen (cancel now)** (Germany, BGB): the prescribed label for the confirmation button on the cancellation confirmation page a Kündigungsschaltfläche leads to (§ 312k Abs. 2 Satz 3 Nr. 2 BGB).

## K

- **Known child** (US states, privacy statutes): a child in circumstances where the controller has actual knowledge of, or willfully disregards, the child's age; "child" is generally under 13, mirroring COPPA (Tex. Bus. and Com. Code § 541.001(5), (17)).
- **Kündigungsschaltfläche (cancellation button)** (Germany, BGB): a permanently available, immediately and easily accessible button a trader must provide on a website letting consumers conclude a continuing paid contract online, routing directly to a confirmation page (§ 312k Abs. 1, 2 BGB).

## M

- **Mailing date** (US federal, Negative Option Rule): the time a seller specifies by or within which a subscriber's form must be mailed to prevent shipment of a selection under a negative option plan (16 C.F.R. § 425.1(c)(9)).
- **Mixed audience** (US federal, COPPA): a website or online service meeting the directed-to-children factors but not targeting children as its primary audience; may run a neutral age screen before collecting personal information beyond the narrow no-consent exceptions (16 C.F.R. § 312.2 "Mixed audience website or online service").
- **Mobile service commercial message** (US federal, CAN-SPAM): a commercial email message sent directly to a wireless device used by a subscriber of commercial mobile service, in connection with that service; triggers additional FCC rules on top of the general CAN-SPAM duties (15 U.S.C. § 7702(d)).

## N

- **Negative option** (US federal, ROSCA and Negative Option Rule): a term under which a seller treats a customer's silence or failure to act as acceptance of an offer or as consent to continued charges; covers prenotification plans, continuity plans, automatic renewals and free-to-pay conversions (16 C.F.R. § 310.2(w), incorporated by cross-reference at 15 U.S.C. § 8403).
- **Negative option plan** (US federal, Negative Option Rule): a contractual plan where a seller periodically announces merchandise it proposes to send, and the subscriber receives and is billed for it unless the subscriber instructs otherwise by a stated date; narrower than the ROSCA negative option definition, confined to physical merchandise shipped by mail (16 C.F.R. § 425.1(c)(1)).

## O

- **Online intermediation service** (EU, P2B Regulation): the service a provider of online intermediation services offers to business users to sell goods or services to consumers; the Regulation's fairness and transparency duties (terms and conditions, ranking, differentiated treatment, data access, parity, complaint handling) attach to providers of such services established or operating in the Union (Art. 1(2) Regulation (EU) 2019/1150).
- **Online marketplace** (EU, CRD/DSA): a service using software to allow a trader to offer goods or services to a consumer, including through a trader acting on its behalf (Art. 2(17) Directive 2011/83/EU).
- **Online platform** (EU, DSA): a hosting service that also stores and disseminates information to the public at a recipient's request, unless that is a minor, purely ancillary or non-circumventing functionality of another principal service (Art. 3(i) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).
- **Operator** (US federal, COPPA; California, CalOPPA): COPPA: a person running a commercial website or online service that collects or maintains personal information from or about users, including through an agent collecting on its behalf (16 C.F.R. § 312.2 "Operator"). CalOPPA: a person or entity that owns a commercial website or online service collecting PII from California residents; a third-party host or processor is not an operator (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 22577(c)).

## P

- **Parent** (US federal, COPPA): includes a legal guardian (15 U.S.C. § 6501(7)).
- **Personal information / personal data**: broadly similar across jurisdictions but not identical in scope. EU: any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (Art. 4(1) GDPR). California: information that identifies, relates to, describes or could reasonably be linked with a consumer or household, across eleven listed categories (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(v)). COPPA: eleven named categories including persistent identifiers and biometric identifiers (16 C.F.R. § 312.2 "Personal information").
- **Personally identifiable information (PII)** (California, CalOPPA): individually identifiable information collected online and kept in accessible form, including name, address, email, phone number, Social Security number, or another identifier permitting contact, plus linked behavioral data (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 22577(a)).
- **Precise geolocation data**: a sensitive-data category across most US state privacy statutes, defined by a radius within which a device's location is identified: 1,750 feet (Virginia, Connecticut, Texas), 1,850 feet (Colorado from 2025-05-23, Vermont), or specific to the statute (Wash. Rev. Code § 19.373.010(19); Va. Code § 59.1-575).
- **Predispute joint-action waiver** (US federal, FAA chapter 4): an agreement that would prohibit, or waive the right to, participation in a joint, class or collective action in any forum, judicial, arbitral or administrative; unenforceable at the claimant's election for a sexual assault or sexual harassment dispute arising on or after March 3, 2022 (9 U.S.C. § 401(2)).
- **Prenotification plan**: see negative option plan.
- **Privacy notice**: the generic term for the disclosure instrument EU GDPR calls the Art. 13/14 notice and that US state statutes call a "privacy notice" or "privacy policy," each requiring categories of data, purposes, sharing, and rights information, though the exact content list differs by instrument (Art. 13, 14 GDPR; Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.130(a)(5); Va. Code § 59.1-578(C)).
- **Processor**: EU: a natural or legal person who processes personal data on the controller's behalf (Art. 4(8) GDPR). US states use a parallel, fact-based definition (Va. Code § 59.1-575; Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(ag)).
- **Profiling** (EU, GDPR): any form of automated processing of personal data to evaluate certain personal aspects of a natural person, such as performance, economic situation, health, preferences or behaviour (Art. 4(4) GDPR).
- **Provider** (EU, AI Act): a natural or legal person, public authority, agency or other body that develops an AI system or general-purpose AI model, or has one developed, and places it on the market or puts it into service under its own name or trademark, whether for payment or free of charge (Art. 3, point 3 Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).
- **Promotional material** (US federal, Negative Option Rule): an advertisement containing or accompanying material a prospective subscriber uses to request acceptance into a negative option plan (16 C.F.R. § 425.1(c)(4)).
- **Public injunctive relief** (California case law): relief whose primary purpose and effect is to stop unlawful conduct that threatens future injury to the general public, benefiting the plaintiff only incidentally if at all; distinct from private injunctive relief, and cannot be waived by a predispute arbitration clause (McGill v. Citibank, N.A., 2 Cal.5th 945, 955 (2017)).

## Q

- (This glossary has no term beginning with Q.)

## R

- **Recipient of the service** (EU, DSA): any natural or legal person using an intermediary service, in particular to seek or provide access to information (Art. 3(b) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).
- **Rechtsmangel (defect of title)** (Germany, BGB): an assertable third-party right in the sold item beyond what the contract transferred, including a false land-register entry, as distinguished from a Sachmangel (defect of the thing) (§ 435 BGB).
- **Related service** (EU, Data Act): a digital service, other than an electronic communications service, connected to a product such that its absence stops the product working, or connected later to add, update or adapt the product's functions (Art. 2(6) Regulation (EU) 2023/2854); the Product Liability Directive treats a comparable "related service" as a product component once it sits within the manufacturer's control (Directive (EU) 2024/2853, recital 17).
- **Return date** (US federal, Negative Option Rule): the date a seller specifies as the date by which a subscriber's form must be received to prevent shipment of a selection (16 C.F.R. § 425.1(c)(8)).

## S

- **Sachmangel (defect of the thing)** (Germany, BGB): a good that fails the combined subjective, objective and (where owed) assembly conformity requirements at the moment risk passes (§ 434 BGB).
- **Sale / sell**: definitions diverge sharply by jurisdiction. California CCPA: selling, renting, disclosing, or otherwise communicating personal information to a third party for money or other value (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(ad)). Virginia-model states: exchange of personal data for monetary consideration only, excluding disclosure to a processor, an affiliate, or a consumer-requested service (Va. Code § 59.1-575). Washington MHMDA: the exchange of consumer health data for monetary or other valuable consideration (Wash. Rev. Code § 19.373.010(26)).
- **Scrollwrap**: an online-assent mechanism requiring the user to scroll through displayed terms before the "I agree" control activates; gives the strongest notice among the four recognized assent-solicitation types (Nguyen v. Barnes & Noble, 763 F.3d 1171 (9th Cir. 2014)).
- **Selection** (US federal, Negative Option Rule): the merchandise identified by a seller under a negative option plan as what the subscriber will receive and be billed for unless they instruct otherwise (16 C.F.R. § 425.1(c)(5)).
- **Sell** (CCPA): see sale / sell.
- **Sender** (US federal, CAN-SPAM): the person who initiates a commercial email message and whose product, service or website it advertises; a separately branded line of business holding itself out as such, rather than the parent entity, is the sender (15 U.S.C. § 7702(16)).
- **Sensitive data / sensitive personal information**: a heightened category needing opt-in consent (most US states) or a prohibition absent a lawful ground (EU Art. 9 GDPR special categories). Typically covers racial or ethnic origin, religious belief, health, sexual orientation, genetic and biometric data for identification, and precise geolocation; the exact list varies (compare Art. 9(1) GDPR; Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(ae); Va. Code § 59.1-575; Wash. Rev. Code § 19.373.010(8) which uses "consumer health data" instead).
- **Service provider** (CCPA): a person processing personal information on a business's behalf under a written contract barring sale, sharing, retention or use outside the contracted purpose, requiring certification of compliance and letting the business monitor it at least annually (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(ag)).
- **Share / sharing** (CCPA): disclosing personal information to a third party for cross-context behavioral advertising, whether or not for consideration (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(ah)). Washington MHMDA defines "share" for consumer health data with its own carve-outs for processor disclosures and consumer-directed sharing (Wash. Rev. Code § 19.373.010(27)).
- **Sign-in wrap**: an online-assent mechanism with no explicit click on the terms themselves, where text states that a specific action (such as creating an account) constitutes agreement to linked terms; needs both conspicuous notice and unambiguous assent proven, unlike clickwrap (Berman v. Freedom Fin. Network, 30 F.4th 849, slip op. at 29 (9th Cir. 2022)).
- **Social media platform**: Virginia: a public or semipublic internet service letting users interact socially, build a profile and post content (Va. Code § 59.1-575). Connecticut: a closely parallel definition with carve-outs for messaging-only services and primarily provider-selected content (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-528(a)(5)).
- **Standard technical measures** (US federal, Copyright Act § 512): technical measures copyright owners and service providers develop by broad, open, multi-industry consensus, available on reasonable and nondiscriminatory terms, that a qualifying service provider must not interfere with (17 U.S.C. § 512(i)(2)).
- **Subscriber** (US federal, Negative Option Rule): a person who has agreed to receive the benefits of, and assume the obligations of, membership in a negative option plan, whose membership the seller has approved and accepted (16 C.F.R. § 425.1(c)(2)).

## T

- **Targeted advertising**: Virginia-model states: selecting an advertisement for a consumer based on personal data from that consumer's activity over time and across nonaffiliated sites or applications to predict preferences; excludes a controller's own-site advertising and context-based advertising (Va. Code § 59.1-575; Tex. Bus. and Com. Code § 541.001(31)).
- **Terminal equipment (Endeinrichtung)** (Germany, TDDDG; EU, ePrivacy): a device connected, directly or indirectly, to a public telecommunications network interface, covering laptops, phones, connected vehicles and smart-home devices; the anchor concept for the German and EU cookie-consent rule (§ 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 6 TTDSG; Art. 5(3) Directive 2002/58/EC concept of "terminal equipment").
- **Third party** (multiple instruments): consistently means a person other than the consumer, the controller/business and its processors or service providers, though exact scope (for example whether an affiliate counts) varies by statute (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(ai); Va. Code § 59.1-575; Tex. Bus. and Com. Code § 541.001(32)).
- **Transactional or relationship message** (US federal, CAN-SPAM): a message whose primary purpose is completing an agreed transaction, giving warranty or safety information, notifying an account or relationship change, or delivering goods or services already owed; excluded from CAN-SPAM's commercial-message duties (15 U.S.C. § 7702(17)).
- **Trusted flagger** (EU, DSA): an entity holding Digital-Services-Coordinator-awarded trusted-flagger status; a notice it submits through the Art. 16 mechanism, acting within its designated area of expertise, gets priority, undue-delay processing, which ends automatically on suspension or revocation of that status (Art. 22(1), (6), (7) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).

## U

- **Unfair term** (EU, UCTD): a contract term not individually negotiated that, contrary to good faith, causes a significant imbalance in the parties' rights and obligations arising under the contract, to the detriment of the consumer; both a breach of good faith and a resulting significant imbalance are required (Art. 3(1) Directive 93/13/EEC).
- **Unternehmer (trader)** (Germany, BGB): a natural or legal person, or a legally capable partnership, acting in its trade, business or independent professional activity when concluding a transaction (§ 14 BGB); the EU-law equivalent is "trader" under the Consumer Rights Directive and UCPD (Art. 2(b) Directive 2011/83/EU).

## V

- **Verbraucher (consumer)** (Germany, BGB): see consumer, Germany entry (§ 13 BGB).
- **Verbrauchervertrag (consumer contract)** (Germany, BGB): a contract between a trader and a consumer to which Chapters 1 and 2 of the BGB's consumer-contract rules apply, triggered where the consumer pays a price or provides personal data for a purpose beyond the trader's own contractual or legal duty (§ 312 Abs. 1, 1a BGB).

## W

- **Widerrufsfunktion (withdrawal function)** (Germany, BGB): a permanently available, prominently placed, easily accessible online control letting a consumer declare withdrawal from a distance contract without needing to register or authenticate first (§ 356a Abs. 1, 2 BGB); the EU-law equivalent is the Art. 11a withdrawal function under the Consumer Rights Directive (Art. 11a Directive 2011/83/EU, as inserted by Directive (EU) 2023/2673).
- **Widerrufsrecht (right of withdrawal)** (Germany, BGB): the consumer's right to release both sides from their contract-formation declarations by a timely, unambiguous declaration, needing no reason (§ 355 Abs. 1 BGB); the EU-law counterpart is the Art. 9 Consumer Rights Directive withdrawal right (Art. 9(1) Directive 2011/83/EU).
- **Written release** (Illinois, BIPA): informed written consent, an electronic signature, or, in the employment context, a release executed as a condition of employment, required before collecting a person's biometric identifier or biometric information (740 ILCS 14/10).
- **Written warranty** (US federal, Magnuson-Moss): a written affirmation of fact or promise, made in connection with a sale, that material or workmanship is defect-free or will meet a specified performance level over a specified time, or a written undertaking to refund, repair or replace on failure, that becomes part of the basis of the bargain (15 U.S.C. § 2301(6)).

## Z

- **Zahlungspflichtig bestellen (order with obligation to pay)** (Germany, BGB): the prescribed label for an order-submission button carrying a payment obligation in German-facing e-commerce (§ 312j Abs. 3 Satz 2 BGB); the EU-law equivalent is the Art. 8(2) Consumer Rights Directive order-button wording (Art. 8(2) Directive 2011/83/EU).

# Directive (EU) 2024/1799 on common rules promoting the repair of goods

Citation: Directive (EU) 2024/1799 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 June 2024 on common rules promoting the repair of goods and amending Regulation (EU) 2017/2394 and Directives (EU) 2019/771 and (EU) 2020/1828
Version: OJ L 2024/1799 of 10.7.2024
Official text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024L1799
Law as of: 2026-10-20

This file covers a repair regime that sits outside and alongside the ordinary consumer sales guarantee: a
standalone manufacturer's duty to repair certain goods, a voluntary repair information form, and a European
online platform for repair. The Directive applies from 31 July 2026 (Art. 22), so it is not yet in force as of
this file's law-as-of date, but a repair-services document meant to run past that date should already follow
it. The Directive also amends Directive (EU) 2019/771 to add repairability to the seller's conformity test and
extend the liability period on a repair remedy; that amendment is covered in `references/eu/sgd.md`, in the
section headed Repair inside the seller's liability, and not repeated here, because it changes the seller's
existing liability regime rather than adding a new one.

## When it applies

- The Directive applies to repair of goods bought by consumers where a defect occurs or becomes apparent
  outside the seller's liability under Art. 10 of Directive (EU) 2019/771 (Art. 1(2)). A defect still inside
  that liability period follows the sales guarantee in `references/eu/sgd.md`, not this file.
- The manufacturer's repair obligation and its information duty (Art. 5 and 6 of this Directive) apply only to
  goods for which, and to the extent that, repairability requirements exist under Union legal acts listed in
  this Directive's Annex II, typically delegated acts under the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation
  (EU) 2024/1781 (Art. 1(3)). A good outside Annex II's reach carries no Art. 5 or 6 duty, whatever its repair
  information form or platform listing may say.
- Goods takes the Directive (EU) 2019/771 definition but excludes water, gas and electricity (Art. 2(9)).
  A repairer is any natural or legal person who, in connection with their trade, business, craft or profession,
  provides a repair service, including a manufacturer or seller that repairs, and an independent repair
  provider or one affiliated with a manufacturer or seller (Art. 2(2)). A repair-services document should use
  these Article 2 definitions rather than a looser in-house sense of repair or repairer (Art. 2).
- The Directive is fully harmonised: a Member State may not keep or introduce a national rule diverging from
  it (Art. 3). A drafter cannot rely on an assumed Member State variation on these specific rules.
- This Directive leaves Directive (EU) 2018/958 on the proportionality test for regulated professions untouched
  (Art. 1(4)).

## What the documents must say

### The European Repair Information Form (Art. 4)

- Use of the form is voluntary for any repairer, a manufacturer included (Art. 4(1)). Once a repairer chooses
  to issue it, Article 4 fixes its content and timing.
- Where provided, the form must be on a durable medium and given within a reasonable time after the consumer's
  request, before the consumer is bound by a repair services contract (Art. 4(1)).
- The form must be free of charge (Art. 4(2)). The sole exception is a diagnostic service, a physical or remote
  examination to identify the defect, the type of repair and estimate the price: the repairer may charge the
  consumer its necessary costs (Art. 4(3)), but only after informing the consumer of those costs before the
  consumer requests the diagnostic service and before the form is provided (recital 12).
- The form must specify, in a clear and comprehensible manner, all of: the repairer's identity (Art. 4(4)(a));
  the repairer's geographical address, telephone number, email address and, if available, another online
  contact means letting the consumer reach the repairer quickly, efficiently and accessibly (Art. 4(4)(b)); the
  good to be repaired (Art. 4(4)(c)); the nature of the defect and the type of repair suggested (Art. 4(4)(d));
  the price, or, where it cannot reasonably be calculated in advance, how it will be calculated and the maximum
  price (Art. 4(4)(e)); the time needed to complete the repair (Art. 4(4)(f)); the availability of temporary
  replacement goods during repair and any cost of that replacement to the consumer (Art. 4(4)(g)); the place
  where the consumer hands over the good for repair (Art. 4(4)(h)); where applicable, the availability and
  detailed cost of ancillary services such as removal, installation and transportation (Art. 4(4)(i)); the
  form's period of validity (Art. 4(4)(j)); and, where applicable, additional information (Art. 4(4)(k)).
  Missing any one item takes the form outside the Article 4(6) deeming effect below.
- The repairer must not alter the form's conditions, including the price, for 30 calendar days from the date
  the form was provided, unless repairer and consumer agree a longer validity period (Art. 4(5), first and
  second sentences). The form's own Art. 4(4)(j) content item is this validity period, so it must be stated as
  a date or duration, not left blank.
- Where the consumer accepts the form's conditions within its validity period, the repairer is obliged to
  perform the repair service under those conditions (Art. 4(5), third sentence).
- A complete and accurate form deems satisfied, by Art. 4(6): the main-features duties of Art. 5(1)(a) and
  Art. 6(1)(a) of Directive 2011/83/EU and Art. 22(1)(j) of Directive 2006/123/EC (Art. 4(6)(a)); the identity
  and contact-information duties of Art. 5(1)(b) and Art. 6(1)(b) and (c) of Directive 2011/83/EU, Art. 22(1)(a)
  of Directive 2006/123/EC, and Art. 5(1)(a), (b) and (c) of Directive 2000/31/EC (Art. 4(6)(b)); the
  price-information duties of Art. 5(1)(c) and Art. 6(1)(e) of Directive 2011/83/EU and Art. 22(1)(i) and
  (3)(a) of Directive 2006/123/EC (Art. 4(6)(c)); and the performance-arrangements and timing duties of
  Art. 5(1)(d) and Art. 6(1)(g) of Directive 2011/83/EU (Art. 4(6)(d)). A form missing a required item does not
  get this deeming effect, and those separate duties must then be met by other means.

### The manufacturer's obligation to repair (Art. 5 and 6)

- On the consumer's request, the manufacturer must repair goods for which, and to the extent that,
  repairability requirements exist under Annex II legal acts (Art. 5(1), first sentence). This duty runs
  directly to the consumer and sits outside the seller's liability under Directive (EU) 2019/771.
- The manufacturer need not repair where repair is impossible, and may sub-contract repair to fulfil the
  obligation (Art. 5(1), second and third sentences). Sub-contracting moves only the performance, not the
  obligation: the manufacturer stays liable to the consumer even where a sub-contracted repairer does the work
  (recital 17). A document that points the consumer to a repair partner cannot be read as discharging the
  manufacturer's own Art. 5 duty.
- Where the manufacturer is established outside the Union, the obligation passes in a fixed sequence: first to
  its authorised representative in the Union; if there is none, to the importer; if there is no importer, to
  the distributor. Each of these may also sub-contract the repair (Art. 5(3)).
- The repair under Art. 5(1) must be: carried out free of charge or for a reasonable price (Art. 5(2)(a));
  carried out within a reasonable time from when the manufacturer gets physical possession of, receives, or is
  given access to the good by the consumer (Art. 5(2)(b)); and, where repair is impossible, the manufacturer
  may offer the consumer a refurbished good instead (Art. 5(2)(d)). During the repair, the manufacturer may
  offer the consumer the loan of a replacement good, free of charge or for a reasonable fee (Art. 5(2)(c)).
- The reasonable price in Art. 5(2)(a) may take into account labour costs, spare-parts costs, the cost of
  operating the repair facility and a customary margin, but must be set so that consumers are not intentionally
  deterred from using the manufacturer's Art. 5 repair obligation (recital 16).
- A manufacturer that makes spare parts and tools available for Annex II goods must offer them at a reasonable
  price that does not deter repair (Art. 5(4)).
- A manufacturer, or whichever of the authorised representative, importer or distributor holds the Art. 5
  obligation, must ensure consumers can access, via a free-access website, indicative prices for the typical
  repair of goods covered by Annex II legal acts (Art. 5(5)).
- Article 6 separately requires the manufacturer, or where applicable the authorised representative, importer
  or distributor, to make information on their repair services available free of charge, in an easily
  accessible, clear and comprehensible manner, for at least the entire duration of the Art. 5 obligation.
- Article 5(8) preserves consumer choice: the Art. 5 obligation does not stop the consumer going to any
  repairer of their choice instead of the manufacturer.

## What the documents must not say

- A manufacturer's terms must not use contractual clauses, hardware or software techniques that impede repair
  of Annex II goods, unless justified by legitimate and objective factors, including intellectual-property
  protection under Union or national law (Art. 5(6), first sentence).
- In particular, a manufacturer must not impede an independent repairer's use of original or second-hand spare
  parts, compatible spare parts, or spare parts from 3D printing, where those parts comply with Union or
  national law such as product-safety or intellectual-property requirements (Art. 5(6), second sentence).
- A manufacturer must not refuse to repair Annex II goods for the sole reason that another repairer or person
  already performed a previous repair (Art. 5(7)). Cost alone (for example, the price of spare parts) is also
  not a valid ground to refuse: the exemption for cases where repair is impossible does not reach a refusal
  for purely economic reasons or solely because of a prior third-party repair (recital 24).
- A document or marketing line that leads a consumer to believe a good cannot be repaired because an
  independent repairer, a non-professional repairer or the consumer already repaired or inspected it, or that
  falsely claims such a prior repair or inspection creates a safety risk, can itself be an unfair commercial
  practice under Directive 2005/29/EC, separately from whether Art. 5(7) even applies to that good (recital
  19).
- A repair-services or repair-contract clause must not exclude, derogate from, or vary to the consumer's
  detriment the effect of the national measures transposing this Directive; such a clause is simply not binding
  on the consumer, however it was agreed to (Art. 14(1)). This does not stop a repairer offering the consumer
  contractual arrangements that go beyond the Directive's protections (Art. 14(2)).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- A repairer that wants the Art. 4(6) deeming effect builds its quote or intake flow to collect and present all
  eleven Art. 4(4) items before the consumer is bound, states the form's validity period explicitly, and locks
  the quoted price and conditions for at least 30 days from issue unless a longer period is agreed with the
  consumer.
- Where a diagnostic service carries a fee, the flow discloses that fee to the consumer before the consumer
  requests the diagnostic service and before the form is provided (recital 12), not folded silently into the
  final repair price.
- Where a repair services contract is concluded on the strength of the form, the contract incorporates the
  form's stated conditions and price by reference, since they become an integral part of that contract and
  define the repairer's contractual obligations under it (recital 13).
- A manufacturer, or whichever of the authorised representative, importer or distributor holds the Art. 5
  obligation, maintains a free-access website showing indicative repair prices for Annex II goods, and
  separately publishes its repair-services information in an easily accessible, clear and comprehensible manner
  for the full duration of that obligation.
- Where the manufacturer is established outside the Union, the repair-services or warranty document names the
  authorised representative, importer or distributor that holds the Art. 5 obligation, following the fixed
  order and never letting the manufacturer pick freely among them.
- Repair intake and refusal processes record the stated reason for any refusal to repair an Annex II good, so a
  refusal grounded only in cost or a prior third-party repair, which Art. 5(7) does not allow, is visible before
  it reaches the consumer.
- A repairer or manufacturer that wants to appear in the European online platform for repair (Art. 7) registers
  voluntarily and keeps its contact information, services and any adherence to quality standards current, since
  national sections let repairers regularly update this information and let consumers request the repair
  information form directly through the platform (Art. 7(6)(c), (d), (e)).

## Prescribed wording

None. This Directive fixes content elements the European Repair Information Form must carry (Art. 4(4)) and
substantive conditions a repair must meet, not a specific form of words a document must reproduce. Annex I sets
out a standardised form template; this file does not reproduce that template, so treat the exact layout and
wording of the form as an open point to check against the Annex I text itself.

## Dates

- 31 July 2026: general transposition and application deadline (Art. 22(1)); also the deadline for Member
  States to notify the Commission of their national contact point or national online platform choice for the
  European online platform (Art. 9(1)), any access conditions for a national section (Art. 9(2)), and penalty
  rules under Articles 4, 5 and 6 (Art. 15).
- 31 July 2027: the Commission's deadline to develop the common online interface for the European online
  platform (Art. 7(2)); also the deadline for a national online platform relied on under Art. 7(3) to be
  operational.
- 31 July 2029: Member States' deadline to notify the Commission of repair-promotion measures taken under
  Art. 13(1) (Art. 13(2)).
- 2026-10-20: law-as-of date for this file.

## Open points to raise

- Which national transposition implements this Directive for a given deployment, including the penalty regime
  under Art. 15 and any national access conditions for the European online platform's national section under
  Art. 9(2), since the Directive sets the floor but leaves the exact enforcement and access mechanics to each
  Member State's own transposing law.
- Whether the goods sold are covered by Annex II (a Union legal act laying down repairability requirements,
  typically a delegated act under Regulation (EU) 2024/1781), since that decides whether the Art. 5 and 6
  manufacturer's obligation and information duty apply at all; this file does not list what is in Annex II, so
  check the current Annex II acts directly or ask counsel.
- Whether the manufacturer is established outside the Union and, if so, which of its authorised representative,
  importer or distributor in fact holds the Art. 5 obligation under the Art. 5(3) sequence for the goods in
  question.
- Whether the repairer wants to register with the European online platform for repair or a qualifying national
  online platform (Art. 7), since registration is voluntary and changes what the repair-services document
  should say about where the consumer can find or compare the repairer.

# Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065)

Citation: Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 19 October 2022 on a Single Market For Digital Services and amending Directive 2000/31/EC (Digital Services Act)
Version: original, no amendment found
Official text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065
Law as of: 2026-10-20

This file covers Art. 1 to 32: the conditional liability exemptions for intermediary services, the due diligence obligations that apply to all intermediary services, to hosting services, and to online platforms, and the additional obligations for online platforms that let consumers conclude distance contracts with traders. It does not cover Art. 33 onward: the designation and enhanced obligations of very large online platforms and very large online search engines (systemic-risk assessment, crisis response), the Digital Services Coordinators' own powers, the Board, the Commission's supervisory and enforcement powers, or penalties. Several rules below name the Art. 33 very-large-platform threshold only as the trigger for a duty that itself sits inside this file's Art. 1-32 range; where that happens, this file states the trigger and points to Art. 33 for the designation criteria themselves.

## When it applies

- The Regulation reaches any intermediary service offered to recipients established or located in the Union, whatever the provider's own place of establishment (Art. 2(1)). A provider offers services in the Union where it enables people in one or more Member States to use its services and it has a substantial connection to the Union, from an establishment there or from factual criteria such as a significant recipient base relative to population, or activity targeted at one or more Member States (Art. 3(d), (e)).
- Classify the service before drafting against any later Article: it is a mere conduit (passive transmission or access provision), a caching service (automatic, intermediate, temporary storage for more efficient onward transmission), or a hosting service (storage at a recipient's request) (Art. 3(g), sub-points i to iii). A hosting service that also stores and disseminates recipient-requested information to the public is an online platform, unless that is a minor, purely ancillary feature of another service, or a minor functionality of the principal service that cannot for objective and technical reasons be used without it, provided the integration is not a means to avoid the Regulation (Art. 3(i)). The due diligence obligations stack: every intermediary service owes the Art. 11 to 15 duties, every hosting service adds the Art. 16 to 18 duties, and every online platform adds the Art. 20 to 28 duties on top of the Art. 19 exclusion provision that marks where the online-platform Section starts; an online platform that also lets consumers conclude distance contracts with traders adds the Art. 29 to 32 duties on top.
- A micro or small enterprise under Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC is excluded from the online-platform Section (Art. 19 to 24, except Art. 24(3)) and from the marketplace Section (Art. 29 to 32), including for 12 months after losing that status, unless it is designated a very large online platform under Art. 33, in which case both Sections apply regardless of enterprise size (Art. 19(1), (2); Art. 29(1), (2)).
- The Regulation sits alongside, without displacing, Directive 2000/31/EC and a named list of other Union acts: audiovisual media services, copyright and related rights, terrorist content online, product-safety market surveillance, platform-to-business fairness, consumer protection and product safety (including Regulations (EU) 2017/2394 and (EU) 2019/1020 and Directives 2001/95/EC and 2013/11/EU), personal data protection (Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and Directive 2002/58/EC), and judicial cooperation in civil and criminal matters (Art. 2(2) to (4), points a to j). Meeting a duty in this file never substitutes for a duty under GDPR, the e-Privacy Directive, the Consumer Rights Directive or the platform-to-business Regulation; each runs on its own text.
- A consumer is any natural person acting outside their trade, business, craft or profession; a trader is any natural or legal person, public or private, acting for trade, business, craft or professional purposes, including through someone acting in their name (Art. 3(c), (f)). A recipient of the service is any natural or legal person using the service, in particular to seek or provide access to information (Art. 3(b)).

## What the documents must say

### Two separate points of contact, plus a legal representative

- Designate a single electronic point of contact for Member States' authorities, the Commission and the Board, make the identifying information public, accessible and current, and state which official Member State language or languages, beyond one broadly understood language, it operates in, including at least one official language of the Member State of the provider's main establishment or its legal representative's residence (Art. 11(1) to (3)).
- Designate a separate single point of contact for recipients of the service, letting them communicate directly and rapidly by electronic means, in a user-friendly way, with a choice of communication means that is not solely automated; publish the identifying information, kept accessible and current (Art. 12(1), (2)).
- A provider without an establishment in the Union that offers services there must designate, in writing, a legal or natural person as legal representative in a Member State where it offers services, mandated to be addressed by Member States' authorities, the Commission and the Board on every issue needed to receive, comply with and enforce decisions under the Regulation, with real powers and sufficient resources to cooperate and comply (Art. 13(1), (2)). The representative can be held personally liable for non-compliance, without prejudice to action against the provider itself (Art. 13(3)). Notify the representative's name, postal address, email address and phone number to the Digital Services Coordinator where they reside or are established, and keep that information public, accurate and current (Art. 13(4)); designating a representative does not itself create an establishment in the Union (Art. 13(5)).

### Terms and conditions content

- State, in clear, plain, intelligible, user-friendly, unambiguous language, publicly available in an easily accessible, machine-readable format: any restriction imposed on the use of the service in respect of recipient-provided information; the policies, procedures, measures and tools used for content moderation, including whether algorithmic decision-making or human review applies; and the internal complaint-handling system's rules of procedure (Art. 14(1)). Inform recipients of any significant change to the terms and conditions (Art. 14(2)). Apply and enforce the stated restrictions diligently, objectively and proportionately, with due regard to all parties' rights and legitimate interests, including recipients' fundamental rights such as freedom of expression and media freedom and pluralism (Art. 14(4)).
- Where the service is primarily directed at, or predominantly used by, minors, explain the conditions and restrictions in a way minors can understand (Art. 14(3)).
- A very large online platform or very large online search engine (Art. 33) must also give a concise, easily accessible, machine-readable summary of the terms and conditions, including available remedies and redress, in clear and unambiguous language, and must publish the full terms and conditions in the official languages of every Member State where it offers its services (Art. 14(5), (6)); neither duty replaces the full Art. 14(1) disclosure.

### Transparency reporting on content moderation

- Publish, at least once a year, in a machine-readable, easily accessible format, a clear and comprehensible report on content moderation during the relevant period, covering: the number of Art. 9 and 10 authority orders received, categorised by illegal-content type and issuing Member State, with the median response time; for hosting services, the number of Art. 16 notices received, categorised by alleged-content type, the number from trusted flaggers, the action taken distinguishing a legal from a contractual ground, the number processed by automated means, and the median action time; meaningful information on own-initiative content moderation, including automated-tool use and moderator training, categorised by illegal-content or terms-and-conditions-violation type, detection method and restriction type; for online platforms, internal complaint data under Art. 20, including the complaint basis, the decisions taken, the median decision time and the reversal count; and a qualitative description of any automated means used, their precise purposes, accuracy and error-rate indicators, and the safeguards applied (Art. 15(1), points a to e). This duty does not apply to a micro or small enterprise provider under Recommendation 2003/361/EC that is not a very large online platform (Art. 15(2)).
- An online platform adds to that report: the number of disputes submitted to Art. 21 out-of-court settlement bodies, their outcomes, the median completion time, and the share where the provider implemented the body's decision; and the number of Art. 23 suspensions, split between manifestly illegal content, manifestly unfounded notices and manifestly unfounded complaints (Art. 24(1), points a, b).
- By 17 February 2023, and at least every six months after, publish, for each online platform or search engine, in a public section of the online interface, the average monthly active recipients in the Union over the preceding six months, per the methodology in any Art. 33(3) delegated acts adopted (Art. 24(2)); on request, give the Digital Services Coordinator of establishment and the Commission the updated figure and, where asked, further calculation detail excluding personal data (Art. 24(3)). Where that Coordinator has reason to consider the Art. 33(1) threshold is met from this information, it informs the Commission (Art. 24(4)).
- Submit, without undue delay, every Art. 17(1) decision and statement of reasons to the Commission for a publicly accessible, machine-readable database it manages, with no personal data included (Art. 24(5)).

### Notice-and-action mechanism (hosting services)

- Put in place a mechanism letting any individual or entity notify specific items of allegedly illegal content, easy to access, user-friendly, and submittable only by electronic means (Art. 16(1)). Build it to accept a notice containing: a substantiated explanation of why the information is alleged to be illegal content; a clear indication of its exact electronic location, such as a URL, with further identifying information as needed; the notifier's name and email address, except for the offence categories in Art. 3 to 7 of Directive 2011/93/EU; and a statement that the notifier believes in good faith that the notice's information and allegations are accurate and complete (Art. 16(2), points a to d). A notice meeting this four-element standard is deemed to give the provider actual knowledge or awareness for Art. 6 purposes, where it lets a diligent provider identify the illegality without a detailed legal examination (Art. 16(3)).
- Send a confirmation of receipt without undue delay where the notice includes electronic contact information (Art. 16(4)), and notify the notifier, also without undue delay, of the decision taken on the content, with redress-possibility information (Art. 16(5)). Process and decide every notice in a timely, diligent, non-arbitrary, objective manner, and disclose in the Art. 16(5) notification any use of automated means in processing or deciding it (Art. 16(6)).

### Statement of reasons for a content or account restriction

- Give a clear and specific statement of reasons to any affected recipient for a restriction imposed on the ground that the recipient's information is illegal content or incompatible with the terms and conditions, covering: a visibility restriction (removal, access-disabling, demotion); suspension, termination or restriction of monetary payments; suspension or termination of the service in whole or part; or suspension or termination of the recipient's account (Art. 17(1), points a to d). This duty runs regardless of whether the restriction started from a notice or from an own-initiative investigation, and applies only where the relevant contact details are known, from the moment the restriction is imposed (Art. 17(2), first subparagraph).
- The statement must cover: what the decision entails, its territorial scope and its duration where relevant; the facts and circumstances relied on, including whether based on an Art. 16 notice (and, where strictly necessary, the notifier's identity) or an own-initiative investigation; any use of automated means in the decision; for an illegal-content decision, the legal ground and why the information is illegal on that ground; for a terms-and-conditions decision, the contractual ground and why the information is incompatible with it; and clear, user-friendly redress information covering internal complaints, out-of-court settlement and judicial redress (Art. 17(3), points a to f). Write it clear, easily comprehensible, and as precise and specific as reasonably possible, sufficient for the recipient to actually use the stated redress routes (Art. 17(4)).

### Internal complaint-handling system (online platforms)

- Give recipients, including anyone who submitted a notice, access, for at least six months from the Art. 16(5) or Art. 17 notification, to an effective internal complaint-handling system, letting them lodge a complaint electronically and free of charge against a decision taken on a notice, or against a decision to remove, disable access to or restrict visibility of information; suspend or terminate the service in whole or part; suspend or terminate the recipient's account; or suspend, terminate or otherwise restrict monetisation of the recipient's information (Art. 20(1), points a to d; Art. 20(2)).
- Make the system easy to access, user-friendly, and able to accept sufficiently precise, adequately substantiated complaints (Art. 20(3)). Handle every complaint in a timely, non-discriminatory, diligent, non-arbitrary manner, and reverse the original decision without undue delay where the complaint shows sufficient grounds that the decision was unfounded, that the content is not illegal and not incompatible with the terms and conditions, or that the complainant's conduct did not warrant the measure (Art. 20(4)). Inform the complainant without undue delay of the reasoned decision and of the Art. 21 out-of-court settlement possibility and other redress options (Art. 20(5)). Decide every complaint under the supervision of appropriately qualified staff, never solely by automated means (Art. 20(6)).

### Out-of-court dispute settlement (online platforms)

- A recipient addressed by an Art. 20(1) decision, including an unresolved complaint, may select any certified out-of-court dispute settlement body to resolve the dispute; make the information on this route easily accessible on the online interface, clear and user-friendly, and preserve the recipient's unaffected right to go to court at any stage (Art. 21(1)). Engage in good faith with the selected body; a provider may refuse only where a dispute over the same information and the same grounds was already resolved, and the body cannot impose a binding settlement (Art. 21(2)).
- Where the body decides for the recipient, the provider pays all the body's fees and reimburses the recipient's other reasonable expenses; where it decides for the provider, the recipient owes nothing for the provider's fees or expenses unless found to have acted manifestly in bad faith. Fees charged to the provider must be reasonable and never exceed the body's actual costs; settlement for the recipient is free or at a nominal fee. Fees, or the mechanism for determining them, are disclosed to both parties before settlement begins (Art. 21(5)). A certified body must decide within 90 calendar days of the complaint, extendable once by up to 90 further days for a highly complex dispute, 180 days total at most (Art. 21(4), third subparagraph).
- This route is without prejudice to Directive 2013/11/EU and its own consumer alternative dispute resolution procedures and entities (Art. 21(9)).

### Trusted flaggers (online platforms)

- Give priority, undue-delay processing to a notice submitted through the Art. 16 mechanism by an entity holding Digital-Services-Coordinator-awarded trusted-flagger status, acting within its designated area of expertise (Art. 22(1)). Where a provider has information that a trusted flagger has submitted a significant number of insufficiently precise, inaccurate or inadequately substantiated notices, communicate that, with explanations and supporting documents, to the awarding Digital Services Coordinator, which may open an investigation and suspend the status for its duration; the Coordinator revokes the status if the investigation finds the entity no longer meets the award conditions, after giving it a chance to respond (Art. 22(6), (7)). Priority processing ends automatically with suspension or revocation; a provider cannot extend it to a flagger acting outside its designated area, and cannot de-prioritise unilaterally in place of reporting to the Coordinator.

### Misuse suspension policy (online platforms)

- Suspend, for a reasonable period and after a prior warning, service to a recipient who frequently provides manifestly illegal content, and separately suspend, for a reasonable period after a prior warning, processing of notices or complaints from an individual, entity or complainant who frequently submits manifestly unfounded ones (Art. 23(1), (2)). Base every suspension decision on a case-by-case, timely, diligent, objective assessment weighing at least: the absolute number of manifestly illegal items or manifestly unfounded notices or complaints in a given timeframe; their relative proportion against the total items or notices submitted in that timeframe; the gravity of the misuse, including the illegal content's nature and consequences; and, where identifiable, the intention behind the conduct (Art. 23(3), points a to d).
- Set out the misuse policy, clearly and in detail, in the terms and conditions, with concrete examples of the facts and circumstances considered and the suspension's duration (Art. 23(4)).

### Interface design, advertising and recommender systems (online platforms)

- Do not design, organise or operate the online interface in a way that deceives or manipulates recipients, or otherwise materially distorts or impairs their ability to make a free, informed decision (Art. 25(1)). This bar does not apply to a practice already covered by Directive 2005/29/EC or Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (Art. 25(2)).
- For each advertisement shown to each individual recipient, let the recipient identify, clearly, concisely, unambiguously and in real time: that the information is an advertisement; the natural or legal person on whose behalf it is presented; the natural or legal person who paid for it, if different; and meaningful, directly and easily accessible information on the main targeting parameters and, where applicable, how to change them (Art. 26(1), points a to d). Let a recipient declare whether their own content is or contains commercial communications, and when they do, let other recipients identify that clearly, unambiguously and in real time (Art. 26(2)). Never present an advertisement based on profiling (within the meaning of Art. 4, point (4), of Regulation (EU) 2016/679) that uses the special categories of personal data under Art. 9(1) of that Regulation (Art. 26(3)).
- Set out, in plain, intelligible language in the terms and conditions, the main parameters of a recommender system and any options a recipient has to modify or influence them, including the criteria most significant in determining the suggested information and the reasons for their relative importance (Art. 27(1), (2)). Where more than one ordering option exists, provide a functionality, directly and easily accessible from the interface section where the information is prioritised, letting the recipient select and change their preferred option at any time (Art. 27(3)).
- Put in place appropriate, proportionate measures to ensure a high level of privacy, safety and security for minors on a service accessible to them (Art. 28(1)). Never present an advertisement based on profiling (within the meaning of Art. 4, point (4), of Regulation (EU) 2016/679) using a recipient's personal data when aware, with reasonable certainty, that the recipient is a minor (Art. 28(2)); this duty does not oblige collecting additional personal data to assess whether a recipient is a minor (Art. 28(3)).

### Trader onboarding and marketplace duties (online platforms allowing distance contracts with traders)

- Before letting a trader use the platform to promote or offer products or services to Union consumers, obtain, where applicable to that trader: name, address, phone number and email address; a copy of an identification document or other electronic identification under Art. 3 of Regulation (EU) No 910/2014; payment account details; the trade register and registration number (or equivalent) where the trader is registered in one; and a self-certification committing to offer only products or services that comply with applicable Union law (Art. 30(1), points a to e). On receiving this information, make best efforts, through a freely accessible official database or interface or by requesting supporting documents from reliable sources, to assess whether it is reliable and complete; the trader itself is liable for the accuracy of what it provides (Art. 30(2), first subparagraph). Where there is sufficient indication that an item is inaccurate, incomplete or outdated, ask the trader to remedy it without delay or within the applicable legal period, and swiftly suspend service to a trader who fails to correct or complete it, until full compliance (Art. 30(3)).
- Store the information obtained securely for six months after the contractual relationship with the trader ends, then delete it, and disclose it to a third party only where applicable law requires it, including an Art. 10 order or an order by a Member State competent authority or the Commission performing tasks under the Regulation (Art. 30(5), (6)). Make the trader's name, trade-register details and self-certification available to recipients, clearly, accessibly and comprehensibly, at least on the interface page where the product or service information is presented (Art. 30(7)).
- Design the online interface so a trader can comply with its own pre-contractual information, compliance and product-safety duties under applicable Union law, in particular letting it supply the economic operator's name, address, phone number and email address under Art. 3, point (13), of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, information for clear and unambiguous product or service identification, any trademark, symbol or logo identifying the trader, and, where applicable, labelling and marking information under applicable product-safety and product-compliance Union law (Art. 31(1), (2), points a to c). Make best efforts to assess whether a trader has provided this information before letting it offer products or services, and, once allowed, make reasonable efforts to randomly check, in official, freely accessible, machine-readable databases or interfaces, whether the offered products or services have been identified as illegal (Art. 31(3)).
- Where aware, by any means, that an illegal product or service was offered by a trader to Union consumers through the platform, inform every consumer who purchased it, insofar as their contact details are held, of the product or service's illegality, the trader's identity and any relevant means of redress, limited to purchases made in the six months before the provider became aware (Art. 32(1)). Where contact details are not held for all affected consumers, make that same information publicly available and easily accessible on the online interface instead (Art. 32(2)).

## What the documents must not say

- No clause may assert unconditional non-liability for user-provided or transmitted content: the Art. 4 mere-conduit, Art. 5 caching and Art. 6 hosting exemptions each apply only while their stated conditions hold (Art. 4(1), 5(1), 6(1)). The Art. 6 hosting exemption does not apply where the recipient acts under the provider's authority or control (Art. 6(2)), nor, for liability under consumer protection law, where an online platform presents the item or enables the transaction in a way that would lead an average consumer to believe the online platform itself, or a recipient acting under its authority or control, is the one providing the information, product or service (Art. 6(3)).
- No clause may suggest that the Regulation itself requires blanket pre-screening or general monitoring of content: no general obligation to monitor transmitted or stored information, and no general obligation to actively seek facts or circumstances indicating illegal activity, may be imposed on an intermediary-service provider (Art. 8). Conversely, running voluntary, good-faith, diligent own-initiative content investigations does not by itself make a provider ineligible for the Art. 4 to 6 exemptions, so a clause reserving the right to such sweeps is consistent with both rules (Art. 7).
- No internal complaint-handling clause may shorten the six-month minimum access window from the Art. 16(5) or Art. 17 notification, treat reversal on sufficient grounds as discretionary, or route the Art. 20(5) decision through automated means alone (Art. 20(1), (2), (4), (6)).
- No misuse-suspension clause may describe the policy only in generic terms, such as a bare statement that abusive accounts may be suspended; it must give concrete examples of the facts and circumstances considered and state the suspension's duration (Art. 23(4)), and no suspension may be imposed without the prior warning and the case-by-case assessment (Art. 23(1) to (3)).
- No advertising or recommender clause may permit profiling-based targeting with special-category personal data, or any personal-data profiling of a recipient known or reasonably presumed to be a minor, even where the terms otherwise allow personalised advertising generally (Art. 26(3), Art. 28(2)).
- No interface, sign-up, consent, subscription or cancellation flow may give a trader-preferred choice disproportionate prominence, repeatedly re-prompt an already-made choice (particularly through interrupting pop-ups), or make ending the service harder than starting it, except to the extent the practice is already addressed under Directive 2005/29/EC or Regulation (EU) 2016/679 instead (Art. 25(1) to (3)).
- No statement-of-reasons clause may narrow the duty to notice-triggered restrictions only; it applies equally to an own-initiative restriction, with only two carve-outs: deceptive high-volume commercial content, and an Art. 9 authority order (Art. 17(2), (5)).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- The service's content-moderation policies, procedures, measures, tools (including any algorithmic decision-making and human review) and the internal complaint system's rules of procedure are published in full, in clear and plain language, in a machine-readable format, and recipients are notified of a significant change (Art. 14(1), (2)).
- Every authority order received under Art. 9 or 10 is checked against its required elements (legal basis, statement of reasons, identification, redress information, territorial-scope limit) before being acted on, and the affected recipient is notified, with the same information, at the latest when effect is given (Art. 9(2), (5); Art. 10(2), (5)).
- The notice-and-action intake form captures all four Art. 16(2) elements (substantiated explanation, exact location, notifier identity with its narrow exception, bona fide statement), confirms receipt without undue delay, and records whether automated means decided the outcome (Art. 16(2), (4) to (6)).
- Every restriction (removal, demotion, access-disabling, payment restriction, account suspension or termination) generates its own statement of reasons, whether it started from a notice or an own-initiative investigation, except the two Art. 17(2) carve-outs (Art. 17(1) to (3)).
- The internal complaint system keeps a case open for at least six months from notification, is staffed by qualified reviewers rather than solely automated decision-making, and triggers mandatory reversal once a complaint shows sufficient grounds (Art. 20(1), (2), (4), (6)).
- The redress page signposts the Art. 21 out-of-court settlement route and the unaffected right to go to court, alongside the internal complaint system, and states the asymmetric fee rule (losing provider pays all fees and the recipient's reasonable expenses; losing recipient, absent bad faith, pays nothing) (Art. 21(1), (5)).
- A trusted-flagger notice is routed to priority processing automatically while the flagger's status and designated area are current, and a pattern of poor-quality notices from a flagger is escalated to the awarding Digital Services Coordinator rather than handled by unilateral de-prioritisation (Art. 22(1), (6), (7)).
- A misuse suspension is preceded by a documented prior warning and a case-by-case assessment recorded against the four Art. 23(3) factors, and the policy with its examples and stated duration is kept current in the terms and conditions (Art. 23(1) to (4)).
- Annual transparency reports (and, for online platforms, the additional Art. 24(1) data) are compiled from the operational records the mechanisms above already generate: order counts and response times, notice counts and action taken, own-initiative moderation data, complaint data, and automated-means descriptions (Art. 15(1); Art. 24(1)).
- Each advertisement carries its own real-time label (advertisement status, who it is for, who paid, targeting parameters and how to change them), and the recommender system's main parameters and any recipient-facing modification options are kept current in the terms and conditions (Art. 26(1); Art. 27(1) to (3)).
- A marketplace's trader-onboarding flow collects and verifies the five Art. 30(1) items before activation, suspends a trader who fails to correct or complete them, retains the record for six months after the relationship ends then deletes it, and displays the trader's name, trade-register details and self-certification on the product or service page (Art. 30(1), (2), (3), (5), (7)).
- The marketplace interface lets a trader supply its own economic-operator and product-safety information, and the platform runs a recurring, random check of offered products or services against official illegal-product databases (Art. 31).

## Prescribed wording

None: this Regulation's Art. 1 to 32 fix what must be disclosed, designed, collected, verified or reported, and the conditions attaching to each duty, not a specific wording a terms document or an interface control must display.

## Dates

- 17 February 2023: first deadline for an online platform's or online search engine's average-monthly-active-recipient publication, then at least every six months after (Art. 24(2)).
- 17 February 2024: the date against which the 12-month grace period for a trader already using a marketplace platform runs, for obtaining the Art. 30(1) information from that trader (Art. 30(2), second subparagraph).
- 2026-10-20: law-as-of date for this file.

## Open points to raise

- Whether the service, or a specific feature of it, is an "online platform" under Art. 3(i) at all, given the minor-ancillary-feature carve-out in that definition, since that classification decides whether the whole Art. 20 to 28 Section applies.
- Whether the provider (or a specific platform it operates) is designated, or is approaching designation, as a very large online platform or very large online search engine under Art. 33, since that designation is outside this file's Art. 1-32 range but is the trigger for the Art. 14(5), (6), 19(2), 24(2) to (4) and 29(2) duties named above.
- Whether the provider qualifies as a micro or small enterprise under Recommendation 2003/361/EC, and if so, how recently it may have lost that status, since both affect which Sections of this file apply (Art. 19, Art. 29).
- Whether the platform lets consumers conclude distance contracts with traders at all, since only then do the Art. 29 to 32 marketplace duties apply on top of the online-platform duties.
- What the provider's internal threshold is for a "significant change" to the terms and conditions that triggers the Art. 14(2) notification duty, since the Regulation does not define the term.

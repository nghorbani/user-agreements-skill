# GDPR privacy notice content

Citation: Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 27 April 2016
Version: original text, no substantive consolidation exists
Official text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679
Law as of: 2026-10-20

This file covers what a privacy notice must say and how it must say it: the Article 12 quality
standard, the Article 13 and 14 content lists, and the timing and update rules that follow from
them. For the lawful bases a notice has to name (consent, contract, legitimate interest and the
rest) see `references/eu/gdpr-legal-bases.md`. For the data subject's substantive rights beyond
what a notice must disclose about them, and for controller liability, see
`references/eu/gdpr-rights-liability.md`.

## When it applies

- Every controller must give a privacy notice before or at the point personal data is collected,
  whichever article applies: Article 13 where the data comes from the data subject (an online
  form, an account sign-up, a device sensor), Article 14 where it comes from elsewhere (another
  controller, a public register, a data broker, a third party) (Art. 13(1); Art. 14(1)).
- The notice duty runs throughout the processing and does not end at collection: a material change
  to an already-given notice triggers a fresh, active notification, and a breach communicated under
  Article 34 must meet the same quality standard as the original notice (Art. 12(1)).
- The duty falls away only where the data subject already has the information, and, for Article 14
  only, where giving it proves impossible, demands disproportionate effort, is barred by a specific
  law, or the data is under professional secrecy (Art. 13(4); Art. 14(5)(a) to (d)).

## What the documents must say

### The Article 12(1) quality standard

- Write the notice, and every later communication about a data subject's rights, in a concise,
  transparent, intelligible and easily accessible form, in clear and plain language, with extra
  care where the information targets a child (Art. 12(1)).
- State the purpose and the legal basis in concrete, specific terms; a purpose phrased with an
  open-ended qualifier such as "may", "might", "some" or "possible" fails this standard unless the
  controller can show why the qualifier could not be avoided (Art. 12(1); EDPB Guidelines on
  transparency, paras. 12 to 13).
- Give the notice in writing by default; other means, including electronic delivery, are allowed,
  and oral delivery is available on the data subject's request once their identity is confirmed by
  other means for a rights-exercise communication (Art. 12(1)).
- Keep the notice free: information under Articles 13 and 14, and every communication and action
  under Articles 15 to 22 and 34, must be provided at no charge, and may never be made conditional
  on a purchase or other financial transaction (Art. 12(5); EDPB Guidelines on transparency,
  para. 22).

### The Article 13 content list (data collected from the data subject)

Give the data subject, at the time of collection, all of the following (Art. 13(1), (2)):

- the identity and contact details of the controller and, where it has one, its representative;
- the data protection officer's contact details, where the controller has one;
- the purposes of the processing and the legal basis for each;
- where the basis is legitimate interest, the specific legitimate interests pursued, named for the
  controller or the third party relying on them, not a generic reference to the article (Art. 6(1)
  point (f); EDPB Guidelines on transparency, Annex notes);
- the recipients or categories of recipients, where there are any, named specifically where
  possible rather than only by category (EDPB Guidelines on transparency, Annex notes);
- whether the controller intends a third-country or international-organisation transfer, and,
  where it does, the adequacy decision it relies on, or, for a transfer under Article 46 or 47 or
  the second subparagraph of Article 49(1), the safeguard relied on and how to get a copy of it;
- the storage period, or, where a fixed period is not possible, the criteria that set it, stated so
  the data subject can work out roughly how long their own data will be kept; a bare statement that
  data is kept as long as necessary, with no further detail, does not meet this standard (EDPB
  Guidelines on transparency, Annex notes);
- the existence of the rights to access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and
  portability;
- where the basis is consent or explicit consent, the right to withdraw it at any time, without
  affecting the lawfulness of processing carried out before withdrawal;
- the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority;
- whether providing the data is a statutory or contractual requirement, or needed to enter a
  contract, whether the data subject must provide it, and what happens if they do not;
- the existence of automated decision-making, including profiling, that falls under Article 22(1)
  or (4), with meaningful information about the logic involved and the significance and envisaged
  consequences for the data subject.

### The Article 14 content list (data obtained elsewhere)

Give the same list as Article 13, with two differences (Art. 14(1), (2)):

- add the categories of personal data concerned, since the data subject was not the source and so
  has no existing awareness of what the controller holds;
- add the source the data came from, and whether it was publicly accessible; name the specific
  source unless doing so genuinely proves impossible, in which case describe the type and sector
  of the source instead (Art. 14(2)(f); EDPB Guidelines on transparency, Annex notes).

Article 14 drops the Article 13(2)(e) item on mandatory provision and its consequences; that item
has no Article 14 counterpart, since the data subject did not supply the data themselves.

### Timing

- Article 13 information is due at the time of collection, with no later alternative (Art. 13(1)).
- Article 14 information is due within a reasonable period and at the latest within one month,
  brought forward to the time of the first communication with the data subject if that happens
  sooner, and brought forward again to the time of the first disclosure to another recipient if
  that happens sooner still, each earlier trigger displacing the one-month backstop only where it
  falls earlier (Art. 14(3)(a) to (c)).
- Where the controller means to use the data for a new purpose beyond the one it collected the data
  for, it must give the data subject the new purpose and the Article 13(2) or 14(2) information
  before that further processing starts, with a gap before processing begins; the more intrusive or
  surprising the new use, the longer that gap should be (Art. 13(3); Art. 14(4); EDPB Guidelines on
  transparency, para. 48).

### Keeping the notice current

- A change in processing purpose, in the controller's identity, or in how a data subject exercises
  their rights is a material change; notify it through a dedicated, active communication, sent well
  before the change takes effect, and separate from marketing content (Art. 12(1); EDPB Guidelines
  on transparency, paras. 29 to 31).
- A typo fix or a stylistic or grammatical correction is not a material change and needs no active
  notification (EDPB Guidelines on transparency, para. 29).
- Where the change is fundamental to the processing itself, such as a materially wider recipient
  category or a new third-country transfer, give the data subject a real opportunity to react, by
  objecting or by withdrawing consent, before the change takes hold; notifying the change does not
  by itself excuse processing that has become incompatible with the original purpose (Art. 5(1)(b);
  EDPB Guidelines on transparency, paras. 30 to 31).

### Layering and delivery

- Online, structure the notice in layers: a first layer giving the purposes, the controller's
  identity, the rights available, and whichever part of the processing most affects or could
  surprise the data subject, with full detail reachable, consistently, from there (EDPB Guidelines
  on transparency, paras. 35 to 36).
- Surface the first layer at the point of collection itself, for example beside the form field it
  explains, not only inside a separately linked document (EDPB Guidelines on transparency,
  para. 36).
- For an app, keep a dedicated privacy or data-protection option reachable in no more than two taps
  from the app's main functionality, and make the same information available, specific to that app,
  from its listing in the app store before download (EDPB Guidelines on transparency, para. 11 and
  worked example).
- A screenless device needs a channel beyond an online notice, such as a URL on the printed
  packaging or setup instructions, or an audio alert if the device has audio output (EDPB
  Guidelines on transparency, para. 19).
- Icons under Article 12(7) may sit alongside the written notice to give a quick, visible overview,
  and must be machine-readable wherever shown electronically, but they only supplement the written
  Article 13 or 14 information and never stand in for it (Art. 12(7)).

### Objection and automated decisions inside the notice

- State the right to object to direct marketing, including related profiling, separately from the
  rest of the notice and bring it explicitly to the data subject's attention no later than the
  first communication with them (Art. 21(4)).
- Where the notice describes automated decision-making with a legal or similarly significant
  effect, disclose the logic involved and the significance and envisaged consequences, and, where
  the decision rests on contract necessity or explicit consent, state the path to human
  intervention, to express a view, and to contest the decision (Art. 13(2)(f); Art. 22(3)).

## What the documents must not say

- No purpose statement may rest on an indefinite qualifier such as "may", "might", "some" or
  "possible" without the controller being able to show why it could not be avoided; a purpose
  phrased only as developing new services, conducting research, or personalising the service, with
  no further detail, does not meet the Article 12(1) clarity standard (EDPB Guidelines on
  transparency, para. 12).
- No clause may fold the objection right for direct marketing into a general rights paragraph; it
  must stand apart and be flagged at first contact (Art. 21(4)).
- No clause may tell the data subject to check back periodically for policy updates in place of
  actively notifying a material change; the Guidelines treat that pattern as both insufficient and
  unfair (Art. 5(1)(a); EDPB Guidelines on transparency, para. 29).
- No notice may charge a fee, or make access conditional on a purchase, for the ordinary Article 13
  or 14 information or an ordinary rights request; a fee is available only for a request shown to
  be manifestly unfounded or excessive, with the burden of showing that on the controller
  (Art. 12(5)).
- No notice may claim an Article 14 exception (impossibility, disproportionate effort, a legal
  obligation, professional secrecy) without being able to document the specific fact it rests on;
  disproportionate effort in particular should not be invoked outside archiving, research or
  statistical processing under Article 89(1) safeguards (Art. 14(5)(a) to (d); EDPB Guidelines on
  transparency, para. 61).
- An icon display may not replace the written Article 13 or 14 information; it may only
  supplement it (Art. 12(7)).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- The collection form, sign-up flow or data-intake point surfaces the first-layer notice content
  (purpose, controller identity, rights available, the most impactful part of the processing) at
  or before the point of collection, with a working link through to the full notice.
- A material-change pipeline exists: a change to purpose, controller identity, or how rights are
  exercised triggers an active, dedicated notification (email, in-product message, or equivalent)
  before the change takes effect, distinct from the ordinary release-notes or marketing channel.
- The rights-request channel (access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection, portability)
  is free by default, responds within one month, and can extend to three months with a logged
  reason sent to the data subject inside the first month.
- Records capture what notice content was shown to a given data subject, and when, since the
  Article 14(5) exceptions and any Article 12(1) qualifier the controller does use both need to be
  justified after the fact.
- Where data is obtained from another source, the intake process records that source (and whether
  it was public) so the Article 14(2)(f) item can be filled in accurately rather than left generic.

## Prescribed wording

Article 17 carries its own title as fixed text: "Right to erasure ('right to be forgotten')"
(Art. 17 heading); in German, Recht auf Löschung („Recht auf Vergessenwerden“) (Art. 17
Überschrift). Use this exact title in a notice's rights section rather than a paraphrase.
No other wording in this file is fixed by the Regulation; the Article 12(1) "clear and plain
language" standard and the Article 13/14 content lists set required substance, not a required
form of words.

## Dates

- 25 May 2018: the GDPR's application date; Articles 12 to 14 have applied, in this form, since
  then, and this is the original text with no substantive consolidation (Art. 99(2)).
- 2026-10-20: law-as-of date for this file.

## Open points to raise

- Whether the Article 12(7)/(8) standardised icon scheme has been adopted by Commission delegated
  act; this file states the permissive rule that icons may supplement a notice, but the icon
  content itself is not fixed by a source this file cites.
- Whether any processing described in the notice relies on an Article 14(5)(b) exception
  (impossibility, disproportionate effort, serious impairment of the processing's aim); that
  determination turns on facts about a specific processing activity this file cannot settle.
- Whether the product's data intake records the source of indirectly obtained data in enough
  detail to fill in the Article 14(2)(f) item, since a notice that cannot name the source has to
  fall back to a sector-level description instead.

# Regulation (EU) 2019/1150: fairness and transparency for business users of online intermediation services (P2B)

Citation: Regulation (EU) 2019/1150 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 20 June 2019 on promoting fairness and transparency for business users of online intermediation services
Version: original, no amendment found
Official text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32019R1150
Law as of: 2026-10-20

This file covers Articles 1 to 11 of the P2B Regulation: terms and conditions, restriction, suspension and termination of a business user's access, ranking transparency, ancillary goods and services, differentiated treatment (self-preferencing), specific contractual terms, access to data, parity restrictions, and the internal complaint-handling system. Alongside the Regulation it covers the Commission's Guidelines on ranking transparency pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2019/1150 (2020/C 424/01, OJ C 424, 8.12.2020), non-binding guidance that interprets Art. 5 and is cited here as the ranking guidance. The ranking guidance is without prejudice to a provider's own duty to comply with Art. 5 and to the enforcement powers of the courts and authorities of the member states; only the Court of Justice of the EU can bindingly interpret the Article (ranking guidance, para. 9).

## When it applies

- The Regulation applies to providers of online intermediation services and providers of online search engines offered to business users and corporate website users respectively that have their place of establishment or residence in the Union and that, through those services, offer goods or services to consumers located in the Union, regardless of the provider's own place of establishment or residence and regardless of what law would otherwise apply (Art. 1(2)).
- It does not apply to online payment services, or to online advertising tools or online advertising exchanges that are not provided with the aim of facilitating the initiating of direct transactions and that do not involve a contractual relationship with consumers (Art. 1(3)).
- It is without prejudice to national rules that, consistent with Union law, prohibit or sanction unilateral conduct or unfair commercial practices, to the extent the Regulation does not cover the relevant aspects, and it does not affect national civil law, including the rules on a contract's validity, formation, effects or termination, on the same condition (Art. 1(4)). It is also without prejudice to Union law on judicial cooperation in civil matters, competition, data protection, trade secrets, consumer protection, electronic commerce and financial services (Art. 1(5)).
- A provider that is vertically or horizontally integrated, or that bundles several online intermediation services or search engines in one corporate structure, owes the Art. 5 ranking-transparency duties separately for each distinct service, because a single combined explanation is normally not meaningful to the users of any one of them; the same separate assessment applies across different devices where a service operates differently per device, across embedded storefronts or multiple domain names, across syndicated or sub-syndicated search results, where business users' offers are integrated onto a third-party online intermediation service, and where "buy buttons" are integrated into social media (ranking guidance, para. 26 to 31).
- The small-enterprise exemption in Art. 11(5) applies only to the internal complaint-handling duty in Art. 11; it does not excuse a small-enterprise provider from Art. 3 to 10.

## What the documents must say

### Terms and conditions generally (Art. 3)

- Draft the terms and conditions in plain and intelligible language (Art. 3(1)(a)).
- Make them easily available to business users at every stage of the commercial relationship, including pre-contractually (Art. 3(1)(b)).
- State the grounds for a decision to suspend, terminate or otherwise restrict, in whole or in part, the provision of the service to a business user (Art. 3(1)(c)).
- Disclose any additional distribution channels and potential affiliate programmes through which the provider might market the business user's goods or services (Art. 3(1)(d)).
- Give general information on how the terms affect the ownership and control of the business user's intellectual property rights (Art. 3(1)(e)).
- Keep the identity of the business user supplying the goods or services on the service clearly visible (Art. 3(5)).
- Notify, on a durable medium, any proposed change to the terms and conditions to the business users concerned (Art. 3(2), first subparagraph). The notice period must be reasonable and proportionate to the nature, extent and consequences of the change, and at least 15 days from the date of that notice; grant a longer period where business users need it for technical or commercial adaptation (Art. 3(2), first and second subparagraph).
- Give the business user a right to terminate the contract before the notice period expires, taking effect within 15 days of receiving the notice unless a shorter period already applies to the contract (Art. 3(2), third subparagraph). Let the business user waive that notice period at any time after receiving the notice, by a written statement or a clear affirmative action (Art. 3(2), fourth subparagraph). Treat a business user's submission of new goods or services during the notice period as such a waiver, except where the period is longer than 15 days because the change requires the business user to make significant technical adjustments; in that case the submission does not automatically count as a waiver (Art. 3(2), fifth subparagraph).
- The 15-day-minimum notice period does not apply where the provider is subject to a legal or regulatory obligation requiring a terms change in a manner incompatible with that period (Art. 3(4)(a)), or where the provider must exceptionally change its terms to address an unforeseen and imminent danger related to defending the service, consumers or business users from fraud, malware, spam, data breaches or other cybersecurity risks (Art. 3(4)(b)).

### Restriction, suspension and termination (Art. 4)

- Where restricting or suspending the service to a business user for individual goods or services, give that business user a statement of reasons on a durable medium, no later than when the restriction or suspension takes effect (Art. 4(1)).
- Where terminating the whole of the service to a business user, give a statement of reasons on a durable medium at least 30 days before termination takes effect (Art. 4(2)). That 30-day period does not apply where the provider is subject to a legal or regulatory obligation requiring termination in a manner that does not allow for it (Art. 4(4)(a)), where the provider exercises a right of termination under an imperative reason under national law consistent with Union law (Art. 4(4)(b)), or where the provider can show the business user has repeatedly infringed the applicable terms and conditions, resulting in the termination (Art. 4(4)(c)). Where one of these three applies, still give the business user a statement of reasons without undue delay (Art. 4(4), second subparagraph).
- In every case of restriction, suspension or termination, let the business user clarify the facts and circumstances through the Art. 11 internal complaint-handling process (Art. 4(3), first sentence).
- Where a restriction, suspension or termination is revoked, reinstate the business user without undue delay, including restoring any access to personal or other data that resulted from its use of the service before the measure took effect (Art. 4(3), second sentence).
- Write the statement of reasons to reference the specific facts or circumstances, including the contents of third-party notifications, that led to the decision, together with the applicable ground under Art. 3(1)(c) (Art. 4(5), first subparagraph). No statement of reasons is owed at all where the provider is subject to a legal or regulatory obligation not to disclose the specific facts, circumstances or applicable ground, or where the provider can show the business user has repeatedly infringed the applicable terms and conditions, resulting in termination of the whole service (Art. 4(5), second subparagraph).

### Ranking (Art. 5)

- Set out in the terms and conditions the main parameters determining ranking and the reasons for the relative importance of those main parameters as against other parameters (Art. 5(1), providers of online intermediation services). A provider of an online search engine instead gives an easily and publicly available description, drafted in plain and intelligible language, of the main parameters, individually or collectively most significant in determining ranking, and their relative importance, and keeps that description up to date (Art. 5(2)).
- Identify the main parameters through a case-by-case assessment of what actually determines ranking under the mechanism in question, then select the main subset; a true reflection of what actually determines ranking is required, and it is irrelevant whether or not a parameter can be influenced by the user (ranking guidance, para. 39, 47). A generic statement that ranking is determined by algorithms or artificial intelligence does not stand in for naming the actual main parameters (ranking guidance, para. 47).
- Go beyond a bare enumeration of the main parameters and provide at least a second layer of explanatory detail, for example the provider's own internal process for deciding what the main parameters are (ranking guidance, para. 22, 96, 97). A vague label such as "quality" or "search history," without explaining what makes up the quality score or which history elements are used, is insufficiently specific (ranking guidance, para. 98, 99).
- Where any of the following functions in fact as a main parameter for the provider's own service, describe it with the same level of detail as any other main parameter (ranking guidance, para. 48): personalisation, including its key features, impact on ranking and volatility between consumers (ranking guidance, para. 49, 50); consumer search behaviour and intent, and the effect of a user's own history or past performance (ranking guidance, para. 54, 55); default settings and sorting or filtering mechanisms, including whether a filtered view covers all offers or only offers meeting inclusion criteria, and the effect on the weighting of other parameters (ranking guidance, para. 56, 57); cross-platform presence treated as a quality or relevance signal (ranking guidance, para. 58); external factors such as star ratings, brand-appeal surveys, third-party trust marks, industry awards or third-party media coverage (ranking guidance, para. 59, 60); third-party notifications about offers, including effects from a high notification volume or the provider's policy on the type of illegal content concerned (ranking guidance, para. 62); randomisation, including its frequency, duration and a facts-based indication of its potential amplitude (ranking guidance, para. 63); housekeeping practices that edit or demote old, unsold offers (ranking guidance, para. 64); the relationship with the provider's own ancillary services or technical tools (ranking guidance, para. 65 to 67); the effect of machine learning, focused on the expected amplitude of its impact and the timing and frequency of resulting changes (ranking guidance, para. 68); a search engine's evaluation of websites on trust, safety, authenticity, popularity or technical features such as load speed, mobile-friendliness, domain age or security (ranking guidance, para. 69 to 71); fraud-prevention mechanisms, at least as to their existence and high-level effect on ranking (ranking guidance, para. 72, 73, treated further below); user reviews, including whether they originate on the service or are hosted elsewhere and what, if anything, verifies their veracity (ranking guidance, para. 74); and measures against illegal content, including how its presence, temporary presence or removal affects the visibility of business users' legitimate offers (ranking guidance, para. 75).
- Where an external factor a user might expect to matter is in fact not taken into account, make the description clear enough that users understand this omission (ranking guidance, para. 60).
- Where the main parameters include the possibility to influence ranking in exchange for any direct or indirect remuneration paid by business users or corporate website users to the provider, add a description of that possibility and of the effects of such remuneration on ranking, meeting the same Art. 5(1) or (2) standard (Art. 5(3)). Direct remuneration is a payment made with the main or sole aim of improving ranking; indirect remuneration is the acceptance of an additional obligation of any kind that can have that practical effect, such as using an ancillary service or a premium feature, whether or not it also affects performance measured by another parameter (Recital 25; Art. 5(3); ranking guidance, para. 88, 90 to 92). Best practice combines the written explanation with a technological tool such as a dynamic simulator of the anticipated effects of remuneration on ranking (ranking guidance, para. 89), and cross-references the differentiated-treatment description a provider gives under Art. 7(3)(c) and (d) (ranking guidance, para. 102).
- Make the description sufficient for business users or corporate website users to obtain an adequate understanding of whether, and if so how and to what extent, the ranking mechanism accounts for the characteristics of the goods or services offered to consumers through the service, the relevance of those characteristics to those consumers, and, for a search engine, the design characteristics of the corporate website user's website (Art. 5(5)).
- Where an online search engine has altered the ranking order in a specific case, or delisted a website, following a third-party notification, offer the corporate website user the possibility to inspect the contents of that notification (Art. 5(4)).
- Keep the description up to date. Art. 5(2) states this expressly for search engines; the same duty is implicit in Art. 5(1), which by its terms describes parameters that currently determine ranking, not ones that determined ranking in the past (ranking guidance, para. 120). Assess regularly, considering the frequency and effects of changes and whether experiments such as A/B testing, or techniques such as machine learning, are changing which parameters are main (ranking guidance, para. 121).
- Place the description, for an online intermediation service, inside the terms and conditions (in any format: a dashboard, Q&A section, tutorial, pop-up or video, provided the information stays consistent and locatable), available to prospective as well as current business users (Art. 5(1); Art. 3(1)(b); ranking guidance, para. 111 to 114). Place the description, for an online search engine, in an easily and publicly accessible location on the search engine's own webpage that needs no login, matching how users already navigate the service, for example leading a one-click link directly to the content (Art. 5(2); ranking guidance, para. 115 to 119).

### Ancillary goods and services (Art. 6)

- Where ancillary goods and services, including financial products, are offered to consumers through the service, whether by the provider itself or by a third party, describe in the terms and conditions the type of ancillary goods and services offered, and whether and under what conditions the business user may also offer its own ancillary goods and services through the service (Art. 6).

### Differentiated treatment (Art. 7)

- Describe in the terms and conditions any differentiated treatment the provider gives, or might give, between, on one side, the provider itself or a business user it controls, and, on the other side, other business users, for goods or services offered to consumers through the service, with the main economic, commercial or legal considerations for that treatment (Art. 7(1), online intermediation services). An online search engine owes the same description for corporate website users (Art. 7(2)).
- Cover, where applicable, differentiated treatment in: access to personal data or other data that business users, corporate website users or consumers provide, or that is generated through the service (Art. 7(3)(a)); ranking or other settings that influence consumer access to competing offers (Art. 7(3)(b)); direct or indirect remuneration charged for using the service (Art. 7(3)(c)); and access to, conditions for, or remuneration charged for services, functionalities or technical interfaces that are relevant to the business user or corporate website user and connected or ancillary to using the service (Art. 7(3)(d)).

### Specific contractual terms (Art. 8)

- Do not impose retroactive changes to the terms and conditions, except where required by a legal or regulatory obligation or where the change benefits business users (Art. 8(a)).
- State in the terms and conditions the conditions under which a business user can terminate the contractual relationship (Art. 8(b)).
- Describe in the terms and conditions the technical and contractual access, or its absence, that the provider maintains after the contract expires to the information the business user provided or generated (Art. 8(c)).

### Access to data (Art. 9)

- Describe in the terms and conditions the technical and contractual access, or its absence, that business users have to any personal data or other data that business users or consumers provide for the service, or that is generated through providing it (Art. 9(1)).
- Through that description, adequately inform business users, in particular, whether the provider itself has access to such data and, if so, to which categories and under what conditions (Art. 9(2)(a)); whether a business user has access to data it provided, or that was generated through its own use of the service and its consumers, and if so, which categories and under what conditions (Art. 9(2)(b)); whether, in addition, a business user has access to data, including in aggregated form, provided by or generated through the service across all business users and consumers, and if so, which categories and under what conditions (Art. 9(2)(c)); and whether any data under point (a) is given to third parties, together with, where that sharing is not necessary for the service's proper functioning, the purpose of the sharing and the business user's option to opt out (Art. 9(2)(d)). This data-access description is without prejudice to Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Directive (EU) 2016/680 and Directive 2002/58/EC (Art. 9(3)); it does not substitute for, or narrow, whatever those instruments separately require.

### Parity restrictions (Art. 10)

- Where restricting a business user's ability to offer the same goods or services to consumers under different conditions by other means than through the provider's own service, state the grounds for that restriction in the terms and conditions and make those grounds easily available to the public, including the main economic, commercial or legal considerations (Art. 10(1)). This duty to state grounds does not affect any prohibition or limitation on imposing such a restriction that follows from another act of Union law, or from a member state's law consistent with Union law, to which the provider is subject (Art. 10(2)); a parity restriction permitted under Art. 10 can still be unlawful under one of those other instruments.

### Internal complaint-handling system (Art. 11)

- Provide an internal system for business users to lodge complaints about alleged non-compliance by the provider with an obligation under the Regulation that affects the complainant (Art. 11(1)(a)), a technological issue directly related to providing the service that affects the complainant (Art. 11(1)(b)), or a measure taken by, or behaviour of, the provider directly related to providing the service that affects the complainant (Art. 11(1)(c)). Make the system easily accessible and free of charge for business users, handle complaints within a reasonable time frame, apply the principles of transparency and equal treatment of equivalent situations, and treat complaints proportionately to their importance and complexity (Art. 11(1), second subparagraph).
- Duly consider every complaint and the follow-up it needs to adequately address the issue raised (Art. 11(2)(a)); process complaints swiftly and effectively, given their importance and complexity (Art. 11(2)(b)); and communicate the outcome to the complainant individually, in plain and intelligible language (Art. 11(2)(c)).
- State in the terms and conditions all relevant information on access to and the functioning of the system (Art. 11(3)).
- Establish, and make easily available to the public, information on the system's functioning and effectiveness, covering the total number of complaints lodged, the main types of complaint, the average processing time, and aggregated outcome information; verify that information at least annually and update it where significant changes are needed (Art. 11(4)).

## What the documents must not say

- A terms document must not state only that the service may restrict, suspend or terminate access at its discretion, without the Art. 3(1)(c) grounds; a provision that fails Art. 3(1) is null and void, as is a change implemented in breach of the Art. 3(2) notice-period rules (Art. 3(3)).
- A ranking section must not describe ranking only as determined by the provider's algorithm, or refer generically to artificial intelligence as the explanation; this does not satisfy the obligation to name the actual main parameters (ranking guidance, para. 47).
- A ranking section must not withhold a main parameter solely because the provider has never disclosed any parameter before, or because the information is commercially sensitive in a general sense; Art. 5(6) and Recital 27 confine the carve-out to algorithms or information that, with reasonable certainty, would enable deception of consumers or consumer harm through manipulation of search results, and consideration of a provider's commercial interests alone must never lead to a refusal to disclose the main parameters (Art. 5(6); Recital 27; ranking guidance, para. 77, 78, 82). A provider invoking trade-secret status for ranking information must independently show all three elements of the Trade Secrets Directive's definition: the information is secret (not generally known or readily accessible within the relevant circles), it has commercial value because it is secret, and it has been subject to reasonable steps by the person lawfully in control of it to keep it secret (Art. 2(1), Directive (EU) 2016/943; ranking guidance, para. 81).
- A fraud-prevention disclosure must not describe the operational detail of the mechanism where doing so would undermine its purpose, but it must still disclose the mechanism's existence and give high-level information on how it can affect ranking (ranking guidance, para. 72, 73).
- A terms-change notice must not label a change to the main ranking parameters as "editorial" to avoid the 15-day notice period; a change to a main parameter by definition alters the content or meaning of the terms and conditions and can never qualify for that exemption (Art. 3(2); ranking guidance, para. 128).
- A self-preferencing clause must not be a single general sentence announcing that the provider may treat its own goods or services differently, without naming the main economic, commercial or legal considerations across the four areas Art. 7(3) lists: data access, ranking or settings, remuneration, and access to connected services or technical interfaces.
- A parity clause restricting a business user from offering the same goods elsewhere under different conditions must not stand on its own with no stated grounds; Art. 10(1) requires the grounds, including the main economic, commercial or legal considerations, to be in the terms and made public.
- A complaint-handling clause must not fold the Art. 11 internal system into a generic contact-support line with no information on access to or the functioning of that system (Art. 11(3)).
- A data-access section must not describe access in one undifferentiated sentence; Art. 9(2) requires the four distinct strands (the provider's own access, the business user's access to its own data, the business user's access to aggregated cross-user data, and third-party sharing) to be addressed separately, with the opt-out for sharing not necessary to the service's proper functioning.

## Product behaviour it assumes

- A durable-medium channel exists for every Art. 3(2) terms-change notice, every Art. 4(1), (2) and (4) statement of reasons, and every other durable-medium communication this file requires, so the business user can store the information addressed to it for future reference and reproduce it unchanged (Art. 2(13)).
- A terms-change workflow enforces the minimum 15-day notice period (longer where adaptation needs more time), flags any change to the main ranking parameters as never eligible for an editorial-change fast path, and gives the business user a working path to terminate or to waive the notice period as Art. 3(2) describes.
- A restriction, suspension or termination workflow issues the timed statement of reasons (prior to or at the time for a partial restriction, at least 30 days ahead for a whole-service termination), routes the business user to the internal complaint-handling system, and, on revocation, restores the business user's access to its own data without undue delay (Art. 4).
- A maintained, versioned description of the main ranking parameters exists, with a record of when and why it changed, kept inside the terms and conditions for an online intermediation service or on the public search-engine webpage for a search engine, and reachable without a login in the latter case (Art. 5(1), (2); ranking guidance, para. 131).
- Where paid ranking or an ancillary service can affect a business user's ranking, a documented classification exists for each such mechanism as direct or indirect remuneration, feeding both the Art. 5(3) ranking description and the Art. 7(3)(c), (d) differentiated-treatment description.
- An internal complaint-handling system is operating with a working intake channel, a complaint log (counts, types, average processing time, aggregated outcomes) refreshed at least annually, and a published functioning-and-effectiveness page, except where the provider qualifies as a small enterprise under the Annex to Recommendation 2003/361/EC (Art. 11(4), (5)).
- A data-access description exists and is kept current for the four Art. 9(2) strands, together with a working opt-out mechanism for any third-party data sharing that is not necessary for the service's proper functioning.
- For an online search engine, a notice-inspection mechanism exists that lets a corporate website user view the contents of a third-party notification that led to a ranking change or a delisting of its website (Art. 5(4)).

## Prescribed wording

This file cites no prescribed wording of its own. The Regulation and the ranking guidance fix no button labels or model forms; where this file quotes a defined term or a guidance sentence character for character, the quotation sits in context above with its citation.

## Dates

- Articles 1 to 11 state no separate application or transitional date of their own. This file does not state the Regulation's general application date; the Regulation is published as OJ L 186, 11.7.2019, p. 57 (ranking guidance, footnote 3). Treat the general application date as an open point below.
- 8 December 2020: publication date of the Commission's ranking-transparency guidelines, 2020/C 424/01, OJ C 424. The guidance has not been superseded as of 2026-10-20; the Commission may revise it in light of future developments (ranking guidance, para. 10).
- At least 15 days: minimum notice period for a terms and conditions change (Art. 3(2)); at least 30 days: minimum notice period before a whole-service termination takes effect (Art. 4(2)); at least annually: minimum frequency for verifying the public complaint-handling effectiveness information (Art. 11(4)).
- 2026-10-20: law-as-of date for this file.

## Open points to raise

- Whether the provider operates more than one distinct online intermediation service or search engine, including across devices, domains, embedded storefronts, syndicated search feeds or integrated third-party offers, since each distinct service needs its own ranking-transparency assessment and description rather than one combined explanation (ranking guidance, para. 26 to 31).
- For each ranking parameter the provider treats as non-main, whether that classification still holds; the obligation turns on what actually determines ranking, not on a parameter's position on a generic checklist, so a parameter that in substance drives ranking must be described even if it was not expected to (ranking guidance, para. 47, 48).
- Whether any mechanism by which a business user can pay, or take on an obligation, to improve its own ranking has been classified as direct or indirect remuneration and cross-checked against the Art. 7(3)(c), (d) differentiated-treatment description, since the same mechanism can trigger both duties.
- Whether a planned or live experiment (an A/B test, an evolving fraud-prevention mechanism) is isolated from the rest of the user base, and if not, whether the tested parameter is itself main or would become main on a full roll-out, since either case can trigger an update to the ranking description (ranking guidance, para. 126, 127).
- Whether the provider qualifies as a small enterprise under the Annex to Recommendation 2003/361/EC, since that status exempts it from Art. 11 alone and from no other article in this file.
- This file gives only the Regulation's OJ publication citation, not its general application date; confirm the application date against the Regulation's own final provisions before relying on a compliance timeline.

# Sale of Goods Directive (EU) 2019/771

Citation: Directive (EU) 2019/771 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 20 May 2019 on certain aspects concerning contracts for the sale of goods, amending Regulation (EU) 2017/2394 and Directive 2009/22/EC, and repealing Directive 1999/44/EC, as amended by Directive (EU) 2024/1799
Version: consolidated text of 31 July 2026 (CELEX 02019L0771-20260731), including the Directive (EU) 2024/1799 amendments
Official text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02019L0771-20260731
Law as of: 2026-10-20

This file covers Art. 1 to 21 of the consolidated text: scope, conformity of goods, liability, remedies for
lack of conformity, commercial guarantees and the no-waiver rule for any sale of goods, including goods with
digital elements, between a consumer and a seller. It does not cover Art. 22 to 27 or the Annex (amendments
to other instruments, repeal of the predecessor directive, transposition and review mechanics), which carry
no drafting content of their own. For a contract for digital content or a digital service as such, not goods,
consult Directive (EU) 2019/770 directly; this Directive does not reach that regime and this file does not
restate it. The Directive sets the floor; national transposition law decides the exact wording and
enforcement that apply in each Member State, and a seller's documents must follow the transposition, not this
Directive's text directly. Germany's transposition is covered separately in `references/de/bgb-kauf.md`; for
any other Member State, the national transposition is an open point.

## When it applies

- The Directive applies to sales contracts between a consumer and a seller, including a contract for goods to
  be manufactured or produced, which counts as a sales contract too (Art. 3(1), (2)).
- It applies to goods with digital elements: tangible movable items that incorporate or are inter-connected
  with digital content or a digital service such that the absence of that content or service would stop the
  goods performing their functions (Art. 2(5)(b)). It does not apply to digital content or a digital service as such, but it
  does apply to digital content or a service incorporated in or inter-connected with goods in that sense and
  supplied under the sales contract, whoever supplies it; in doubt, such content or service is presumed
  covered by the sales contract (Art. 3(3)). Treat a connected or "smart" product's bundled software as part
  of the sales contract, not a separate digital-content agreement, unless the two are genuinely severable.
- It does not apply to a tangible medium serving exclusively as a carrier for digital content, or to goods
  sold by way of execution or otherwise by authority of law (Art. 3(4)).
- A Member State may exclude second-hand goods sold at public auction and living animals from the Directive's
  scope. Where a seller relies on the public-auction exclusion, it must make clear, full information that the
  Directive's rights do not apply easily available to consumers (Art. 3(5)).
- The Directive leaves Member States free to regulate general contract law (formation, validity, nullity,
  effects, termination consequences, damages) to the extent it does not itself regulate that ground, and free
  to let consumers choose a specific remedy for a defect apparent within 30 days of delivery, or to keep
  non-consumer-specific national remedies for a defect not apparent at conclusion (Art. 3(6), (7)).
- The Directive sets full harmonisation: a Member State must not keep or introduce a national rule that
  diverges from it, whether stricter or looser, unless the Directive itself allows it (Art. 4). A clause
  relying on an assumed national variation should be checked against the actual transposing law, not against
  this Directive's text alone.
- This file applies only to a sales contract concluded on or after 1 January 2022, the Directive's own scope
  cutoff (Art. 24(2), outside the articles this file covers but stated here since it decides whether the rest
  of this file applies at all). The amendments on repair (the repairability conformity criterion, the 12-month
  liability extension, the pre-remedy disclosure duty, see "Repair inside the seller's liability" below) apply
  only to a sales contract concluded on or after 31 July 2026 (Directive (EU) 2024/1799, Art. 21). A contract
  concluded on or after 1 January 2022 but before 31 July 2026 follows the rest of this file without those
  repair amendments.

## What the documents must say

### Conformity of goods (Art. 5 to 9)

- State that the goods meet both the subjective and the objective conformity requirements; a document naming
  only the subjective (contractual) layer invites a reading that the objective floor was disclaimed (Art. 5).
- Subjective requirements: the goods match the contract's description, type, quantity, quality, functionality,
  compatibility, interoperability and other features it specifies; are fit for the consumer's particular
  purpose, made known to the seller at the latest at conclusion and accepted by the seller; are delivered with
  the accessories and instructions, including on installation, the contract stipulates; and are supplied with
  the updates the contract stipulates (Art. 6(a) to (d)).
- Objective requirements, in addition: the goods are fit for the normal purposes of goods of that type, given
  applicable Union and national law, technical standards or, absent such standards, applicable sector-specific
  codes of conduct; match any sample or model the seller made available before conclusion; come with the
  accessories, packaging and instructions the consumer may reasonably expect; and possess the quantity and
  qualities, including durability, repairability, functionality, compatibility and security, normal for that
  type of goods and reasonably expected given their nature and any public statement by the seller, the
  producer or an earlier party in the chain of transactions, in particular in advertising or on labelling
  (Art. 7(1)).
- A deviation clause validly carves a disclosed shortfall out of the objective standard only where the
  specific characteristic and its deviation were disclosed to the consumer before conclusion and the consumer
  expressly and separately accepted that exact deviation at conclusion (Art. 7(5)). A general "sold as seen"
  or no-warranty line does not meet this bar; the objective requirements still apply in full without it.
- The seller is not bound by a public statement under Art. 7(1)(d) if it shows it was not, and could not
  reasonably have been, aware of the statement; the statement was corrected by conclusion in a comparably
  prominent way; or the purchase decision could not have been influenced by the statement (Art. 7(2)).
- For goods with digital elements, state that the consumer is informed of, and supplied with, updates
  including security updates necessary to keep the goods in conformity, for the period the consumer may
  reasonably expect (a single-supply contract) or for the period Art. 10(2) or (5) sets (a continuous-supply
  contract) (Art. 7(3)).
- A clause excluding seller liability for non-conformity caused by the consumer's own failure to install a
  supplied update in reasonable time is valid only where the seller informed the consumer of the update's
  availability and the consequences of not installing it, and the failure or faulty installation was not
  itself due to deficient installation instructions (Art. 7(4)).
- A lack of conformity from incorrect installation counts as a lack of conformity of the goods themselves
  where the seller carried out or was responsible for the installation under the sales contract, or where the
  consumer's own installation failed because of shortcomings in the seller's installation instructions, or,
  for goods with digital elements, the seller's or the digital-content or service supplier's installation
  instructions (Art. 8).
- Where a third party's right, in particular an intellectual property right, restricts or prevents use of the
  goods consistent with Art. 6 and 7, the consumer keeps the full Art. 13 remedy set unless national law
  instead provides for nullity or rescission of the contract (Art. 9).

### Liability period and burden of proof (Art. 10, 11)

- State the baseline: the seller is liable for a lack of conformity existing at delivery that becomes apparent
  within two years of delivery, including for goods with digital elements (Art. 10(1)).
- For goods with digital elements under a continuous-supply contract, the seller is also liable for a lack of
  conformity of the digital content or service occurring or becoming apparent within two years of delivery, or,
  where supply runs longer than two years, throughout the whole supply period (Art. 10(2)).
- Where repair is chosen as the remedy under Art. 13(2), state that the liability period is extended once by
  12 months (Art. 10(2a); see "Repair inside the seller's liability" below for the date this applies from). A
  Member State may maintain or introduce a longer time limit than the two-year baseline, the continuous-supply
  period or the 12-month extension (Art. 10(3)).
- A Member State may instead keep only a limitation period for the Art. 13 remedies, provided it still lets
  the consumer exercise those remedies for a lack of conformity apparent within the Art. 10(1) or (2) period
  (Art. 10(5)). For second-hand goods, the seller and the consumer may agree a shorter liability or limitation
  period, never under one year (Art. 10(6)).
- A lack of conformity apparent within one year of delivery, or two years where the Member State chose that
  option, is presumed to have existed at delivery unless disproved, or unless the presumption is incompatible
  with the nature of the goods or of the defect; this presumption applies to goods with digital elements too
  (Art. 11(1), (2)). For continuous-supply goods with digital elements, the seller carries the burden of
  proving conformity throughout the Art. 10(2) period for a defect apparent within it (Art. 11(3)).
- Where the Member State requires it, a notification clause may condition the consumer's rights on notifying
  the seller of a lack of conformity, but never faster than at least 2 months from the consumer's discovery of
  it (Art. 12).

### Remedies for lack of conformity (Art. 13 to 16)

- On a lack of conformity, the consumer is entitled to have the goods brought into conformity (repair or
  replacement), a proportionate price reduction, or termination, under the conditions this Article sets
  (Art. 13(1)).
- The consumer chooses between repair and replacement. A clause may not funnel every defect to one
  seller-chosen remedy: the seller may refuse only where the chosen remedy is impossible or, measured against
  the other remedy's cost, disproportionate to the seller, taking into account the goods' conformity-value,
  the defect's significance, and whether the alternative avoids significant inconvenience to the consumer
  (Art. 13(2), (3)).
- Before providing any remedy, the seller must inform the consumer of the right to choose between repair and
  replacement and of the possible liability extension under Art. 10(2a) (Art. 13(2a); this disclosure duty
  applies only from the date "Repair inside the seller's liability" states below).
- The consumer moves to price reduction (Art. 15) or termination (Art. 16) where the seller has not completed
  repair or replacement, or not completed it properly, or has refused it; where a defect appears again despite
  an attempted fix; where the defect is serious enough to justify immediate price reduction or termination; or
  where the seller has declared, or it is otherwise clear, that it will not remedy within a reasonable time or
  without significant inconvenience to the consumer (Art. 13(4)).
- Termination is unavailable for a merely minor lack of conformity, and the seller carries the burden of
  proving minority (Art. 13(5)). Until the seller performs, the consumer may withhold payment of any
  outstanding part of the price (Art. 13(6)).
- Repairs and replacements must be free of charge, carried out within a reasonable time from the seller being
  informed of the lack of conformity, and without significant inconvenience to the consumer given the goods'
  nature and purpose (Art. 14(1)(a) to (c)). The consumer makes the goods available to the seller; the seller
  takes back replaced goods at its own expense (Art. 14(2)). Where repair or replacement requires removing
  goods installed consistent with their nature before the defect appeared, that removal and the installation
  of the replacement or repaired goods, or their costs, fall on the repair or replacement obligation
  (Art. 14(3)). The consumer owes nothing for normal use of replaced goods before their replacement (Art.
  14(4)).
- A price reduction must be proportionate to the decrease in value compared with goods that were in
  conformity (Art. 15).
- Termination is exercised by a statement to the seller; where the defect affects only some delivered goods,
  termination may be limited to those goods, plus any goods acquired together with them that the consumer
  cannot reasonably be expected to keep alone (Art. 16(1), (2)). On termination, the consumer returns the
  goods at the seller's expense, and the seller reimburses the price on receipt of the goods or of evidence
  that the consumer sent them back (Art. 16(3)).

### Repair inside the seller's liability (Art. 7(1)(d), 10(2a), (5a), 13(2a), 14(1))

These five provisions carry the repair-promotion amendments Directive (EU) 2024/1799 made to this Directive.
They apply only to a sales contract concluded on or after 31 July 2026 (Directive (EU) 2024/1799, Art. 21); for an
earlier contract within this Directive's own 1 January 2022 scope cutoff, apply Art. 7(1)(d), 10, 13 and 14 as
this file otherwise states them, without the repair-specific additions below.

- Repairability sits inside the objective conformity test alongside durability, functionality, compatibility
  and security (Art. 7(1)(d), already stated above); a good unusually hard to repair for its type can fail
  conformity on that ground alone, independent of any functional defect.
- Choosing repair as the remedy extends the liability period once by 12 months (Art. 10(2a), already stated
  above). A Member State without a fixed time limit for seller liability, or with only a limitation period for
  remedies, may derogate from this extension only if it instead ensures that the liability, or the limitation
  period for remedies, is at least equivalent to three years where repair took place (Art. 10(5a)).
- Before providing the remedy, the seller must disclose the consumer's right to choose between repair and
  replacement and the possible 12-month extension (Art. 13(2a), already stated above); this is a disclosure
  step the document must build in before the remedy is performed, not a line buried in general terms.
- Repair or replacement stays free of charge, within a reasonable time, and without significant inconvenience
  to the consumer (Art. 14(1)(a) to (c)). During repair, depending on the category of goods, in particular the
  consumer's need to have such goods permanently available, the seller may offer a free loan replacement good,
  including a refurbished one. At the consumer's explicit request, the seller may provide a refurbished good
  to fulfil the replacement obligation (Art. 14(1)).

A separate, standalone manufacturer's duty to repair goods subject to repairability requirements (Directive
(EU) 2024/1799, Art. 5) operates outside this Directive's own seller liability and is not covered by this
file; see Open points to raise.

### Commercial guarantees (Art. 17)

- A commercial guarantee binds the guarantor under the conditions the guarantee statement and any associated
  advertising available at or before conclusion set. Where a producer offers a commercial guarantee of
  durability for certain goods over a certain period, the producer is directly liable to the consumer
  throughout that period for repair or replacement under Art. 14, and may offer more favourable conditions
  than the statement requires (Art. 17(1), first subparagraph).
- Where the guarantee statement is less advantageous to the consumer than the associated advertising, the
  advertising's conditions bind instead, unless the advertising was corrected, before conclusion, in a
  comparably prominent way (Art. 17(1), second subparagraph).
- The guarantee statement must be given to the consumer on a durable medium at the latest at delivery, in
  plain, intelligible language, and must include: a clear statement that the consumer is entitled by law to
  free remedies from the seller for a lack of conformity, unaffected by the guarantee; the guarantor's name
  and address; the procedure to invoke the guarantee; the goods the guarantee covers; and the guarantee's
  terms (Art. 17(2)(a) to (e)).
- Missing one or more of these five elements does not excuse the guarantor: the guarantee still binds exactly
  as the statement, or any more favourable uncorrected advertising, states it (Art. 17(3)). There is no safe
  harbor for under-disclosure here, only an incentive to disclose fully.
- A Member State may lay down its own rules on other aspects of a commercial guarantee not regulated by
  Art. 17, including which language or languages the statement must be made available in (Art. 17(4)). A
  seller drafting one statement for several EU markets should check each target Member State's transposing law
  for a language requirement on top of the Art. 17(2) content.

### Redress, enforcement and consumer information (Art. 18 to 20)

- Where the seller is liable to the consumer for a lack of conformity caused by an act or omission of an
  earlier party in the chain of transactions, including a failure to supply Art. 7(3) updates, the seller is
  entitled to pursue remedies against that party, under conditions national law sets (Art. 18).
- A Member State must ensure adequate and effective means of compliance, including standing for public
  bodies, consumer organisations with a legitimate interest and professional organisations with a legitimate
  interest to bring action before a court or a competent administrative body (Art. 19).
- A Member State must take appropriate measures so consumers have access to information on their rights under
  the Directive and on how to enforce them (Art. 20).

## What the documents must not say

- No clause may define conformity more narrowly than Art. 6 and 7 together require; a clause limiting
  conformity to the subjective (contractual) requirements alone, silent on the objective baseline, misstates
  the law (Art. 6, 7(1)).
- No general "sold as seen" or no-warranty clause may substitute for the Art. 7(5) specific-disclosure-plus-
  separate-acceptance requirement; without both elements, the goods remain measured against the full objective
  standard as if the clause did not exist (Art. 7(5)).
- No clause may shorten the two-year Art. 10(1) liability period, except for second-hand goods by agreement
  and never below one year (Art. 10(6)); a clause may lengthen it (Art. 10(3)).
- No clause may require notification faster than the 2-month floor a Member State has set under Art. 12; a
  shorter contractual notice window is not enforceable against the consumer to that extent.
- No clause may force the consumer into one specific remedy, repair only or replacement only, without the
  Art. 13(2) disproportionate-cost or impossibility test; that clause misstates the consumer's right to choose
  (Art. 13(2)).
- No clause may charge the consumer for return shipping on termination, or for the removal or reinstallation
  costs tied to repair or replacement; Art. 14(2), (3) and Art. 16(3)(a) place those costs on the seller.
- No clause may present the commercial guarantee as the consumer's only recourse; Art. 17(2)(a) requires the
  statement itself to say the statutory remedies are unaffected.
- No clause may exclude, derogate from or vary, to the consumer's detriment, the effect of the national
  measures transposing this Directive, before the consumer has brought the lack of conformity to the seller's
  attention; such a clause simply does not bind the consumer (Art. 21(1)). The Directive does not stop a
  seller offering contractual arrangements that exceed its protections (Art. 21(2)).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- The checkout or product page states both conformity layers (the specific contractual promise and the
  objective floor) rather than only the features list, so neither reads as a disclaimer of the other.
- A deviation from standard conformity (a display unit, an open-box item, a deliberately downgraded spec) is
  disclosed as its own, specific item before conclusion, with a dedicated acceptance step (a named checkbox or
  equivalent) separate from general terms acceptance, not bundled into a blanket agreement.
- For a connected or "smart" product, the seller tracks and can show, for the applicable period, that update
  availability (including security updates) was communicated to the consumer, since the Art. 7(4) liability
  shift for an uninstalled update depends on that notice having been given.
- The returns and repair flow lets the consumer choose between repair and replacement, surfaces that choice
  and the possible 12-month liability extension before the remedy starts, and does not charge the consumer for
  postage, removal, reinstallation or return shipping at any step.
- Records capture the delivery date, when a lack of conformity was reported, and which remedy was chosen,
  since those three dates drive the Art. 10, 11 and 13 periods and presumptions.
- Any commercial guarantee offered (extended warranty, durability guarantee, "lifetime guarantee") is built as
  a durable-medium statement delivered at the latest at delivery, carrying all five Art. 17(2) elements, kept
  at least as favourable as any advertising for it.

## Prescribed wording

None. This Directive fixes required content elements, most notably the five Art. 17(2) guarantee-statement
elements and the Art. 7(5) deviation disclosure, not a specific form of words a document must reproduce.

## Dates

- 1 January 2022: this Directive's own scope cutoff; it does not apply to a contract concluded before that
  date (Art. 24(2), outside the Articles this file otherwise covers).
- 31 July 2026: the date from which this Directive's own repair amendments (this file's Art. 7(1)(d), 10(2a),
  (5a), 13(2a), 14(1), as amended by Directive (EU) 2024/1799) apply; they reach a sales contract concluded on
  or after this date (Directive (EU) 2024/1799, Art. 21).
- 31 July 2026: Member States' deadline to transpose and apply Directive (EU) 2024/1799 (Directive (EU)
  2024/1799, Art. 22(1)).
- 2026-10-20: law-as-of date for this file.

## Open points to raise

- Which Member State's national transposition applies to a given deployment, since every duty above operates
  through that state's own implementing law, which decides the exact wording and the sanction for missing a
  duty; for any state other than Germany, the transposing statute and any national variant (a longer liability
  period under Art. 10(3), a two-year burden-of-proof window under Art. 11(2), a notification floor under
  Art. 12) is an open point until named.
- Whether the product is, or includes, a connected or "smart" good whose bundled software is genuinely
  severable from the sales contract, since that decides whether this file's conformity rules apply to the
  whole product or only to the physical good.
- Whether a separate, standalone manufacturer's duty to repair goods subject to repairability requirements
  (Directive (EU) 2024/1799, Art. 5, outside this Directive's own seller liability under Art. 10) applies to
  the goods sold, since that is a different regime this file does not cover and is not mapped to another file
  in this skill as of this pass.
- Whether the seller's own Member State has derogated from the Art. 10(2a) 12-month repair extension under the
  Art. 10(5a) three-year-equivalent route, since that changes what liability period a repair-remedy clause can
  state as the floor.

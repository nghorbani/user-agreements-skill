# Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 on harmonised rules on fair access to and use of data (Data Act)

Citation: Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 December 2023 on harmonised rules on fair access to and use of data and amending Regulation (EU) 2017/2394 and Directive (EU) 2020/1828
Version: original text, no amendment found
Official text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32023R2854
Law as of: 2026-10-20

This file covers the parts of the Data Act that change what a connected-product, related-service, data-sharing or cloud-service terms document must say: user access to product and related-service data, sharing that data with third parties, a three-tier test for unfair terms one enterprise imposes on another, and the switching duties of a data-processing-service provider. It does not cover the Regulation's statutory data-sharing chapter (Art. 8 to 12), the public-sector exceptional-need regime or the rest of Chapter IV (Art. 14 to 22), the interoperability and smart-contract chapter (Art. 32 to 36), or enforcement and final provisions (Art. 37 to 50); a cross-reference to any of those articles is stated as a pointer only, without restating what that article requires.

## When it applies

- Applies to manufacturers of connected products placed on the Union market and providers of related services, wherever they are established; to Union users of those products and services; to data holders, wherever established, that make data available to data recipients in the Union; to Union data recipients; and to providers of data processing services, wherever established, serving customers in the Union (Art. 1(3)(a) to (d), (f)).
- A "connected product" is an item that obtains, generates or collects data about its use or environment and can communicate that data through an electronic communications service, a physical connection or on-device access, provided its main function is not storing, processing or transmitting data on behalf of someone other than the user (Art. 2(5)). A "related service" is a digital service, other than an electronic communications service, connected to the product at purchase, rental or lease in a way that its absence would stop the product working, or connected later by the manufacturer or a third party to add to, update or adapt the product's functions (Art. 2(6)). Both terms also reach a virtual assistant insofar as it interacts with a connected product or related service (Art. 1(4)).
- The "user" is the natural or legal person who owns the connected product, or holds a contractually transferred temporary right to use it, or receives the related service (Art. 2(12)). The "data holder" is the person with the right or obligation to use and make available the data, including product data or related-service data it retrieved or generated while providing a related service (Art. 2(13)). The "data recipient" is a person acting for trade, business, craft or profession purposes, other than the user, to whom the data holder makes data available (Art. 2(14)).
- This Regulation does not affect Union or national law on personal data protection, privacy, confidentiality of communications or integrity of terminal equipment, in particular Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Regulation (EU) 2018/1725 and Directive 2002/58/EC; insofar as a user is a data subject, the Chapter II rights in this file complement the GDPR access and portability rights, and a conflict between this Regulation and personal-data or privacy law is resolved in favour of that other law (Art. 1(5)).
- This Regulation complements and does not affect Union consumer-protection law, in particular Directive 93/13/EEC, Directive 2005/29/EC and Directive 2011/83/EU (Art. 1(9)), and does not affect Union or national intellectual-property law, in particular Directive 2001/29/EC, Directive 2004/48/EC and Directive (EU) 2019/790 (Art. 1(8)). It does not apply to or pre-empt voluntary data-sharing arrangements, and does not block a voluntary data-sharing contract that otherwise complies with it (Art. 1(6), (10)).
- A microenterprise or small enterprise is outside the Chapter II obligations for data generated by its own connected products or related services, unless it has a disqualifying partner or linked enterprise that is not itself a micro or small enterprise, or it is subcontracted to manufacture, design or provide the product or service for someone else. The same exemption reaches an enterprise that has qualified as medium-sized for less than a year, and a connected product for one year after a medium-sized enterprise places it on the market (Art. 7(1)).

## What the documents must say

### Pre-contract disclosures

- Before a contract for the purchase, rent or lease of a connected product, the seller, rentor or lessor (which may be the manufacturer) must disclose, clearly and comprehensibly: the type, format and estimated volume of product data the product can generate; whether it generates data continuously and in real time; whether and for how long it stores data on-device or remotely; and how the user can access, retrieve or erase the data, including the technical means, terms of use and quality of service (Art. 3(2)(a) to (d)).
- Before a contract for a related service, the provider must separately disclose, clearly and comprehensibly: the nature, estimated volume and collection frequency of the product data it expects to obtain, with the arrangements for the user to access or retrieve it and the provider's storage and retention plans; the nature and estimated volume of related-service data to be generated, with the same access, storage and retention detail; whether the provider expects to use the readily available data itself, for what purposes, and whether it will let third parties use it on terms agreed with the user; the provider's identity, including trading name and geographical address, and that of any other data processing party; a means of contacting the provider quickly and efficiently; how the user can request sharing with a third party and end that sharing; the user's right to complain to the competent authority designated under Art. 37 about an infringement of Chapter II; whether the provider holds trade secrets in the data, and if not, who does; and the contract's duration and termination arrangements (Art. 3(3)(a) to (i)). These are nine separate items, not one disclosure.

### Access and sharing commitments

- The product and the related service must be designed so that product data and related-service data, with the metadata needed to interpret and use them, are by default easily, securely, free of charge, in a full, structured, commonly used, machine-readable format, and, where relevant and technically feasible, directly accessible to the user (Art. 3(1)).
- Where data cannot be directly accessed by the user, the data holder must make the readily available data and its interpreting metadata accessible to the user without undue delay, at the same quality available to the data holder, easily, securely, free of charge, in a full, structured, commonly used, machine-readable format, and, where relevant and technically feasible, continuously and in real time, on a simple electronic request where technically feasible (Art. 4(1)).
- On the user's request, or a request from a party acting on the user's behalf, the data holder must make the same readily available data and metadata available to a named third party, to the same standard, without undue delay and free of charge to the user (Art. 5(1)). This duty does not reach readily available data generated in testing a connected product, substance or process not yet placed on the market, unless the third party's use of it is contractually permitted (Art. 5(2)).
- A document must state that exercising these access and sharing choices will not be verified against more information than necessary, and that the data holder will not keep information, including log data, on a user's or third party's access beyond what the request's execution and the data infrastructure's security and maintenance require (Art. 4(5), Art. 5(4)).
- A document may reserve a contractual right for the user and the data holder to restrict or prohibit accessing, using or further sharing data where that could undermine the connected product's security requirements laid down by Union or national law and cause a serious adverse effect on the health, safety or security of natural persons, with notice to the Art. 37 competent authority where the data holder refuses to share data on this ground (Art. 4(2)). The user may challenge that restriction, without prejudice to seeking redress before a court or tribunal of a Member State, by lodging a complaint with the Art. 37 competent authority or by agreeing with the data holder to refer the matter to a dispute settlement body under Art. 10(1) (Art. 4(3)).

### Trade secrets

- Where the data may contain the data holder's or a third party's trade secrets, the data holder (or, if different, the trade secret holder) must identify the trade-secret-protected parts, including in the metadata, and agree proportionate technical and organisational measures with the recipient before disclosure, such as model contractual terms, confidentiality agreements, strict access protocols, technical standards or codes of conduct (Art. 4(6), Art. 5(9)).
- A document must reserve a right to withhold or suspend sharing of the data identified as trade secrets where no agreement on those measures is reached, or where the recipient fails to implement the agreed measures or undermines their confidentiality, provided the decision is substantiated in writing to the recipient without undue delay and the competent authority designated under Art. 37 is notified of the withholding or suspension and of which measures failed (Art. 4(7), Art. 5(10)).
- In exceptional circumstances, where the data holder is itself the trade secret holder and can show it is highly likely to suffer serious economic damage from disclosure despite the agreed measures, it may refuse a specific request case by case, substantiated in writing on objective grounds such as the enforceability of trade secret protection in third countries, the confidentiality level of the data and the uniqueness of the connected product, with the same competent-authority notification (Art. 4(8), Art. 5(11)).
- A user or third party facing a withholding, suspension or refusal of trade-secret data sharing under the two bullets above may, without prejudice to seeking redress before a court or tribunal of a Member State, challenge the data holder's decision by lodging a complaint with the Art. 37 competent authority, which decides without undue delay whether and on what conditions sharing is to start or resume, or by agreeing with the data holder to refer the matter to a dispute settlement body under Art. 10(1) (Art. 4(9), Art. 5(12)).

### Recipient and third-party obligations

- A third party receiving data under Art. 5 may use it only for the purposes and conditions agreed with the user, subject to Union and national personal-data law, and must erase the data once it is no longer necessary for that purpose, unless the user has agreed otherwise for non-personal data (Art. 6(1)).
- A document binding a third-party recipient must commit it not to: make exercising the user's Art. 5 and Art. 6 choices unduly difficult, including through non-neutral option framing or coercive, deceptive or manipulative interface design; use the data for profiling beyond what the requested service needs; pass the data to another third party except on a further contract with the user and with the same trade-secret confidentiality measures preserved; pass the data to an undertaking designated a gatekeeper under Art. 3 of Regulation (EU) 2022/1925; use the data to develop a competing connected product, or derive insights about the data holder's economic situation, assets or production methods; use the data in a way that adversely affects the connected product's or related service's security; or disregard the agreed trade-secret measures (Art. 6(2)(a) to (g)).
- A third party may not prevent a consumer user, including by a contract term, from making the data it receives available to other parties (Art. 6(2)(h)).

### The gatekeeper exclusion

- An undertaking designated a gatekeeper under Art. 3 of Regulation (EU) 2022/1925 is not an eligible third party under Art. 5, and a document must not permit it to: solicit or commercially incentivise a user, by monetary or other compensation, to route Art. 4(1) data to one of its services; solicit or incentivise a user to request that the data holder make Art. 5(1) data available to one of its services; or receive data a user obtained under Art. 4(1) (Art. 5(3)(a) to (c)).

### The B2B unfair-terms test

- A contractual term on data access, data use, or liability and remedies for breach or termination of a data-related obligation, unilaterally imposed by one enterprise on another, does not bind the second enterprise if the term is unfair (Art. 13(1)). A term that reflects mandatory Union law, or Union law that would apply if the contract were silent on the point, is never unfair on that ground (Art. 13(2)). A term is unfair generally where it grossly deviates from good commercial practice in data access and use, against good faith and fair dealing (Art. 13(3)).
- A term is unfair, without more, if its object or effect is to: exclude or limit the imposing party's liability for intentional acts or gross negligence; exclude the other party's remedies for the imposing party's non-performance, or exclude the imposing party's own liability for breach; or give the imposing party the sole right to decide whether the data supplied conforms to the contract, or to interpret any contractual term (Art. 13(4)(a) to (c)).
- A term is presumed unfair, rebuttably, if its object or effect is to: inappropriately limit the other party's remedies or liability for breach, or extend the other party's own liability; let the imposing party access and use the other party's data in a way significantly detrimental to that party's legitimate interests, in particular commercially sensitive data or data protected by trade secrets or intellectual property; stop the other party using data it provided or generated during the contract, or limit that use so it cannot adequately capture, access, control or exploit the data's value; stop the other party terminating within a reasonable period; stop the other party obtaining a copy of its own contributed or generated data during the contract or within a reasonable period after termination; let the imposing party terminate at unreasonably short notice, weighed against the other party's realistic chance of switching to a comparable service and the financial harm of the termination, absent serious grounds; or let the imposing party unilaterally change the contract price or another substantive condition on the data's nature, format, quality or quantity, with no valid reason stated and no termination right given for the change (Art. 13(5), first subparagraph, (a) to (g)).
- A clause reserving a unilateral-change right for a contract of indeterminate duration escapes the last presumption only where the contract states a valid reason for the change, requires the imposing party to give the other party reasonable advance notice, and lets the other party terminate at no cost when the change is made (Art. 13(5), second subparagraph).
- A term counts as unilaterally imposed where one party supplied it and the other could not influence its content despite trying to negotiate; the party that supplied the term bears the burden of proving it was not unilaterally imposed, and cannot itself argue that its own term is unfair (Art. 13(6)). Where an unfair term can be severed from the rest, the remaining terms still bind the parties (Art. 13(7)). This test does not reach a term defining the contract's main subject matter or the adequacy of the price against the data supplied in exchange (Art. 13(8)), and the parties cannot exclude, derogate from or vary Art. 13 itself (Art. 13(9)).

### Cloud and data-processing-service switching

- The duties below (Art. 23, 25, 29, 30 and 34) bind the source provider, the one the customer is switching away from, and not a destination provider receiving the customer; a provider drafting terms as the destination side of a switch is not the one these duties fall on (Art. 24).
- Any written contract for switching between data processing services, or to an on-premises ICT infrastructure, must be made available to the customer before signing, in a way the customer can store and reproduce, and must include at least the following nine items (Art. 25(1), (2)):
  - a clause letting the customer, on request, switch to a different provider's same-service-type offering or port all exportable data and digital assets to an on-premises infrastructure, without undue delay and within a mandatory maximum transitional period of 30 calendar days from the end of the maximum notice period, during which the provider must give reasonable assistance to the customer and any third party the customer authorises, act with due care to maintain business continuity, disclose known continuity risks, and keep a high level of security throughout, including during the retrieval period (Art. 25(2)(a), (i) to (iv));
  - an obligation to support the customer's exit strategy for the contracted services, including by providing all relevant information (Art. 25(2)(b));
  - a clause stating the contract is terminated, with notice to the customer, either on successful completion of the switching process, or at the end of the maximum notice period where the customer chooses to erase its data and assets instead of switching (Art. 25(2)(c)(i), (ii));
  - a maximum notice period for starting the switching process, capped at two months (Art. 25(2)(d));
  - an exhaustive list of every category of data and digital asset that can be ported, including at minimum all exportable data (Art. 25(2)(e));
  - an exhaustive list of the categories of data specific to the provider's internal functioning that are exempt from that portable-data list where sharing them risks the provider's trade secrets, provided the exemption does not impede or delay switching (Art. 25(2)(f));
  - a minimum data-retrieval period of at least 30 calendar days starting after the transitional period ends (Art. 25(2)(g));
  - a clause guaranteeing full erasure of all exportable data and digital assets generated by or relating directly to the customer, after the retrieval period (or a later agreed date) expires, provided switching completed successfully (Art. 25(2)(h));
  - the switching charges that may be imposed, limited to what Art. 29 permits (Art. 25(2)(i)).
- The contract must let the customer notify the provider, at the end of the maximum notice period, of a decision to switch to a different provider (naming it), to switch to an on-premises infrastructure, or to erase its exportable data and digital assets (Art. 25(3)(a) to (c)), and must give the customer a one-time right to extend the transitional period to a length the customer considers more appropriate (Art. 25(5)).
- Where the 30-day transitional period is technically unfeasible, the provider must notify the customer within 14 working days of the switching request, with a duly justified reason and an alternative transitional period of no more than seven months, during which service continuity must still be maintained (Art. 25(4)).
- The provider must give the customer information on the available switching and porting procedures, methods and formats, and known restrictions or technical limits, and a reference to an up-to-date online register listing all the data structures, data formats, relevant standards and open interoperability specifications in which the Art. 25(2)(e) exportable data are available (Art. 26(a), (b)).
- All parties to a switch, including the destination provider, must cooperate in good faith to make the process effective, transfer data on time, and keep the service continuous (Art. 27).
- The provider must publish on its website, kept up to date, the jurisdiction governing the ICT infrastructure used for each service, and a general description of the technical, organisational and contractual measures it has taken to prevent international governmental access to or transfer of non-personal data held in the Union where that access or transfer would conflict with Union or the relevant Member State's national law; every contract for a data processing service must list that website (Art. 28(1)(a), (b), (2)).
- Before contracting, the provider must give the prospective customer clear information on standard service fees, early termination penalties, and any reduced switching charges that might apply in the period before the full charge withdrawal, and must flag separately any service for which switching is highly complex, costly, or impossible without significant interference in the data, digital assets or service architecture (Art. 29(4), (5)). That information must be made publicly available, where applicable, through a dedicated website section or another easily accessible means (Art. 29(6)).
- Before contracting for a data processing service that is genuinely custom-built for, or developed solely for, one customer and not offered at broad commercial scale through the provider's service catalogue, or for a service offered only as a non-production test or evaluation version for a limited time, the provider must tell the prospective customer exactly which Chapter VI obligations do not apply to that service (Art. 31(1) to (3)).

## What the documents must not say

- A clause cannot make it unduly difficult for a user to exercise an Art. 4 access or sharing choice, whether by offering the choice in a non-neutral manner or by subverting or impairing the user's autonomy, decision-making or choices through the design or operation of a user digital interface (Art. 4(4)).
- A document cannot let the user (or a third party acting with the user) use data obtained under Art. 4(1) to develop a competing connected product, share it with that intent, or derive insights about the manufacturer's or data holder's economic situation, assets or production methods; nor can it let the user or third party use coercive means or abuse gaps in a data holder's protective technical infrastructure to gain access (Art. 4(10), (11), Art. 5(5)).
- A data holder cannot use readily available non-personal data except on a contractual basis with the user, and cannot use it to derive insights about the user's or a third party's economic situation, assets, production methods or use, in a way that could undermine that user's or third party's commercial position, unless the third party has permitted the use and can easily withdraw that permission at any time (Art. 4(13), Art. 5(6)).
- A data holder cannot make non-personal product data available to third parties for any purpose beyond fulfilling its contract with the user, and where it does share with a third party, the contract must bind that party not to share the data onward (Art. 4(14)).
- A contractual term that excludes, derogates from or varies, to the user's detriment, a right the user holds under Chapter II (the access, sharing and trade-secret-handling provisions above) does not bind the user; there is no clause wording that achieves that carve-out even with the user's express agreement (Art. 7(2)).
- A document cannot grant any eligible-third-party status under Art. 5 to an undertaking designated a gatekeeper under Art. 3 of Regulation (EU) 2022/1925 (Art. 5(3); see the gatekeeper exclusion above).
- A B2B data contract cannot carry a term listed as per-se or presumed unfair under Art. 13(4) or (5) (see the B2B unfair-terms test above), and the parties cannot contract out of Art. 13 itself (Art. 13(9)).
- Where the user is not the data subject whose personal data is requested, a document cannot commit to sharing that personal data with the user or a third party unless a valid legal basis exists under Art. 6 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and, where relevant, Art. 9 of that Regulation and Art. 5(3) of Directive 2002/58/EC are satisfied; any failure of the data holder and third party to agree transmission arrangements cannot be allowed to hinder a data subject's GDPR rights, in particular the Art. 20 portability right (Art. 4(12), Art. 5(7), (8), (13)).
- The switching duties above (Art. 25 to 30) do not require a provider to develop new technology or services, to disclose or transfer digital assets protected by intellectual property rights or constituting a trade secret, to the customer or to a different provider, or to compromise the customer's or the provider's own security and integrity of service; a document can rely on this limit but cannot read it more broadly than stated (Art. 30(6)).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- The connected product and related service are built, by default, to make product data and related-service data (with interpreting metadata) directly accessible to the user, free of charge, in a structured, machine-readable format, continuously and in real time where feasible (Art. 3(1)).
- Where direct access is not built in, the product or service supports a simple electronic access request from the user or an authorised third party, answered without undue delay at the data holder's own data quality (Art. 4(1), Art. 5(1)).
- The access-request flow does not collect more identity information than necessary to confirm user or third-party status, and does not log more about an access request than its execution and the infrastructure's security and maintenance require (Art. 4(5), Art. 5(4)).
- The interface offering access or sharing choices presents them neutrally, without design that nudges the user away from sharing or otherwise impairs the user's decision-making (Art. 4(4)).
- A mechanism exists to flag which parts of a shared data set are trade-secret protected, including in metadata, and to record the confidentiality measures agreed with each recipient before disclosure (Art. 4(6), Art. 5(9)).
- A data-processing-service product supports: initiating a switch or porting request; exporting all exportable data and digital assets in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format; completing the transitional period within 30 calendar days of the notice period ending (or the justified alternative of up to seven months); retaining exported data for at least 30 days after the transitional period for retrieval; and fully erasing the customer's exportable data and digital assets once that retrieval period (or an agreed later date) expires and switching has completed (Art. 25(2)).
- An up-to-date online register of the provider's data structures, formats, and relevant standards or open interoperability specifications is published and kept current (Art. 26(b)).
- A provider whose service is limited to infrastructural computing resources (not the software or applications running on them) gives reasonable switching assistance toward functional equivalence in the destination environment; any other provider makes open interfaces available, free of charge and equally, to all customers and the concerned destination providers (Art. 30(1), (2)).
- The provider's website publishes, and keeps current, the jurisdiction governing each service's ICT infrastructure and a description of the measures taken against unlawful international governmental access to or transfer of non-personal Union data (Art. 28(1)).

## Prescribed wording

None of the cut sections of this Regulation prescribe exact wording a document must reproduce verbatim; every requirement above is a content or behaviour duty stated in the drafter's own words, not a model clause or label.

## Dates

- From 11 January 2024 to 12 January 2027, a provider of data processing services may impose a reduced switching charge on a customer, capped at the costs directly linked to that switching process (Art. 29(2), (3)).
- From 12 January 2027, a provider may not impose any switching charge on a customer for switching (Art. 29(1)). A terms document drafted before that date needs a planned revision for the full withdrawal; a compliant capped figure on its own will not stay compliant past the sunset.
- Outside the infrastructure-only case, a provider must ensure compatibility with common specifications or harmonised standards for interoperability within 12 months after those specifications or standards are published in the central Union standards repository for data-processing-service interoperability, following publication of the underlying implementing acts in the Official Journal (Art. 30(3)). That repository publication had not yet happened as of this source's text; see Open points.
- Until that repository publication happens, a provider other than the infrastructure-only kind must still, on the customer's request, export all exportable data in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format when the customer switches to a different provider of the same service type (Art. 30(5)). This duty applies for as long as the Art. 30(3) gap above is open.

## Open points to raise

- Art. 30(3)'s 12-month compatibility clock runs from a central-repository publication date that this source does not state as having occurred; a drafter cannot yet fix a compliance date for that duty and should check for the implementing act before committing to one.
- This file does not cover Art. 8 to 12 (the statutory data-sharing obligations) or the public-sector exceptional-need regime of the rest of Chapter IV; a document that also triggers a statutory data-sharing duty or a public-sector data request needs a source covering those articles before its content can be confirmed complete.
- Chapter V (interoperability standards and smart contracts, Art. 32 to 36) and Chapter VII (enforcement, penalties, final provisions, Art. 37 to 50) are outside this file; a question about a specific competent authority's powers, penalty levels, or the smart-contract essential-requirements regime needs a source covering those articles.
- Whether a given cloud or data-processing service qualifies for the Art. 31 custom-built or evaluation-service carve-out is a factual question about how the service is built and sold that this file's rules cannot settle in the abstract; each case needs checking against the custom-built-for-one-customer and not-offered-at-broad-commercial-scale tests on its own facts (Art. 31(1)).

# Consumer Rights Directive (2011/83/EU)

Citation: Directive 2011/83/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council of 25 October 2011 on consumer rights, amending Council Directive 93/13/EEC and Directive 1999/44/EC and repealing Council Directive 85/577/EEC and Directive 97/7/EC, as amended by Directive (EU) 2019/770, Directive (EU) 2019/771, Directive (EU) 2023/2673, Directive (EU) 2023/2225 and Directive (EU) 2024/825
Version: consolidated text as of 27 September 2026 (CELEX 02011L0083-20260927)
Official text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02011L0083-20260927
Law as of: 2026-10-20

This file is the directive-level reference for every EU member state. The Directive sets the floor; national transposition law decides the exact wording, deadlines and enforcement that apply in each member state, and a trader's documents must follow the transposition, not the Directive text directly. Germany's transposition is covered in `references/de/egbgb.md`, `references/de/bgb-fernabsatz.md` and `references/de/bgb-widerruf.md`. For any other member state, the national transposition is an open point: name the country and flag it rather than drafting against this Directive's text as if it applied directly to consumers.

## When it applies

- The Directive applies to any contract where a consumer pays or undertakes to pay a price, including water, gas, electricity or district heating supply (Art. 3(1)), and to a contract for digital content not on a tangible medium, or a digital service, supplied against the consumer's personal data, unless the trader processes that data only to supply the content and for no other purpose (Art. 3(1a)). "Price" reaches a voucher, gift card or loyalty points, not money alone; a free trial that converts into a paid contract unless the consumer cancels first is a contract against payment from the start.
- Classify the trader, the consumer and the contract before drafting anything else; the classification decides which article set and which withdrawal trigger apply, and courts read all three broadly against the trader. A natural person still counts as a consumer in a dual-purpose contract if the trade purpose is not predominant in the overall context (Recital 17); a blanket B2B tick-box exclusion is not self-enforcing, and a member state may go further still, extending consumer-style protection by national law to an entity that is not a natural person, such as a building's commonhold association, even though this Directive's own consumer definition requires a natural person (Recital 13). A mixed goods-and-services contract is classified by its real main purpose, not by the price split between the parts, because that classification decides the withdrawal-period trigger (Art. 9(2)); state one classification and apply its withdrawal rule to the whole contract, not a separate rule per component.
- A trader must sort an offer into "online digital content" (a single act of supply of a specific file) or a "digital service" (ongoing access, storage, processing or sharing), since the two carry different withdrawal regimes; genuine doubt defaults to the service rule, and the withdrawal exception for content once performance begins (Art. 16(m)) is read narrowly against the trader.
- For distance contracts for financial services, only a named subset of articles applies: Art. 1, 2, 3(2), (5) and (6), 4, 6a, 8(6), 11a, 16a to 16e, 19, 21 to 23, 24(1) and (6), 25 to 27, 29 (Art. 3(1b)). Draft a financial-services distance contract against that subset, never against Art. 5 to 16 directly.
- A sector-specific exclusion list removes social services, healthcare, gambling, financial services outside the Art. 3(1b) subset, immovable property, new-build construction, substantial conversion of existing buildings, residential rental, timeshare, long-term holiday product, resale and exchange contracts under Directive 2008/122/EC, named public-office-holder contracts, frequent-round delivery of household foodstuffs and beverages, passenger transport (Art. 8(2), 19, 21 and 22 still apply), vending machines, single-connection payphone or telecom contracts, and goods sold under legal execution (Art. 3(3)). Every exclusion is read narrowly: an architect's design contract is not "construction" merely because a building might follow it (Art. 3(3)(f); CJEU C-208/19 NK, ECLI:EU:C:2020:382), and a card that confers a later discount on transport tickets is not itself a "passenger transport" contract and stays fully inside the Directive (Art. 3(3)(k); CJEU C-583/18 DB Vertrieb, ECLI:EU:C:2020:199), because the card sale and the later transport contract are two separate contracts. A public-office-holder contract is excluded only where that holder carries a statutory duty of independence and impartiality and gives full legal information so the consumer acts on careful legal consideration; a holder who only certifies the parties' identity under the same duty does not meet the test (Art. 3(3)(i)). The automated-sales exclusion (Art. 3(3)(l)) needs the consumer's physical presence at the point of sale itself; the single-connection exclusion (Art. 3(3)(m)) covers one wireless-access-point session, not a pre-paid SIM card or an advance-purchased code for repeated or ongoing access, since both cover a period or volume of usage rather than one connection.
- Where a package under the Package Travel Directive (EU) 2015/2302 is excluded (Art. 3(3)(g)), Art. 6(7), 8(2) and (6), 19, 21 and 22 still apply to it in relation to the traveller; a linked travel arrangement that does not add up to a package gets the full pre-contractual information regime on each of its own service contracts, with a passenger-transport contract inside it carrying only Art. 8(2), 19, 21 and 22.
- Member states may also exempt off-premises contracts up to EUR 50, or a lower nationally defined value (Art. 3(4)); where the consumer concludes two or more related contracts at the same time, their combined value counts toward that threshold, so splitting one order into several nominal contracts does not avoid it. Confirm which bucket a contract falls into before drafting the full regime against it.
- A contract is off-premises if concluded in the simultaneous physical presence of trader and consumer away from the trader's business premises, on an offer the consumer made there, immediately after the consumer was personally and individually addressed somewhere else (even where conclusion itself happens on the trader's own premises or at a distance), or during a trader-organised excursion aimed at promoting or selling (Art. 2(8)(a) to (d)). A common aisle between stands at a trade fair is not the trader's business premises even where the trader's own stand is, so a contract signed at the stand immediately after the consumer was addressed in the aisle is off-premises (CJEU C-465/19 B & L Elektrogeräte, ECLI:EU:C:2019:1091).
- A "public auction" narrow enough to lose the withdrawal right under Art. 16(k) needs consumers who can attend, or be given the chance to attend, in person through a transparent, competitive bidding procedure run by an auctioneer (Art. 2(13)); an online-only bidding format does not qualify and stays inside the ordinary withdrawal regime.
- The Directive sets full harmonisation: member states must not keep or introduce national rules that diverge from it unless the Directive itself allows it (Art. 4); a trader may always grant more than the Directive requires, never less (Art. 3(6)).

## What the documents must say

### Information duties (Art. 6, including the 2024/825 additions)

- Before the consumer is bound by a distance or off-premises contract, or any corresponding offer, give the Art. 6(1) list in a clear and comprehensible manner: main characteristics; trader identity and contact details, plus a second complaints address if different; total price including taxes, with the billing-period total for a subscription and the monthly total for a fixed-rate contract; whether the price was personalised by automated decision-making; distance-communication cost above basic rate; payment, delivery and performance arrangements and complaint-handling policy; withdrawal conditions, time limit and procedure under Art. 11(1), the Annex I(B) model form, and, where applicable, the Art. 11a withdrawal function's existence and placement; return-cost liability and liability for early-performance costs; absence of a withdrawal right or the circumstances in which it is lost; conformity-guarantee reminder and the durability-guarantee label where the producer offers one; after-sales terms; existence of any relevant code of conduct and how to obtain a copy, where applicable; contract duration and termination conditions; minimum duration of the consumer's obligations; existence and conditions of any deposit or financial guarantee the trader requires; for goods with digital elements, digital content and digital services, applicable functionality and technical protection measures, and any relevant compatibility and interoperability the trader is or should reasonably be aware of; out-of-court redress; reparability score, or spare-parts information where no score applies (Art. 6(1), points a to v).
- Give the digital-content conformity-guarantee reminder, and the minimum software-update period where the producer or provider discloses it, using the harmonised notice and label for the conformity and durability reminders, both set by Commission implementing act rather than by the Directive's own text (Art. 6(1)(l), (la), (lb), (lc); Art. 22a).
- Disclose the existence and conditions of after-sale customer assistance, after-sales services and commercial guarantees (Art. 6(1)(m)). For a commercial guarantee the manufacturer gives, the duty does not arise from the guarantee's mere existence: it arises where the consumer has a legitimate interest in that information to decide whether to contract with the trader, in particular where the trader makes the guarantee a central or decisive element of its offer, and it then covers every condition of applying and using the guarantee that bears on that decision (CJEU C-179/21 absoluts -bikes and more-, judgment of 5 May 2022, paras. 53 and 65).
- For an on-premises contract (not distance, not off-premises), give the shorter Art. 5(1) list instead, covering the same ground at the point of sale rather than before conclusion at a distance; day-to-day transactions performed immediately at conclusion may be exempted from it by the member state (Art. 5(1), (3)).
- A provider of an online marketplace must disclose, in a dedicated, directly and easily accessible interface section, before the consumer is bound: the main ranking parameters and their relative weight; whether the third-party offeror is a trader, based on that offeror's own declaration; that Union consumer law does not apply if not; and how contractual obligations split between the third party and the marketplace provider (Art. 6a(1)). The ranking disclosure may stay general and need not change per search query, provided it sits in that dedicated section rather than inside the general terms; the non-trader warning does not change the offeror's actual legal status if the declaration was wrong, since the ordinary trader test still decides whether consumer protections in fact apply.
- The Art. 6(1) information may be given only in general terms and conditions that the consumer actively accepts by ticking a box, provided it is brought to the consumer's attention in a clear and comprehensible manner; this never replaces the confirmation of the contract on a durable medium under Art. 8(7). An intermediary acting in the name of or on behalf of a trader is itself a trader, alongside the principal trader (CJEU C-536/20 Tiketa, judgment of 24 February 2022, paras. 36 and 53).
- The burden of proving that an information duty was met sits with the trader throughout (Art. 6(9)); keep records of what was shown to a given consumer and when.
- Information already apparent from the context need not be repeated for an on-premises contract (Art. 5), and a member state may exempt a day-to-day, immediately performed on-premises transaction from the Art. 5(1) list altogether (Art. 5(3)); for a service the controlling test is immediate performance at the moment of conclusion, not a low price as such.

### Formal requirements and the order button (Art. 7, 8)

- Off-premises: give the Art. 6(1) information on paper, or on another durable medium with the consumer's agreement, legibly and in plain language, and provide a signed contract copy or a confirmation the same way (Art. 7(1), (2)).
- Distance: give or make the Art. 6(1) information available in a way appropriate to the distance-communication means, and confirm the concluded contract on a durable medium within a reasonable time, at latest by delivery or before service performance begins, repeating any item not already durably given before conclusion. State, at latest at the start of the ordering process, any delivery restrictions and the accepted means of payment (Art. 8(1), (3), (7)). A durable medium is any instrument letting the consumer or the trader store information addressed personally to them, accessible for future reference long enough for its purpose, and reproducible unchanged (Art. 2(10)); a bare hyperlink to a webpage never qualifies (CJEU C-49/11 Content Services, ECLI:EU:C:2012:419), while the trader's own private online account can, if the trader cannot unilaterally alter what is stored there and actively notifies the consumer, for example by email to an agreed mailbox, that new information is available (CJEU C-375/15 BAWAG, ECLI:EU:C:2017:38). A channel that lets the sender delete a sent message from the recipient's device within a short window risks failing this test for the same reason.
- "Distance contract" needs an organised distance sales or service-provision scheme used exclusively up to and including conclusion, with no simultaneous physical presence of trader and consumer (Art. 2(7)); a one-off, unsolicited contact outside such a scheme is not a distance contract.
- Where the distance-communication means allows only limited space or time to display information (telephone, SMS, voice assistant), the trader may limit the pre-order disclosure to main characteristics, trader identity, total price, withdrawal-right basics and duration or termination conditions, leaving out the model withdrawal form, provided the full Art. 6(1) set is supplied another way (Art. 8(4)). A smartphone checkout is never a "limited space or time" medium on that ground alone: expandable content and additional pages stay available on the device, so the full Art. 6(1) disclosure must still be reachable there (CJEU C-430/17 Walbusch Walter Busch, ECLI:EU:C:2019:47).
- Where placing an order activates a button or similar function carrying a payment obligation, label it in an easily legible manner only with PW-EU-1 or an unambiguous equivalent, with the Art. 6(1)(a), (e), (la), (o), (p) information surfaced clearly and prominently immediately before it (Art. 8(2)); see Prescribed wording. A soft label such as "register", "confirm" or "order now" alone does not name the payment obligation and is unlikely to satisfy this; a label naming the obligation itself, such as "buy now", "pay now" or "confirm purchase", conveys the required message. Only the words on the button or similar function decide whether they correspond to PW-EU-1; the rest of the ordering process cannot make an unclear label compliant (CJEU C-249/21 Fuhrmann-2, judgment of 7 April 2022, para. 34). Whether a word such as booking signals a payment obligation in the consumer's language is for the national court to judge (paras. 32 and 33).
- A trader making a sales call must disclose, at the start of the call, their identity, the identity of anyone they act for, and the call's commercial purpose (Art. 8(5)); limiting further information under the limited-space-or-time rule above does not excuse delaying this disclosure itself.

### The right of withdrawal and its periods (Art. 9, 10)

- Give the consumer 14 days to withdraw from a distance or off-premises contract, no reason needed, no cost beyond Art. 13(2) and 14, unless an Art. 16 exception applies; some member states extend this to 30 days for unsolicited home-visit or promotional-excursion contracts, so check the national transposition before stating 14 days as a fixed figure (Art. 9(1), (1a)).
- Start the period from contract conclusion for service contracts, for water, gas, electricity or district heating supply not put up for sale in a limited volume or set quantity, and for non-tangible digital-content supply; from physical possession by the consumer, or their nominated third party other than the carrier, for sales contracts, with the last good counting for a multi-item order delivered separately, the last lot or piece for multi-lot goods, and the first good for regular periodic delivery (Art. 9(2)). A water, gas or electricity contract sold in a limited volume or set quantity falls instead under the sales-contract, physical-possession trigger (Art. 9(2)(c)).
- Extend the period to 12 months from the end of the original period if the trader omitted the Art. 6(1)(h) withdrawal-right information; once supplied within those 12 months, the period then expires 14 (or 30) days after the consumer receives it (Art. 10(1), (2)).

### The withdrawal function (Art. 11a)

- For a distance contract concluded via an online interface, provide a withdrawal function in addition to the Art. 11(1) statement and form route, not instead of it: label it with PW-EU-2 or an unambiguous equivalent, continuously available throughout the withdrawal period, prominently displayed and easily accessible (Art. 11a(1)); see Prescribed wording.
- Let the consumer readily supply or confirm their name, contract-identifying details, and an electronic means for the withdrawal confirmation (Art. 11a(2)), then submit via a second, separate confirmation function labelled with PW-EU-3 or an unambiguous equivalent (Art. 11a(3)); see Prescribed wording.
- On activation, send a durable-medium acknowledgment of receipt without undue delay, with the declaration's content, date and time of submission; submitting before the period expires counts as exercising the right in time (Art. 11a(4), (5)).
- Where the trader offers an electronic fill-in-and-submit option for the Annex I(B) form or any other statement instead, send a durable-medium acknowledgment of receipt without delay for that channel too (Art. 11(3)).

### The effects of withdrawal (Art. 12 to 14)

- Withdrawal terminates both parties' obligations to perform, or, where the consumer made the offer, to conclude the contract (Art. 12).
- Reimburse all payments including delivery cost, using the original payment means unless otherwise agreed, with no fees, within 14 days of being informed of the withdrawal, withholding only the cheapest-standard-delivery excess for a pricier delivery choice; for sales contracts, reimbursement may be withheld until the goods are returned or evidence of return is given, unless the trader offered to collect them (Art. 13(1) to (3)). Let the consumer retrieve digital content or service content they provided, free and in a common machine-readable format, except in four listed cases where the trader may keep or use it (Art. 13(5) to (8)).
- The consumer returns goods within 14 days of notifying withdrawal unless the trader offered to collect, at the trader's expense for an off-premises home delivery that cannot normally go by post; liability for diminished value is limited to handling beyond what establishes the goods' nature and function, with none at all if the trader failed the Art. 6(1)(h) notice (Art. 14(1), (2)). The test tracks what a consumer could do in a physical shop (trying on a garment, testing sound quality) free of charge; only handling beyond that, and only cleaning- or resale-discount-type costs, count toward diminished value (CJEU C-489/07 Messner, ECLI:EU:C:2009:502).
- After an early-performance request under Art. 7(3) or 8(8), the consumer pays a proportionate amount based on the contract price for what was provided before the withdrawal notice, owing nothing at all if the trader omitted the Art. 6(1)(h) or (j) information, or never made an express early-start request (Art. 14(3), (4)).
- An ancillary contract terminates automatically, at no cost beyond Art. 13(2) and 14, when the main contract is withdrawn from, subject to the Consumer Credit Directive's (2008/48/EC) own Article 15 for a linked credit agreement (Art. 15(1)).

### Delivery and risk (Art. 18, 20)

- Absent a different agreement, deliver goods within 30 days of conclusion of a sales contract. On a missed deadline, the consumer must first grant a reasonable additional period before terminating, unless the original deadline was essential to the deal or the trader already refused to deliver, in which case the consumer may terminate immediately; on termination, reimburse all sums paid without undue delay, without prejudice to other remedies national law provides (Art. 18(1), (2), (3), (4)).
- Risk of loss or damage passes to the consumer on physical possession by them or their named third party other than the carrier, unless the consumer independently chose a carrier the trader did not offer, in which case risk passes on handover to that carrier (Art. 20).

### Payment fees and additional payments (Art. 19, 21, 22)

- Do not charge the consumer, for a given payment means, a fee exceeding the trader's own cost of accepting it (Art. 19); since the capped interchange-fee regime and the SEPA credit-transfer and direct-debit regime removed most card and bank-transfer surcharging, this now mainly bites on commercial cards, three-party-scheme cards and cash. A fee by any label (administration, booking, handling) is covered if the consumer could avoid it by choosing a different payment method; an unfavourable exchange rate used in place of a disclosed fee is still a fee.
- Charge no more than the basic telephone rate on a line the trader operates for contacting them about a concluded contract (Art. 21); the trader stays liable for the excess even where the consumer could have used a basic-rate number the trader also offered but used a pricier one instead (CJEU C-332/17 Starman, ECLI:EU:C:2018:721).
- Seek the consumer's express consent, before they are bound, to any extra payment beyond the agreed remuneration; a pre-ticked default the consumer must actively reject does not count as consent, and the consumer can claim the payment back if the trader inferred consent that way, with no need to show the consumer noticed the charge (Art. 22).

### Financial services run on a separate rulebook (Art. 16a to 16e)

- Use the Art. 16a(1) information list, not Art. 6(1), as the skeleton for a distance financial-services contract: trader identity and contact details, register entry, supervisory-authority contact where relevant, main characteristics and total price of the service, late-payment consequences, personalised-pricing notice, risk warnings for market-fluctuation instruments, withdrawal conditions and the withdrawal function's placement where applicable, minimum contract duration, termination rights and penalties, out-of-court redress, and guarantee-fund information (Art. 16a(1), points a to w).
- Give 14 calendar days to withdraw, extended to 30 days for personal pension contracts, running from conclusion or from receipt of terms and the Art. 16a information if later, unless a market-fluctuation, short-term-insurance or fully-performed-by-request exception applies (Art. 16b(1), (2)).
- Give, free of charge and before conclusion, an adequate explanation of whether the contract suits the consumer's needs and financial situation, with human intervention available on request where online tools are used (Art. 16d(1), (3)).
- Do not design an online interface for a distance financial-services contract in a way that deceives, manipulates or materially distorts the consumer's free decision; address at least disproportionate prominence for certain choices, re-prompting an already-made choice, and making termination harder than sign-up (Art. 16e(1)).

## What the documents must not say

- No clause may waive or restrict a right the national transposition confers; a term that does, directly or by a design that circumvents it, does not bind the consumer (Art. 25).
- No order button may carry a generic label such as "continue" or "buy now" in place of PW-EU-1 or an equally unambiguous formulation naming the payment obligation; a non-compliant button means the consumer is not bound by the contract or order at all, a stronger remedy than a fine (Art. 8(2)).
- No withdrawal-function label may substitute brand language for PW-EU-2 or PW-EU-3 without being equally unambiguous; treat these as fixed strings, not areas for brand voice (Art. 11a(1), (3)).
- No clause may claim a withdrawal exception beyond the closed Art. 16 list, or recite its label without the underlying facts it depends on, nor impose a formal requirement on the Annex I(B) model form beyond what the Annex itself specifies (Art. 16; Art. 11(1)). The list is read narrowly against the trader on every point: the exception for goods made to the consumer's specifications or clearly personalised (Art. 16(c)) does not reach an intellectual service with only an incidental tangible result, such as an architect's design (CJEU C-208/19 NK, ECLI:EU:C:2020:382), but once it genuinely applies it is absolute regardless of how far production had progressed (CJEU C-529/19 Möbel Kraft, ECLI:EU:C:2020:846); the exception for sealed goods unsuitable for return for health protection or hygiene reasons (Art. 16(e)) does not reach goods, such as a mattress, that can be resold after cleaning without a genuine health or hygiene bar (CJEU C-681/17 slewo, ECLI:EU:C:2019:255); the exception for a requested urgent repair or maintenance visit (Art. 16(h)) covers only the work the consumer specifically requested and the parts that repair needed, leaving any further service or goods supplied on the same visit under the ordinary withdrawal right; and the specific-date-or-performance exception (Art. 16(l)) reads "transport of goods" broadly enough to cover making a vehicle available for a dated haul (CJEU C-336/03 easyCar, ECLI:EU:C:2005:150), but never reaches storage services even when dated, and never reaches a leisure service with no capacity limit tied to that date. An intermediary that sells access to a leisure activity in its own name but on behalf of the organiser may rely on the Art. 16(l) exception too, provided withdrawal would put the risk of the released capacity on the organiser and the activity is scheduled for a specific date or period (CJEU C-96/21 CTS Eventim, judgment of 31 March 2022, para. 55).
- No financial-services interface may give a trader-preferred choice disproportionate prominence, re-prompt an already-made choice through an interrupting pop-up, or make termination harder than sign-up (Art. 16e(1)).
- No clause may rely on a messaging or account channel that the trader can unilaterally alter, or that a message can be deleted from within a short window, as the durable medium for a confirmation or a withdrawal acknowledgment (Art. 2(10)).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- The order button and the withdrawal and confirmation functions are fixed-label controls (PW-EU-1 to PW-EU-3), not configurable strings a marketing or design change could silently alter.
- The checkout, order-confirmation and withdrawal-policy surfaces reuse one maintained copy of the Annex I model text (PW-EU-4, PW-EU-5) rather than a bespoke notice drafted independently for each.
- Records capture what information was shown to a given consumer and when, since the trader carries the burden of proof for every duty in this file (Art. 6(9)); the durable-medium confirmation (Art. 7(1), (2), Art. 8(7)) is the practical anchor for that proof, and a plain webpage link does not serve as one.
- The confirmation or withdrawal channel actively alerts the consumer when new information is posted there, rather than relying on the consumer to check an account on their own, wherever that channel is the only route used.
- Enforcement bodies, consumer organisations and professional organisations with a legitimate interest can bring action over any duty in this file (Art. 23); a handful of these duties (Art. 6(6) undisclosed charges, Art. 10 the withdrawal-period extension, Art. 18(2) termination for late delivery, Art. 22 unticked extra payments) give the consumer a direct remedy that operates whether or not a regulator acts, so a compliance review of this file checks those four first.

## Prescribed wording

- PW-EU-1: order button wording, Art. 8(2); goes on the final order-submission control for any electronically concluded distance contract carrying a payment obligation.
- PW-EU-2: withdrawal function label, Art. 11a(1); goes on the persistent, prominently placed control that starts the online withdrawal declaration.
- PW-EU-3: confirmation function label, Art. 11a(3); goes on the second control that submits the completed withdrawal declaration.
- PW-EU-4: model withdrawal information, Annex I part A; goes into the withdrawal-information section, correctly completed for the contract type, as the safe harbor for Art. 6(1)(h), (i), (j) (Art. 6(4)).
- PW-EU-5: model withdrawal form, Annex I part B; goes alongside the withdrawal information as the form the consumer may use, never the only way to withdraw.
- PW-EU-6 to PW-EU-10: the same five items in German; use these, not a translation of PW-EU-1 to PW-EU-5, for a German-facing document.

## Open points to raise

- Which member states the product is sold into, since every duty above operates through that state's national transposition; for any state other than Germany, the transposing statute and any national variant (the Art. 9(1a) 30-day extension, the Art. 3(4) low-value exemption) are an open point until named.
- Whether any contract is, or includes, a distance financial-services contract under Art. 3(1b), since that narrows the applicable article set and changes the information skeleton from Art. 6(1) to Art. 16a(1).
- Whether the Art. 22a harmonised notice and label implementing act has been adopted and what it requires, since this file only establishes the duty to use them.
- Whether the seller's online interface is itself an online marketplace under Art. 2(17), adding the Art. 6a disclosure duties on top of the seller's own terms.
- Whether a travel-adjacent offer (a single connecting flight, a stand-alone hotel booking sold alongside a package) is a "linked travel arrangement" under the Package Travel Directive (EU) 2015/2302, since that changes which articles of this Directive apply to it from the full set to the Art. 6(7), 8(2), 8(6), 19, 21, 22 subset.

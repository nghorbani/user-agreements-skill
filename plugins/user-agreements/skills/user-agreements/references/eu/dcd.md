# Digital Content Directive (EU) 2019/770

Citation: Directive (EU) 2019/770 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 20 May 2019 on certain aspects
concerning contracts for the supply of digital content and digital services
Version: original text, no amendment found
Official text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32019L0770
Law as of: 2026-10-20

This file is the directive-level reference for digital content and digital services supplied to consumers across
the Union. The Directive fully harmonises the rules it covers: a Member State must not keep or introduce a national
rule that diverges from it, stricter or looser, unless the Directive itself allows the difference (Art. 4). National
transposition law supplies the exact wording and procedure a given Member State requires; for Germany, the
transposition is an open point until a dedicated file covers it (see Open points).
The sibling Sale of Goods Directive (EU) 2019/771 covers goods, including goods with digital elements; this file
does not restate that boundary beyond naming it below.

## When it applies

- The Directive applies to any contract where a trader supplies or undertakes to supply digital content or a
  digital service to a consumer, and the consumer pays or undertakes to pay a price (Art. 3(1), first subparagraph).
  The price itself reaches money or a digital representation of value due in exchange, including a recognised
  virtual currency (Art. 2(7)).
- It applies equally where the consumer provides or undertakes to provide personal data instead of a price, unless
  the trader processes that data exclusively to supply the content or service in line with the Directive or to meet
  a legal requirement the trader is subject to, and processes it for no other purpose (Art. 3(1), second
  subparagraph). A nominally free app or service that otherwise uses the consumer's data, for advertising profiling
  for example, is in scope on this ground alone.
- It applies where the digital content or digital service is developed to the consumer's own specifications,
  including tailor-made software (Art. 3(2)).
- With the exception of Art. 5 and 13, it also applies to a tangible medium that serves exclusively as a carrier of
  digital content, such as a DVD, CD, USB stick or memory card (Art. 3(3)).
- It does not apply to digital content or a digital service that is incorporated in or inter-connected with goods
  within the meaning of "goods with digital elements" (Art. 2(3)) and is supplied with those goods under a sales
  contract for them, whoever actually supplies it, seller or third party; in a case of doubt whether such supply
  forms part of the sales contract, it is presumed to be covered by the sales contract instead, not by this
  Directive (Art. 3(4)). Content or a service the goods can function without, or one the consumer buys under a
  contract separate from the goods sale, is not covered by this carve-out and can fall under this Directive instead.
- It does not apply to a contract whose subject matter is one of eight listed exclusions: a non-digital service,
  whatever digital means the trader uses to produce or deliver its output; an electronic communications service
  other than a number-independent interpersonal communications service; healthcare; a gambling service involving a
  pecuniary stake; a financial service; software offered under a free and open-source licence where the consumer
  pays no price and the trader processes the consumer's data only to improve that specific software's security,
  compatibility or interoperability; digital content made available to the public other than by signal transmission
  as part of a performance or event; and digital content a public-sector body supplies under the applicable
  open-data directive (Art. 3(5), points a to h).
- Where one contract bundles elements of digital content or digital service supply with elements of other goods or
  services, the Directive applies only to the digital content or digital service elements of that contract, without
  prejudice to the goods-with-digital-elements carve-out above (Art. 3(6), first subparagraph). The Directive's own
  modification rule (Art. 19) does not apply to the digital content or digital service element of a bundle that
  falls within the electronic-communications Code's own bundle rules, which govern that element instead (Art. 3(6),
  second subparagraph); the effect that ending one bundle element has on the others is a matter for national law
  (Art. 3(6), third subparagraph).
- Where a provision of this Directive conflicts with a provision of another Union act governing a specific sector or
  subject matter, the other act's provision takes precedence (Art. 3(7)).
- Union law on the protection of personal data applies in full to any personal data processed in connection with a
  contract this Directive covers; in particular, this Directive leaves Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and Directive
  2002/58/EC unaffected, and in the event of a conflict between this Directive and Union data-protection law, the
  latter prevails (Art. 3(8)).
- This Directive leaves Union and national copyright and related-rights law unaffected (Art. 3(9)), and leaves a
  Member State free to regulate general contract law, such as formation, validity, nullity or the effects of a
  contract including the consequences of its termination, or the right to damages, to the extent this Directive does
  not regulate the point (Art. 3(10)).

## What the documents must say

### The conformity standard (Art. 6 to 9)

- Supply digital content or a digital service that meets both the subjective and the objective requirements for
  conformity set out below, without prejudice to the third-party-rights rule (Art. 6).
- Subjective requirements are what the specific contract promises. Where applicable, the digital content or service
  must be of the contract's description, quantity and quality, and possess the functionality, compatibility,
  interoperability and other features the contract requires; be fit for a particular purpose the consumer made
  known to the trader by conclusion at the latest and that the trader accepted; come with the accessories,
  instructions (including on installation) and customer assistance the contract requires; and be updated as the
  contract stipulates (Art. 7, points a to d).
- Objective requirements apply on top of the subjective ones and do not depend on what the contract itself says.
  Where applicable, the digital content or service must: be fit for the purposes digital content or services of the
  same type would normally serve, taking into account applicable Union and national law, technical standards, or,
  failing those, applicable sector-specific codes of conduct; possess the quantity and the qualities and performance
  features, including functionality, compatibility, accessibility, continuity and security, that are normal for
  content or services of the same type and that a consumer may reasonably expect given its nature, taking into
  account a public statement made by or on behalf of the trader or an earlier person in the chain of transactions,
  particularly in advertising or on labelling, unless the trader shows it was not and could not reasonably have been
  aware of the statement, that the statement had been corrected by conclusion in a comparable way to how it was
  made, or that the statement could not have influenced the decision to acquire the content or service; come, where
  applicable, with the accessories and instructions a consumer may reasonably expect to receive; and match any trial
  version or preview the trader made available before conclusion (Art. 8(1), points a to d).
- The trader must ensure the consumer is informed of, and supplied with, updates including security updates that are
  necessary to keep the content or service in conformity, for the whole period the contract provides for continuous
  supply, or, for a single act of supply or a series of individual acts of supply, for whatever period a consumer
  may reasonably expect given the content's or service's type and purpose and the contract's circumstances and
  nature (Art. 8(2), points a, b).
- Where the consumer does not install a supplied update within a reasonable time, the trader is not liable for a
  lack of conformity that results solely from missing that update, provided the trader told the consumer of the
  update's availability and the consequences of not installing it, and the consumer's failure to install it, or
  incorrect installation of it, was not due to defective installation instructions the trader gave (Art. 8(3),
  points a, b).
- Where the contract provides for continuous supply over a period, the content or service must stay in conformity
  throughout that period (Art. 8(4)).
- Where continuous supply is interrupted for more than a negligible time, or the interruption recurs, that already
  counts as a lack of conformity (recital 51); an uptime or availability clause should not treat only a total outage
  as the trigger for a remedy.
- There is no lack of conformity under Art. 8(1) or (2) if, at conclusion, the consumer was specifically informed
  that a stated characteristic deviated from the objective requirements, and the consumer expressly and separately
  accepted that deviation when concluding the contract (Art. 8(5)). Both conditions must be met together: specific
  information on the particular deviating characteristic, and a separate, express acceptance distinct from any
  general terms-acceptance step; ticking a box, pressing a button or a similarly active and unequivocal act can
  satisfy both (recital 49).
- Unless the parties agree otherwise, the digital content or service must be supplied in the most recent version
  available at conclusion (Art. 8(6)).
- A lack of conformity that results from incorrect integration into the consumer's digital environment counts as a
  lack of conformity of the content or service itself where the trader, or someone under the trader's
  responsibility, carried out the integration, or where the consumer was meant to integrate it and the incorrect
  integration traces to defective integration instructions the trader gave (Art. 9, points a, b).
- Where a restriction from a violation of a third party's right, in particular an intellectual property right,
  prevents or limits using the content or service in line with Art. 7 and 8, the consumer is entitled to the
  remedies for lack of conformity, unless national law provides for the nullity or rescission of the contract in
  such a case (Art. 10).

### Trader liability and burden of proof (Art. 11, 12)

- The trader is liable for any failure to supply the digital content or service in line with Art. 5 (Art. 11(1)).
- For a single act of supply or a series of individual acts of supply, the trader is liable for a lack of conformity
  under Art. 7, 8 and 9 that exists at the time of supply, without prejudice to Art. 8(2)(b); where national law only
  holds the trader liable for a lack of conformity that becomes apparent within a period after supply, that period
  must be at least two years from supply, again without prejudice to Art. 8(2)(b) (Art. 11(2), first and second
  subparagraphs). Where national law also, or only, subjects the Art. 14 remedies to a limitation period, that
  period must let the consumer exercise them for a lack of conformity existing at supply that becomes apparent
  within the two-year (or longer) window (Art. 11(2), third subparagraph).
- For continuous supply over a period, the trader is liable for a lack of conformity under Art. 7, 8 and 9 that
  occurs or becomes apparent within the period the contract provides for supply; a limitation period under national
  law must equally let the consumer exercise the Art. 14 remedies for such a defect (Art. 11(3)).
- The burden of proving the content or service was supplied in line with Art. 5 sits with the trader (Art. 12(1)).
- For a single act or series of acts of supply, the trader bears the burden of proving conformity at the time of
  supply, for a lack of conformity that becomes apparent within one year of supply (Art. 12(2)). For continuous
  supply, the trader bears that burden for a lack of conformity that becomes apparent within the period the content
  or service is to be supplied under the contract (Art. 12(3)).
- Art. 12(2) and (3) do not apply where the trader demonstrates the consumer's digital environment is not
  compatible with the content's or service's technical requirements, and the trader informed the consumer of those
  requirements in a clear and comprehensible manner before conclusion (Art. 12(4)). This defense needs no finding on
  the consumer's cooperation.
- The consumer must cooperate with the trader, to the extent reasonably possible and necessary, to establish whether
  the cause of a lack of conformity at the relevant Art. 11(2) or (3) time lay in the consumer's digital environment;
  that duty to cooperate is limited to the technically available means that are least intrusive for the consumer.
  Where the consumer fails to cooperate, and the trader had told the consumer of the cooperation duty in a clear and
  comprehensible manner before conclusion, the burden of proving the lack of conformity existed at the relevant
  Art. 11(2) or (3) time shifts to the consumer (Art. 12(5)). This burden shift needs no finding of incompatibility.

### Remedies (Art. 13, 14)

- Where the trader has failed to supply in line with Art. 5, the consumer must call on the trader to supply it; if
  the trader then still fails to supply without undue delay, or within an additional period the parties expressly
  agreed, the consumer is entitled to terminate the contract (Art. 13(1)).
- That call-upon step does not apply, and the consumer may terminate immediately, where the trader has declared, or
  it is equally clear from the circumstances, that it will not supply the content or service, or where the parties
  agreed, or it is clear from the circumstances at conclusion, that a specific time for supply was essential for the
  consumer and the trader missed it (Art. 13(2), points a, b). Where the consumer terminates under either path,
  Art. 15 to 18 apply (Art. 13(3)).
- In the case of a lack of conformity, the consumer is entitled to have the content or service brought into
  conformity, to a proportionate price reduction, or to terminate the contract, under the conditions this Article
  sets (Art. 14(1)).
- The consumer is entitled to have the content or service brought into conformity unless that would be impossible,
  or would impose a disproportionate cost on the trader; disproportion is judged against all the circumstances,
  including the value the content or service would have had without the defect, and the defect's significance
  (Art. 14(2), points a, b).
- The trader must bring the content or service into conformity within a reasonable time from being informed of the
  defect, free of charge and without significant inconvenience to the consumer, taking account of the content's or
  service's nature and the purpose the consumer needed it for (Art. 14(3)).
- The consumer is entitled to a proportionate price reduction, where the content or service was supplied for a
  price, or to terminate the contract, in any of five cases: bringing it into conformity is impossible or
  disproportionate; the trader has not done so within the Art. 14(3) terms; a defect reappears despite the trader's
  attempt to fix it; the defect is serious enough to justify an immediate price reduction or termination; or the
  trader has declared, or it is clear from the circumstances, that it will not bring the content or service into
  conformity within a reasonable time or without significant inconvenience (Art. 14(4), points a to e).
- The price reduction is proportionate to the decrease in value of the content or service as supplied compared to
  its value had it been in conformity; where the contract provides for supply over a period against a price, the
  reduction applies to the period during which the content or service was not in conformity (Art. 14(5)).
- Where the content or service is supplied for a price, the consumer may terminate only if the lack of conformity is
  not minor; the burden of proving the defect is minor sits with the trader (Art. 14(6)).

### Termination mechanics (Art. 15 to 18)

- The consumer exercises the right to terminate by a statement to the trader expressing the decision to terminate
  (Art. 15).
- On termination, the trader must reimburse the consumer for all sums paid under the contract; where the contract
  provided for continuous supply against a price and the content or service had been in conformity for part of that
  period, the trader need only reimburse the proportionate part of the price corresponding to the period it was not
  in conformity, plus any part of the price the consumer paid in advance for a remaining period the contract would
  have run (Art. 16(1)).
- On the consumer's personal data, the trader must comply with the applicable obligations under Regulation (EU)
  2016/679 (Art. 16(2)).
- The trader must refrain from using any content, other than personal data, that the consumer provided or created
  while using the supplied content or service, except where that content has no utility outside the trader's
  content or service; relates only to the consumer's own activity when using it; was aggregated by the trader with
  other data and cannot be disaggregated, or only at disproportionate effort; or was generated jointly by the
  consumer and others who are able to keep using it (Art. 16(3), points a to d).
- Except in the first three of those cases (points a to c), the trader must, at the consumer's request, make that
  content available to the consumer; the consumer may retrieve it free of charge, without hindrance from the
  trader, within a reasonable time and in a commonly used, machine-readable format (Art. 16(4)).
- The trader may prevent further use of the content or service by the consumer, including by disabling access or
  the consumer's account, without prejudice to the Art. 16(4) retrieval duty (Art. 16(5)).
- After termination, the consumer must stop using the content or service and stop making it available to third
  parties (Art. 17(1)). Where digital content was supplied on a tangible medium, the consumer must return it to the
  trader, at the trader's request and expense, without undue delay; a return request must be made within 14 days of
  the trader learning of the consumer's decision to terminate (Art. 17(2)). The consumer is not liable to pay for
  any use made before termination during a period the content or service was not in conformity (Art. 17(3)).
- A reimbursement owed under Art. 14(4), (5) or Art. 16(1) must be carried out without undue delay and, in any case,
  within 14 days of the trader learning of the consumer's decision to invoke a price reduction or to terminate
  (Art. 18(1)). The trader must reimburse using the same payment means the consumer used, unless the consumer
  expressly agrees otherwise, and the consumer must incur no fee from the reimbursement (Art. 18(2)); the trader
  must not charge the consumer a fee for the reimbursement itself (Art. 18(3)).

### Modification during the contract (Art. 19)

- Where the contract provides that the content or service is to be supplied or kept accessible over a period, the
  trader may modify it beyond what conformity with Art. 7 and 8 requires only where all of these conditions are met:
  the contract allows the modification and states a valid reason for it; the modification carries no extra cost for
  the consumer; the consumer is informed of it in a clear and comprehensible manner; and, in the case below where
  termination applies, the consumer is told reasonably in advance, on a durable medium, of the modification's
  features and timing and of the right to terminate, or of the chance instead to keep the content or service
  unmodified (Art. 19(1), points a to d).
- The consumer is entitled to terminate the contract if the modification negatively affects the consumer's access
  to, or use of, the content or service, unless that negative effect is only minor; in that case, the consumer may
  terminate free of charge within 30 days of receiving the information, or of the modification taking effect,
  whichever is later (Art. 19(2)). Where the consumer terminates on this ground, Art. 15 to 18 apply (Art. 19(3)).
- Art. 19(2) and (3) do not apply where the trader has let the consumer keep the content or service, at no extra
  cost, unmodified and in conformity (Art. 19(4)).

### Right of redress (Art. 20)

- Where the trader is liable to the consumer for a failure to supply, or for a lack of conformity resulting from an
  earlier act or omission in the chain of transactions, the trader is entitled to pursue remedies against the person
  or persons liable further up that chain; national law determines who the trader may pursue and on what conditions
  (Art. 20).

## What the documents must not say

- No contractual term may, to the consumer's detriment, exclude, derogate from or vary the effect of a national
  measure transposing this Directive, where that happens before the consumer brought a failure to supply or a lack
  of conformity to the trader's attention, or before the trader brought an Art. 19 modification to the consumer's
  attention; such a term does not bind the consumer (Art. 22(1)). This reaches a clause drafted to take effect only
  after the relevant trigger as much as one that takes effect immediately, since the rule turns on the term's
  effect, not its labelled timing.
- No clause may claim a deviation from the objective requirements for conformity without both a specific disclosure
  of the particular deviating characteristic at conclusion and the consumer's separate, express acceptance of it;
  a general terms-acceptance checkbox, or a blanket disclaimer that does not name the specific characteristic, does
  not meet this (Art. 8(5); recital 49).
- No modification clause may let the trader change a feature beyond what conformity requires without the contract
  itself stating a valid reason, without the change being free to the consumer, or without clear and comprehensible
  notice; where the change is more than minor in its negative effect, the clause cannot omit the advance
  durable-medium notice naming the modification's features, timing and the consumer's termination right, unless a
  genuine no-cost unmodified option is also offered (Art. 19(1), (2), (4)).
- No clause may shorten the national liability window below two years from supply for a single act of supply or a
  series of individual acts of supply, nor confine trader liability for continuous supply to less than the whole
  period the contract provides for supply (Art. 11(2), (3)).
- No clause may shift the Art. 12(1) to (3) burden of proof onto the consumer outside the two narrow conditions the
  Directive itself sets (the pre-disclosed incompatibility defense in Art. 12(4), and the pre-disclosed
  non-cooperation flip in Art. 12(5)); a clause purporting to reverse the burden on any other ground does not bind
  the consumer, by operation of the anti-waiver rule (Art. 22(1)).
- No clause may limit the consumer's post-termination content-export right to anything less than free, unhindered,
  within a reasonable time, and in a commonly used, machine-readable format, except in the three cases under
  Art. 16(3)(a) to (c) where the trader may keep using the content without exporting it; a clause relying on
  Art. 16(3)(d) to withhold export is not covered by this carve-out (Art. 16(3), (4)).
- No clause may impose a fee on the consumer for a reimbursement owed under Art. 14 or Art. 16(1), or reimburse by
  a different payment means than the consumer used without the consumer's express agreement (Art. 18(2), (3)).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- The conformity check runs on two layers together: what the contract specifically promised (subjective
  requirements, Art. 7) and what a product of that type normally needs regardless of the contract's own wording
  (objective requirements, Art. 8(1)); a conformity or warranty clause in the documents must track both layers, since
  the contract's own promises alone are not enough.
- The product ships, and keeps shipping, the updates (including security updates) needed to stay in conformity, for
  the contract's whole term on continuous supply or for a reasonable expected period otherwise (Art. 8(2)); where
  the consumer can decline an update, the product discloses the consequence of declining before the consumer does
  so, since that disclosure is a condition of the trader's own liability defense (Art. 8(3)).
- Continuous-supply products (cloud storage, streaming, SaaS, a subscription service) log interruptions, since a
  short interruption that is more than negligible, or one that recurs, is itself a lack of conformity (recital 51);
  an uptime dashboard or incident report should not define availability only by a total outage.
- Where a deviation from objective conformity is used (a feature flag, a known limitation), the product captures the
  consumer's separate, specific acceptance of that named deviation at conclusion, distinct from the general terms
  acceptance, since this is what the deviation rule requires to be effective (Art. 8(5)).
- The support or incident process can show, for a dispute within the relevant Art. 11(2) or (3) window, that the
  product was in conformity at the time that matters, since the trader carries that burden by default (Art. 12(1) to
  (3)); where the product relies on the incompatibility defense, it records what technical requirements it disclosed
  to the consumer and when, before conclusion (Art. 12(4)); where it relies on the non-cooperation flip, it records
  that the cooperation duty itself was clearly and comprehensibly disclosed before conclusion (Art. 12(5)).
- A termination or cancellation flow reimburses within 14 days of learning of the consumer's decision, through the
  consumer's original payment method unless the consumer agrees otherwise, with no fee for the reimbursement itself
  (Art. 18).
- A termination flow also triggers the content-export and content-reuse-restriction duties: the product can produce
  the consumer's non-personal content in a common machine-readable format on request, unless one of the first three
  listed cases (Art. 16(3)(a) to (c)) applies; content generated jointly under point (d) must still be exported even
  though the trader may keep using it, and otherwise the product stops using that content going forward
  (Art. 16(3), (4)).
- A feature-change or product-update process that is more than a conformity fix checks, before shipping: whether
  the change is free to the consumer; whether the contract already states a valid reason for this kind of change;
  and, where the change is more than minor in its negative effect on the consumer's access or use, whether advance
  durable-medium notice and a 30-day termination window (or a genuine unmodified opt-out) are in place before the
  change goes out (Art. 19).

## Prescribed wording

This file has no prescribed wording of its own; the Directive sets no model form, label or fixed phrase for a
digital-content or digital-service document. Any prescribed label used in a checkout or cancellation flow that
involves digital content or services (such as PW-EU-1 to PW-EU-5 under the Consumer Rights Directive) is covered in
`references/eu/crd.md` and in `references/prescribed-wording.md`, not here.

## Dates

- 1 July 2021: deadline for a Member State to adopt and publish its transposing measures and inform the Commission
  of them (Art. 24(1), first subparagraph).
- 1 January 2022: date from which Member States apply those transposing measures (Art. 24(1), second subparagraph).
- 1 January 2022: date from which the Directive's provisions apply to the supply of digital content or digital
  services, with one exception: Art. 19 (modification) and Art. 20 (right of redress) apply only to a contract
  concluded from that date onward (Art. 24(2)).
- 12 June 2024: deadline for the Commission to review the Directive's application and report to the European
  Parliament, the Council and the European Economic and Social Committee, examining among other things whether
  rules should be harmonised for digital-content or digital-service contracts the Directive does not already cover,
  including content supplied against advertisements (Art. 25).
- 2026-10-20: law-as-of date for this file.

## Open points to raise

- Which Member States the product is sold into, since every duty above binds through that state's own
  transposing measure, not through this Directive's text directly (Art. 24(1)); this file covers only the
  Directive's own text, not any one Member State's transposing measure. For Germany, the transposing measure is
  `references/de/bgb-digital.md` (§§ 327 to 327u BGB); for any other Member State, treat its transposing measure
  as an open point for counsel.
- Whether an offer bundles digital content or a digital service with goods or other services under one contract,
  since that decides whether the Directive applies to the whole contract or only to its digital-content or
  digital-service elements, and whether the electronic-communications bundle carve-out for Art. 19 applies
  (Art. 3(6)).
- Whether specific content or a specific service is incorporated in or inter-connected with goods and supplied
  under a sales contract for those goods, since that is a case-by-case boundary question with Directive (EU)
  2019/771 that this file does not resolve on its own (Art. 3(4)).
- Whether the free and open-source software exclusion applies to a specific offering, since it depends on how the
  trader actually processes any personal data the consumer provides, and the licence's own terms do not settle that
  on their own (Art. 3(5)(f)).

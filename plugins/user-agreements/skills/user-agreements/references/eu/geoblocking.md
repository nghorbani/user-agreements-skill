# Regulation (EU) 2018/302: unjustified geo-blocking and other nationality-based discrimination

Citation: Regulation (EU) 2018/302 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 28 February 2018 on addressing unjustified geo-blocking and other forms of discrimination based on customers' nationality, place of residence or place of establishment within the internal market
Version: original, no amendment found
Official text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32018R0302
Law as of: 2026-10-20

## When it applies

- The Regulation binds every trader selling goods or services within the Union to a customer, whether the trader is established in a Member State or in a third country (Art. 1(1); recital 17).
- A "customer" means a consumer who is a national of, or resident in, a Member State, or an undertaking established in a Member State, buying or receiving a good or service within the Union for end use only, not for resale, transformation, processing, renting or subcontracting (Art. 2(12), 2(13); recital 16).
- It does not apply to a purely internal situation where every relevant element of the transaction (the parties' nationality, residence or establishment, the place of execution, the means of payment, the offer, the online interface used) is confined to one Member State (Art. 1(2)).
- It does not apply to the service activities Art. 2(2) of Directive 2006/123/EC excludes, which include transport services, audiovisual services (including services whose main purpose is access to sports broadcasts under exclusive territorial licences), and retail financial services other than the Art. 5 payment rule (Art. 1(3); recitals 8, 9).
- It leaves taxation rules untouched (Art. 1(4)), and leaves copyright and neighbouring-rights rules, in particular Directive 2001/29/EC, untouched (Art. 1(5)).
- It does not affect Union law on judicial cooperation in civil matters. Where a trader, acting under Articles 3 to 5, does not block or limit interface access, does not redirect a consumer based on nationality or residence, does not apply different general conditions of access in the Art. 4 situations, and accepts out-of-state payment instruments on a non-discriminatory basis, that compliance alone does not mean the trader is directing activities to the consumer's Member State for the purpose of Art. 6(1)(b) of Regulation (EC) No 593/2008 (Rome I) or Art. 17(1)(c) of Regulation (EU) No 1215/2012 (Brussels Ia); giving the consumer post-contract information or assistance that results from that compliance does not count against the trader either (Art. 1(6); recital 13).
- The "general conditions of access" that Art. 4 covers means every term, condition and other item, including net sale prices, that a trader sets and makes available to the public at large to govern customers' access to its goods or services, applied in the absence of an individually negotiated agreement; terms negotiated individually between the trader and a customer are not general conditions of access (Art. 2(14); recital 15).

## What the documents must say

### Access to the online interface (Art. 3)

- A trader must not block or limit a customer's access to its online interface (a website or app, operated by or for the trader, through which customers reach the trader's goods or services) through technological measures or otherwise, for reasons tied to the customer's nationality, place of residence or place of establishment (Art. 2(16), 3(1)).
- A trader running several locally targeted versions of its interface must not redirect a customer to a different version for those reasons unless the customer has explicitly consented to the redirection; once given, that consent covers every later visit to the same interface until the customer withdraws it, so the trader need not ask again each time (Art. 3(2), first subparagraph; recital 20).
- Where redirection happens with explicit consent, the version the customer originally sought must stay easily reachable (Art. 3(2), second subparagraph).
- Both prohibitions give way where the blocking, limiting or redirection is necessary to meet a legal requirement set by Union law, or by a Member State's law consistent with Union law, that applies to the trader's activities; in that case the trader must give the customer a clear, specific explanation of why compliance makes the step necessary, in the language of the interface the customer first tried to reach (Art. 3(3)).
- This non-discrimination duty does not require a trader to enter into a transaction with any given customer; it bars only nationality, residence or establishment-based blocking, limiting or redirection, not a refusal to deal taken for other reasons (recital 18).

### Access to goods and services (Art. 4)

- A trader must not apply different general conditions of access, including different net sale prices, for reasons tied to a customer's nationality, place of residence or place of establishment, in three situations (Art. 4(1)):
  - the customer buys goods that the trader delivers to a Member State the trader's own general conditions already cover, or collects them at a location in a Member State the trader's general conditions already offer as a collection point (Art. 4(1)(a));
  - the customer receives electronically supplied services from the trader, other than services whose main feature is giving access to and use of copyright-protected works or other protected subject matter, including the sale of such works or subject matter in intangible form (Art. 4(1)(b)); this situation does not apply to a trader exempted from VAT under Chapter 1 of Title XII of Directive 2006/112/EC (Art. 4(4)); "electronically supplied services" means services delivered over the internet or an electronic network whose nature makes their supply essentially automated, needing minimal human involvement and impossible without information technology (Art. 2(1)); for example, cloud services, data warehousing services, website hosting, firewall provision, and search engines or internet directories are electronically supplied services (recital 24);
  - the customer receives a service other than an electronically supplied service at a physical location within a Member State where the trader operates (Art. 4(1)(c)).
- For the delivery or collection situation, this duty does not require the trader to deliver cross-border beyond what it already offers, or to bear postage, transport, assembly or disassembly costs beyond what the contract already provides; the customer may have to collect the goods in the Member State of delivery, in another Member State the trader delivers to, or arrange cross-border delivery privately (recital 23, recital 28).
- Where a trader bundles goods with services, or several services together, and one or more of the bundled items would fall under Art. 4(1) if sold alone, the trader must either honour the Art. 4 prohibitions for the whole bundle or, if it already sells that item separately to other customers, offer it on an individual, non-discriminatory basis as well; outside the bundle, the trader stays free to price a standalone item as it chooses provided the pricing is not driven by the customer's nationality, residence or establishment (recital 10).
- The Art. 4(1) prohibition gives way wherever a specific provision of Union law, or of a Member State's law consistent with Union law, bars the trader from selling the goods or providing the service to certain customers or in certain territories (Art. 4(5), first subparagraph); for books specifically, it does not stop a trader from pricing differently by territory where a Member State's resale-price-maintenance law, consistent with Union law, requires that (Art. 4(5), second subparagraph).
- The Art. 4(1) prohibition does not stop a trader from offering general conditions of access, including net sale prices, that differ between Member States or within a Member State, as long as they are offered to customers on a specific territory or to a specific group of customers on a non-discriminatory basis (Art. 4(2)). This covers, for example, country-specific online interfaces, targeted offers to particular Member States or customer groups, general conditions that differ for a reason unrelated to nationality, residence or establishment such as membership of an association or contributions made to the trader, and different conditions, including different prices, offered on a non-discriminatory basis at different points of sale such as a shop and a website, or targeted at a specific territory within a Member State (recital 27). What stays barred in every case is a difference in general conditions of access, including price, driven by the customer's nationality, place of residence or place of establishment.

## What the documents must not say

- A general conditions of access, checkout or terms document must not condition price, payment terms or delivery terms in the three Art. 4(1) situations on the customer's nationality, place of residence or place of establishment, or on a proxy for them such as an IP address, a delivery address, a language setting or the Member State that issued a payment instrument (Art. 4(1); recital 6).
- A checkout or payment-terms document must not apply different payment conditions within the trader's own accepted payment means based on the customer's nationality, residence, establishment, the location of the customer's payment account, the place of establishment of the customer's payment service provider, or the Member State where the customer's payment instrument was issued, once the transaction meets the Art. 5(1) conditions (Art. 5(1)).
- A reseller, distribution or franchise agreement must not contain a term that, in respect of passive sales under Regulation (EU) No 330/2010, obliges a trader to act against the Art. 3 to 5 prohibitions; such a term is automatically void (Art. 6(2)).
- A terms document must not present a legal-requirement block, limit or redirection under Art. 3(3) without the required explanation, and must not give that explanation in a language other than the one of the interface the customer first tried to reach.

## Product behaviour it assumes

- The online interface serves every customer the same content and functionality regardless of detected nationality, residence or establishment, with no geolocation-based blocking or limiting beyond what Art. 3(3) allows.
- Where more than one locally targeted interface version exists, a stored, revocable consent flag per customer governs redirection; the version first requested stays one click away after a consented redirect, and withdrawing consent stops future redirects.
- Checkout logic reads delivery country and collection-point country from the trader's own published delivery and collection terms, not from the customer's nationality or residence, when setting price and delivery conditions in the Art. 4(1)(a) situation.
- Payment processing accepts a payment instrument issued in any Member State, and a payment account or payment service provider located in any Member State, on the same terms as a domestic one, once the transaction is an electronic credit transfer, direct debit or card payment within the same brand and category, strong customer authentication under Directive (EU) 2015/2366 is met, and the currency is one the trader accepts (Art. 5(1)).
- A documented, objective policy exists for any case where the trader withholds delivery or the service pending confirmed payment, or requests advance payment by credit transfer before dispatch for a direct debit transaction, so that practice can be shown to rest on reasons unrelated to nationality, residence or establishment (Art. 5(2); recital 33).
- Where the trader surcharges a card-based payment instrument for which interchange fees are unregulated under Chapter II of Regulation (EU) 2015/751, or for a payment service outside Regulation (EU) No 260/2012, the surcharge is capped at the trader's direct cost of accepting that instrument, unless the Member State where the trader operates has itself barred or limited such surcharges under Art. 62(5) of Directive (EU) 2015/2366 (Art. 5(3)).

## Prescribed wording

This file cites no prescribed wording of its own. The Regulation fixes no button label or model form for a trader to reproduce; the defined terms quoted above sit in context with their citation.

## Dates

- 3 December 2018: date from which the Regulation applied (Art. 11(1), second subparagraph); it entered into force on the twentieth day after its Official Journal publication (Art. 11(1), first subparagraph).
- 23 March 2020: date from which Art. 6 (the passive-sales void rule) applied to agreements concluded before 2 March 2018 that already complied with Art. 101 TFEU and with equivalent national competition rules (Art. 11(2)).
- By 23 March 2020 and every five years after, the Commission reports to the European Parliament, the Council and the European Economic and Social Committee on the Regulation's evaluation, covering its impact on the internal market and cross-border e-commerce and any resulting administrative or financial burden on traders (Art. 9(1)). The first such evaluation also assesses whether the scope should extend to electronically supplied services whose main feature is access to copyright-protected works, where the trader holds the rights needed for the relevant territories (Art. 9(2)).
- 2026-10-20: law-as-of date for this file.

## Open points to raise

- Whether the trader's goods or services fall inside the Art. 4(1)(b) carve-out for electronically supplied services whose main feature is access to copyright-protected works, since that carve-out is itself under review by the Commission and its outcome is not settled by this Regulation (Art. 9(2)).
- Whether a Union or Member State legal requirement applies to the trader's activities in a way that would justify blocking, limiting or redirecting access under Art. 3(3), and if so, what explanation the interface gives for it.
- Whether the trader sells books subject to a Member State's resale-price-maintenance law consistent with Union law, since that law can justify territorial price differences the Art. 4(1) prohibition would otherwise bar (Art. 4(5), second subparagraph).
- Whether the trader is exempt from VAT under Chapter 1 of Title XII of Directive 2006/112/EC, since that status carves the trader out of the Art. 4(1)(b) electronic-services prohibition specifically (Art. 4(4)).

# Children's Online Privacy Protection Act and Rule (COPPA)

Citation: 15 U.S.C. 6501-6506 (Act); 16 C.F.R. Part 312 (Rule), as amended by 90 FR 16918 (2025)
Version: Act current through Pub. L. 119-121 (2026-10-09); Rule current as codified in the eCFR as of 2026-10-16
Official text: https://uscode.house.gov/browse/prelim@title15&edition=prelim
Law as of: 2026-10-20

This file covers the whole Act (§§ 6501 to 6506) and the whole Rule (§§ 312.1 to 312.13) as it reads
after the Commission's 2025 amendments, which took effect June 23, 2025 with a general compliance
date of April 22, 2026 (one year after the amendments were published). It does not reproduce FTC
guidance documents or enforcement actions; a drafter needing those should consult them directly.

## When it applies

- The Rule binds an "operator": a person running a commercial website or online service, on the
  internet, that collects or maintains personal information from or about its users or visitors, or
  on whose behalf such information is collected or maintained, involving interstate, territorial, DC,
  or foreign commerce (15 U.S.C. § 6501(2); 16 C.F.R. § 312.2 "Operator"). Personal information is
  collected or maintained on an operator's behalf when an agent or service provider collects it, or
  when the operator benefits from letting another person collect it directly from its users (§ 312.2
  "Operator"). A nonprofit otherwise exempt under 15 U.S.C. § 45 is not an operator (15 U.S.C. §
  6501(2)(B)).
- The duties attach in either of two situations: the product is a "website or online service directed
  to children," or the operator has actual knowledge it is collecting personal information from a
  child regardless of the product's own content (16 C.F.R. § 312.3). Actual knowledge of collecting
  directly from users of a known children's site forces coverage on that basis alone (§ 312.2
  "Website or online service directed to children", para. (2)).
- Whether a product is directed to children is decided by a multi-factor test: subject matter, visual content, animated
  characters or child-oriented activities and incentives, music or audio content, model age, child
  celebrities or celebrities who appeal to children, language, whether advertising on the product is
  directed to children, and competent empirical evidence of audience composition and intended
  audience (§ 312.2 "Website or online service directed to children", para. (1)). The test applies to
  the whole product or to one portion of it, so a general-audience product with a children's area can
  treat only that area as directed to children. A product is not directed to children solely for
  linking to a children's site through a directory, index, reference, pointer, or hypertext link
  (15 U.S.C. § 6501(10)(B); 16 C.F.R. § 312.2 "Website or online service directed to children", para.
  (4)).
- A "mixed audience" product is one that meets the directed-to-children factors but does not target
  children as its primary audience. It may run a neutral age screen before collecting personal
  information (beyond the narrow § 312.5(c) exception purposes), provided the screen does not default
  to a set age and does not encourage a false answer (§ 312.2 "Mixed audience website or online
  service"). A mixed-audience product is not deemed directed to children with respect to any visitor
  not identified as under 13 (§ 312.2 "Website or online service directed to children", para. (3)).
- "Child" means an individual under 13 (15 U.S.C. § 6501(1); 16 C.F.R. § 312.2 "Child"). "Parent"
  includes a legal guardian (15 U.S.C. § 6501(7); 16 C.F.R. § 312.2 "Parent").
- "Personal information" reaches eleven categories: name, physical address, online contact
  information, a screen or user name that functions as online contact information, telephone number,
  a government-issued identifier (Social Security, state ID, birth certificate, or passport number,
  among others), a persistent identifier usable to recognize a user over time and across sites or
  services (a cookie-held customer number, IP address, processor or device serial number, or unique
  device identifier), a photo, video, or audio file containing a child's image or voice, street-level
  geolocation, a biometric identifier usable for automated or semi-automated recognition (fingerprints,
  handprints, retina or iris patterns, genetic data, voiceprints, gait patterns, facial templates, or
  faceprints), and information about the child or the child's parents that the operator collects
  online from the child and combines with any of the foregoing (16 C.F.R. § 312.2 "Personal
  information"). A persistent identifier and a government-issued identifier beyond a Social Security
  number were both added by the 2025 amendments and are not limited to the named examples (90 FR
  16918).

## What the documents must say

- Direct notice to the parent must precede any collection, use, or disclosure of a child's personal
  information, and its content depends on the purpose: general consent-seeking notice (16 C.F.R. §
  312.4(c)(1)), voluntary participation notice where no other information is collected (§
  312.4(c)(2)), notice of intent to communicate with the child multiple times (§ 312.4(c)(3)), or
  child-safety notice (§ 312.4(c)(4)). Each variant must include a hyperlink to the online notice
  required under § 312.4(d) (§ 312.4(c)(1)(v), (c)(2)(iv), (c)(3)(vi), (c)(4)(v)). A posted online
  link does not substitute for direct notice where consent is being sought (§ 312.4(a)-(b)).
- The general consent-seeking direct notice must state that the parent's consent is required and that
  the operator will not collect, use, or disclose the child's information without it; the specific
  items of information to be collected and how they will be used; the identities or specific
  categories of any third-party recipients (including the public, if the information is made publicly
  available) and the purpose of each disclosure; and that the parent can consent to collection and use
  without consenting to disclosure, except where the disclosure is integral to the website or online
  service (16 C.F.R. § 312.4(c)(1)(ii) to (iv)). Naming third-party recipients by name or category in
  this notice, rather than a general "third parties" reference, was added by the 2025 amendments (90 FR
  16918).
- A posted online notice must sit on the home or landing page and at every point personal information
  is collected from children, in close proximity to the information request at each such point (16
  C.F.R. § 312.4(d)). A general-audience product with a separate children's area posts its own link on
  that area's home or landing page (§ 312.4(d)). The online notice must list every operator collecting
  or maintaining the information (or one designated contact operator, provided every collecting
  operator is still named), what is collected and why, disclosure practices including third-party
  recipients by name or category and the purpose of each disclosure, and the written data retention
  policy required under § 312.10 (§ 312.4(d)(1) to (2)). Where the operator collects a persistent
  identifier under the internal-operations exception, the notice must state the specific internal
  operations the identifier supports and how the operator ensures it is not used or disclosed to
  contact a specific individual, including through behavioral advertising, to amass a profile on a
  specific individual, or for any other purpose (§ 312.4(d)(3)). Where the operator collects an audio
  file under the audio-file exception, the notice must describe how the file is used and state that it
  is deleted immediately after the operator responds to the request (§ 312.4(d)(4)).
- An operator must obtain verifiable parental consent before collecting, using, or disclosing a
  child's personal information, including before any material change to collection, use, or
  disclosure practices the parent already consented to (16 C.F.R. § 312.5(a)(1)). The parent must be
  given the option to consent to collection and use without consenting to third-party disclosure,
  unless the disclosure is integral to the website or online service; where that option applies, the
  operator must obtain separate verifiable parental consent to the disclosure itself (§ 312.5(a)(2)).
  A disclosure to a third party is integral when it is necessary to provide the product or service the
  consumer is asking for; a disclosure for monetary or other consideration, for advertising, or to
  train or otherwise develop artificial intelligence technologies is not integral and needs this
  separate consent (90 FR 16918, discussing § 312.5(a)(2)). The separate consent can be sought during
  the initial consent flow or later, at the point the feature implicating the disclosure is used, but
  it must be freely given, informed, specific, and obtained through an affirmative action distinct from
  the collection-and-use consent (90 FR 16918).
- A parent, on request, has a standing right to a description of the categories of personal
  information collected from the child, an ongoing opportunity to refuse further use, retrievable
  storage, or future collection of that information and to have it deleted, and a means of reviewing
  the information collected, so long as the operator can confirm the requestor is the child's parent
  and the means is not unduly burdensome to the parent (16 C.F.R. § 312.6(a)). Subject to the
  over-conditioning rule below, the operator may terminate service to a child whose parent exercised
  this refusal or deletion right (§ 312.6(c)).
- A written, public data retention policy must state the purposes for which children's personal
  information is collected, the business need for retaining each category, and a timeframe for
  deletion, and this policy is itself a required element of the § 312.4(d) online notice (16 C.F.R. §
  312.10). A timeframe tied to a defined triggering event, such as last use or subscription end, is
  permitted if a business need supports it and the policy discloses the trigger (90 FR 16918).
- The operator must establish and maintain a written information security program containing
  safeguards appropriate to the sensitivity of children's personal information and to the operator's
  size, complexity, and activities (16 C.F.R. § 312.8(b)). One general security program covering
  children's and other data satisfies this requirement (90 FR 16918).
- A safe harbor program approved under 16 C.F.R. § 312.11 lets its member operators be deemed
  compliant with §§ 312.2 through 312.8 and 312.10 (§ 312.11(h)). A document relying on safe harbor
  membership should name the specific certified website or online service, since the program's public
  posting duty is scoped to the service actually certified, not a blanket company-wide claim (§
  312.11(d)(4)).

## What the documents must not say

- No clause may condition a child's participation in a game, the offering of a prize, or another
  activity on the child disclosing more personal information than is reasonably necessary to
  participate in that activity (16 C.F.R. § 312.7). This ceiling applies even where the operator has
  obtained consent for the broader collection; consent does not cure an over-conditioning violation
  (90 FR 16918). The Rule does not categorically bar consented behavioral advertising to children, but
  such advertising must be off absent the parent's separate consent under § 312.5(a)(2) and access
  cannot be conditioned on giving that consent (90 FR 16918).
- No clause may claim that personal information collected for support of the website's or online
  service's internal operations (maintaining or analyzing the service's functioning, network
  communications, authenticating or personalizing content, serving or capping contextual advertising,
  protecting security or integrity, legal or regulatory compliance, or fulfilling a child's own
  request) may also be used or disclosed to contact a specific individual, including through
  behavioral advertising, to amass a profile on a specific individual, or for any other purpose (16
  C.F.R. § 312.2 "Support for the internal operations of the website or online service", paras.
  (1)-(2)).
- No clause may claim that information collected under a § 312.5(c) no-consent exception may be used
  beyond that exception's stated purpose. Each of the nine exceptions is purpose-bound: information
  collected solely to seek consent or give notice must be deleted if consent is not obtained within a
  reasonable time (§ 312.5(c)(1)); information collected to respond once to a child's own request
  must not be used to recontact the child and must be deleted promptly after the response (§
  312.5(c)(3)); information collected to respond repeatedly to one ongoing request must not be used
  beyond that request's scope and is gated by parental notice (§ 312.5(c)(4)); a child's name and
  contact information collected for safety purposes must not be used or disclosed for any purpose
  unrelated to the child's safety and must not be disclosed on the site (§ 312.5(c)(5)); information
  collected for site security, liability precautions, judicial process, or a lawful law-enforcement or
  public-safety disclosure must not be used for any other purpose (§ 312.5(c)(6)); a persistent
  identifier collected solely to support internal operations must not be used for any other purpose (§
  312.5(c)(7)); and an audio file collected to respond to a child's request must not be used for any
  other purpose, must not be disclosed, and must be deleted immediately after the response (§
  312.5(c)(9)).
- No clause may describe indefinite retention of children's personal information, or a retention
  practice without a stated purpose and timeframe, as compliant: § 312.10 bars indefinite retention
  outright regardless of how a policy is worded.
- No clause may rely on a parental consent method the Rule does not enumerate, except one a safe
  harbor program has approved for its own members under § 312.5(b)(3), or one the Commission has
  approved on request under § 312.12(a); a method outside those channels does not satisfy § 312.5(b)(1)
  on its own.
- The "email plus" and "text plus" consent methods (16 C.F.R. § 312.5(b)(2)(viii)-(ix)) are available
  only to an operator that does not disclose children's personal information to third parties; a
  document describing either method for a service that does disclose misstates the Rule.

## Product behaviour it assumes

- The signup or data-collection flow runs a "directed to children" classification per portion of the
  product (not one whole-product flag), checks for actual knowledge of collecting from a known
  children's site, and, for a mixed-audience product, runs a neutral age screen before collecting any
  personal information beyond the § 312.5(c) exception purposes.
- The parental consent flow matches its verification method to whether the product discloses
  children's data to third parties: email-plus or text-plus only where it does not disclose; one of
  the heavier methods (signed form, payment-system transaction, trained-staff call or video session,
  ID-database check, child-resistant knowledge-based authentication, or ID-plus-facial-recognition
  match) otherwise. Several of the heavier methods require prompt deletion of the verification artifact
  itself once the match or check is confirmed (16 C.F.R. § 312.5(b)(2)(v), (vii)).
- Where the product seeks separate consent for non-integral third-party disclosure, that consent step
  is built as a distinct, affirmative action from the collection-and-use consent, free of manipulative
  or deceptive choice architecture, and not a condition of accessing the product.
- The parent-facing account or support tooling provides, on request and after confirming the requestor
  is the child's parent: the categories of information collected, an ongoing opt-out and deletion
  control, and a means to review the collected information that is not unduly burdensome to the
  parent.
- The data retention system enforces a deletion timeframe tied to each collection purpose, rather than
  keeping children's data by default, and that timeframe (and its trigger, if event-based) matches what
  the published retention policy states.
- The information security program is a single written program (it need not be children-specific) with
  a designated coordinator, a risk assessment repeated at least annually, safeguards sized to the
  volume and sensitivity of the data at risk, regular testing and monitoring, and an at-least-annual
  reevaluation (16 C.F.R. § 312.8(b)(1) to (5)). Before another operator, service provider, or third
  party collects or maintains children's data on the product's behalf, or before the product releases
  such data to one, the vendor-onboarding process checks that entity's capability to maintain
  confidentiality, security, and integrity and obtains its written assurance to do so (§ 312.8(c)).
- Advertising tooling keeps targeted or behavioral advertising to children off by default and enables
  it only after the parent's separate, affirmative consent under § 312.5(a)(2), without conditioning
  product access on that consent.

## Prescribed wording

None. The Act and Rule require specific notice content and consent-method
elements (see above) but do not prescribe exact phrases a drafter must quote verbatim.

## Dates

- The Act was enacted October 21, 1998 (Pub. L. 105-277, div. C, title XIII). The duties under §§
  6502(a), 6504, and 6505 took effect on the later of April 21, 2000 (18 months after enactment) or the
  date the Commission ruled on the first safe harbor application, but no later than April 21, 2001 (30
  months after enactment) (15 U.S.C. § 6501, note).
- The 2025 Rule amendments were published April 22, 2025 (90 FR 16918) and took effect June 23, 2025,
  60 days after publication (90 FR 16918). The general compliance date for most of the amended
  requirements is April 22, 2026, one year after publication (90 FR 16918); during that one-year
  window an operator may follow either the pre-2025 or the amended version of a given provision (90 FR
  16918). A drafter should still treat each document as following one version consistently rather than
  mixing the two.
- Three safe harbor program duties carry their own dates, each computed from the April 22, 2025
  publication date rather than from the general one-year compliance date: the public-posting duty
  under § 312.11(d)(4) by July 21, 2025 (16 C.F.R. § 312.11(d)(4)); the annual-report and
  guideline-modification duties under § 312.11(d)(1) and (g) by October 22, 2025 (§ 312.11(d)(1),
  (g)); and the new triennial technology report under § 312.11(f) by April 22, 2028 and every three
  years after that (§ 312.11(f)).
- The Commission must review the Act's implementation and report to Congress within five years of the
  initial effective date of the regulations issued under § 6502 (15 U.S.C. § 6506).

## Open points to raise

- Whether a specific product's content, audience evidence, and advertising mix cross the § 312.2
  "directed to children" multi-factor threshold is a fact-specific call the Commission makes case by
  case; a drafter cannot settle it from the rule text alone and should document the factors considered
  for the specific product.
- Whether a specific third-party disclosure counts as "integral to the website or online service," for
  purposes of the separate-consent requirement in § 312.5(a)(2), turns on whether the disclosure is
  functionally necessary to the product or feature the user is asking for; the Commission confirmed
  disclosures for consideration, advertising, or AI training or development are never integral, but
  left the integral/non-integral line for other disclosures to be drawn case by case rather than by a
  fixed list (90 FR 16918).
- Whether a given mixed-audience age screen is "neutral" enough, and whether a given deletion timeframe
  in a retention policy is tied to a sufficient business need, are both implementation questions that
  depend on the specific flow and cannot be settled from the rule text alone.

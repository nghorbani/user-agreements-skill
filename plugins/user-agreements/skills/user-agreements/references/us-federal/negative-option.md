# Negative option marketing: ROSCA and the Negative Option Rule

Citation: 15 U.S.C. § 8401 to § 8405 (ROSCA); 16 C.F.R. Part 425 (Negative Option Rule)
Version: ROSCA current through Pub. L. 119-121 (2026-10-09); 16 C.F.R. Part 425 as restored by 91 FR 6507 (Feb. 12, 2026), the pre-2024 text
Official text: https://uscode.house.gov/browse/prelim@title15&edition=prelim
Law as of: 2026-10-20

A negative option is a term under which a seller treats a customer's silence, or a failure to act,
as acceptance of an offer or as consent to keep paying for one. Four patterns recur: prenotification
plans (a seller periodically announces a selection and ships and bills for it unless the subscriber
declines by a stated date), continuity plans (periodic shipments or services the subscriber agreed
to in advance, continuing until cancelled), automatic renewals (a subscription that renews and
charges automatically at expiry unless the subscriber cancels), and free-to-pay or
nominal-fee-to-pay conversions (a trial that converts to a paid charge unless the subscriber cancels
or returns the goods).

No single federal rule reaches all four patterns. 16 C.F.R. Part 425 covers only prenotification
plans for goods and merchandise. The Restore Online Shoppers Confidence Act (ROSCA) reaches any
negative option feature, of any of the four patterns, but only where the transaction is effected on
the internet. Outside internet sales and outside prenotification plans, the general unfair-or-deceptive
standard of FTC Act section 5 is what applies, read in light of the FTC's own enforcement guidance.
A 2024 rule that would have extended disclosure, consent and cancellation duties to every negative
option pattern in every medium was vacated by a federal court of appeals on a procedural ground
before most of it took effect, and the pre-2024 Part 425 text is what currently binds. A 2026
advance notice of proposed rulemaking is asking whether to bring some or all of the vacated rule
back; nothing in it is a present duty.

## When it applies

- ROSCA's general negative option provision applies to any person who charges or attempts to charge
  a consumer for goods or services sold in a transaction effected on the internet through a negative
  option feature, as the FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule defines that term in 16 C.F.R. Part 310; ROSCA
  does not define the term itself, it borrows it by cross-reference (§ 8403).
- ROSCA separately regulates a narrower scenario: a post-transaction third party seller is a person
  who sells or offers goods or services on the internet, who solicits that purchase through an
  initial merchant after the consumer has already started a transaction with that merchant, and who
  is not the initial merchant, a subsidiary or corporate affiliate of it, or a successor of either
  (§ 8402(d)(2)). The initial merchant is the person who obtained the consumer's billing information
  directly from the consumer through a consumer-initiated internet transaction (§ 8402(d)(1)). This
  post-transaction duty and the general § 8403 duty are independent requirements: a seller who fits
  the post-transaction scenario has to clear both, and meeting one says nothing about the other.
- 16 C.F.R. Part 425, as restored, applies to a seller using a negative option plan in connection
  with the sale, offering for sale, or distribution of goods and merchandise in or affecting commerce
  as the FTC Act defines commerce (§ 425.1(a), § 425.1(b)). A negative option plan under this rule is
  a contractual plan where a seller periodically sends an announcement of merchandise it proposes to
  send, and the subscriber receives and is billed for it unless the subscriber instructs the seller,
  by a stated date or time, not to send it; annual supplements to merchandise already acquired are
  excluded (§ 425.1(c)(1)). The rule's mailing, announcement and crediting mechanics are built for a
  physical selection travelling by mail. A negative option plan for a pure digital service with no
  merchandise ever shipped, such as a software or streaming subscription, is not what this rule's
  machinery is written for, even where it otherwise fits the plan definition; such a plan sits outside
  Part 425's current scope and is governed instead by FTC Act section 5 generally, by ROSCA where the
  sale is on the internet, and by any applicable state auto-renewal statute.
- The rule's crediting, cancellation and shipping duties in § 425.1(b) apply to the same goods-and-
  merchandise scope as § 425.1(a); continuity plans, automatic renewals and free-to-pay conversions
  are outside both subsections of the current rule.
- A seller offering a negative option feature by telephone also falls under the Telemarketing
  Sales Rule, 16 C.F.R. Part 310, which requires disclosure of the feature's material terms and bars
  certain payment methods associated with deceptive marketing; that rule is not reproduced in this
  file.
- FTC Act section 5 applies to negative option marketing generally, in any medium and any pattern,
  as the backstop where ROSCA, the Telemarketing Sales Rule and Part 425 do not reach: 15 U.S.C.
  § 45(a) declares unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce unlawful.

## What the documents must say

- Under ROSCA's general negative option provision, before obtaining the consumer's billing
  information the seller must provide text that clearly and conspicuously discloses all material
  transaction terms, must obtain the consumer's express informed consent before charging the
  consumer's credit card, debit card, bank account or other financial account, and must provide
  simple mechanisms for the consumer to stop recurring charges (§ 8403(1) to (3)). These three
  conditions are conjunctive: meeting two does not excuse the third.
- Under ROSCA's post-transaction provision, before obtaining the consumer's billing information the
  post-transaction third party seller must clearly and conspicuously disclose a description of the
  goods or services offered, the fact that it is not affiliated with the initial merchant (which may
  be shown by presenting its own name in a way that clearly differentiates it from the initial
  merchant), and the cost (§ 8402(a)(1)(A) to (C)). It must then obtain the consumer's express
  informed consent by collecting, directly from the consumer, the full account number to be charged
  plus the consumer's name, address and a contact method, and by requiring a distinct affirmative
  act, such as clicking a confirmation button or checking a consent box, tied to the disclosed charge
  amount (§ 8402(a)(2)(A), (B)). Disclosure and consent are both required; neither substitutes for
  the other.
- An initial merchant's own checkout and billing systems must not disclose a customer's card number,
  bank account number, or other billing information used to charge that customer, to any
  post-transaction third party seller for an internet sale, regardless of what the receiving third
  party does with the information or whether it separately complies with § 8402(a) (§ 8402(b)).
- Under 16 C.F.R. § 425.1(a)(1), promotional material for a prenotification plan must clearly and
  conspicuously disclose seven items before a prospective subscriber enrolls: the opt-out mechanism
  by which the subscriber tells the seller it does not want the selection; any minimum-purchase
  obligation; the contract-complete subscriber's right to cancel membership at any time; whether
  billing includes postage and handling; that the subscriber will have at least ten days to mail a
  rejection form; the seller's guarantee to credit a returned selection and to guarantee return
  postage, to the Postal Service or to the subscriber, when the announcement and form arrive too
  late for that ten-day window; and the frequency and twelve-month cap on announcements sent to the
  subscriber.
- Before shipping any selection, the seller must mail an announcement identifying the selection and
  a form, in or with the announcement, disclosing that the selection will ship unless the subscriber
  instructs otherwise, with a procedure for giving that instruction and either a return date or a
  mailing date stated (§ 425.1(a)(2)). The mailing must be timed under one of three schedules, each
  of which must still leave the subscriber at least ten days to mail the form back: at least twenty
  days before a stated return date, at least fifteen days before a stated mailing date, or a mailing
  date at least ten days after the subscriber's receipt (§ 425.1(a)(3)).
- The seller must credit the full invoiced amount and guarantee return postage, to the Postal Service
  or to the subscriber, in four situations: the opt-out form reached the seller by the return date,
  or was mailed by the mailing date; the form reached the seller after the return date but was mailed
  and postmarked at least three days before it; a contract-complete subscriber's written cancellation
  notice was already on file before shipment, limited to the first shipment after that notice (every
  later shipment is unordered merchandise under section 3009 of the Postal Reorganization Act of
  1970, as the FTC adopted it in its public notice of September 11, 1970); or the announcement and
  form arrived too late to leave at least ten days to respond (§ 425.1(b)(1)(i) to (iv)). The seller
  must also affirmatively notify any subscriber it knows falls into one of those four situations that
  the subscriber may return the selection, with postage guaranteed, for credit (§ 425.1(b)(2)); this
  notice duty is separate from honoring a credit once claimed.
- The seller must ship introductory and bonus merchandise within four weeks of receiving an order,
  unless it cannot deliver the original offer because of unanticipated circumstances beyond its
  control and it promptly offers a reasonably equivalent alternative; if the subscriber declines that
  alternative and instead wants to cancel because the original item never arrived, the seller must
  grant the cancellation once the subscriber returns whatever introductory merchandise already
  shipped (§ 425.1(b)(3)).
- The seller must terminate promptly the membership of a properly identified contract-complete
  subscriber on written request (§ 425.1(b)(4)).
- A terms document that uses the rule's defined terms should track them precisely, since
  "contract-complete subscriber" status (a subscriber who has purchased the plan's required minimum
  quantity, § 425.1(c)(3)) gates both the anytime-cancellation right and the first-shipment crediting
  rule; stating the exact minimum quantity that completes the contract avoids ambiguity in both
  rights at once.
- Beyond the vacated and restored rule, the FTC's 2021 enforcement guidance states the practical
  disclosure standard the Commission applies under section 5 and ROSCA today: disclose, clearly and
  conspicuously, the existence of the negative option feature, that the consumer will be charged (or
  that charges will increase or recur) unless the consumer timely acts, every deadline to act, the
  amount or range and frequency of charges, the date each charge will be submitted, and all
  information needed to cancel. A disclosure tied to the negative option feature should sit
  immediately next to the control that records consent; any other material disclosure should appear
  before the consumer decides to buy. In an interactive electronic medium the disclosure must be
  unavoidable: a consumer should not have to click a link or hover over an icon to see it.
- The same 2021 guidance states the practical consent standard: obtain the consumer's acceptance of
  the negative option feature separately from the rest of the transaction, obtain unambiguously
  affirmative consent to both the feature and the whole transaction, keep nothing in the flow that
  undermines the consumer's ability to give that consent, and be able to verify that consent was
  given. A pre-checked box does not amount to affirmative consent: the box a consumer uses to record
  consent to the negative option feature must start unchecked, with the consumer's own click or tap
  the act that checks it. The billing entity's name should be clearly disclosed.
- The same guidance states the practical cancellation standard: a mechanism at least as easy to use
  as the sign-up method, offered through at least the same medium used to sign up. Where a phone line
  is offered for cancellation, it should be staffed during normal business hours, answer promptly,
  and keep the call no more burdensome than the sign-up call.

## What the documents must not say

- ROSCA creates no private right of action for a consumer. Enforcement runs through the FTC, treating
  a violation as a violation of an FTC Act rule on unfair or deceptive acts or practices with the same
  powers and penalties as under the FTC Act (§ 8404(a), (b)), and through a State attorney general or
  other authorized State officer, who may sue in federal district court for injunctive relief for
  State residents after giving the FTC prior written notice, or immediate notice where prior notice is
  not feasible (§ 8405(a), (b)). The FTC may intervene in a State's suit and appeal a decision in it
  (§ 8405(c)). A State may not bring a separate suit over the same alleged violation while an FTC or
  federal action on it is already pending (§ 8405(e)). A document must not represent that a consumer
  can sue directly under ROSCA.
- A terms document must not condition the § 425.1(b)(1) full-invoice credit on anything beyond the
  four listed situations: no restocking fee, no partial credit, no added condition narrowing the
  right below what those four situations guarantee.
- A terms document must not make continued billing, or an additional purchase, a precondition for
  processing a contract-complete subscriber's written cancellation request.
- A plan must not ship substituted merchandise for what the subscriber ordered without the
  subscriber's express consent obtained before shipment; a missed opt-out deadline or a subscriber's
  silence is not consent to a substitution, and is a distinct concept from the opt-out that defines
  the negative option plan itself (§ 425.1(b)(5)).
- A document must not represent current compliance with 16 C.F.R. Part 425 in the sense of the 2024
  amendment (the misrepresentation ban at former § 425.3, the universal clear-and-conspicuous
  disclosure duty at former § 425.4, the separate affirmative-consent duty at former § 425.5, or the
  equal-dignities cancellation duty at former § 425.6). That 2024 amendment was vacated in full by the
  United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit (Custom Commc'ns, Inc. v. FTC, No. 24-3137
  (8th Cir. July 8, 2025) (per curiam), consolidated with Nos. 24-3388, 24-3415, 24-3442, 24-3469);
  the Federal Trade Commission then recodified 16 C.F.R. Part 425 to the pre-2024 text effective
  February 12, 2026, with no transition or grace period for a seller already operating 2024-amendment
  flows (91 FR 6507). Citing the 2024 amendment's specific mechanics as a current
  compliance target misstates binding federal rule-level law for a continuity, automatic-renewal or
  free-to-pay-conversion plan; the correct statement is that no rule-level federal requirement beyond
  the restored prenotification text currently governs those plans, subject to ongoing exposure under
  section 5, ROSCA and any applicable state statute.
- A document must not represent that the vacatur removes all federal exposure for a continuity,
  automatic-renewal or free-to-pay-conversion plan. The Eighth Circuit vacated the 2024 amendment on
  one procedural ground alone, that the Commission never issued the preliminary regulatory analysis
  required once a rule amendment is found to cross a $100,000,000 annual economic-effect threshold
  (15 U.S.C. § 57b-3(a)(1)(A), (b)(1)), and the court expressly declined to decide whether the
  amendment also failed the specificity and prevalence requirements of 15 U.S.C. § 57a(a)(1)(B),
  (b)(3), or was arbitrary and capricious under the Administrative Procedure Act. The court's own
  description of the record states that section 5 and ROSCA exposure for the practices the vacated
  rule targeted remains untouched by the judgment.
- A document must not describe a pending petition, comment period or rulemaking question from the
  2026 advance notice of proposed rulemaking as a present legal duty. The Commission has not decided
  whether to retain the current rule, adopt some or all of the vacated rule's four requirements,
  adopt some other provision, or pursue a non-regulatory alternative; comments on that notice closed
  April 13, 2026, and nothing in it binds a seller as of this file's date.
- A document must not represent that offering a discount or other incentive to remain enrolled
  (a "Save") before processing a cancellation is settled lawful or unlawful practice. The Commission
  has asked, without deciding, whether offering a Save instead of promptly honoring a cancellation
  request is itself unfair or deceptive, and has solicited data on how often consumers accept a Save,
  how much longer cancellation takes when a Save is declined, and the effect of state laws regulating
  Save attempts. The FTC's 2021 guidance states only that a single request to consider an offer or
  discount is not itself an unreasonable delay, while multiple requests to hear further offers,
  lengthy pitches, or ignoring a consumer's request to decline further offers can amount to one.

## Product behaviour it assumes

- A checkout or enrollment flow for any internet-sold negative option feature discloses all material
  transaction terms, in text, before it collects billing information, and separately captures an
  express, informed affirmative act as consent before the first charge, regardless of the plan's
  pattern (prenotification, continuity, automatic renewal, or free-to-pay conversion), so that it
  satisfies ROSCA's § 8403 test independent of whatever Part 425 covers. Where that affirmative act is
  a checkbox, the box renders unchecked by default; the seller never pre-checks it for the consumer.
- A cancellation path exists and is wired to actually stop recurring charges once invoked, built to
  be at least as easy to use as the sign-up flow and reachable through the same medium used to sign
  up; ROSCA's "simple mechanisms" standard is tested by whether cancellation actually works, and
  the mere presence of a cancellation link or number does not settle that question.
- Where the product is a prenotification plan shipping physical goods or merchandise, the system
  tracks each subscriber's contract-complete status against a stated minimum-purchase quantity, since
  that status gates both the anytime-cancellation right and the first-shipment crediting rule, and
  generates the announcement-and-form mailing on a schedule that leaves at least ten days to respond
  in every case, rather than as an average across cases.
- The same prenotification system flags, for staff review, every subscriber whose opt-out form
  arrived late through no apparent fault of the subscriber (postmarked at least three days before the
  return date, or an announcement that left fewer than ten days to respond), so the required notice
  of credit and postage rights under § 425.1(b)(2) goes out without the subscriber having to ask.
- A post-transaction offer flow, where the seller is a post-transaction third party seller under
  ROSCA's definition, is built so that the non-affiliation disclosure, the goods-or-services
  description, and the cost are shown before billing information is requested, and the consent step
  is a distinct, logged affirmative act tied to the exact disclosed amount, separate from the capture
  of the account number, name, address and contact method.
- An initial merchant's systems that hand a consumer off to a post-transaction third party offer do
  not pass that consumer's card number, bank account number, or other billing information used to
  charge that consumer, to the third party; the handoff is built so the third party collects its own
  billing information directly from the consumer.
- A subscription-management or billing system records, for each negative option enrollment, the
  medium of sign-up (online, phone, in person) and offers cancellation through at least that same
  medium, so a compliance review can confirm cancellation is not harder than sign-up without manual
  reconstruction.
- Any flow that inserts a retention offer or discount pitch before completing a cancellation request
  logs how many such offers were presented and whether the consumer explicitly asked to decline
  further offers, so that a repeated-pitch pattern (as opposed to a single offer) can be identified
  and is not built as a default multi-step gate in front of cancellation.

## Prescribed wording

Neither ROSCA nor 16 C.F.R. Part 425 fixes a sentence a seller's own materials must reproduce
verbatim; both require disclosure, consent and a stop-charges or cancellation mechanism in substance,
checked against the operative text below, not copied into consumer-facing wording (§ 8402(a),
§ 8403; § 425.1(a), (b)).

The restored rule's operative disclosure and procedure text, § 425.1(a) through (c), reads:

> (a) In connection with the sale, offering for sale, or distribution of goods and merchandise in or
> affecting commerce, as "commerce" is defined in the Federal Trade Commission Act, it is an unfair
> or deceptive act or practice, for a seller in connection with the use of any negative option plan to
> fail to comply with the following requirements:
>
> (1) Promotional material shall clearly and conspicuously disclose the material terms of the plan,
> including:
>
> (i) That aspect of the plan under which the subscriber must notify the seller, in the manner
> provided for by the seller, if he does not wish to purchase the selection;
>
> (ii) Any obligation assumed by the subscriber to purchase a minimum quantity of merchandise;
>
> (iii) The right of a contract-complete subscriber to cancel his membership at any time;
>
> (iv) Whether billing charges will include an amount for postage and handling;
>
> (v) A disclosure indicating that the subscriber will be provided with at least ten (10) days in
> which to mail any form, contained in or accompanying an announcement identifying the selection, to
> the seller;
>
> (vi) A disclosure that the seller will credit the return of any selections sent to a subscriber,
> and guarantee to the Postal Service or the subscriber postage to return such selections to the
> seller when the announcement and form are not received by the subscriber in time to afford him at
> least ten (10) days in which to mail his form to the seller;
>
> (vii) The frequency with which the announcements and forms will be sent to the subscriber and the
> maximum number of announcements and forms which will be sent to him during a 12-month period.

The crediting, notice, shipping and substitution duties, § 425.1(b), read:

> (b) In connection with the sale or distribution of goods and merchandise in or affecting commerce,
> as "commerce" is defined in the Federal Trade Commission Act, it shall constitute an unfair or
> deceptive act or practice for a seller in connection with the use of any negative option plan to: (1)
> Refuse to credit, for the full invoiced amount thereof, the return of any selection sent to a
> subscriber, and to guarantee to the Postal Service or the subscriber postage adequate to return such
> selection to the seller, when: (i) The selection is sent to a subscriber whose form indicating that
> he does not want to receive the selection was received by the seller by the return date or was
> mailed by the subscriber by the mailing date; (ii) Such form is received by the seller after the
> return date, but has been mailed by the subscriber and postmarked at least 3 days prior to the
> return date; (iii) Prior to the date of shipment of such selection, the seller has received from a
> contract-complete subscriber, a written notice of cancellation of membership adequately identifying
> the subscriber; however, this provision is applicable only to the first selection sent to a
> canceling contract-complete subscriber after the seller has received written notice of cancellation.
> After the first selection shipment, all selection shipments thereafter are deemed to be unordered
> merchandise pursuant to section 3009 of the Postal Reorganization Act of 1970, as adopted by the
> Federal Trade Commission in its public notice, dated September 11, 1970; (iv) The announcement and
> form are not received by the subscriber in time to afford him at least ten (10) days in which to
> mail his form. (2) Fail to notify a subscriber known by the seller to be within any of the
> circumstances set forth in paragraphs (b)(1)(i) through (iv) of this section, that if the subscriber
> elects, the subscriber may return the selection with return postage guaranteed and receive a credit
> to his account. (3) Refuse to ship within 4 weeks after receipt of an order merchandise due
> subscribers as introductory and bonus merchandise, unless the seller is unable to deliver the
> merchandise originally offered due to unanticipated circumstances beyond the seller's control and
> promptly makes a reasonably equivalent alternative offer. However, where the subscriber refuses to
> accept alternatively offered introductory merchandise, but instead insists upon termination of his
> membership due to the seller's failure to provide the subscriber with his originally requested
> introductory merchandise, or any portion thereof, the seller must comply with the subscriber's
> request for cancellation of membership, provided the subscriber returns to the seller any
> introductory merchandise which already may have been sent him. (4) Fail to terminate promptly the
> membership of a properly identified contract-complete subscriber upon his written request. (5)
> Ship, without the express consent of the subscriber, substituted merchandise for that ordered by
> the subscriber.

The nine defined terms, § 425.1(c), read:

> (1) Negative option plan refers to a contractual plan or arrangement under which a seller
> periodically sends to subscribers an announcement which identifies merchandise (other than annual
> supplements to previously acquired merchandise) it proposes to send to subscribers to such plan,
> and the subscribers thereafter receive and are billed for the merchandise identified in each such
> announcement, unless by a date or within a time specified by the seller with respect to each such
> announcement the subscribers, in conformity with the provisions of such plan, instruct the seller
> not to send the identified merchandise. (2) Subscriber means any person who has agreed to receive
> the benefits of, and assume the obligations entailed in, membership in any negative option plan and
> whose membership in such negative option plan has been approved and accepted by the seller. (3)
> Contract-complete subscriber refers to a subscriber who has purchased the minimum quantity of
> merchandise required by the terms of membership in a negative option plan. (4) Promotional material
> refers to an advertisement containing or accompanying any device or material which a prospective
> subscriber sends to the seller to request acceptance or enrollment in a negative option plan. (5)
> Selection refers to the merchandise identified by a seller under any negative option plan as the
> merchandise which the subscriber will receive and be billed for, unless by the date, or within the
> period specified by the seller, the subscriber instructs the seller not to send such merchandise.
> (6) Announcement refers to any material sent by a seller using a negative option plan in which the
> selection is identified and offered to subscribers. (7) Form refers to any form which the subscriber
> returns to the seller to instruct the seller not to send the selection. (8) Return date refers to a
> date specified by a seller using a negative option plan as the date by which a form must be received
> by the seller to prevent shipment of the selection. (9) Mailing date refers to the time specified by
> a seller using a negative option plan as the time by or within which a form must be mailed by a
> subscriber to prevent shipment of the selection.

ROSCA incorporates the Telemarketing Sales Rule's own definition of a negative option feature by
cross-reference (§ 8403), rather than defining the term itself. That rule defines the term as a
provision:

> "under which the customer's silence or failure to take an affirmative action to reject goods or
> services or to cancel the agreement is interpreted by the seller as acceptance of the offer"

(16 C.F.R. § 310.2(w), as quoted in the 2026 advance notice of proposed rulemaking and in the FTC's
2021 enforcement guidance). This is the test for whether a given sales term is a negative option
feature at all under ROSCA, not text for a consumer-facing document.

## Dates

- 16 C.F.R. Part 425 first promulgated in 1973 under the FTC Act.
- The 2024 amendment to Part 425 was published November 15, 2024, with an effective date of
  January 14, 2025 and a compliance date of May 14, 2025 for certain parts, later deferred to
  July 14, 2025.
- The United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit vacated the 2024 amendment in its
  entirety on July 8, 2025 (Custom Commc'ns, Inc. v. FTC, No. 24-3137).
- The Federal Trade Commission recodified 16 C.F.R. Part 425 to the pre-2024 text by direct final
  rule, published and effective February 12, 2026 (91 FR 6507), without notice and comment, under the

  the action as a ministerial conformance to the court's judgment, not a new policy choice, and the

  rule from the Code of Federal Regulations, neither of which touches negative option drafting.
- A 2026 advance notice of proposed rulemaking on 16 C.F.R. Part 425 was published March 13, 2026
  (91 FR 12318), with a public comment period that closed April 13, 2026. As of this file's date, the
  Commission has not announced a decision on that notice.
- The text in this file is current through Pub. L. 119-121 (2026-10-09) for ROSCA, and reflects
  16 C.F.R. Part 425 as restored effective February 12, 2026.

## Open points to raise

- Whether, and in what form, the Commission will act on the 2026 advance notice of proposed
  rulemaking, including whether it will re-adopt some or all of the four requirements the 2024
  amendment imposed (the misrepresentation ban, separate recorded consent, disclosure placement next
  to the consent control, and the equal-dignities cancellation mechanism), is open as of this file's
  date; a drafter building for the possibility of a reissued rule should track that proceeding
  directly rather than rely on this file for its outcome.
- Whether offering a discount or other incentive to remain enrolled before processing a cancellation
  request ("Saves") is itself an unfair or deceptive practice is an open question the Commission is
  actively asking about; this file states only the 2021 guidance's narrower single-offer-versus-
  repeated-pitch distinction, which predates and does not resolve the Saves question.
- Whether, and on what terms, the Commission might treat B2B negative option transactions between
  sophisticated business entities differently from consumer transactions is a question the 2026
  notice raises without answering; a drafter working on enterprise or B2B subscription terms should
  not assume any such distinction currently exists in binding federal law.
- Whether the specificity and prevalence challenges to the 2024 amendment, which the Eighth Circuit
  expressly declined to decide, would succeed against a reissued rule is untested; a reissued rule
  that supplies the missing preliminary regulatory analysis could still face those arguments.
- Whether a particular digital-only subscription (no merchandise ever shipped) falls within 16 C.F.R.
  Part 425's goods-and-merchandise scope as restored is not settled by the sources in this file for
  every product type; where a product is not clearly a physical-shipment plan, the safer compliance
  target is ROSCA (for internet sales) and FTC Act section 5 generally, applying the disclosure,
  consent and cancellation principles above.

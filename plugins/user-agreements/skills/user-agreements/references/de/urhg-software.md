# UrhG software-copyright chapter (§§ 69a to 69g)

Citation: Gesetz über Urheberrecht und verwandte Schutzrechte (Urheberrechtsgesetz, UrhG), §§ 69a to 69g, as last amended by Art. 2 G v. 26.5.2026 I Nr. 157
Version: zuletzt geändert durch Art. 2 G v. 26.5.2026 I Nr. 157
Official text: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/
Law as of: 2026-10-20

This file covers the UrhG's dedicated chapter for computer programs: the protection threshold,
default ownership for programs an employee or contractor creates, the four acts reserved to the
rightholder, the user-side exceptions a licence cannot remove, decompilation for interoperability,
the destruction remedy, and the voidness rule for conflicting contract terms. It does not cover the
separate EU-law reading of exhaustion for a permanently licensed copy sold for a one-off fee, which
sits in the EU instrument this chapter transposes.

## When it applies

- A computer program in any form, including the design material produced before the finished code
  (§ 69a Abs. 1 UrhG).
- Protection attaches to every form in which a program is expressed; the ideas and principles
  underlying any element of a program, including those underlying its interfaces, are never
  protected, so a drafter cannot claim copyright over an interface concept, only over its expression
  (§ 69a Abs. 2 UrhG).
- The only eligibility test is that the program is the result of its author's own intellectual
  creation (eigene geistige Schöpfung); no other criterion, in particular no qualitative or
  aesthetic one, may decide whether it qualifies (§ 69a Abs. 3 UrhG).
- The rules for literary works (Sprachwerke) apply to computer programs too, except where this
  chapter states otherwise, and the general author-remuneration and reversion-of-rights provisions
  (§§ 32 to 32g, 36 to 36d, 40a and 41) do not apply to computer programs at all (§ 69a Abs. 4, 5
  UrhG).
- A program an employee creates in carrying out their duties or on the employer's instructions, or
  a program created under a service relationship (Dienstverhältnis) on the same basis: ownership
  then follows § 69b, not the general authorship rules (§ 69b Abs. 1, 2 UrhG).

## What the documents must say

- The rightholder's exclusive right covers four acts, and a licence enumerating restricted acts
  should track these four rather than a different list:
  - permanent or temporary reproduction of the program, in whole or in part, by any means and in
    any form; where loading, displaying, running, transmitting or storing the program itself
    requires reproduction, that act too needs the rightholder's consent, so routine technical use
    can fall inside the reserved act (§ 69c Nr. 1 UrhG);
  - translation, adaptation, arrangement and any other alteration of the program, and reproduction
    of the results, without prejudice to the rights of the person who carries out the alteration
    (§ 69c Nr. 2 UrhG);
  - any form of distribution of the original program or of copies, including rental; once a copy is
    put into circulation by sale with the rightholder's consent inside the European Union or
    another EEA contracting state, the distribution right exhausts for that copy, but the rental
    right never exhausts (§ 69c Nr. 3 UrhG);
  - wired or wireless public communication of the program, including making it available so members
    of the public can access it from a place and at a time they individually choose (§ 69c Nr. 4
    UrhG).
- When an employee creates the program in carrying out their duties or on instruction, or the same
  happens under a service relationship, every economic right (vermögensrechtliche Befugnis) in the
  program vests in the employer alone, unless the parties agreed otherwise; the agreement can
  displace this default in either direction, so a contract stating a different split is as valid as
  one restating the default (§ 69b Abs. 1, 2 UrhG).
- Absent special contractual terms, reproduction and alteration (§ 69c Nr. 1 and 2) need no consent
  when necessary for the program's intended use, including error correction, by anyone entitled to
  use a copy of it; this is the one exception the chapter leaves open to contractual override
  (§ 69d Abs. 1 UrhG).
- A person entitled to use a copy may observe, study or test how the program functions, without the
  rightholder's consent, to work out the ideas and principles underlying any element of it, as long
  as this happens through acts of loading, displaying, running, transmitting or storing that the
  person is entitled to carry out (§ 69d Abs. 3 UrhG).
- Programs may be used for text and data mining under § 44b, drawing on the § 69c Nr. 2 permission
  (§ 69d Abs. 4 UrhG); § 60d (the separate scientific-research text-and-data-mining exception) does
  not apply to computer programs at all (§ 69d Abs. 6 UrhG).
- The teaching-and-instruction exception (§ 60a) applies to programs, but only once all four of its
  own conditions are met together: the use is digital and happens under an educational
  institution's responsibility, on its premises, elsewhere, or in a secured electronic environment;
  the use may also rely on § 69c Nr. 2; the program may be used in full; and the use is justified by
  the purpose of illustrating teaching and instruction (§ 69d Abs. 5 UrhG).
- The cultural-heritage-institution exceptions (§§ 61d to 61f) apply to programs on the basis that
  the program may also be used under § 69c Nr. 2 (§ 69d Abs. 7 UrhG).
- Decompilation, meaning reproducing the code or translating its form under § 69c Nr. 1 and 2, needs
  no consent when indispensable to get the information needed to make an independently created
  program interoperate with other programs, but only once three conditions all hold together: the
  acts are carried out by the licensee or another person entitled to use a copy, or on their behalf
  by someone they authorised; the interoperability information is not already readily available to
  those persons; and the acts stay confined to the parts of the original program necessary for
  interoperability (§ 69e Abs. 1 Nr. 1 to 3 UrhG).
- Information obtained through decompilation may be used only to achieve that interoperability,
  passed to third parties only where necessary for that interoperability, and never used to
  develop, produce or market a program with substantially similar expression, or for any other act
  that infringes copyright (§ 69e Abs. 2 Nr. 1 to 3 UrhG). Both paragraphs must be read so their
  application neither impairs normal exploitation of the work nor unreasonably prejudices the
  rightholder's legitimate interests (§ 69e Abs. 3 UrhG).
- Where it demands destruction, the rightholder may require the owner or possessor to destroy every
  copy that was unlawfully produced, distributed, or intended for unlawful distribution, and the
  same remedy reaches a means whose sole purpose is to facilitate removing or circumventing a
  technical program-protection mechanism, except a means a cultural-heritage institution uses for
  its own statutory permission under § 61d (§ 69f Abs. 1, 2 UrhG). This destruction and
  anti-circumvention remedy does not reach every technical program-protection mechanism: for the
  cases of § 44b (in conjunction with § 69d Abs. 4), § 60a (in conjunction with § 69d Abs. 5),
  § 60e Abs. 1 or 6, and § 60f Abs. 1 or 3, only § 95b applies correspondingly to the technical
  program-protection mechanism instead, so a drafter relying on § 69f Abs. 1, 2 for one of those
  cases is citing the wrong remedy (§ 69f Abs. 3 UrhG).
- This chapter leaves untouched other legal regimes that also apply to computer programs: invention
  protection, semiconductor-topography protection, trademark protection, protection against unfair
  competition including trade and business secrets, and the general law of contract (§ 69g Abs. 1
  UrhG).

## What the documents must not say

- A clause must not purport to forbid a backup copy that is necessary to secure the program's
  future use: such a term is void regardless of the parties' agreement to it (§ 69d Abs. 2 Satz 1,
  § 69g Abs. 2 UrhG).
- A clause must not purport to forbid observing, studying or testing the program through acts the
  licensee is otherwise entitled to perform, to work out the ideas and principles behind it (§ 69d
  Abs. 3, § 69g Abs. 2 UrhG).
- A clause must not purport to block the teaching-and-instruction use once all four of § 69d Abs. 5's
  own conditions are met (§ 69d Abs. 5, § 69g Abs. 2 UrhG), and must not purport to block the
  cultural-heritage-institution uses § 69d Abs. 7 extends to programs (§ 69d Abs. 7, § 69g Abs. 2
  UrhG).
- A clause must not purport to restrict decompilation once § 69e Abs. 1's three conditions are
  satisfied: any contractual term in conflict with § 69d Abs. 2, 3, 5 or 7, or with § 69e, is void
  (nichtig) by operation of the statute itself, and drafting around this with careful wording does
  not change the result (§ 69g Abs. 2 UrhG). This voidness list is closed: it does not reach § 69d
  Abs. 1, which the chapter itself leaves open to a contrary agreement.
- A resale or transfer clause for a copy sold inside the EU or another EEA state with the
  rightholder's consent must not purport to block further distribution of that copy once the
  distribution right has exhausted; the rental right is the one part of the distribution bundle
  that never exhausts and so may still be restricted (§ 69c Nr. 3 Satz 2 UrhG).
- A clause must not claim ownership or exclusive-right status for an employer or licensor beyond
  what § 69b actually confers: the provision shifts rights only for a program an employee or
  service-provider creates in carrying out their duties or on instruction, and only absent a
  contrary agreement (§ 69b Abs. 1, 2 UrhG).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- A licensed copy ships, or the support channel can provide, a way for the user to make and keep a
  backup copy necessary to secure future use of the program, since a term blocking this has no
  effect (§ 69d Abs. 2 UrhG).
- Loading, displaying, running, transmitting or storing the program for diagnostic observation,
  study or testing is not technically blocked for a user otherwise entitled to perform those acts,
  since a contractual bar on this conduct is void (§ 69d Abs. 3 UrhG).
- Interoperability information is published, or made available on request, where it is not already
  readily accessible, since withholding it does not stop a lawful user from decompiling for that
  purpose once § 69e Abs. 1's conditions are met; a rightholder who wants to avoid decompilation
  requests is better served by publishing the interoperability information itself (§ 69e Abs. 1 Nr. 2
  UrhG).
- Group or volume licensing and any ownership-transfer or account-reassignment flow track which
  agreement governs the economic rights in an employee- or contractor-created program, since the
  default employer-ownership rule in § 69b only operates absent a contrary agreement and a signed
  development or work-for-hire agreement can validly shift it either way (§ 69b Abs. 1, 2 UrhG).
- A take-down or anti-circumvention enforcement path supports a demand that unlawfully produced,
  distributed or distribution-intended copies be destroyed, and extends the same demand to means
  whose sole purpose is removing or circumventing technical protection, while carrying a
  carve-out for cultural-heritage-institution use under § 61d (§ 69f Abs. 1, 2 UrhG).

## Prescribed wording

None. §§ 69a to 69g fix legal categories, conditions and a voidness rule (individuelle Werke, eigene
geistige Schöpfung, vermögensrechtliche Befugnisse) rather than sentences a document must reproduce
verbatim.

## Open points to raise

- Whether the licence in question grants the user a permanent right to a copy against a one-off fee,
  since that combination raises the EU-law exhaustion question the Court of Justice answered for the
  Software Directive; this file does not cover that question, and the EU-level reading is in
  references/eu/software-directive.md instead.
- Whether a development or work-for-hire agreement with an employee or contractor states expressly
  who holds the economic rights in the resulting program, since § 69b's employer-ownership rule
  applies only by default and a silent or ambiguous agreement leaves the point open to dispute.
- Whether the business's own interoperability information is already readily available to a person
  entitled to use a copy, since that fact decides whether a decompilation request under § 69e Abs. 1
  is lawful without the rightholder's consent in the first place.
- Whether any licence term touches § 69d Abs. 1 (intended use including error correction) by special
  contractual provision, since that is the one exception the chapter leaves open to override, unlike
  Abs. 2, 3, 5, 7 and § 69e, which no drafting can displace.

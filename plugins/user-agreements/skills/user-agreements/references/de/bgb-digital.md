# BGB contracts for digital products

Citation: Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch, Buch 2, Abschnitt 3, Untertitel 1 and 2, §§ 327 to 327u, as last amended by
Art. 6 G v. 23.7.2026 I Nr. 226
Version: Neugefasst durch Bek. v. 2.1.2002 I 42, 2909; 2003, 738
Official text: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/
Law as of: 2026-10-20

## When it applies

- A consumer contract where a trader supplies digital content or a digital service (together digitale Produkte)
  against a price, including a price paid as a digital representation of value (§ 327 Abs. 1 BGB).
- A contract where the consumer supplies personal data to the trader, or agrees to, instead of or alongside a
  price, unless the trader processes that data only to perform its own contractual obligation or to meet a legal
  requirement it is subject to, and for no other purpose (§ 327 Abs. 3 i.V.m. § 312 Abs. 1a Satz 2 BGB). The
  moment the trader uses that data for anything else, such as advertising, the exception falls away and the whole
  subtitle applies.
- A digital product developed to the consumer's own specifications (§ 327 Abs. 4 BGB).
- A tangible medium that serves only as a carrier of digital content, such as a disc or a memory card, except that
  §§ 327b and 327c (supply timing and non-supply remedies) do not apply to the medium itself; its own supply runs
  under the general sales rules instead (§ 327 Abs. 5 BGB).
- A package contract (Paketvertrag): one contract between the same parties that bundles a digital product with
  other goods or services. This subtitle then applies only to the parts of that contract that concern the digital
  product, except where this subtitle's own later provisions say otherwise (§ 327a Abs. 1 BGB).
- A contract for goods that contain or are linked with a digital product, for the digital-product part only,
  whatever the contract type over the good (§ 327a Abs. 2 BGB).
- Not a sale of goods with digital elements (Waren mit digitalen Elementen): goods that cannot perform their functions without the digital content or service they contain or are linked to. Their sale stays under the sales rules, and in doubt the seller's duty includes supplying the digital content or service (§ 327a Abs. 3 BGB; see `references/de/bgb-kauf.md`).
- A B2B contract between traders that serves supplying digital products within the chain reached by §§ 327 and
  327a, where Untertitel 2 applies on top of Untertitel 1 (§ 327t BGB).
- Not this subtitle: goods that cannot function without the bundled digital product (Waren mit digitalen
  Elementen). The digital component follows the sales-law rules for goods instead, and in case of doubt the
  seller's obligation is presumed to include supplying the digital content or service (§ 327a Abs. 3 BGB).
- Not this subtitle: the consumer's statutory withdrawal right for distance and off-premises contracts
  (Widerrufsrecht). It sits in § 355 BGB and Art. 246a EGBGB (see references/de/bgb-widerruf.md). The termination
  rights this subtitle grants (§§ 327c, 327m, 327n) answer non-supply or a defect after the contract already
  exists; they are not a substitute for the pre-contract withdrawal-information duty in a checkout flow.
- Not this subtitle: non-digital services delivered by digital means, regardless of the delivery channel, for
  example a digitally delivered architect's or lawyer's work product. A digital product layered on top of such a
  service, such as a document-generation tool, is covered in its own right (§ 327 Abs. 6 Nr. 1 BGB).
- Also not this subtitle: most telecommunications services other than number-independent interpersonal
  communications services such as webmail or messaging apps (Nr. 2); treatment contracts under § 630a (Nr. 3);
  gambling services requiring a stake of monetary value and accessed on individual request (Nr. 4, so a free
  gambling simulation with no stake stays in scope); financial-services contracts (Nr. 5); free and open-source
  software the trader supplies without a price, where any personal data the consumer supplies is processed only to
  secure, or keep compatible or interoperable, that same software, and for no other purpose (Nr. 6, so any other
  use such as advertising loses the exclusion); digital content made public other than by signal transmission as
  part of a performance or event, such as a cinema screening, while a digital television transmission stays in
  scope (Nr. 7); and certain public-sector information re-use contracts (Nr. 8) (§ 327 Abs. 6 BGB).

## What the documents must say

- State what counts as supply for the specific product. Digital content is supplied once it, or the suitable means
  to access it or retrieve it to a device, has been made available or accessible to the consumer directly or
  through a facility the consumer designated; a digital service is supplied once it is made accessible the same
  way (§ 327b Abs. 3, 4 BGB). Do not describe a different trigger, such as the consumer's first login or first
  use, as the moment of supply.
- State whether an ongoing product is a series of individual supplies, each judged on its own, or a continuous
  supply judged over the whole agreed supply period; the two are treated differently for the burden of proof, the
  limitation start date, and the conformity test (§ 327b Abs. 5, § 327e Abs. 1 Satz 3 BGB).
- State the agreed characteristics (quantity, functionality, compatibility, interoperability), fitness for the use
  the contract assumes, agreed accessories, instructions and support, and any agreed updates (§ 327e Abs. 2 BGB).
  Any characteristic stated here becomes part of the legally binding conformity test, not marketing language the
  product can fall short of.
- State the version supplied and whether updates are promised. Absent other agreement, the product must be in the
  newest version available at conclusion, and updates needed to maintain conformity, including security updates,
  must be supplied with the consumer informed of them, for the whole supply period or for the period the consumer
  can otherwise expect given the kind of product (§ 327e Abs. 3 Satz 1 Nr. 5, 6, § 327f Abs. 1 BGB). A trader
  relying on an older-version baseline carries the burden of showing the parties agreed to it; a bare version label
  is not enough on its own.
- Name the specific feature and get a separate, express agreement wherever the product deviates from the objective
  requirements, the update duty, or the legal-defect standard; a checkbox, a button, or an equivalent active step
  can satisfy the express-and-separate standard, but silence, a term inside standard conditions, a pre-ticked box,
  or later ratification cannot (§ 327h BGB).
- Where a disadvantageous change to a continuously-supplied product is reserved, name the good reason the contract
  relies on, such as adapting to a new technical environment or a changed user base, state there is no added cost to
  the consumer, and give clear information about the change (§ 327r Abs. 1 BGB).
- Name the conditions under which the cooperation duty displacing the reversed burden of proof applies, such as
  the product's technical requirements for the consumer's digital environment, so the trader can later rely on
  § 327k Abs. 3 if needed (§ 327k Abs. 4 BGB).
- Name whether the bundle includes a telecommunications component, since that single fact changes which
  package-level exit rights the consumer has for non-supply and for a defect (§ 327c Abs. 6, § 327m Abs. 4 BGB).
- Where the digital product is bundled with a good under § 327a Abs. 2 (a good with digital elements), the
  consumer can exit the whole contract if the good itself becomes unfit for ordinary use because the digital
  product was not supplied or is defective (§ 327c Abs. 7, § 327m Abs. 5 BGB).

## What the documents must not say

- A clause cannot describe supply as happening only when the consumer actually retrieves the file, logs in for
  the first time, or first uses it, since the statute fixes the moment at making the product available or
  accessible, not at the consumer's own action (§ 327b Abs. 3, 4 BGB).
- A clause cannot impose a particular form on the consumer's demand for supply, since § 327c Abs. 1 BGB requires
  none; only a later, different supply time needs to be agreed expressly.
- A clause cannot assume the consumer must always demand supply before terminating. A disclosed, essential fixed
  delivery date or period that the trader misses lets the consumer terminate immediately with no prior demand;
  the same applies where the trader refuses supply or it is otherwise evidently clear supply will not happen
  (§ 327c Abs. 3 BGB).
- A clause cannot assume the trader may choose whether to repair or re-supply as a matter of the consumer's prior
  election; the trader has free choice of how to cure, at no cost to the consumer, and the consumer has no right to
  pick between methods the way sales law gives for goods (§ 327l Abs. 1 BGB).
- A clause cannot treat an insignificant defect as barring termination in a contract where the consumer pays solely
  with personal data; the insignificance threshold does not apply there (§ 327m Abs. 2 Satz 2 BGB).
- A clause cannot name a single limitation trigger for every claim under this subtitle. The start date differs by
  claim and supply type: supply, for cure and damages claims on a one-off or repeated supply; the end of the supply
  period for continuous supply; and the end of the relevant update-expectation period for an update-duty breach
  outside continuous supply (§ 327j Abs. 1 to 3 BGB).
- A clause cannot shift the burden of proving supply onto the consumer; the trader bears it, departing from the
  general rule (§ 363 BGB) that a debtor's performance is presumed once the creditor accepts a receipt (§ 327b
  Abs. 6 BGB).
- A clause cannot shift the burden of disproving a defect existed at supply onto the consumer within the statutory
  window (§ 327k Abs. 1, 2 BGB), except where the trader proves the consumer's digital environment was incompatible
  with the disclosed technical requirements, or the consumer failed a reasonable, least-invasive cooperation request
  (§ 327k Abs. 3 BGB), and only where the trader clearly disclosed the relevant point in advance (§ 327k Abs. 4 BGB).
- A clause cannot assert the subtitle's remedies do not apply to a legal defect; a third party's right restricting
  the consumer's use, for example an end-user licence the consumer must accept, is folded into the same remedy
  ladder as a product defect, with no separate regime (§ 327d, § 327g BGB).
- A clause cannot claim a use-compensation charge against the consumer alongside the price-reduction or termination
  refund mechanics this subtitle sets out; neither § 327n Abs. 4 nor § 327o Abs. 2 to 4 BGB provides for one, unlike
  the ordinary sales-law refund rule they otherwise track.
- A refund clause cannot name an alternative payment method that costs the consumer anything beyond what their
  original method would have cost (§ 327n Abs. 4 BGB).
- A content-retention clause cannot claim the trader keeps using all consumer-provided or consumer-created content
  after termination; only four named categories allow it: content with no use outside the trader's own context
  where conversion is not feasible, content tied solely to using the contracted product, content aggregated with
  other data and not reasonably separable, and content jointly created for other users' continued benefit. Anything
  outside those four must be handed back to the consumer free of charge, unhindered, in reasonable time, and in a
  common, machine-readable format (§ 327p Abs. 2, 3 BGB).
- A clause cannot make continuing the contract, or any payment obligation, conditional on the consumer not
  exercising a data-protection right; exercising such a right, or withdrawing consent, does not by itself affect
  the contract's validity, and the trader has no damages or reimbursement claim against the consumer for the
  resulting narrower scope of processing (§ 327q Abs. 1, 3 BGB).
- A change-rights clause cannot reserve a disadvantageous change without also disclosing the resulting 30-day
  termination right; that right exists independently of what the trader chose to disclose, and letting the consumer
  keep the unchanged version at no extra cost is a separate alternative, not a substitute for disclosing the
  termination right itself (§ 327r Abs. 2 to 4 BGB).
- No agreement reached before the consumer's notice of non-supply or a defect can deviate from this subtitle to the
  consumer's disadvantage (§ 327s Abs. 1 BGB); for a product change, the same applies to an agreement reached before
  the § 327r information (§ 327s Abs. 2 BGB); no other arrangement can evade the subtitle either way (§ 327s Abs. 3
  BGB). This does not reach an agreed exclusion or limit on damages claims (§ 327s Abs. 4 BGB), and § 327h deviations
  stay available on their own terms (§ 327s Abs. 5 BGB).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- The checkout or account flow records, for each product, whether it is a one-off supply, a series of individual
  supplies, or a continuous supply, since that classification drives the burden-of-proof period and the limitation
  start date that later disputes depend on (§ 327b Abs. 5, § 327j Abs. 2, § 327k Abs. 1, 2 BGB).
- The system can show, after the fact, exactly when a given product was made available or accessible to a specific
  consumer, since the trader carries the burden of proving supply happened (§ 327b Abs. 6 BGB).
- Any deviation from the objective requirements, the update duty, or the legal-defect standard is captured through
  an active step tied to the exact feature, such as a dedicated checkbox or confirmation button presented
  separately from the rest of the terms, not folded into general acceptance of standard conditions (§ 327h BGB).
- Update delivery is tracked per product for the whole supply period, or for the period an average, reasonable
  consumer would expect given the kind and purpose of the product; the system records when the consumer was told a
  security or conformity update was available and what was explained about the consequences of not installing it,
  since that record is what lets the trader rely on § 327f Abs. 2 if the consumer's own delay causes a defect.
- Where several updates are bundled into one installation, each update's own purpose, meaning which conformity
  point it addresses, is still disclosed separately, and a bundle is not used to force personal-data processing the
  consumer did not need for one part of it.
- The cancellation and defect-handling flow lets a consumer demand cure, and separately lets them choose price
  reduction or termination once the conditions for either are met, without defaulting to termination as the normal
  first step (§ 327i, § 327l to 327n BGB).
- Refund processing uses the consumer's original payment method by default, completes any refund within 14 days of
  the triggering declaration without a separate consumer request, and never adds the cost of an alternative method
  unless the consumer agreed to it expressly and it costs them nothing extra (§ 327n Abs. 4 BGB).
- A dedicated export or hand-over flow gives the consumer back their non-personal content after termination, free
  of charge, within reasonable time, in a common, machine-readable format, converting from a proprietary format
  where needed, except for the four named categories that stay with the trader (§ 327p Abs. 2, 3 BGB).
- Where a disadvantageous change to a continuously-supplied product is pushed, the system sends advance notice on a
  durable medium stating the change's features, its timing, and the consumer's 30-day termination right or the
  option to keep the current version at no extra cost, before the change takes effect (§ 327r Abs. 2, 3 BGB).
- Where the consumer withdraws data-processing consent or objects to further processing on a continuous or
  repeated-supply contract, the system can flag the account for the trader's own notice-free exit where continuing
  becomes unreasonable, without billing the consumer anything for that exercise of rights (§ 327q Abs. 2, 3 BGB).
- B2B order records trace, for a digital product moving through a distribution chain, which upstream trader
  (Vertriebspartner) supplied it, so a trader facing a consumer's non-supply or defect claim can pursue its own
  six-month recourse claim upstream (§ 327u BGB).
- A clause may state that the consumer must stop using the digital product and may not hand it to a third party
  after termination, and that the trader may technically block further use (§ 327p Abs. 1 BGB).

## Prescribed wording

None. This subtitle sets standards for disclosure, timing, and agreement (clear and understandable, specific,
express and separate, on a durable medium), not fixed phrases a document must reproduce.

## Dates

- 2026-10-20: law-as-of date for this file.
- The subtitle entered into force 1 January 2022 for contracts concluded on or after that date. A contract
  concluded earlier is nonetheless governed by it if the digital product's actual supply happens after that date,
  except for the change-rights regime (§ 327r) and the B2B recourse regime (§§ 327t, 327u), which apply only to
  contracts concluded after entry into force (Art. 229 EGBGB transitional provision; the transitional provision
  itself is not covered in this file).
- No section in this file carries a section-specific effective date separate from the general version line.

## Open points to raise

- Whether the product in question is better described as a series of individual supplies or a continuous supply.
  The distinguishing test is whether the consumer's access to each supplied item stays available indefinitely
  afterward (a series) or only for the agreed supply period (continuous), and that classification changes the
  burden-of-proof window and the limitation start date the business will later rely on.
- Whether the business can show, for a specific past consumer, exactly when a product was made available or
  accessible, given the trader carries the burden of proving supply happened at all.
- Whether any deviation the business wants from the objective requirements, the update duty, or the legal-defect
  standard is captured through a specific, separately presented active step for that exact feature, since a general
  disclaimer inside standard terms has no effect under § 327h.
- Whether the business's update practice for each product category matches what an average, reasonable consumer
  would expect given that product's kind and purpose, since the expected update period is not capped at the
  limitation period and can run longer for some product types than for others.
- Whether the business processes any personal data supplied as or alongside a price for a purpose beyond
  performing the contract or meeting a legal requirement, since any such additional use, including advertising,
  removes the narrow exception and brings the full subtitle into play regardless of whether that processing is
  itself lawful under data-protection law.
- Whether any bundled offering includes a telecommunications component, since that single fact changes which
  package-level exit rights apply for non-supply, for a defect, and for a later product change.
- Whether the business's post-termination content handling matches the four named exceptions exactly, or claims a
  broader retention right than those exceptions support.
- As of the bill's own introduction in 2021, the German government declined to add a dedicated consent control
  confirming a consumer's choice to pay with data (a "Bezahlen mit Daten" button), reading no room under
  Art. 8 Abs. 2 Unterabsatz 2 of the Consumer Rights Directive (2011/83/EU), which is limited to
  zahlungspflichtige Verträge (contracts carrying a payment obligation). A separate proposal for mandatory
  optical labelling of algorithm-based applications and a duty to disclose their underlying logic was not
  rejected but deferred for examination, pending the EU Commission's then-forthcoming AI-regulation proposals.
  Whether either reading has since changed through the Commission's review of the underlying directive, due by
  12 June 2024, is a question for current EU-level material, not for this file's sources.

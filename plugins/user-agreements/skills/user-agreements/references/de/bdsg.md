# BDSG: German exceptions to GDPR data subject rights

Citation: Bundesdatenschutzgesetz (BDSG), §§ 32 to 37, zuletzt geändert durch Art. 3 G v. 12.5.2026 I Nr. 139
Official text: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bdsg_2018/
Law as of: 2026-10-20

This file covers §§ 32 to 37 BDSG only: the national grounds on which a German controller
(Verantwortlicher) may skip or limit the GDPR information, access, erasure, objection and
automated-decision rights, each paired with a safeguard. The GDPR articles these sections narrow
(Art. 13 to 15, 17, 18, 21, 22 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679) are covered in
`references/eu/gdpr-privacy-notice.md` and `references/eu/gdpr-rights-liability.md`; this file
cites those articles but does not restate them. Other parts of the BDSG (special categories of
data, the Bundesbeauftragte's general powers, sanctions) are outside this cut.

## When it applies

- §§ 32 and 33 apply at the point a controller must otherwise give the Art. 13 or Art. 14 GDPR
  notice: § 32 where the data was collected from the data subject, § 33 where it was not
  (§ 32 Abs. 1 BDSG; § 33 Abs. 1 BDSG).
- § 34 applies on an access request under Art. 15 GDPR; § 35 Abs. 1 wherever erasure of
  non-automated data is impossible or disproportionately burdensome because of the special way it
  is stored, and the data subject's interest in erasure is slight (§ 35 Abs. 1 Satz 1 BDSG); § 36 on
  an objection under Art. 21 Abs. 1 GDPR against a public body; § 37 to an
  automated decision under Art. 22 Abs. 1 GDPR made in the course of performance under an insurance
  contract (Versicherungsvertrag) (§ 34 Abs. 1 BDSG; § 35 Abs. 1 Satz 1 BDSG; § 36 BDSG;
  § 37 Abs. 1 BDSG).
- Several grounds are formally limited by who the controller is. § 32 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 BDSG applies
  only where the controller is a public body (öffentliche Stelle); § 33 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 BDSG likewise
  applies only to a public body, and § 33 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 BDSG only to a non-public body
  (nichtöffentliche Stelle), on a different fact pattern: civil-law claims impaired, or
  crime-prevention data processed from a civil-law contract, unless the data subject's legitimate
  interest in the notice outweighs that (§ 33 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 Buchst. a BDSG); or the competent public
  body has found, towards the controller, that disclosure would endanger public security or order
  or otherwise harm the federal or a state interest, a finding not needed at all for data processed
  for criminal-prosecution purposes (§ 33 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 Buchst. b BDSG). § 32 Abs. 1 Nr. 1, 4 and 5
  BDSG carry no such qualifier in their wording and are open to any controller. § 32 Abs. 1 Nr. 1
  BDSG applies only where every one of four conditions is met together: the further processing is
  of analogue (non-digitally stored) data; the controller contacts the data subject directly as
  part of that further processing; the purpose is compatible with the original GDPR collection
  purpose; the communication with the data subject does not happen digitally; and the data
  subject's interest in being informed is, on the individual facts, slight, especially given the
  context the data was originally collected in (§ 32 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 BDSG). § 32 Abs. 1 Nr. 3 BDSG
  (public security or order, or detriment to the federal or a state interest) also carries no
  explicit public-body qualifier in the statute's own text, unlike Nr. 2; a drafter should not
  assume it is closed to a non-public controller merely because the ground is one a public body
  would ordinarily invoke (§ 32 Abs. 1 Nr. 3 BDSG).
- § 32 does not displace the Art. 13 Abs. 4 GDPR exception itself; it adds a further, narrower
  exception on top of that one exception (§ 32 Abs. 1 BDSG).
- § 33 does not displace the Art. 14 Abs. 5 GDPR exception either, but it sits on top of two
  exceptions, not one: the Art. 14 Abs. 5 GDPR exception and the exception already named in
  § 29 Abs. 1 Satz 1 BDSG, a provision outside this cut (§ 33 Abs. 1 BDSG).
- § 34 Abs. 1 BDSG works the same way for the access right: it adds a further exception on top of
  the exceptions already named in § 27 Abs. 2, § 28 Abs. 2 and § 29 Abs. 1 Satz 2 BDSG, none of
  which this file reproduces, since all three sit outside this cut (§ 34 Abs. 1 BDSG).

## What the documents must say

- Where a controller relies on § 32 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 to 3 BDSG or § 33 Abs. 1 BDSG to skip the
  individual Art. 13 or Art. 14 GDPR notice, it must still take appropriate measures to protect the
  data subject's legitimate interests, including making the Art. 13 Abs. 1, 2 or Art. 14 Abs. 1, 2
  GDPR information available to the public in precise, transparent, intelligible and easily
  accessible form, in clear and plain language, typically as a public-facing privacy page separate
  from the individual notice that was skipped (§ 32 Abs. 2 Satz 1 BDSG; § 33 Abs. 2 Satz 1 BDSG).
- The controller must record in writing the reasons for withholding the notice (§ 32 Abs. 2 Satz 2
  BDSG; § 33 Abs. 2 Satz 2 BDSG). For § 32, Sentences 1 and 2 of Abs. 2 do not apply to the Nr. 4
  and Nr. 5 grounds (legal claims; confidential transmission to public bodies): no public
  publication or written reasons are owed for those two (§ 32 Abs. 2 Satz 3 BDSG).
- Where the withholding under § 32 Abs. 1 BDSG rests on a temporary obstacle (vorübergehender
  Hinderungsgrund), the controller must give the notice within a reasonable period once the
  obstacle lapses, and at the latest within two weeks (§ 32 Abs. 3 BDSG).
- Notice to a data subject about a public body's transmission of their data to a
  Verfassungsschutzbehörde (constitutional-protection authority), the Bundesnachrichtendienst
  (Federal Intelligence Service), the Militärischer Abschirmdienst (Military Counter-Intelligence
  Service) or, where federal security is touched, another authority of the federal defence
  ministry, is permitted only with that body's consent (§ 33 Abs. 3 BDSG).
- A controller that denies an access request under one of the § 34 Abs. 1 BDSG grounds must
  document the grounds for refusal, and must state the actual and legal grounds to the data subject
  unless doing so would itself defeat the purpose the refusal serves (§ 34 Abs. 2 Satz 1, 2 BDSG).
  Data held only to prepare and give that response may be used solely for that purpose and for
  data-protection-control purposes; any other use must be restricted under Art. 18 GDPR
  (§ 34 Abs. 2 Satz 3 BDSG).
- Where a federal public body refuses access, the data subject may demand the access instead be
  given to the Bundesbeauftragte für den Datenschutz (Federal Commissioner for Data Protection),
  unless the competent supreme federal authority finds in the individual case that this would
  endanger federal or state security; the Commissioner's reply to the data subject must not let the
  controller's actual state of knowledge be inferred, unless the controller agrees to a fuller
  answer (§ 34 Abs. 3 BDSG).
- Where personal data held by a public body is processed neither in automated form nor in
  non-automated form in a filing system, the access right exists only if the data subject supplies
  enough detail to let the data be found, and the effort needed is not disproportionate to the
  data subject's stated interest in the information (§ 34 Abs. 4 BDSG).
- Where § 35 Abs. 1 Satz 1 BDSG applies, restriction of processing under Art. 18 GDPR substitutes
  for erasure, rather than erasure simply being skipped (§ 35 Abs. 1 Satz 2 BDSG). This same
  substitution applies, by extension, to an Art. 17 Abs. 1 Buchstabe a or d GDPR erasure ground
  under Art. 18 Abs. 1 Buchstabe b or c GDPR, wherever the controller has reason to believe erasure
  would harm the data subject's protection-worthy interests (§ 35 Abs. 2 Satz 1 BDSG), and also to
  an Art. 17 Abs. 1 Buchstabe a GDPR ground where statutory or contractual retention periods stand
  in the way of erasure (§ 35 Abs. 3 BDSG). In every case the controller must inform the data
  subject of the restriction unless that proves impossible or would take disproportionate effort
  (§ 35 Abs. 2 Satz 2 BDSG).
- Where an automated decision under § 37 Abs. 1 BDSG is not granted to the data subject's request
  in full, the controller must, no later than the notice stating that, also inform the data subject
  of the right to obtain intervention by a person on the controller's part, to state their own view,
  and to contest the decision (§ 37 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 BDSG). A § 37 Abs. 1 BDSG decision may rest on
  health data within the meaning of Art. 4 Nr. 15 GDPR (§ 37 Abs. 2 Satz 1 BDSG); where it does, the
  controller must provide the appropriate and specific measures under § 22 Abs. 2 Satz 2 BDSG to
  safeguard the data subject's interests (§ 37 Abs. 2 Satz 2 BDSG; § 22 Abs. 2 Satz 2 BDSG is
  outside this cut).

## What the documents must not say

- A privacy notice cannot assert that skipping the individual notice under § 32 or § 33 BDSG
  removes the duty to publish the Art. 13 or Art. 14 GDPR information for the public; the two duties
  are separate, and the public-publication and written-reasons duty survives the individual notice
  being skipped for the Nr. 1 to 3 grounds (§ 32 Abs. 2 BDSG; § 33 Abs. 2 BDSG).
- A document cannot claim the § 34 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 BDSG access-refusal ground applies on its own
  terms; it only applies where the data subject was already excused from being informed under
  § 33 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 BDSG, § 33 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 Buchstabe b BDSG or § 33 Abs. 3 BDSG, so the refusal
  depends on one of those earlier grounds actually being met (§ 34 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 BDSG).
- A document cannot claim the § 34 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 BDSG access-refusal ground (disproportionate
  effort for data kept only under a retention duty, or only for data-security or data-protection
  control) applies unless suitable technical and organisational measures also rule out any
  processing of that data for other purposes; the proportionality and the purpose-limitation
  conditions are cumulative, not alternatives (§ 34 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 BDSG).
- A document cannot assert that § 35 Abs. 1 Satz 1, 2 BDSG lets a controller substitute restriction
  for erasure where the original processing was itself unlawful; that substitution is expressly
  unavailable in that case (§ 35 Abs. 1 Satz 3 BDSG).
- A document cannot cite § 36 BDSG to defeat an objection made against a non-public (private
  sector) controller; the exception runs only against a public body, and only where a compelling
  public interest outweighs the data subject's interest or a legal provision compels the processing
  (§ 36 BDSG).
- A document cannot cite § 37 Abs. 1 BDSG outside the scope of performance under an insurance
  contract, or present it as a general carve-out from Art. 22 Abs. 1 GDPR for automated decisions in
  other industries; the ground only displaces Art. 22 Abs. 1 GDPR where the request was granted in
  full, or the decision rests on binding fee schedules for medical treatment with the Nr. 2 rights
  floor attached (§ 37 Abs. 1 Nr. 1, 2 BDSG).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- Where the individual Art. 13 or Art. 14 GDPR notice is skipped under a § 32 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 to 3 or
  § 33 Abs. 1 BDSG ground, the system keeps a public-facing privacy page carrying the Art. 13
  Abs. 1, 2 or Art. 14 Abs. 1, 2 GDPR information, separate from any individual notice flow, and a
  record of which ground was relied on and why (§ 32 Abs. 2 BDSG; § 33 Abs. 2 BDSG).
- Where a temporary obstacle delays a § 32 notice, the system tracks the obstacle and triggers the
  make-up notice once it lapses, with a hard ceiling of two weeks from that point
  (§ 32 Abs. 3 BDSG).
- Access-request handling records, for each refusal, the specific ground relied on under
  § 34 Abs. 1 BDSG and whether reasons were given to the data subject or withheld because giving
  them would defeat the refusal's purpose; data kept only to prepare the response is flagged so it
  is not reused for other purposes (§ 34 Abs. 2 BDSG).
- Where a federal public body is the controller, the access-handling process supports routing a
  refused request to the Bundesbeauftragte on the data subject's demand, and keeps the controller's
  own knowledge state from leaking into the Commissioner's reply to the data subject
  (§ 34 Abs. 3 BDSG).
- Erasure workflows apply restriction under Art. 18 GDPR instead of deletion wherever the
  § 35 Abs. 1 Satz 1 BDSG conditions are met, or on one of its two extensions (a protection-worthy
  interest under Abs. 2, or a retention period under Abs. 3), and notify the data subject of that
  restriction unless notice is impossible or disproportionately burdensome; the workflow checks
  first whether the underlying processing was lawful, since an unlawful original processing takes
  this substitution off the table (§ 35 Abs. 1 Satz 3 BDSG).
- An automated insurance-decision system that does not grant a request in full sends the
  § 37 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 BDSG human-intervention, own-view and contest-the-decision notice together with,
  and no later than, the refusal notice itself, not on a later or on-request basis
  (§ 37 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 BDSG).

## Prescribed wording

None: §§ 32 to 37 BDSG fix no mandatory label, button text or form phrase. They set content and
timing duties (what must be published, documented or notified, and by when), not fixed wording for
a notice, button or declaration.

## Dates

- 2026-10-20: law-as-of date for this file.
- § 32 Abs. 3 BDSG: at the latest two weeks from the lapse of a temporary obstacle, for the

## Open points to raise

- Whether a given controller counts as an öffentliche Stelle or a nichtöffentliche Stelle for a
  specific processing activity, since several grounds in §§ 32 to 34 and 36 BDSG turn on that
  classification and this file does not settle it in the abstract.
- Whether § 32 Abs. 1 Nr. 3 BDSG (public security or order; detriment to the federal or a state
  interest) is in practice available to a non-public controller, since the provision's own wording
  carries no "im Fall einer öffentlichen Stelle" qualifier (unlike Nr. 2), but the two footnotes in
  the statute point to § 4 Abs. 4 and § 85 Abs. 3 BDSG for the application of § 32, and neither
  of those sections is in this cut.
- Whether § 32 Abs. 2 BDSG (the publish-and-record safeguard) is in fact modified for a specific
  processing activity by § 85 Abs. 3 BDSG, which the statute's own footnote flags as relevant
  to § 32 Abs. 2 but which sits outside this cut.
- Whether the processing in question is governed by a sector-specific provision outside §§ 32 to
  37 that itself addresses the point (for example § 22 Abs. 2 Satz 2 BDSG for the health-data
  safeguard § 37 Abs. 2 BDSG cross-refers to), since this file does not reproduce those sections.
- What the exceptions named in § 27 Abs. 2, § 28 Abs. 2 and § 29 Abs. 1 (Satz 1 and Satz 2) BDSG
  actually allow, since § 33 Abs. 1 and § 34 Abs. 1 BDSG each sit on top of one or more of those
  provisions and none of them is in this cut; a controller relying on § 33 Abs. 1 or § 34 Abs. 1
  BDSG needs those sections read directly to know the full extent of what can be withheld.

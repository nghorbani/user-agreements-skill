# Consumer dispute resolution notice (VSBG and the ODR platform repeal)

Citation: Verbraucherstreitbeilegungsgesetz (VSBG), §§ 36 to 37; Regulation (EU) 2024/3228 of 9 December 2024
Version: VSBG zuletzt geaendert durch Art. 16 G v. 8.12.2025 I Nr. 318; Regulation (EU) 2024/3228, original, no amendment found
Official text: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/vsbg/
Law as of: 2026-10-20

## When it applies

- A trader (Unternehmer) who maintains a website, or who uses standard terms (Allgemeine Geschaeftsbedingungen) toward consumers, owes the standing information duty of § 36 VSBG (§ 36 Abs. 1 VSBG).
- A trader involved in an actual dispute over a consumer contract (Verbrauchervertrag) that could not be settled between the trader and the consumer owes the separate, case-specific notice of § 37 VSBG, triggered only once that settlement attempt has failed (§ 37 Abs. 1 Satz 1 VSBG).
- Regulation (EU) No 524/2013, which until 20 July 2025 required online traders and online marketplaces to link to the EU Online Dispute Resolution platform (the ODR platform), is repealed with effect from that date (Art. 1 Regulation (EU) 2024/3228). The ODR platform itself stopped taking new complaints on 20 March 2025 and was discontinued (Art. 2(1), 2(2) Regulation (EU) 2024/3228). Both dates are in the past as of this file's law-as-of date, so the repeal and the discontinuation are settled fact, not a pending step.
- §§ 36-37 VSBG are a separate regime from the old ODR-link duty and are not affected by its repeal; a trader still owes the VSBG notices regardless of what, if anything, replaces the ODR platform link (§§ 36, 37 VSBG; Regulation (EU) 2024/3228).

## What the documents must say

- On the trader's website, if it maintains one: whether and to what extent the trader is willing or obliged to take part in dispute-resolution proceedings (Streitbeilegungsverfahren) before a consumer arbitration body (Verbraucherschlichtungsstelle), given easily accessible, clearly and comprehensibly (leicht zugaenglich, klar und verstaendlich) (§ 36 Abs. 1 Nr. 1, Abs. 2 Nr. 1 VSBG).
- Together with the trader's standard terms, if it uses them: the same information, delivered alongside those terms rather than merely published somewhere on the same website (§ 36 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 VSBG).
- Where the trader has committed to taking part in proceedings before a consumer arbitration body, or is legally obliged to, on both of the above locations as they apply: that body's address and website, and a statement that the trader will take part in dispute-resolution proceedings before it (§ 36 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 VSBG).
- Once an actual consumer-contract dispute has failed to settle between the trader and the consumer: a notice naming the competent consumer arbitration body for that consumer, with its address and website, stating at the same time whether the trader is willing or obliged to take part in proceedings before that body, and naming the specific body or bodies if the trader is willing or obliged to take part before one or more of them (§ 37 Abs. 1 VSBG).
- Where a document still needs to point consumers toward an out-of-court dispute-resolution option after the ODR platform's repeal: the Commission's list of alternative consumer dispute resolution entities, kept under Article 20(4) of Directive 2013/11/EU, is the surviving reference point the EU legislator substituted for the old ODR-platform link in the one Union act this source shows being amended for it (Art. 4 Regulation (EU) 2024/3228, replacing point 7 of Annex III to Regulation (EU) 2018/1724). That substitution is made at Union-annex level, not as a duty placed on an individual trader's own terms; this file does not state that a trader's own notice must use that wording, only that it is the reference point available where a replacement is wanted.

## What the documents must not say

- A trader cannot discharge the § 36 standing duty by serving only one of its two triggered locations: publishing on the website alone does not satisfy the standard-terms delivery duty under Abs. 2 Nr. 2 where the trader also uses standard terms, and the reverse omission does not satisfy the website duty under Abs. 2 Nr. 1 where the trader also maintains a website (§ 36 Abs. 2 VSBG).
- A trader exempt under § 36 Abs. 3 cannot treat the exemption as covering Abs. 1 Nr. 2 as well: the exemption reaches only the general willingness/obligation statement of Nr. 1, so an exempted trader that has in fact committed, or is legally obliged, to take part before a named consumer arbitration body must still name that body, its address and its website (§ 36 Abs. 3 VSBG).
- A trader cannot rely on its standing § 36 notice to cover the § 37 step, and cannot give the § 37 notice informally: § 37 is triggered only by an actual failed settlement attempt and must be delivered in text form (Textform), which a purely oral statement does not satisfy (§ 37 Abs. 2 VSBG).
- From 20 July 2025, a document must not present the ODR platform as a live dispute-resolution channel or cite Regulation (EU) No 524/2013 as still in force for that purpose: the platform's legal basis is repealed and the platform itself was already discontinued on 20 March 2025 for new complaints (Art. 1, Art. 2(1), 2(2) Regulation (EU) 2024/3228). This does not retroactively void any contract that once carried the old link; it only means the link no longer describes a real channel and should not still appear.

## Product behaviour it assumes

- A trader that both maintains a website and uses standard terms runs the § 36 disclosure in both places, not as a single combined page unless that page is in fact reached from both the website and wherever the standard terms are presented (§ 36 Abs. 2 VSBG).
- Employee headcount for the § 36 Abs. 3 exemption is checked each year against the prior 31 December figure, since the exemption can lapse or newly apply as headcount changes, and the check always covers Nr. 2 separately from Nr. 1 regardless of the exemption's effect on Nr. 1 (§ 36 Abs. 3 VSBG).
- A workflow step fires once a consumer dispute over a consumer contract has failed to settle, sending a text-form notice (letter, email, or a comparably durable medium, not an oral statement) naming the competent consumer arbitration body and the trader's willingness or obligation position, kept distinct from the standing § 36 notice (§ 37 VSBG).
- Any still-present link to the ODR platform, or citation of Regulation (EU) No 524/2013 as a live redress channel, is removed from documents issued or updated for use from 20 July 2025 onward (Art. 1 Regulation (EU) 2024/3228).

## Prescribed wording

None. §§ 36-37 VSBG fix what information must be given, where, and in what form (easily accessible, clear and comprehensible; in text form for § 37), not a sentence to reproduce verbatim. Regulation (EU) 2024/3228's Article 4 wording replaces text in Annex III to Regulation (EU) 2018/1724 at Union level; it is not a label or statement a trader's own document is required to quote.

## Dates

- 20 March 2025: submission of new complaints to the ODR platform stopped, and the Commission was by this date required to have informed users with ongoing cases of the discontinuation and offered them help retrieving their case data (Art. 2(2), 2(3) Regulation (EU) 2024/3228).
- 20 July 2025: Regulation (EU) No 524/2013 was repealed, and all information, including personal data, related to ODR platform cases was to be deleted by this date (Art. 1, Art. 2(4) Regulation (EU) 2024/3228). Both duties fall on the Commission as platform operator, not on individual traders.
- VSBG version line: zuletzt geaendert durch Art. 16 G v. 8.12.2025 I Nr. 318.

## Open points to raise

- Whether the business's own terms or notices still carry a link to the ODR platform or a citation of Regulation (EU) No 524/2013, since any such reference should already be gone or replaced given both repeal dates above are in the past as of this file's law-as-of date.
- Whether the business wants to keep pointing consumers to an out-of-court dispute-resolution option at all in its § 36 clause once an ODR-platform link is removed, and if so, how it wants to frame a reference to the Commission's ADR entity list described under "What the documents must say", since this source fixes that list as the Union-level replacement reference point but does not fix wording for a trader's own document.
- Whether any clause in the business's documents traces to Directive (EU) 2015/2302 (package travel), Directive (EU) 2019/2161 (the omnibus directive) or Directive (EU) 2020/1828 (representative actions): those three Directives also referenced Regulation (EU) No 524/2013, and as of recital 8 Regulation (EU) 2024/3228 they were intended to be amended by a separate legal act, not by this Regulation. This repeal clears the ODR-platform reference in Regulation (EU) No 524/2013 itself; it does not state that those three Directives' own references have been amended, so a clause derived from one of them needs its own check rather than relying on this repeal alone.

# BGB sale contract, defects, and consumer goods sales

Citation: BGB §§ 433 to 445c, 474 to 479g, as last amended by Art. 6 G v. 23.7.2026 I Nr. 226
Version: Neugefasst durch Bek. v. 2.1.2002 I 42, 2909; 2003, 738
Official text: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/
Law as of: 2026-10-20

## When it applies

- Any sale contract: the seller owes handover of the item plus procuring ownership of it, free of both a Sachmangel (defect of the thing) and a Rechtsmangel (defect of title); the buyer owes the agreed price plus taking delivery (§ 433 Abs. 1, 2 BGB).
- The item is free of a Sachmangel only if, at the moment risk passes, it clears three layers together: the subjective requirements the contract itself fixes (agreed condition, presupposed use, agreed accessories and instructions), the objective requirements (ordinary use, usual condition including durability and safety, sample match, expected accessories), unless validly agreed otherwise, and, where assembly is owed, the assembly requirements (§ 434 Abs. 1 to 4 BGB). Delivering a different item than the one owed counts as a Sachmangel on its own (§ 434 Abs. 5 BGB). A Rechtsmangel is any assertable third-party right beyond what the contract transferred, including a land-register entry for a right that does not actually exist (§ 435 BGB).
- The whole consumer-sales subtitle (§§ 474 to 479g) switches on the moment a consumer buys a Ware (§ 241a Abs. 1 BGB) from a business; a contract that also has the business owe a service alongside the goods is still a Verbrauchsgüterkauf (§ 474 Abs. 1 BGB). It does not switch on for used goods sold in a publicly accessible auction where the consumer was given clear, complete, easily available information that this subtitle does not apply (§ 474 Abs. 2 BGB).
- Where the thing bought is a physical carrier that serves exclusively as a carrier for digital content, the sale-of-goods defect rules in this file (§ 433 Abs. 1 Satz 2, §§ 434 to 442, § 475 Abs. 3 Satz 1, Abs. 4 to 7, §§ 475b to 475e, 476, 477) do not apply at all; the digital-content subtitle's rules apply instead (§ 475a Abs. 1 BGB). Where the thing bought is a Ware that works without its digital products, the same set of rules is switched off only for the contract components concerning those digital products, alongside § 433 Abs. 1 Satz 1 and § 475 Abs. 1 on handover and performance timing for that portion (§ 475a Abs. 2 BGB).
- Where the business undertakes that it or a third party will provide digital elements with the Ware (a Ware mit digitalen Elementen, § 327a Abs. 3 Satz 1 BGB), the goods-with-digital-elements rules in this file apply on top of the general Sachmangel standard (§ 475b Abs. 1 BGB).
- The manufacturer's statutory repair obligation (§§ 479a to 479g) applies only where all three conditions are met together: the goods belong to a product group listed in Anhang II of Directive (EU) 2024/1799, a consumer bought them, and the consumer no longer has the § 437 remedies for them (§ 479a BGB).

## What the documents must say

- State the agreed condition, the presupposed use, and the included accessories and instructions explicitly; whatever is fixed there becomes the subjective-requirement benchmark the item is measured against, ahead of the more open-ended objective fallback (§ 434 Abs. 2 BGB).
- To depart from an objective requirement (ordinary use, usual condition, sample match, expected accessories) for a consumer, specifically inform the consumer, before the consumer's contractual declaration, which particular feature of the Ware departs from the objective requirements, then record that departure expressly and separately in the contract; both steps are needed, and neither works once notice of an actual defect has reached the business (§ 434 Abs. 3 Satz 1, § 476 Abs. 1 Satz 2 BGB).
- Before carrying out Nacherfüllung for a consumer, inform the consumer that the consumer has the choice between repair and replacement under § 439 Abs. 1, and that cure by repair extends the original limitation period for defect claims, once, by twelve months (§ 475 Abs. 4 BGB).
- For a Ware with digital elements, fix in the contract the period during which the agreed updates will be provided; that period becomes part of the subjective-requirement benchmark on its own (§ 475b Abs. 3 Nr. 2 BGB). Before relying on a consumer's own failure to install an update as a defence, the business must have told the consumer of the update's availability and the consequences of not installing it, and the installation instructions given must themselves have been adequate (§ 475b Abs. 5 BGB).
- A Garantieerklärung (guarantee declaration) given to a consumer must be drafted simply and comprehensibly and contain five items: a reference to the consumer's statutory defect rights, that exercising them is free of charge, and that the Garantie does not restrict them; the Garantiegeber's name and address; the procedure the consumer must follow to assert the Garantie; the Ware the Garantie covers; and the Garantie's terms, in particular its duration and territorial scope (§ 479 Abs. 1 BGB). It must reach the consumer on a durable medium no later than delivery of the Ware (§ 479 Abs. 2 BGB). None of this is quoted verbatim as a fixed phrase (see Prescribed wording).
- For as long as a manufacturer is obliged to repair under § 479b Abs. 1, it must provide information on that repair service free of charge, in an easily accessible, clear and comprehensible way, and must publish, on a freely accessible website, the indicative prices calculated for typical repairs of goods within § 479a's scope; the Preisangabenverordnung applies alongside this duty, not instead of it (§ 479d Abs. 1, 2 BGB).

## What the documents must not say

- A clause cannot make the buyer bear the cost of removing and reinstalling a defective item that was built into or attached to another item before the defect became apparent, where the statutory conditions in § 439 Abs. 3 are met (§ 439 Abs. 3 BGB).
- A liability-exclusion or liability-limitation clause, and the liability limit for an item sold as a pledge under a lien at a public auction, are both simply unavailable to the seller to the extent the seller fraudulently concealed the defect or itself gave a Garantie on the item's condition, regardless of how the clause is worded (§ 444, § 445 BGB).
- A clause cannot reinstate the usage-value offset that § 475 Abs. 3 strips from § 439 Abs. 6 for a consumer sale, and cannot reintroduce §§ 442, 445 or 447 Abs. 2 into one; it cannot shift the cost of returning the Ware onto the consumer on withdrawal or damages instead of full performance (§ 475 Abs. 3, 7 BGB).
- A clause cannot shorten the objective-requirement update period for a Ware with digital elements below what the consumer can expect given the Ware's kind and purpose, and cannot excuse the business from the two conditions (availability-and-consequences notice, adequate installation instructions) before relying on a consumer's own failure to update; it cannot shorten the continuous-provision minimum liability period below two years from delivery (§ 475b Abs. 4, 5, § 475c Abs. 2 BGB). The one opening is the same two-step route described above for an objective-requirement departure (specific pre-contract notice of the departing feature, then an express and separate contract term): that route reaches both the general objective requirements in § 434 Abs. 3 and § 475b Abs. 4's own update period (§ 476 Abs. 1 Satz 2 BGB).
- Agreed before notice of a defect, a clause cannot worsen a consumer's position under §§ 433 to 435, 437, 439 to 441 and 443 or this subtitle; the only two openings are the two-step, specifically-flagged, expressly-and-separately-agreed route for an objective-requirement departure under § 434 Abs. 3 or § 475b Abs. 4, and the same two-step route for a limitation period down to not less than two years (one year for used goods); neither survives once notice of an actual defect has reached the business, and an alternative structure built to the same end is treated as if the dodge had not happened (§ 476 Abs. 1, 2, 4 BGB). This carve-out does not reach the damages claim, which §§ 307 to 309 govern on their own terms (§ 476 Abs. 3 BGB), and it cannot make the pre-cure information duty under § 475 Abs. 4 optional either, since that duty is one of the provisions the carve-out protects.
- A recourse-limiting agreement between a seller and its Lieferant, made before notice of a defect to the Lieferant, cannot be relied on against § 478 Abs. 1 itself or §§ 433 to 435, 437, 439 to 443, 445a Abs. 1 and 2, 445b, 475b and 475c unless the recourse creditor is given an equivalent compensation; the same circumvention ban and the same damages carve-out under § 307 apply (§ 478 Abs. 2 BGB).
- A spare-parts or repair-tools pricing policy cannot be set so as to discourage repair (§ 479c BGB); a hardware or software measure that hinders repair of goods within § 479a's scope needs an actual legitimate and objective justification, such as protecting intellectual-property rights, and can never be used to block an independent repair business from using compliant original, used, compatible or 3-D-printed parts (§ 479e BGB). A manufacturer cannot refuse a repair solely because an earlier repair was carried out by another repair business or person (§ 479b Abs. 2 Satz 3 BGB). Any agreement departing to the consumer's disadvantage from the repair-obligation subtitle, directly or by an alternative structure, is simply unavailable to the manufacturer or whoever is obliged under § 479f (§ 479g BGB).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- On a defect, let the buyer choose between repair and replacement as Nacherfüllung, with the seller bearing the necessary expenses (transport, travel, labour, material); only refuse the chosen kind where it is actually disproportionate in cost, weighing the item's defect-free value, the defect's significance, and whether the other kind of cure works without substantial disadvantage to the buyer, and narrow the claim to that other kind, which the seller may also refuse under the same test; once both kinds are properly refused, Nacherfüllung is exhausted and the buyer falls back to withdrawal, reduction or damages (§ 439 Abs. 1, 2, 4 BGB; § 440 BGB). The buyer must make the item available to the seller for Nacherfüllung (§ 439 Abs. 5 BGB), and where the seller delivers a defect-free item for cure, the seller may demand return of the defective item under §§ 346 to 348 and takes it back at the seller's own cost (§ 439 Abs. 6 BGB).
- Where no performance time is fixed for a consumer sale, hand over the Ware no later than 30 days after conclusion (§ 475 Abs. 1 BGB). Carry out Nacherfüllung for a consumer within a reasonable period from notice of the defect and without significant inconvenience, weighing the Ware's kind and the consumer's purpose; a free replacement Ware may be offered for the repair's duration (which may be refurbished), and a refurbished Ware may only be delivered on replacement delivery if the consumer expressly requested it (§ 475 Abs. 6 BGB). The consumer may demand an advance for expenses the business must bear under Nacherfüllung (§ 475 Abs. 5 BGB).
- Compute the applicable limitation period by case: two years from delivery as the general rule, five years for a building or a building-material item used, consistent with its usual mode of use, for a building whose defect the item's own defect caused, thirty years for a registered or in-rem third-party right, with the regular limitation period applying instead wherever the seller fraudulently concealed the defect (§ 438 Abs. 1, 3 BGB). For a consumer sale, layer in five further floors: twelve months after continuous digital-element provision ends, twelve months after an updating-duty period ends, four months after a defect first shows up within the period, two months after a cured item is handed back, and a one-time twelve-month extension once Nacherfüllung is rendered by repair (§ 475e Abs. 1 to 5 BGB).
- For a Ware with digital elements, provide the updates the contract fixes during the agreed period, and, independently, the updates needed for contract-conformity during the period the consumer can expect given the Ware's kind and purpose; where continuous provision is agreed, stay liable for conformity for at least two years from delivery regardless of a shorter contractual period, and where no duration is fixed for continuous provision, apply the objective-requirement period instead (§ 475b Abs. 3, 4, § 475c Abs. 1, 2 BGB). Where installation is owed, the Ware meets the installation requirements for its digital elements only if that installation was done properly, or, if not, the failure is not attributable to the business's own faulty installation or faulty instructions (§ 475b Abs. 6 BGB).
- Treat a condition departing from the requirements within a year of risk-passing (six months for a living animal) as presumed already present at risk-passing, unless that is incompatible with the item's kind or the defect's kind; for continuously-provided digital elements, treat a departure shown during the provision period, or within two years of risk-passing, as presumed to have existed throughout the provision period so far (§ 477 Abs. 1, 2 BGB).
- On withdrawal or damages instead of full performance for a defective Ware in a consumer sale, bear the cost of return shipping, and treat the consumer's proof of having sent the return as equivalent to actual return (§ 475 Abs. 7 BGB).
- A seller gets a recourse claim against its own Lieferant for the expenses it had to bear under § 439 Abs. 2, 3 and 6 Satz 2 and § 475 Abs. 5 toward its buyer, but only where the defect the buyer asserted was already present when risk passed to the seller itself, or stems from a breach of the updating duty under § 475b Abs. 4 (§ 445a Abs. 1 BGB); that recourse claim is time-barred after two years from delivery of the item, but not before two months after the seller has satisfied the buyer's own claims (§ 445b Abs. 1, 2 BGB). Where the last contract in the supply chain is a Verbrauchsgüterkauf, § 477's defect-presumption period applies to this § 445a recourse case with its clock starting at the date risk passed to the consumer rather than at the seller's own risk-passing date (§ 478 Abs. 1 BGB).
- A manufacturer within the repair obligation's scope must repair a qualifying Ware on a consumer's request within a reasonable period once it has possession of or access to the Ware, for a reasonable fee or free of charge, and return it to a condition fulfilling its intended use; where a fee-based repair fails to do that, the consumer gets cure, self-repair reimbursement, fee reduction, or damages (§ 479b Abs. 1, 3, 4 BGB). This repair duty exists only for as long as, and to the extent that, EU law under Anhang II of Directive (EU) 2024/1799 requires the manufacturer to guarantee the Ware's repairability, including spare-part availability, and it does not exist where repair is factually or legally impossible (§ 479b Abs. 2 Satz 1, 2 BGB). Where the manufacturer has no EU presence, the duties fall to its EU representative, then the importer, then the distributor (§ 479f BGB).
- Where a manufacturer has given a consumer a Haltbarkeitsgarantie (a guarantee that the item keeps a stated condition for a stated period), the consumer gets, during that period, a minimum Nacherfüllung claim against the manufacturer directly, running alongside the claim against the seller, under § 439 Abs. 2, 3, 5 and 6 Satz 2 and § 475 Abs. 3 Satz 1 and Abs. 6 (§ 479 Abs. 3 BGB).
- Check the buyer's own knowledge of the defect at conclusion before handling a claim: a defect the buyer actually knew of then excludes the buyer's remedies for it outright; a defect the buyer missed only through gross negligence still excludes the remedies unless the seller fraudulently concealed the defect or gave its own Garantie on the item's condition, in which case the remedies survive despite the buyer's negligence (§ 442 Abs. 1 BGB). A right entered in the land register must still be removed by the seller even where the buyer already knew of it (§ 442 Abs. 2 BGB).
- Where the buyer reduces the price instead of withdrawing, process it as a declaration to the seller, not a self-executing adjustment; calculate the reduced price in the ratio the item's defect-free value would have stood to its actual value at conclusion, estimating where needed, and refund any amount the buyer already paid above the reduced price (§ 441 Abs. 1, 3, 4 BGB).
- For a consumer sale, let withdrawal or a damages claim instead of full performance proceed without first setting the business a cure deadline wherever any one of five conditions is met: the business did not cure within a reasonable period after notice of the defect; a defect still shows up despite the business's attempted cure; the defect is so serious that immediate withdrawal is justified; the business refused proper cure; or it is obvious the business will not properly cure (§ 475d Abs. 1, 2 BGB).

## Prescribed wording

None: §§ 433 to 445c and 474 to 479g fix no mandatory phrase for any notice, label, or declaration in this file. § 479 Abs. 1 fixes five substantive content items a Garantieerklärung must cover and § 479d fixes two information duties a manufacturer must meet, but neither prescribes exact wording for them.

## Open points to raise

- Whether a specific refusal of the buyer's chosen cure method actually clears the disproportionate-cost weighing in § 439 Abs. 4 (item's defect-free value, defect's significance, availability of the other cure method without substantial disadvantage), since this is a case-by-case defence and not a cost ceiling the business can fix in advance.
- Whether the goods a business sells fall within a product group listed in Anhang II of Directive (EU) 2024/1799, since that single fact switches on the whole manufacturer repair-obligation subtitle (§ 479a BGB).
- Whether continuous provision of digital elements is actually agreed for a given Ware with digital elements, and for how long, since an unfixed duration falls back to the objective-requirement period or the two-year floor rather than ending the duty by silence (§ 475b Abs. 4 Nr. 2, § 475c Abs. 1, 2 BGB).
- Whether a Garantieerklärung actually reaches the consumer on a durable medium by delivery and carries all five Abs. 1 content items, since a shortfall does not void the Garantieverpflichtung but does leave the business exposed on a point a consumer can raise (§ 479 Abs. 1, 2, 4 BGB).
- Whether a given deviation clause was agreed before or after notice of a defect reached the business, since the two-step route in § 476 only works before notice and nothing in this file settles the fact question of when notice occurred in a particular case.

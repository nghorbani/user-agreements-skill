# BGB standard contract terms (AGB-Recht)

Citation: BGB §§ 305 to 310, as last amended by Art. 6 G v. 23.7.2026 I Nr. 226
Version: Neugefasst durch Bek. v. 2.1.2002 I 42, 2909; 2003, 738
Official text: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/
Law as of: 2026-10-20

This file covers the inclusion, content-control and scope rules for Allgemeine Geschäftsbedingungen
(AGB, standard contract terms): §§ 305 to 310 BGB. It works alongside the Unfair Contract Terms
Directive (`references/eu/uctd.md`), which sets the EU floor for the same subject, and alongside
`references/de/bgb-fernabsatz.md`, which covers the separate e-commerce order-button and
cancellation-button duties (§§ 312i to 312k BGB) that a standard-terms document must not try to
narrow or displace.

## When it applies

- A term counts as AGB whenever it is preformulated for a multitude of contracts and one party (the
  Verwender, the party putting the term forward) presents it to the other at conclusion; it makes no
  difference whether the term sits in a separate document or in the contract text itself, how long
  it is, what typeface it uses, or what form the contract takes. A term the parties actually
  negotiated point by point is not AGB at all (§ 305 Abs. 1 BGB).
- A term becomes part of the contract only if the Verwender points the other party to it, expressly
  or, where an express pointer is disproportionately difficult given how the contract is concluded,
  by a clearly visible on-site notice; gives the other party a reasonable chance to learn its
  content, accounting for a physical impairment of the other party the Verwender could recognise; and
  the other party agrees to it applying (§ 305 Abs. 2 BGB). The parties can agree in advance that a
  given set of AGB will govern a specified kind of transaction, keeping to these same requirements
  (§ 305 Abs. 3 BGB).
- A narrow list of regulated transactions is included without the pointer and reasonable-opportunity
  conditions, so long as the other party still agrees to the terms applying: transport tariffs and
  conditions approved by the competent transport authority or under international agreements, and
  fare conditions approved under the Personenbeförderungsgesetz for tram, trolleybus and scheduled
  road transport; AGB for electricity, gas, telecommunications, post and rail published in the
  Bundesnetzagentur's official gazette and kept available at the Verwender's premises, where used for
  off-premises postal-box deliveries or for telecommunications, information or other services
  rendered in a single act directly through distance-communication means during performance of a
  telecommunications service, where making the AGB accessible before conclusion would otherwise be
  disproportionately difficult; and the approved investment conditions of a capital management
  company (§ 305a BGB).
- An individually negotiated term always prevails over a conflicting AGB term, however the AGB is
  worded on the point (§ 305b BGB). A term that is so unusual, given the circumstances and especially
  the contract's outward appearance, that the other party need not expect it never becomes part of
  the contract; this is non-inclusion, a stronger result than mere invalidity (§ 305c Abs. 1 BGB).
  Any remaining doubt in interpreting an AGB term is resolved against the Verwender (§ 305c Abs. 2
  BGB).
- The content-control rules of § 307 Abs. 1, 2 and §§ 308, 309 apply only to a term that departs from,
  or adds to, a statutory rule; a term that does nothing more can still be invalid under § 307 Abs. 1
  Satz 2 read with Satz 1, which also catches an unclear or hard-to-follow term (§ 307 Abs. 3 BGB).
- Scope depends on the counterparty. Against a business counterparty, a public-law legal person, or a
  public-law special fund, § 305 Abs. 2, 3, § 308 Nr. 1, 2 to 9 and § 309 all fall away; § 307 Abs. 1,
  2 still applies and can still invalidate a term matching a § 308 Nr. 1, 2 to 9 or § 309 category,
  with commercial custom and usage weighed in appropriately; where VOB/B Teil B is incorporated whole,
  without substantive changes, in the version current at conclusion, § 307 Abs. 1, 2 and § 308 Nr. 1a,
  1b do not reach individual VOB/B terms for content control (§ 310 Abs. 1 BGB).
- § 307 and § 308 Nr. 1a, 1b also do not apply to contracts for banking business (Bankgeschäfte under
  § 1 Abs. 1 Satz 2 KWG), financial services (§ 1 Abs. 1a Satz 2 KWG), investment
  and ancillary investment services (§ 2 Abs. 2, 3 WpIG), payment services (§ 1 Abs. 1 Satz 2 ZAG),
  capital-management-company business (§ 20 Abs. 2, 3 KAGB), or stock-exchange business (§ 2 Abs. 1
  BörsG), where a trader lawfully conducts that business as its trade and contracts with another
  trader who can lawfully conduct the same kind of business at its own seat or branch, or with a
  großer Unternehmer (large undertaking) who can do so; a trader counts as a large undertaking if, in
  each of the two calendar years before conclusion, it met two of: average annual headcount of at
  least 250 employees (§ 267 Abs. 5 HGB); revenue over EUR 50 million; or a balance-sheet total over
  EUR 43 million (§ 267 Abs. 4a HGB). The same carve-out reaches contracts with the Deutsche
  Bundesbank, the Kreditanstalt für Wiederaufbau, a public-debt-management body under § 2 Abs. 1 Nr.
  3a KWG, a resolution institution set up under §§ 8a, 8b Stabilisierungsfondsgesetz, the World Bank,
  the International Monetary Fund, the European Central Bank, the national central banks of EEA and
  UK states, the European Investment Bank, or a comparable international financial organisation (§
  310 Abs. 1a BGB).
- §§ 308, 309 do not apply to electricity, gas, district-heating and water utilities' contracts with
  Sonderabnehmer (special customers, outside standard tariff supply) so long as the supply conditions
  do not disadvantage the customer against the regulations on general conditions for tariff-customer
  supply; the same carve-out applies correspondingly to wastewater-disposal contracts (§ 310 Abs. 2
  BGB).
- Against a consumer (Verbrauchervertrag), the whole Abschnitt applies with three modifications: AGB
  count as put forward by the trader unless the consumer introduced them into the contract; § 305c
  Abs. 2 and §§ 306, 307 to 309, plus Art. 46b EGBGB, apply to a preformulated term even if meant for
  one-off use only, so long as the consumer, because it was preformulated, could not influence its
  content; and assessing unreasonable disadvantage under § 307 Abs. 1, 2 also takes into account the
  circumstances surrounding conclusion (§ 310 Abs. 3 BGB).
- The whole Abschnitt is excluded for contracts in inheritance, family and company law, and for
  Tarifverträge, Betriebs- and Dienstvereinbarungen (collective bargaining agreements, works and
  service agreements, which for § 307 Abs. 3 purposes count as statutory rules themselves). Employment
  contracts get the Abschnitt with its labour-law-specific features taken into account, except that §
  305 Abs. 2, 3 (the inclusion conditions) does not apply to them (§ 310 Abs. 4 BGB).

## What the documents must say

- Where a termination event (withdrawal or ordinary/extraordinary termination by either party) is
  addressed, the Verwender cannot keep a release right the statute makes depend on a justified,
  contract-stated reason, without stating that reason in the contract; a continuing-obligation
  contract is carved out of this specific ban (§ 308 Nr. 3 BGB).
- Where the Verwender reserves a right to change or deviate from the promised performance, state the
  limits of that right so that accepting the change, with the Verwender's interests weighed in, stays
  reasonable for the other party (§ 308 Nr. 4 BGB).
- Where a clause treats the other party's silence or inaction as a deemed declaration, give that party
  a reasonable period to make an express declaration instead, and commit, at the start of that period,
  to flag specifically what that silence or inaction is deemed to mean (§ 308 Nr. 5 Buchst. a, b BGB).
- Where the Verwender reserves a release right for unavailability of performance under the Nr. 3
  carve-out, commit to notify the other party of the unavailability without delay and to refund any
  counter-performance without delay (§ 308 Nr. 8 Buchst. a, b BGB).
- Where the Verwender bars assignment of a right the other party holds against it (beyond a money
  claim, which Nr. 9 Buchst. a covers separately), the bar is permitted only where the Verwender has a
  legitimate interest in it that outweighs the other party's legitimate interest in assignability; a
  clause reaching further than that weighing requires is invalid (§ 308 Nr. 9 Buchst. b BGB).
- An automatic-renewal clause for a recurring-supply or recurring-service continuing obligation is
  valid only if it extends the contract onto an indefinite term and gives the other party a
  contractual right to end the renewed contract at any time on no more than one month's notice; any
  statutory termination right the other party has stands alongside this contractual right and does
  not substitute for it (§ 309 Nr. 9 Buchst. b BGB).
- Where the Verwender reserves a right, after withdrawal or termination by either side, to claim
  compensation for use or for expenses, state that compensation or expense claim at a reasonable
  level; the statute invalidates only an unreasonably high claim, not a cost-based one (§ 308 Nr. 7
  Buchst. a, b BGB).

## What the documents must not say

- No clause may set an unreasonably long or insufficiently determined period for the Verwender to
  accept or reject an offer, or to render performance; a reservation to perform only after the § 355
  Abs. 1, 2 withdrawal period has run is carved out of this specific ban (§ 308 Nr. 1 BGB).
- Against a non-consumer counterparty, no clause may reserve an unreasonably long time for the
  Verwender to satisfy a payment claim of the other party; in case of doubt, more than 30 days after
  receiving the counter-performance, or after receiving an invoice or equivalent payment statement
  following that receipt, is unreasonably long (§ 308 Nr. 1a BGB).
- Against a non-consumer counterparty, no clause may reserve an unreasonably long time for the
  Verwender to satisfy a payment claim only after inspecting or accepting the counter-performance; in
  case of doubt, more than 15 days after receiving the counter-performance is unreasonably long (§ 308
  Nr. 1b BGB).
- No clause may reserve an unreasonably long or insufficiently determined Nachfrist (supplementary
  period) for the Verwender's own performance, departing from statute (§ 308 Nr. 2 BGB).
- No clause may let the Verwender treat an explicit payment-received or receipt declaration as
  deemed access to the other party (fiction of receipt) for a declaration of particular importance
  to the Verwender (§ 308 Nr. 6 BGB).
- No clause may charge a price increase for goods or services to be delivered or rendered within
  four months of conclusion; a continuing-obligation contract is carved out of this specific ban (§
  309 Nr. 1 BGB).
- No clause may exclude or restrict the other party's statutory right to refuse performance under §
  320 BGB, or its right of retention arising from the same contractual relationship, in particular by
  conditioning the retention right on the Verwender's own acknowledgment of a defect (§ 309 Nr. 2
  Buchst. a, b BGB).
- No clause may strip the other party of the power to set off an undisputed or finally established
  claim (§ 309 Nr. 3 BGB).
- No clause may excuse the Verwender from the statutory duty to send a reminder or to set a period
  for performance or supplementary performance (§ 309 Nr. 4 BGB).
- No clause may fix a flat compensation or diminished-value claim that exceeds the damage or
  diminished value ordinarily expected in the regulated cases, or that denies the other party the
  express right to prove no damage or diminished value arose at all, or a materially lower one than
  the flat sum (§ 309 Nr. 5 Buchst. a, b BGB).
- No clause may impose a contractual penalty on the other party for non-acceptance or late acceptance
  of performance, payment default, or the other party's own withdrawal from the contract (§ 309 Nr. 6
  BGB).
- No clause may exclude or limit liability for death, personal injury or harm to health arising from a
  negligent breach of duty by the Verwender or an intentional or negligent breach by its statutory
  representative or vicarious agent (§ 309 Nr. 7 Buchst. a BGB); this exclusion carries no carve-out
  for any contract type. No clause may exclude or limit liability for other damage arising from a
  grossly negligent breach of duty by the Verwender, or an intentional or grossly negligent breach by
  its statutory representative or vicarious agent, with narrow carve-outs for approved tram, trolleybus
  and scheduled-road-transport liability limits that do not disadvantage the passenger against the 27
  February 1970 regulation on general carriage conditions for tram and trolleybus transport, and for
  state-approved lottery or wagering contracts (§ 309 Nr. 7 Buchst. b BGB).
- No clause may exclude or restrict the other party's right to withdraw from the contract for a
  Verwender-attributable breach that is not itself a defect in the goods or the work, subject to the
  same transport-conditions carve-out as Nr. 7 (§ 309 Nr. 8 Buchst. a BGB). For contracts to supply
  newly manufactured goods or to render work services, no clause may: exclude claims for a defect
  entirely or partly, limit them to claims against a third party, or make them depend on first suing a
  third party; limit the other party's claims to a right to supplementary performance alone, unless
  the other party expressly keeps the right to reduce the price, or, where the defect is not in a
  construction service, to withdraw instead, if supplementary performance fails; exclude or limit the
  Verwender's duty to bear or reimburse the expenses needed for supplementary performance under § 439
  Abs. 2, 3 or § 635 Abs. 2 BGB; make supplementary performance conditional on advance payment of the
  full price or of a part disproportionately high given the defect; set a cut-off period for notifying
  a non-obvious defect shorter than the period the next point allows; or shorten the limitation period
  for a defect claim in the cases of § 438 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 or § 634a Abs. 1 Nr. 2 BGB, or, in other cases,
  bring it under one year from the statutory start of limitation (§ 309 Nr. 8 Buchst. b, Doppelbuchst.
  aa to ff BGB).
- For a recurring-supply or recurring-service continuing obligation, no clause may bind the other
  party to an initial term longer than two years, or to a silent renewal the other party is bound by
  (except on the Nr. 9 Buchst. b terms above), or to a termination notice period longer than one month
  before the end of the initially agreed term, to the other party's detriment; contracts for the
  delivery of goods customarily sold together, and insurance contracts, are carved out of this whole
  category (§ 309 Nr. 9 BGB).
- No clause may let a third party step into the Verwender's position under a sale, loan, service or
  work contract, unless the clause names the third party, or gives the other party a right to leave
  the contract (§ 309 Nr. 10 BGB).
- No clause may impose on an agent who concludes the contract for the other party a personal liability
  or guarantee obligation without an express, separate declaration to that effect, or, for an agent
  acting without authority, a liability going beyond § 179 BGB (§ 309 Nr. 11 Buchst. a, b BGB).
- No clause may shift the burden of proof to the other party's disadvantage, in particular by placing
  on it the burden for circumstances within the Verwender's own sphere of responsibility, or by having
  the other party confirm particular facts; a separately signed or separately qualified-electronically-
  signed acknowledgment of receipt is carved out of the confirmed-facts ban (§ 309 Nr. 12 Buchst. a, b
  BGB).
- No clause may bind a notice or declaration owed to the Verwender or a third party to a stricter form
  than written form in a contract the law requires to be notarised, to a stricter form than text form
  in any other contract, or to special access requirements (§ 309 Nr. 13 Buchst. a, b, c BGB); this
  reaches a clause confining a consumer to a single electronic channel, such as a cancellation button,
  where the statute requiring that channel means it to widen the other party's options, not to replace
  every other lawful way of declaring (cross-reference: § 312k BGB in `references/de/bgb-fernabsatz.md`
  is additive in exactly this sense).
- No clause may require the other party to attempt an out-of-court dispute-resolution settlement
  before suing the Verwender (§ 309 Nr. 14 BGB).
- For a works contract, no clause may demand instalment payments materially higher than § 632a Abs. 1
  and § 650m Abs. 1 BGB allow, or reduce the security the Verwender must provide under § 650m Abs. 2
  BGB below what that provision requires (§ 309 Nr. 15 Buchst. a, b BGB).
- No severability clause, alternative contract design, relabelling or restructuring may do § 306's job
  on the Verwender's behalf: where a term fails for non-inclusion or invalidity, the contract stays
  valid and the gap is filled by statutory law, unless holding the other party to the contract, even
  with that statutory substitute, would be an unreasonable hardship for them; nothing in this Abschnitt
  may be circumvented by a different arrangement achieving the same result (§§ 306, 306a BGB).
- A clause letting the Verwender change the contract, including a continuing obligation's main
  payment duty, through the other party's mere silence, with no substantive limit on what can be
  changed or how far the change can reach, deviates from the essential idea that a contract is only
  amended by an actual offer and a matching acceptance (§ 305 Abs. 2, § 311 Abs. 1, §§ 145 ff. BGB) and
  unreasonably disadvantages the other party (§ 307 Abs. 1 Satz 1, Abs. 2 Nr. 1 BGB); this holds even
  where a separate statute, such as § 675g Abs. 2 BGB for payment-services framework contracts, also
  permits a deemed-consent mechanism for the same change, because meeting that other statute's formal
  notice and objection requirements does not by itself satisfy § 307 review, and the two reviews run
  independently of each other (BGH, judgment of 27 April 2021, XI ZR 26/20). Giving the other party a
  matching special termination right alongside the deemed-consent offer does not cure this: it offers
  no real advantage over simply objecting to the change, and it does not narrow the change-scope
  itself (BGH, judgment of 27 April 2021, XI ZR 26/20). The two clause categories this judgment struck
  down, a terms-and-conditions change clause and a fee-change clause for services the customer
  typically uses on a continuing basis, cannot be severed into a valid termination-right part and an
  invalid change-mechanism part, because the two are not separable, self-contained stipulations (BGH,
  judgment of 27 April 2021, XI ZR 26/20).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- The business can show, for a given counterparty and contract, that the § 305 Abs. 2 inclusion
  conditions were actually met (the pointer or on-site notice, the reasonable opportunity to read the
  terms, and the other party's consent) rather than only that the terms existed on a website at the
  time; this is the inclusion trail a drafter has to be able to reconstruct after the fact.
- Any mechanism letting the business change ongoing contract terms, including price or fee changes
  for continuing obligations, uses an actual offer the other party must accept, not a silence-as-
  acceptance design with no substantive cap on what can change or by how much, and no clause in the
  terms frames a matching termination right as making an open-ended deemed-consent mechanism
  acceptable on its own (BGH, judgment of 27 April 2021, XI ZR 26/20).
- The counterparty-classification step (consumer, ordinary business, named financial-sector or large-
  business counterparty, utility special customer, excluded-area contract, employment) happens before
  drafting any specific clause, since § 310 determines which of §§ 305 Abs. 2, 3, 307, 308, 309 even
  apply to that specific relationship.
- A subscription or recurring-service contract's initial term, renewal mechanism and termination
  notice period are checked together against § 309 Nr. 9, since the three elements (up to two years;
  renewal only onto an indefinite term with a matching right to end it on at most one month's notice;
  a notice period of at most one month before the end of the initial term) must all hold at once, and
  a contractual termination right under this clause stands beside, never instead of, any statutory
  termination right the customer separately has.
- Records exist to evidence what information or notice a given counterparty received and when, for
  any term relying on an exception, a weighing test, or a reasonableness standard in this file, since
  several of its invalidity rules turn on what was actually disclosed or offered to that counterparty.

## Dates

- 2026-10-20: law-as-of date for this file. No section in §§ 305 to 310 BGB states its own
  section-specific effective date separate from the general version line; § 308 and § 310 each carry
  only a cross-reference footnote to § 34 BGBEG on their application, with no substantive effect of
  their own.
- 2021-04-27: BGH, judgment XI ZR 26/20 (ECLI:DE:BGH:2021:270421UXIZR26.20.0), holding that § 675g
  Abs. 2 BGB does not displace § 307 BGB content control and that an unlimited deemed-consent clause
  for changing continuing-obligation terms fails that control.
- 2021-06-09: the Rechtsausschuss finished its deliberation and adopted the Beschlussempfehlung
  behind the current § 308 Nr. 9 and § 309 Nr. 9 wording (Drucksache 19/30840, dated 2021-06-18).

## Open points to raise

- Whether any clause in the business's terms lets it change continuing-obligation terms, including a
  fee or price change, through the customer's silence rather than an actual accepted offer, since BGH,
  judgment of 27 April 2021, XI ZR 26/20 treats an unlimited version of exactly this design as invalid
  under § 307 BGB regardless of a separate statute, such as § 675g BGB, that also regulates deemed
  consent for the same change; a narrower, substantively capped version was not before the court and
  its validity is not settled by this judgment.
- Whether the business's counterparty in a given contract is a consumer, an ordinary business, a
  named financial-sector or large-business counterparty, a utility special customer, or falls into one
  of the excluded areas of law, since § 310 changes which rules in this file even apply.
- Whether a subscription-style continuing obligation's renewal and notice-period design in fact meets
  all three § 309 Nr. 9 Buchst. b conditions at once (renewal onto an indefinite term; a matching
  contractual right to end it at any time on at most one month's notice; a matching statutory right
  left unaffected), since a design meeting only some of them is invalid outright under § 309, with no
  room for the Verwender to argue reasonableness in context the way § 308 allows.

# BGB consumer contracts and distance selling

Citation: BGB §§ 13, 14, 126b, 312 to 312m, as last amended by Art. 6 G v. 23.7.2026 I Nr. 226
Version: Neugefasst durch Bek. v. 2.1.2002 I 42, 2909; 2003, 738
Official text: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/
Law as of: 2026-10-20

## When it applies

- A Verbraucher (consumer) is any natural person entering a transaction for purposes predominantly outside their trade, business, or independent professional activity; the purpose of the specific transaction controls, not the person's general occupation (§ 13 BGB). An Unternehmer (trader) is a natural or legal person, or a rechtsfähige Personengesellschaft (legally capable partnership, itself able to acquire rights and incur liabilities), acting in its trade, business, or independent professional activity when concluding the transaction (§ 14 BGB). A terms document may define these terms for convenience but may not narrow either to strip a natural person of consumer status for an out-of-trade transaction (§ 312m Abs. 1 BGB).
- Chapters 1 and 2 of this Untertitel apply to a Verbrauchervertrag (consumer contract) only where the consumer undertakes to pay a price (§ 312 Abs. 1 BGB), or provides or undertakes to provide personal data, unless the trader processes that data solely to perform its own contractual or legal duty and for no other purpose (§ 312 Abs. 1a BGB).
- A long list of contract types is narrowed to only § 312a Abs. 1, 3, 4 and 6: notarized contracts, land-rights transfers, consumer construction, social services, residential tenancies, timeshare, medical treatment, everyday-goods rounds, vending machines, payphones, single telecom connections, low-value off-premises deals under 40 euros paid on the spot, forced sales, and passenger transport (§ 312 Abs. 2 BGB). Social-service and tenancy contracts also get the off-premises/distance definitions, the withdrawal-information duty, and the withdrawal right, except tenancies where the tenant inspected the dwelling first (§ 312 Abs. 3 BGB). Insurance gets only § 312a Abs. 3 to 6 (§ 312 Abs. 4 BGB). A financial-service relationship built from an initial agreement plus a series of connected follow-on transactions, or from a series of same-kind transactions in a row without such an agreement, gets the Chapter 1 and 2 duties only for the first transaction in the series; a new series starts once more than a year passes without a transaction of the same kind (§ 312a Abs. 5 BGB applies to every transaction regardless) (§ 312 Abs. 5 BGB). Package travel gets a narrower named subset, applying even to a non-consumer traveller; an off-premises package contract also gets the withdrawal right unless the oral negotiations were at the consumer's own request (§ 312 Abs. 6 BGB). Confirm which bucket a contract falls into before drafting the full regime against it (§ 312 Abs. 2 to 6 BGB).
- An außerhalb von Geschäftsräumen geschlossener Vertrag (off-premises contract) is one of four fact patterns, all requiring the consumer and trader physically present together away from the trader's business premises, or an immediately preceding such contact before conclusion elsewhere (§ 312b Abs. 1 BGB). A Fernabsatzvertrag (distance contract) uses exclusively distance-communication means, such as letters, phone, fax, email, SMS, or digital services, within a system organized for distance sale (§ 312c Abs. 1, 2 BGB).
- Any statute prescribing Textform (text form) is satisfied by a readable declaration naming the declarant, on a dauerhafter Datenträger (durable medium: lets the recipient store a personally addressed declaration accessibly for a period appropriate to its purpose, and reproduces it unchanged). No signature is required (§ 126b BGB); text form underlies the § 312h cancellation-by-proxy declaration and the § 312k cancellation-receipt confirmation.

## What the documents must say

- Off-premises and distance contracts: inform the consumer per EGBGB Art. 246a, becoming contract content unless expressly agreed otherwise; for financial services, inform instead per EGBGB Art. 246b §§ 1 to 3 and design the online interface per Art. 246b § 4 (§ 312d BGB). Costs may be demanded only to the extent the trader informed the consumer of them per EGBGB Art. 246a § 1 Abs. 1 Satz 1 Nr. 7 (§ 312e BGB).
- Where the trader, or someone calling in its name or on its instructions, phones the consumer to conclude a contract, the caller must disclose, at the start of the conversation, its own identity and, where applicable, the identity of the person it is calling for, plus the commercial purpose of the call; for distance contracts on financial services, the trader must also tell the consumer if the call is being recorded or could be recorded (§ 312a Abs. 1 BGB).
- Off-premises contracts: promptly hand the consumer, on paper or another durable medium with consent, a signed contract copy or a confirmation reproducing the contract content, including the EGBGB Art. 246a information unless already given pre-contractually (§ 312f Abs. 1 BGB). Distance contracts: the same confirmation on a durable medium, within a reasonable time and at the latest on delivery or before the service starts (§ 312f Abs. 2 BGB). For digital content not on a physical medium, the copy or confirmation must also record, where applicable, express consent to an early start and the confirmed knowledge that this means losing the withdrawal right (§ 312f Abs. 3 BGB); § 312f does not apply to financial-service contracts (§ 312f Abs. 4 BGB).
- Off-premises and distance contracts carry the § 355 right of withdrawal, mechanics and effects in references/de/bgb-widerruf.md, subject to the statutory exclusion list (§ 312g Abs. 1 BGB).
- E-commerce generally: means to recognize and correct input errors before order submission; the EGBGB Art. 246c information in good time before submission; prompt electronic confirmation of order receipt; and retrievable, storable contract terms at conclusion (§ 312i Abs. 1 BGB), the first three of which do not apply to a contract concluded exclusively through individual communication (§ 312i Abs. 2 BGB); the fourth (retrievable, storable contract terms) still applies even then. A consumer-facing e-commerce site must also state, no later than the start of the ordering process, whether delivery restrictions exist and which payment means are accepted (§ 312j Abs. 1 BGB).
- A consumer e-commerce contract that obliges payment: make the EGBGB Art. 246a § 1 Abs. 1 Satz 1 Nr. 1, 5 to 8, 11a, 14 and 15 information (price, main characteristics, delivery, duration, minimum term, and the rest of that list) available clearly, comprehensibly, and in a highlighted manner, immediately before order submission; a link to it is not enough (§ 312j Abs. 2 BGB).
- A trader letting consumers conclude a continuing paid contract via a website: provide a Kündigungsschaltfläche (cancellation button), permanently available, immediately and easily accessible, routing directly to a confirmation page (§ 312k Abs. 1, 2, Abs. 2 Satz 4 BGB; labels under Prescribed wording). That page must prompt and let the consumer give: cancellation type and, for extraordinary cancellation, the reason; unambiguous identification; unambiguous contract designation; the desired end date; and an electronic channel for the confirmation (§ 312k Abs. 2 Satz 3 Nr. 1 Buchst. a to e BGB). The statute asks for data that identifies the consumer, not for a login; whether a login step is allowed is pending (see Open points to raise).
- The duty reaches only a continuing obligation (Dauerschuldverhältnis) that obliges the trader to a paid performance, and only ordinary and extraordinary cancellation, not withdrawal or a warranty-based termination; it does not depend on whether the contract being cancelled was itself concluded online, only on whether the trader lets that kind of contract be concluded online at the time of cancellation, and it binds the trader even where a third party runs the website, such as a brokerage platform, in which case the trader must contractually secure that the operator provides the mechanism (§ 312k Abs. 1 Satz 1 BGB). Two carve-outs take a contract out of the duty entirely: one whose cancellation the law requires in a stricter form than text form, such as electronic form, written form, or notarization, and one concerning a financial-services website or a financial-services contract (§ 312k Abs. 1 Satz 2 Nr. 1, 2 BGB).
- If the buttons and confirmation page are not provided as § 312k Abs. 1 and 2 require, including where they are provided but do not meet those requirements, the consumer may cancel the affected contract at any time without observing any notice period at all, whether or not a cancellation right otherwise exists on the merits; the consumer's right to extraordinary cancellation stands either way (§ 312k Abs. 6 BGB).
- The confirmation page serves only to take these details and the cancellation declaration: § 312k Abs. 2 Satz 3 BGB governs its content exhaustively, so it carries no further fields, offers or information, such as an offer to pause the contract instead of cancelling (BGH, judgment of 16 July 2026, I ZR 200/25, BGH press release 128/2026).
- Let the consumer store their own cancellation declaration on a durable medium, timestamped, showing it was made by activating the confirmation button (§ 312k Abs. 3 BGB). Confirm receipt to the consumer immediately, electronically, in text form, with content, timestamp, and the resulting end date (§ 312k Abs. 4 Satz 1 BGB); a declaration made by activating the confirmation button is presumed to have reached the trader immediately (§ 312k Abs. 4 Satz 2 BGB). Where the consumer states no end date, cancellation takes effect, in case of doubt, at the earliest possible time (§ 312k Abs. 5 BGB).
- Online marketplace operators: inform the consumer as EGBGB Art. 246d requires (§ 312l Abs. 1 BGB; see references/de/egbgb.md).

## What the documents must not say

- No blanket exclusion of Chapter 1/2 duties beyond the genuine carve-outs in § 312 Abs. 2 to 6, and no redefinition of off-premises contract or distance contract narrower than §§ 312b, 312c to exclude what the statute covers; nothing in this file may be waived to the consumer's detriment, directly or by an alternative design that circumvents it (§ 312m Abs. 1 BGB).
- An extra-payment agreement beyond the main price needs the trader's express agreement; in e-commerce, such an agreement never becomes part of the contract if reached through a pre-ticked default (§ 312a Abs. 3 BGB).
- A payment-method surcharge agreement is void if no customary, reasonable free payment option exists, or the agreed fee exceeds the trader's actual cost of that method; a premium-rate support-line agreement is void if its fee exceeds the pure telecommunications cost, in which case the consumer owes the telecom provider only the basic usage fee and the provider claims that fee from the trader instead; either voidness leaves the rest of the contract intact (§ 312a Abs. 4 to 6 BGB).
- A checkout cannot bury the EGBGB Art. 246a Abs. 2 information behind a separate terms page reached only by a link; it must sit directly before the submit action, and the order button cannot carry generic wording in place of the prescribed phrase (§ 312j Abs. 2, 3 BGB; see Prescribed wording). A terms document cannot declare a contract concluded despite a non-compliant order button (§ 312j Abs. 4, § 312m Abs. 1 BGB); nor substitute a mere link for the § 312f copy or confirmation itself.
- A cancellation flow cannot ask for more than § 312k Abs. 2 Satz 3 Nr. 1 Buchst. a to e lists, and its confirmation page cannot carry retention offers, pause options or other content (§ 312k Abs. 2 Satz 3 BGB; BGH, judgment of 16 July 2026, I ZR 200/25). Whether the flow may ask the consumer to log in is pending before the BGH (see Open points to raise).
- The confirmation page cannot make the cancellation effective date mandatory: Buchst. d of the input list only lets the consumer state one, it does not require one, so a design that blocks submission without a date goes beyond what § 312k Abs. 2 Satz 3 Nr. 1 permits (§ 312k Abs. 2 Satz 3 Nr. 1 Buchst. d, Abs. 5 BGB).
- A cancellation-by-proxy clause under § 312h cannot require less than text form for the consumer's cancellation declaration or the power of attorney to cancel (§ 312h BGB).
- An order button or similar function placing an obligation to pay can carry wording other than "zahlungspflichtig bestellen", but only words that, read on their own and without anything else on the page, tell the average reasonably well informed and reasonably observant and circumspect consumer that activating it creates a payment obligation; the rest of the checkout, such as price information shown earlier or a terms link, cannot supply a meaning the button's own words lack (§ 312j Abs. 3 Satz 2 BGB; Case C-249/21, Fuhrmann-2-GmbH v B., judgment of 7 April 2022, paras. 26, 28 to 30, 33). A soft, reservation-flavored verb such as a word that in everyday language can also mean a free, no-obligation pre-order is a drafting risk for this reason (Case C-249/21, para. 17, 33).

## Product behaviour it assumes

- The order button, and the two cancellation-flow buttons, each carry exactly their prescribed wording with nothing else on them, placed where the statute requires rather than behind a separate link or an extra click (§ 312j Abs. 2, 3; § 312k Abs. 2 Satz 3, 4 BGB; PW-DE-1 to PW-DE-3). The cancellation button leads straight to the confirmation page, which shows only the statutory fields and the confirmation button (BGH, judgment of 16 July 2026, I ZR 200/25); a flow without a login step is the safe design while the login question is pending (see Open points to raise).
- Both cancellation buttons and the confirmation page stay reachable at any time, without a login step, including during a short maintenance outage, and the cancellation-effective-date field never blocks submission when left blank (§ 312k Abs. 2 Satz 4, Abs. 5 BGB); treat any gap in this, however brief, as exposing every affected contract to an any-time, no-notice cancellation under § 312k Abs. 6 BGB, so the mechanism needs its own monitoring, not a one-time launch check. Where a third party runs the website on the trader's behalf, the contract with that operator obliges it to keep the mechanism in place (§ 312k Abs. 1 Satz 1 BGB).
- Each order button or other control that places a paid order is reviewed on its own words alone, independent of the price display, an earlier screen, or a terms link nearby, for whether it unambiguously tells the consumer that activating it creates a payment obligation; a wording change elsewhere on the page never substitutes for fixing the button's own label (§ 312j Abs. 3 Satz 2 BGB; Case C-249/21, Fuhrmann-2-GmbH v B., judgment of 7 April 2022, paras. 26, 28 to 29, 33).
- The checkout gives an edit step for quantity, item, and address, in the flow itself before submission; a later support channel does not count (§ 312i Abs. 1 Nr. 1 BGB).
- The system generates and dispatches a paper or durable-medium copy or confirmation promptly after an off-premises conclusion, and a durable-medium confirmation within a reasonable time, at the latest by delivery, after a distance conclusion (§ 312f Abs. 1, 2 BGB).
- Records exist to prove, after the fact, which information was shown to a given consumer and when, since the trader bears the burden of proving it met an information duty (§ 312m Abs. 2 BGB); a marketplace operator gives the EGBGB Art. 246d information in the form that article sets (§ 312l Abs. 1 BGB; see references/de/egbgb.md).

## Prescribed wording

- PW-DE-1: see `references/prescribed-wording.md`. Quote: "zahlungspflichtig bestellen" (§ 312j Abs. 3 Satz 2 BGB). Where it goes: the order-submission button for any consumer e-commerce contract that obliges payment, legibly, with nothing else on the button besides this phrase or an equally unambiguous formulation.
- PW-DE-2: see `references/prescribed-wording.md`. Quote: "Verträge hier kündigen" (§ 312k Abs. 2 Satz 2 BGB). Where it goes: the cancellation button on any website letting a consumer conclude a continuing paid contract, legibly, with nothing else on the button besides this phrase or an equally unambiguous formulation; permanently available, immediately and easily accessible.
- PW-DE-3: see `references/prescribed-wording.md`. Quote: "jetzt kündigen" (§ 312k Abs. 2 Satz 3 Nr. 2 BGB). Where it goes: the confirmation button on the cancellation confirmation page the Kündigungsschaltfläche leads to, legibly, with nothing else on the button besides this phrase or an equally unambiguous formulation, submitting the cancellation declaration.

## Open points to raise

- Login in the cancellation flow: the Kammergericht held that a cancellation button leading first to a login with customer number and password (KG, decision of 18 November 2025, 5 UKl 10/25), and a confirmation page asking for a password (KG, decision of 11 November 2025, 5 U 6/25), both breach § 312k Abs. 2 Satz 3 and 4 BGB; the BGH hears both appeals on 5 November 2026 (I ZR 272/25 and I ZR 275/25, BGH press release 133/2026). Until the BGH rules, design the flow without a login step and check the decision before relying on this point.
- Whether the seller's product is in scope at all under § 312 Abs. 1/1a, or falls into one of the narrowed buckets in § 312 Abs. 2 to 6, since the duties in this file differ sharply between buckets.
- Whether any of the seller's contracts is concluded exclusively through individual communication (ausschließlich durch individuelle Kommunikation) for the § 312i/§ 312j carve-outs, since that status removes several e-commerce duties above.
- Whether the online interface in question is itself an Online-Marktplatz under § 312l Abs. 2, adding EGBGB Art. 246d interface-level duties on top of the seller's own terms document.
- Whether the business can reconstruct, after the fact, what information was shown to a specific consumer and when, given that § 312m Abs. 2 puts the burden of proving an information duty was met on the trader.
- Whether § 312k BGB reaches a continuing contract that arose before 1 July 2022: the committee report that introduced § 312k describes a transitional provision to this effect in Art. 229 EGBGB without stating the paragraph number the provision was finally given on promulgation (Deutscher Bundestag, Drucksache 19/30840); check the current EGBGB text for the assigned paragraph before relying on this for an existing contract.

# Assignment

Law as of: 2026-10-20

## Purpose

This clause covers two different things a business should not run together: the business
transferring its own rights and obligations under the contract to someone else (a sale of the
business, a merger, an asset transfer, outsourcing the contract to another group company), and the
other party assigning a right it holds against the business (most often a right to a refund or a
payment) to a third party. It does not cover substituting the other party's own contracting role
for someone else, which stays with whatever clause in this document governs who may hold the
account.

## Model wording

The business may transfer this agreement, including all of its rights and obligations under it, to
another entity, for example as part of a merger, acquisition, reorganisation or sale of some or all
of its assets. Where such a transfer would reduce the guarantees the user has under this agreement,
the business will tell the user before the transfer takes effect and will not make the transfer
without the user's agreement. [Core] The user may not assign a right it holds against the business
under this agreement to a third party without the business's consent, except that the user may
always assign a claim for money the business owes the user. The business will not refuse its
consent to any other assignment without a legitimate interest that outweighs the user's own
legitimate interest in being able to assign that right.

## Modules

### EU consumers

A term letting the business transfer its rights and obligations under the contract, where the
transfer could reduce the consumer's guarantees, without the consumer's agreement, is on the
Annex's indicative list of terms that may be unfair (Annex point (p) UCTD). Build the model
wording's transfer sentence around advance notice and the consumer's agreement whenever the
transfer could have that effect; a clause that reserves an unconditional right to transfer the
contract, with no notice and no consent step tied to a reduction in the consumer's guarantees,
matches this listed pattern directly.

A clause barring the consumer from assigning a money claim against the business (a refund, a
damages claim, a contractual payment owed to the consumer) is void outright, with no weighing test
and no carve-out (§ 308 Nr. 9 Buchst. a BGB). A clause barring the consumer from assigning any other
right against the business is valid only where the business has a legitimate interest in the bar
that outweighs the consumer's own legitimate interest in being able to assign that right; a blanket
non-assignment clause reaching every right the consumer holds, with no such weighing built in, goes
further than this permits (§ 308 Nr. 9 Buchst. b BGB). Keep the model wording's two carve-outs, for
money claims and for the weighing test, separate: collapsing them into one blanket consent
requirement drops the money-claim carve-out the statute gives unconditionally.

Both of these German rules sit inside § 308, which the content-control regime applies without a
reasonableness defence for the business once the clause matches the described pattern; this is a
stricter standard than the Annex's own indicative, rebuttable listing, so a clause that might survive
case-by-case review under the UCTD alone can still fail outright under § 308 Nr. 9 for a German
consumer.

### EU business customers

The UCTD's unfairness test does not reach a contract between a business and a business customer
(Art. 2(b), (c) UCTD), so Annex point (p) does not apply to this module. Against a German business
counterparty, § 308 (including Nr. 9) falls away entirely, but § 307 Abs. 1, 2 still applies to the
same clause and can still invalidate a transfer-of-contract term or an assignment-restriction term
that matches the § 308 Nr. 9 pattern, with commercial custom and usage weighed in appropriately for
that sector (§ 310 Abs. 1 BGB). Keep the model wording's notice-and-consent structure for a transfer
that would reduce the counterparty's guarantees, and keep the weighing test on any assignment bar
reaching beyond money claims, rather than dropping both on the assumption that a business
counterparty gets no protection at all.

### Germany

See the EU consumers and EU business customers modules above: Germany's own rule is § 308 Nr. 9 BGB,
not a separate national transposition of Annex point (p), and it reaches further than the Annex does
by voiding a money-claim assignment bar outright rather than only flagging it as potentially unfair.

### US

The references here do not cover US assignment or anti-assignment clauses; treat the US module as an
open point for counsel until a US federal or state reference on contract assignment and
anti-assignment clauses is added.

## Must not

- A clause reserving the business's right to transfer the contract, where the transfer could reduce
  the consumer's guarantees, must not omit advance notice and the consumer's agreement to that
  specific transfer (Annex point (p) UCTD).
- A clause must not bar a German consumer from assigning a money claim against the business under
  any circumstances; this bar is void outright with no weighing test available to save it (§ 308
  Nr. 9 Buchst. a BGB).
- A clause must not bar a German consumer from assigning any other right against the business
  without a stated weighing test showing the business's legitimate interest in the bar outweighs the
  consumer's legitimate interest in assignability (§ 308 Nr. 9 Buchst. b BGB).
- A clause must not apply the consumer's own money-claim carve-out, or the weighing test for other
  rights, as the operative rule for a German business counterparty instead: § 308 Nr. 9 itself falls
  away there, and only § 307 Abs. 1, 2 governs the same clause for that counterparty (§ 310 Abs. 1
  BGB).

## Drafting notes

`references/conflicts.md` does not cover this clause: it tracks US-versus-EU splits, and the EU
rules here (UCTD, BGB AGB-Recht, see Legal basis below) have no US counterpart in this skill yet to
compare against. Keep the EU consumer module's notice-and-consent condition and the two German
assignment-bar rules as a single set when drafting for a German consumer: a transfer clause that
passes the Annex's indicative test can still need the § 308 Nr. 9 carve-outs added before it is safe
for a German consumer specifically, since § 308 gives no case-by-case defence once a clause matches
its pattern.

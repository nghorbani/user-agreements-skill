# Acceptable use

Law as of: 2026-10-20

## Purpose

This clause states what a user may and may not do with the service: the conduct a user agrees not to
engage in (illegal content, spam and unsolicited bulk email, scraping, circumventing restrictions,
abusing other users or the service itself), stated as a restriction the user accepts rather than as
the provider's moderation process. It does not state how the provider reviews, acts on or responds to
a breach of this clause (notice handling, statements of reasons, suspension mechanics), which belongs
in `references/clauses/moderation-and-suspension.md`; it does not state what rights the provider takes
in content a user posts, or the publisher-immunity position for that content, which belongs in
`references/clauses/user-content.md`; and it does not state the rules for a commercial email program
the provider itself runs to reach users, which belongs in
`references/clauses/marketing-communications.md`.

## Model wording

[User name] agrees not to use [the service] to post, upload, transmit or otherwise make available any
content that is illegal, or to use [the service] for any purpose that is illegal. [User name] agrees
not to attempt to circumvent a restriction [trader name] has placed on the use of [the service], not to
probe, scan or test the vulnerability of [the service] or any system connected to it, and not to access
or use [the service] by any automated means (scraping, crawling or similar) except to the extent
[trader name] has made a documented interface available for that purpose.

[User name] agrees not to use [the service] to send, or to help send, an unsolicited commercial
electronic message that misstates who it is from, carries a subject line [user name] has reason to
think would mislead a reasonable recipient, or lacks a working way for the recipient to ask not to
receive further messages. [User name] agrees not to use an automated means to register for multiple
accounts in order to send messages that would otherwise break this paragraph, and not to relay such a
message through a system [user name] does not have authorization to use.

[Trader name] states this policy, with examples of what counts as a breach, in these terms so that
[user name] can read it before using [the service]; see [the moderation and suspension terms] for how
a reported or detected breach is reviewed and what happens to the account.

## Modules

### EU consumers

State the restrictions on use of the service, in clear, plain, intelligible, user-friendly and
unambiguous language, in the terms and conditions themselves, publicly available in an easily
accessible, machine-readable format (Art. 14(1) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065). Tell users of a significant
change to this clause once it is live (Art. 14(2) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065). Where the service is
primarily directed at, or predominantly used by, minors, write the restrictions so a minor can
understand them (Art. 14(3) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).

Apply and enforce this clause diligently, objectively and proportionately, weighing the rights and
legitimate interests of everyone affected, including a user's freedom of expression (Art. 14(4)
Regulation (EU) 2022/2065). Do not draft the clause, or apply it in practice, as a licence for the
provider to screen content before it is posted or to actively hunt for breaches: the Regulation puts no
general obligation on an intermediary service to monitor the information it transmits or stores, or to
seek out facts or circumstances indicating illegal activity (Art. 8 Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).

Where the provider operates an online platform and suspends a recipient for repeatedly breaching this
clause with manifestly illegal content, give that policy in a clear and detailed manner in the terms
and conditions, with examples of the facts and circumstances taken into account and the suspension's
duration (Art. 23(1), (4) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065); the suspension mechanics themselves, including the
prior-warning and case-by-case requirements, sit in
`references/clauses/moderation-and-suspension.md`.

### EU business customers

The same terms-and-conditions content duty runs regardless of whether the recipient of the service is
a consumer or a business, since the Regulation's recipient-facing duties are not limited by the
recipient's status (Art. 3(b) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).

### Germany

The references here do not cover Germany's own standard-terms-control rules for this clause. For
the German check on a prohibited-conduct list, see `references/de/bgb-agb.md` and
`references/checklists/de-agb.md`; see Drafting notes below for how that check applies to this
clause.

### US

Section 230 lets the clause describe the platform's right, voluntarily and in good faith, to restrict
access to material it considers obscene, lewd, lascivious, filthy, excessively violent, harassing or
otherwise objectionable, whether or not that material is constitutionally protected, and to give users
the technical means to do the same, framed as a discretionary right the platform holds rather than a
guaranteed screening service (47 U.S.C. § 230(c)(2)(A), (c)(2)(B)). The acceptable-use restrictions
themselves describe what the user agrees not to do; keep that separate from any statement that the
platform is not treated as the publisher or speaker of what a user posts, which belongs in
`references/clauses/user-content.md` (47 U.S.C. § 230(c)(1)).

Where the service lets a user send commercial email through or from it, such as an email marketing
feature, an invite function or a message-forwarding tool, state the conduct restriction so it matches
what CAN-SPAM actually requires of a commercial electronic mail message: no materially false or
misleading header information (15 U.S.C. § 7704(a)(1)), no subject heading the sender has reason to
know would mislead a reasonable recipient about the message's content (15 U.S.C. § 7704(a)(2)), and no
message once a recipient has opted out through the sender's own opt-out mechanism, sent more than 10
business days after the opt-out request arrives (15 U.S.C. § 7704(a)(4)(A)(i)). A reference to a
commercial entity or a link to its website does not, by itself, make a message commercial for this
purpose; the test looks at the message's contents and circumstances as a whole (15 U.S.C. §
7702(2)(D)).

State as a separate, freestanding restriction, not conditioned on whether a message is commercial under
CAN-SPAM: no using scripts or other automated means to register for multiple accounts in order to send
a message that would otherwise be unlawful (15 U.S.C. § 7704(b)(2)), and no knowingly relaying or
retransmitting such a message through a protected computer or network the user has no authorization to
access (15 U.S.C. § 7704(b)(3)). Both of these require an underlying breach of the message-content
rules above; neither stands alone.

## Must not

- No clause may state or imply that this policy, or the Digital Services Act, requires or authorizes
  blanket pre-screening or general monitoring of what users post (Art. 8 Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).
- No misuse or suspension policy referenced from this clause may describe suspension only in generic
  terms, such as a bare statement that abusive accounts may be suspended; it must give concrete examples
  of the facts and circumstances considered and state the suspension's duration (Art. 23(4) Regulation
  (EU) 2022/2065).
- No clause may claim the Section 230 good-faith-moderation immunity for content the platform itself
  creates or develops; that immunity, and the separate publisher and speaker immunity, both turn on the
  content being someone else's (47 U.S.C. § 230(f)(3)).
- No clause may promise guaranteed or complete screening of content against this policy before it is
  posted; a guarantee of that kind sits outside the voluntary, discretionary framing the Section 230
  good-faith immunity actually covers (47 U.S.C. § 230(c)(2)(A)).
- No restriction on sending commercial email through the service may be drafted, or enforced, in a way
  that would itself require materially false header information, a misleading subject line, or ignoring
  an opt-out request inside the 10 business day window; the restriction exists to prevent the user doing
  these things, not to excuse the provider from its own obligations where the provider is itself an
  initiator of such messages (15 U.S.C. § 7704(a)(1), (a)(2), (a)(4)(A)(i)).

## Drafting notes

No conflicts.md entry decides a module choice for this clause yet. The closest existing pattern is the
one `references/clauses/user-content.md` already notes for the hosting exemption and Section 230: a
US-style conduct policy commonly reads as a broad, unilateral provider discretion ("we may remove
anything for any reason"), while the EU module above requires that same discretion to be applied
diligently, objectively and proportionately, with the restriction itself written in clear, plain
language in the terms rather than left as a vague catch-all. Draft the core prohibited-conduct list
once, in plain language concrete enough to meet the EU clarity duty, rather than drafting two different
lists for two markets.

Keep the acceptable-use restrictions (what a user agrees not to do) and the misuse-suspension policy
(what happens when a user breaks this clause) as two different documents or sections even where they
sit in the same terms file: Art. 23(4) requires the suspension policy's own examples and stated
duration, which are about enforcement consequences, not about the underlying conduct rule stated here.
A reader of this clause who wants the consequences should be pointed to
`references/clauses/moderation-and-suspension.md` rather than finding them folded into this list.

The CAN-SPAM material in the US module only applies where the service itself is a channel for sending
email, an email marketing product, an invite-a-friend feature, a forwarding or sharing function, rather
than to every service in general; a service with no such feature needs no CAN-SPAM-specific language in
this clause, and the Section 230 and Digital Services Act modules still apply on their own.

Germany's own standard-terms-control check, whether a broad or vaguely worded prohibited-conduct list
survives §§ 305c, 307 BGB review as a surprising or unreasonably disadvantaging clause, is in
`references/de/bgb-agb.md` and `references/checklists/de-agb.md`; run that check against the
prohibited-conduct list drafted here rather than treating this clause as already covering it.

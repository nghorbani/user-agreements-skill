# Changes to these terms

Law as of: 2026-10-20

## Purpose

This clause tells a user how the business may change the terms that govern an ongoing relationship
(a subscription, an account, an app, any contract where the user keeps dealing with the business
after signing up), what notice the user gets, and what happens if the user does not want the new
version. It covers only changes the business itself initiates to its own standard terms; it does not
cover a price change tied to an index or a renewal-time price increase, which the price-and-payment
and term-renewal-and-cancellation clauses carry, and it does not cover a change to a digital
product's features for conformity reasons alone, which the warranty-and-conformity clause carries.

## Model wording

The business may update these terms from time to time. Where a change is more than a minor
clarification, correction of an error, or adjustment required to keep the service lawful, safe or
secure, the business will tell the user about it in advance, state the reason for the change and
when it takes effect, and give the user a reasonable period before the change applies. [For EU
consumers and the digital-content and digital-service module] Where the change is more than a minor
negative effect on the user's access to or use of the service, the user may end the contract free of
charge within the notice period described above. [Core] A change does not apply to a dispute that
already exists between the user and the business before the change takes effect. The business will
not ask the user to accept a change to these terms through silence or continued use alone where the
change affects a right the user already has or a payment the user already makes; where the law lets
the business treat continued use as acceptance, the business will still tell the user clearly, before
the change takes effect, what continuing to use the service after that date will mean, and what the
user can do instead. If the user does not agree to a change, the user's option is to stop using the
service and, where applicable, cancel the account under the term-renewal-and-cancellation clause,
before the change takes effect.

## Modules

### EU consumers

A term letting the business change these terms without a valid, contract-stated reason is itself
treated as unfair and void against the consumer; so is a term letting the business change a
characteristic of the product or service without a valid reason, however that term is framed
(Annex points (j), (k) UCTD). State the reason and the method for any change in the terms
themselves, rather than just in the notice sent when a change happens, since a change clause with no stated
reason and no stated method is exposed under both points (i) and (j) together even where a notice and
an exit right are later offered case by case. Keep the valid-reason requirement and the exit right
attached to every change clause offered to a consumer; dropping either one to shorten the clause is
not an option this module allows (Annex point (j); Guidance section 3.4.7).

Point (j) carries its own general carve-out for exactly this clause's core scenario: any business, not
only a financial-services supplier, may reserve the right to alter the terms of an indefinite-duration
contract unilaterally, provided the business gives the consumer reasonable notice and the consumer
stays free to dissolve the contract (Annex point 2(b), second sentence UCTD). This is why the model
wording above conditions a change on advance notice and the user's own choice to continue or leave
instead of asking for a contract-stated reason for every kind of change; the module still requires a
valid, contract-stated reason for a change falling outside this carve-out, and still requires one under
point (k) for any change to a characteristic of the product or service.

Where the contract is for digital content or a digital service supplied or kept accessible over a
period, add the Digital Content Directive's own four conditions as a single set, since a change that
fails any one of them is not permitted at all: the contract itself allows the change and states a
valid reason; the change carries no extra cost for the consumer; the consumer is told about it in a
clear and comprehensible way; and, where the change does more than minimally and negatively affect
the consumer's access to or use of the content or service, the consumer is told reasonably in
advance, on a durable medium, what the change is, when it takes effect, and that the consumer may
either terminate free of charge or keep the unmodified version instead (Art. 19(1), (4) DCD). Where
that last condition applies, give the consumer at least 30 days from receiving the notice, or from
the change taking effect, whichever is later, to exercise the free termination right (Art. 19(2)
DCD). None of this applies to a change that is itself required to keep the content or service in
conformity, such as a security update (Art. 8(2) DCD; this falls to the warranty-and-conformity
clause instead).

Do not rely on this module's change clause for a term the UCTD treats as the main subject matter or
the price itself: a price-variation mechanism is never covered by the Art. 4(2) main-subject-matter
or price exclusion, however transparently it is worded, so a price change still needs its own stated
reason, its own stated method, and the consumer's own matching right to cancel if the new price is
too high, carried in the price-and-payment clause, not assumed to be covered here (Annex point (l);
Guidance section 3.2.2).

### EU business customers

The UCTD's unfairness test does not reach a contract with a business customer (Art. 2(b), (c) UCTD),
and the Digital Content Directive's Art. 19 change conditions run to consumers only (Art. 3(1) DCD).
The core model wording may be used with a business customer without the consumer-specific exit
rights, but state the notice period and the method for change expressly in the contract, since
nothing in this module supplies a softer default than whatever the parties actually agree.

### Germany

A clause letting the business change these terms, including a price or fee change for a continuing
obligation, through the customer's silence or continued use, with no substantive limit on what can be
changed or by how much, unreasonably disadvantages the customer and is invalid, even where a separate
statute also permits a deemed-consent mechanism for that same kind of change and the business also
offers a matching special termination right (BGH, judgment of 27 April 2021, XI ZR 26/20). This is
why the model wording above states a change clause based on an actual notice and the user's own
choice to continue or to leave, not a clause that treats the user's inaction as acceptance of
whatever the business decides to change. A change clause that fails this way cannot be rescued by
severing it into a valid termination-right part and an invalid change-mechanism part, because German
courts have held the two are not separable (BGH, judgment of 27 April 2021, XI ZR 26/20); draft one
clause that is sound as a whole rather than relying on a severability clause to save part of a bad
one.

For a recurring-supply or recurring-service continuing obligation, any automatic-renewal design
inside the change-and-renewal clauses together must extend the contract only onto an indefinite term
and give the customer a contractual right to end the renewed contract at any time on at most one
month's notice; a statutory termination right the customer separately has stands beside this
contractual right and is never replaced by it (§ 309 Nr. 9 Buchst. b BGB). A clause reserving a
release right for the business that depends on a justified, contract-stated reason must state that
reason in the contract itself, rather than only when the business later relies on it (§ 308 Nr. 3 BGB). A
clause letting the business change or deviate from its own promised performance must state the
limits of that right so that, weighing the business's own interests, accepting the change still
stays reasonable for the other party (§ 308 Nr. 4 BGB).

### US

No change clause may apply to a dispute that has already accrued before the change takes effect, and
no change clause may bind a person who has only browsed the service without transacting to a changed
arbitration or forum term (9 U.S.C. §§ 401, 402; Heckman v. Live Nation Entertainment, Inc., 120 F.4th
670 (9th Cir. 2024)). Do not promise that a change takes effect only on explicit re-acceptance and
then, in an incorporated document the user does not see again, apply it through mere continued use
instead; that mismatch between what the clause promises and what the business actually does is itself
evidence of unconscionability (Heckman, 120 F.4th 670). Where the clause is presented through a
sign-in-wrap or similar design (an account action is stated to itself constitute agreement to linked
terms, including any future change to them), both the conspicuous-notice element and the unambiguous-
assent element of the formation test have to be satisfied on the one screen where that action
happens, not pieced together from notice shown on an earlier visit and an assent statement shown on a
later one (Chabolla v. ClassPass, Inc., 129 F.4th 1147 (9th Cir. 2025); see acceptance-and-formation
clause). A business relying on a legally required written disclosure delivered electronically for a
change notice meets the four-part ESIGN consent gate before substituting that electronic notice for a
paper one (15 U.S.C. § 7001(c)(1)(A) to (D); see acceptance-and-formation clause for the gate itself).

## Must not

- A change clause may not let the business alter these terms without a contract-stated valid reason,
  or alter a characteristic of the product or service without a valid reason, for an EU consumer
  (UCTD Annex points (j), (k)), except where the contract is of indefinite duration and the clause
  gives the consumer reasonable notice and a free right to dissolve the contract (Annex point 2(b),
  second sentence).
- A change clause for a digital content or digital service contract may not skip any of the four
  Art. 19(1) DCD conditions (contract-stated reason, no extra cost, clear notice, and, where the
  negative effect is more than minor, advance durable-medium notice plus a free 30-day termination
  right or a genuine unmodified option) for an EU consumer (Art. 19(1), (2), (4) DCD).
- A change clause may not let the business change continuing-obligation terms, including a fee or
  price, through a German customer's silence or continued use with no substantive cap on what can
  change, even where a separate statute also permits deemed consent for that change and a matching
  termination right is offered alongside it (BGH, judgment of 27 April 2021, XI ZR 26/20).
- A change clause may not apply to a dispute that already accrued before the change took effect, or
  bind a mere site visitor who never transacted, in the US (Heckman, 120 F.4th 670).
- A change clause may not promise one acceptance mechanism (explicit re-acceptance) while an
  incorporated document actually applies the change through continued use alone; that mismatch is
  itself evidence of unconscionability in the US (Heckman, 120 F.4th 670).

## Drafting notes

See `references/conflicts.md` for the governing split: a change clause built around silence or
continued use as acceptance is the single design that fails under the German courts' reading of
§ 307 BGB, under the DCD's Art. 19 conditions for an EU consumer, and, where it reaches an accrued
dispute or a non-transacting visitor, under US arbitration-specific doctrine as well; this clause
file resolves that split by building the core wording around actual notice and the user's own choice
to continue or leave, with the EU consumer module adding the DCD's digital-content conditions on top
and the Germany module explaining why a severability clause cannot rescue a silence-based design
after the fact.

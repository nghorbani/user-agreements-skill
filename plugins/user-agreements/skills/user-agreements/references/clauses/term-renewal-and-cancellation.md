# Term, renewal and cancellation

Law as of: 2026-10-20

## Purpose

This clause states how long the contract runs, how it renews, and how the customer ends it. It
covers a fixed-term agreement, an auto-renewing subscription, and a continuous service that runs
until cancelled. It sets what the sign-up and renewal screens must disclose, what notices must go
out before a renewal or a price change, and what the cancellation path itself must look like,
including the separate German online cancellation-button duty and the EU and German content-control
limits on an auto-renewal clause. It does not cover the withdrawal (cooling-off) right, which sits in
`references/clauses/withdrawal-eu.md`, or the price mechanics of a single transaction, which sit in
`references/clauses/price-and-payment.md`.

## Model wording

This agreement starts on the date [trader name] confirms your order and continues for [initial term].
Unless you cancel before the end of that term, it then renews automatically for successive
[renewal term] periods, at the price and on the terms in effect at the time of each renewal, until
you cancel or we end it as this clause allows.

Before you are charged for the first time, and before each renewal, we will show you, or send you,
the price you will be charged, how often you will be charged, and how to cancel.

You can cancel at any time. Cancelling stops the next renewal; it does not refund a period that has
already started unless this agreement or the law says otherwise. [Where a cancellation during an
already-paid period still gets a partial refund: add the refund terms here.]

[Germany, online continuing contract: add the cancellation button paragraph from the Germany module.]

## Modules

### EU consumers

The EU-wide floor sits in `references/eu/uctd.md` (UCTD Annex point (h): an automatic renewal needs a
reasonable, not unreasonably early, objection deadline) and in `references/checklists/eu-b2c.md`
(cancellation as easy as sign-up, no guilt-inducing barriers). Neither sets the specific term lengths
and notice periods the Germany module below states, so for an EU consumer outside Germany, draft from
the Germany module as the stricter model and treat the gap between the UCTD floor and the Germany
specifics as an open point (see Open points to raise).

### EU business customers

`references/de/bgb-agb.md` states that a business counterparty takes the German module's § 308 and
§ 309 content control out of play, leaving only the general § 307 fairness test; it does not set a
business-specific renewal or cancellation position of its own, and `references/checklists/eu-b2b.md`
carries no renewal-specific item either. Treat a business-facing renewal and cancellation clause as an
open point for counsel until a reference covering the business-customer position is added (see
Open points to raise).

### Germany

Where the contract is a recurring-supply or recurring-service continuing obligation and the terms are
preformulated AGB, state the renewal mechanism so that it extends the contract onto an indefinite
term, not a further fixed term, and gives the customer a contractual right to end the renewed contract
at any time on no more than one month's notice; this contractual right stands beside, not instead of,
any statutory termination right the customer separately has (§ 309 Nr. 9 Buchst. b BGB). Where the
contract instead binds the customer to an initial term, a silent-renewal clause the customer is bound
by, or a termination notice period before the end of the initial term, keep the initial term at two
years or less and the pre-end notice period at one month or less, to the customer's detriment in
neither respect; a contract for goods customarily sold together, and an insurance contract, fall
outside this whole paragraph (§ 309 Nr. 9 BGB). Where any termination event, whether withdrawal or an
ordinary or extraordinary termination by either side, lets [trader name] keep a release right that
depends on a contract-stated, justified reason, state that reason in the clause itself; a continuing-
obligation contract is carved out of this specific requirement (§ 308 Nr. 3 BGB).

Where [trader name] reserves a right to change the contract, including price, through the customer's
silence rather than an actual accepted offer, drop that design: an unlimited deemed-consent mechanism
for changing a continuing obligation's terms fails content control even where a separate statute also
regulates deemed consent for the same change, and pairing it with a matching termination right does
not cure it, because the two are not separable parts of the clause (BGH, judgment of 27 April 2021,
XI ZR 26/20). Where silence is used at all to confirm something the customer stated separately, give
the customer a reasonable period to make an express declaration instead, and flag at the start of that
period what the silence is deemed to mean (§ 308 Nr. 5 Buchst. a, b BGB).

Where [trader name] lets customers conclude the continuing paid contract through a website, add the
cancellation-button paragraph below the model wording, built exactly as this module states; see
`references/de/bgb-fernabsatz.md` for the full § 312k BGB regime this summarises. Provide a
Kündigungsschaltfläche, permanently available, immediately and easily accessible, labelled PW-DE-2,
routing directly to a confirmation page labelled with the PW-DE-3 confirmation button (§ 312k Abs. 1,
2, Abs. 2 Satz 4 BGB). The confirmation page asks for, and only for: cancellation type and, for an
extraordinary cancellation, the reason; data identifying the customer; data identifying the contract;
an optional desired end date the customer is never required to fill in; and an electronic channel for
the confirmation (§ 312k Abs. 2 Satz 3 Nr. 1 Buchst. a to e BGB). This duty binds [trader name] even
where a third-party platform runs the website the contract is concluded through; where that is the
case, secure the mechanism from the platform operator by contract (§ 312k Abs. 1 Satz 1 BGB). Two
contract types fall outside the whole duty: one whose cancellation the law requires in a stricter
form than text form, and a financial-services website or contract (§ 312k Abs. 1 Satz 2 Nr. 1, 2 BGB).

Let the customer store their own cancellation declaration on a durable medium, time-stamped and shown
to have been made by activating the confirmation button (§ 312k Abs. 3 BGB). Confirm receipt
immediately, electronically, in text form, stating the content, the time stamp, and the resulting end
date; a declaration made by activating the confirmation button is presumed to have reached [trader
name] immediately (§ 312k Abs. 4 BGB). Where the customer states no end date, the cancellation takes
effect, in case of doubt, at the earliest possible time (§ 312k Abs. 5 BGB). State plainly that a
missing or non-compliant button or confirmation page lets the customer cancel the affected contract at
any time without any notice period, whether or not a cancellation right otherwise exists on the merits
(§ 312k Abs. 6 BGB).

### US

Disclose, before taking billing information for any auto-renewing or continuous-service plan and in
visual proximity to the control that records consent (temporal proximity for a spoken offer): that the
plan continues until cancelled, the cancellation mechanism, the charge and whether and how it may
change, the renewal term length or that service is continuous, and any minimum-purchase obligation
(Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17601(a)(2)(A) to (E), § 17602(a)(1); N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 527-a(1)(a)).
Where the plan starts with a free gift, trial, or a temporary price, add a clear, conspicuous statement
of the price that will apply once that period ends, or of how the price will change (Cal. Bus. and
Prof. Code § 17602(a)(1); N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 527-a(1)(a)). Under ROSCA, where the sale is effected on
the internet, meet all three of: clear and conspicuous disclosure of the material transaction terms
before taking billing information, the customer's express informed consent before the first charge,
and a simple mechanism to stop future charges; these three conditions are conjunctive (15 U.S.C.
§ 8403(1) to (3)).

Before the first charge for any auto-renewing or continuous-service plan, including one offered at a
promotional or discounted price for a limited period, obtain the customer's affirmative consent to the
agreement containing the offer terms (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17602(a)(2), (a)(4)); in New York,
obtain that same affirmative consent before the initial charge (N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 527-a(1)(b)). Keep
a verifiable record of the consent for at least three years, or one year after the contract ends,
whichever is longer (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17602(a)(6)).

After consent, send a retainable acknowledgment covering the offer terms, the cancellation policy, and
how to cancel; where a free gift or trial is involved, also explain how to cancel before being charged
(Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17602(a)(3)). Send the same acknowledgment content in New York: the
agreement's terms, the charge amount, the charge frequency, the deadline to act to stop a charge, and
the cancellation mechanisms, promptly and in retainable form (N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 527-a(1)(c)(i) to
(v)).

In California, describe the cancellation mechanism itself: a toll-free telephone number, an email
address, a postal address where the business bills the customer directly, or another cost-effective,
timely, and easy-to-use method (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17602(c)(1)). Where a phone channel is
offered, staff it to answer promptly during business hours, and act on, or call back about, a
cancellation voicemail within one business day (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17602(c)(2)(A), (c)(2)(B)).

Describe the cancellation mechanism as reachable through at least the same medium the customer used to
consent, and in New York through every other medium the customer could have used to consent as well
(Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17602(f); N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 527-a(1)(d), (d-1)). Where New York consent
was given in person and an equivalent in-person cancellation method is not practical, offer an online
mechanism or a phone number at least (N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 527-a(1)(d-1)). If online acceptance is
offered in California, add no further step beyond permitted account authentication that obstructs or
delays immediate online termination; state that this duty reaches only the auto-renewal and
continuous-service terms, with the rest of the agreement under other applicable law (Cal. Bus. and
Prof. Code § 17602(d)(1), (d)(2)).

Where a pre-billing notice is owed under either state's timing windows, disclose: that the plan
auto-renews unless cancelled, the renewal period's length and terms, the cost or cost range and
frequency, the cancellation methods, and, for an electronic notice in California, an accessible
electronic route into cancellation (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17602(a)(8)(A) to (F)); in New York,
disclose cancellation instructions through the customer's selected channel (N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law
§ 527-a(1)(f), (h)). Where an annual reminder is owed in California, disclose the product or service
covered, the charge frequency and amount, and how to cancel (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code
§ 17602(h)(2)(A) to (C)).

Where a material change, including a fee change, is made to accepted terms, give clear, conspicuous,
retainable notice with cancellation instructions before the change takes effect (Cal. Bus. and Prof.
Code § 17602(g)(1), (g)(2); N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 527-a(1)(g)). In New York, on a price increase or on a
price higher than what was disclosed at the offer stage, either capture the customer's affirmative
consent to the new price before charging it, or let the customer cancel at any time within at least
fourteen days after the charge, with a pro rata refund for the remaining term as of the cancellation
date; one captured consent covers every later charge at that price, and a further increase needs its
own consent or cancellation window (N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 527-a(1)(b-1)).

State that no information in the agreement interferes with, detracts from, contradicts, or otherwise
undermines the customer's ability to give affirmative consent to the renewal or continuous service,
and that the agreement does not misrepresent, expressly or by implication, whether the plan renews
automatically or a material fact about the underlying goods or service (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code
§ 17602(a)(5), (a)(7)). State that a discount, retention benefit, or cancellation-effect information is
shown only together with the required cancellation trigger, never instead of it or ahead of disclosing
it (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17602(e)(1), (e)(2)); in New York, present such an offer only after a
cancellation request, never as a condition the customer must sit through, and never in a way that
imposes unreasonable or unlawful conditions on, refuses to acknowledge, obstructs, or unreasonably
delays cancellation, including hanging up on a customer who calls to cancel, giving false cancellation
information, or misrepresenting the consequences, costs, or reasons for delay (N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law
§ 527-a(1)(e)(i), (e)(ii)).

Where goods or merchandise are shipped under a prenotification plan (periodic announcement-and-opt-out
selections), add the disclosures 16 C.F.R. § 425.1(a)(1) requires before enrollment and build the
crediting, notice, shipping, and termination mechanics § 425.1(a)(2), (a)(3), (b)(1) to (4) state; this
module does not reach a continuity plan, an automatic renewal, or a free-to-pay conversion for a pure
digital service with no merchandise shipped, which the Part 425 machinery is not written for. State
that an unconsented shipment under a continuous service or automatic renewal is treated as an
unconditional gift, with no invoice, collection, or return-shipping charge against the recipient (Cal.
Bus. and Prof. Code § 17603; N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 527-a(2)).

Where a California or New York statement is drafted, confirm the seller or affiliate does not fall into
one of that state's exempt categories first, since an exempt business carries none of that state's
duties at all (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17605(a) to (f); N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 527-a(4)(a) to (e)).

## Must not

- No clause may let [trader name] change continuing-obligation terms, including price, through the
  customer's silence rather than an actual accepted offer, with no substantive cap on what can change
  or by how much; pairing it with a matching termination right does not cure this, since the change
  mechanism and the termination right are not separable parts of one clause (BGH, judgment of 27 April
  2021, XI ZR 26/20).
- No clause may bind a recurring-supply or recurring-service customer, to their detriment, to an
  initial term over two years, a silent renewal they are bound by outside the indefinite-term-plus-
  one-month-notice design, or a pre-end termination notice period over one month, except for goods
  customarily sold together and insurance contracts (§ 309 Nr. 9 BGB).
- No clause may reserve a release right tied to a contract-stated justified reason without stating
  that reason, except for a continuing-obligation contract (§ 308 Nr. 3 BGB).
- No cancellation confirmation page under the Germany module may ask for more than the five listed
  items, or carry a retention offer, pause option, or other content; it may not make the end-date field
  mandatory (§ 312k Abs. 2 Satz 3, Abs. 5 BGB; BGH, judgment of 16 July 2026, I ZR 200/25).
- No clause may confine the customer's cancellation declaration to the online cancellation button, or
  to any form stricter than text form, since § 312k BGB only adds that channel and does not narrow or
  replace any other lawful way of declaring (§ 309 Nr. 13 Buchst. a, b BGB).
- No clause may claim a German online cancellation flow is complete without the permanently available
  button, the confirmation page, and the receipt confirmation, or may gate any of the three behind a
  login the contract itself did not require.
- A cancellation-by-proxy clause, used where a new continuing contract is meant to replace the
  customer's existing one with another trader, may not require less than text form for the customer's
  cancellation declaration or for the power of attorney to cancel (§ 312h BGB).
- No clause may interfere with, detract from, contradict, or otherwise undermine a US customer's
  ability to give affirmative consent to renewal, or misrepresent whether the plan renews automatically
  (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17602(a)(5), (a)(7)).
- No clause may condition a US customer's ability to cancel on hearing a retention offer first, refuse
  to acknowledge a cancellation request, or add an extra step beyond permitted authentication that
  delays online termination (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17602(d)(1), (e)(1), (e)(2); N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law
  § 527-a(1)(e)(i), (e)(ii)).
- No clause may claim a consumer can sue the seller directly under ROSCA; enforcement runs through the
  FTC and state attorneys general, not a private right of action (15 U.S.C. § 8404(a), (b), § 8405(a),
  (b), (c), (e)).
- No clause may condition the full-invoice credit a prenotification-plan customer is owed on anything
  beyond the four statutory situations, or make continued billing a precondition for honoring a
  contract-complete subscriber's written cancellation (16 C.F.R. § 425.1(b)(1), (b)(4)).

## Drafting notes

See C-7 and C-8 in `references/conflicts.md` for the module choice this clause rests on: the US
practice of a phone or ticket-based cancellation channel, with a retention offer before completing
cancellation, does not satisfy the German online cancellation-button regime, which needs its own
standing button, its own fixed-content confirmation page, and no retention-offer step inserted into
it; keep the US retention-offer language for the US modules only, and build the Germany module's
button paragraph exactly as `references/de/bgb-fernabsatz.md` states rather than adapting the US flow.

Design the Germany cancellation flow and confirmation page without a login step for now: the BGH is
due to rule on 5 November 2026 (I ZR 272/25, I ZR 275/25) on whether a login requirement in that flow
is lawful, and the outcome should be checked before relying on a login step.

Confirm the counterparty's status (consumer, ordinary business, named financial-sector or large-
business counterparty, utility special customer) before drafting the Germany module, since AGB content
control under § 309 Nr. 9 and § 308 Nr. 3 does not reach every counterparty type the same way; see
`references/de/bgb-agb.md` for the classification rule.

Confirm which US state's statute, if any, applies, and whether the seller or an affiliate sits in that
state's exempt category list, before drafting the US module for it; the California and New York timing
windows and disclosure lists differ from each other, and the references here cover only those two
states, so a further state's auto-renewal statute is an open point for counsel until its own reference
file is added.

Where billing runs through a third-party platform, such as an app store, rather than the seller's own
checkout, flag as an open point whether that platform's own cancellation path can stand in for the
same-medium-as-consent duty in California and New York, and whether the seller can contractually
secure the German cancellation-button duty from the platform operator; both reference files raise this
without settling it.

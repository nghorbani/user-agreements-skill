# Dispute resolution

Law as of: 2026-10-20

## Purpose

This clause tells a user how a disagreement with the business gets resolved: an informal complaint
step, then either arbitration or court, and whether claims can be brought together with other
people's claims or only one at a time. It also carries the standing notices Germany requires about
consumer dispute-resolution bodies. It does not set which country's law governs the contract or
which court has jurisdiction when a dispute does go to court; that split sits in a separate clause
this file does not cover.

## Model wording

Before starting a formal proceeding, the user agrees to tell the business about the dispute in
writing and give the business [number] days to try to resolve it directly. [If the business is an
arbitration module] If the dispute is not resolved during that period, it will be resolved by
binding arbitration on an individual basis rather than in court, except where the clause below says
otherwise. Either party may start an individual claim in small claims court instead of arbitration if
the claim qualifies for that court. The arbitration is administered by [arbitration provider] under
its rules then in effect, with a single arbitrator, and the award may be entered as a judgment in any
court with jurisdiction to do so. Each party bears its own share of the arbitration fees as the
administrator's rules set them, and the business will [pay or advance / reimburse] the user's share
where the rules or the law applicable to the user's claim require it. Nothing in this clause stops a
user from bringing a claim in small claims court, from reporting the dispute to a regulator or
consumer agency, or from seeking the forms of relief that applicable law says cannot be arbitrated,
including any claim for relief that would primarily stop unlawful conduct affecting other people
rather than resolve the user's own dispute. Claims under this clause are brought only on an
individual basis: the arbitrator may not combine more than one person's claims into a single
proceeding, preside over a class, consolidated, or representative action, or award relief to anyone
who is not a party to that individual proceeding, unless all parties agree otherwise in writing.

## Modules

### EU consumers

Drop the arbitration requirement and the individual-claims-only requirement for the whole clause.
An EU consumer keeps an unrestricted right to go to court, and a clause steering the consumer away
from that right, or toward a forum distant from the consumer's residence, is itself an unfair term
(Art. 3(1), Annex point (q) UCTD). Keep only the informal-complaint step and the standing notices
below. Where the business maintains a website or uses standard terms toward consumers, add, in both
of those places: whether and to what extent the business is willing or obliged to take part in
proceedings before a consumer arbitration body (Verbraucherschlichtungsstelle), and, where it is
willing or obliged, that body's name, address and website (§ 36 Abs. 1, Abs. 2 VSBG; this duty is
Germany-specific but belongs in this module because it is the standing notice the EU consumer sees
in place of an arbitration clause). Do not keep a link to the EU Online Dispute Resolution platform
or cite Regulation (EU) No 524/2013 as a live channel; that regulation is repealed with effect from
20 July 2025 and the platform stopped taking new complaints on 20 March 2025 (Art. 1, Art. 2(1),
2(2) Regulation (EU) 2024/3228).

### EU business customers

The UCTD and the VSBG notice duty both run to consumers; neither reaches a contract with a business
customer (Art. 2(b), (c) UCTD; § 36 VSBG reads toward consumers). The core model wording, including
the arbitration and individual-claims provisions, may be used with a business customer. Still state
the arbitrator-appointment method, the seat, and the language of the proceeding expressly, since
nothing in this module supplies a default softer than the core wording's own terms.

### Germany

For a German consumer, add the case-specific notice as its own, separate step: once an actual
dispute over the contract has failed to settle between the business and the consumer, send the
consumer, in text form, the name, address and website of the competent consumer arbitration body
for that dispute, and state at the same time whether the business is willing or obliged to take
part in proceedings before it (§ 37 Abs. 1, Abs. 2 VSBG). This is triggered by an actual failed
settlement attempt, not by contract conclusion, so it cannot be satisfied by the standing § 36
notice alone or folded into it (§ 37 Abs. 1 Satz 1 VSBG). Where the business is exempt from the
general willingness statement under § 36 Abs. 3 VSBG because of its size, keep naming any body it
has in fact committed to, or is legally obliged to use, since that exemption does not reach the
naming duty (§ 36 Abs. 3 VSBG).

### US

Use the core model wording. Add, as a separate sentence rather than folded into the individual-
claims provision, that nothing in the clause waives, in any forum, a California consumer's
statutory right to seek public injunctive relief under the Consumers Legal Remedies Act, the unfair
competition law, or the false advertising law; an individual claim for damages or other individually
held relief can still go to arbitration even where a public-injunctive-relief request in the same
dispute proceeds in court instead (Cal. Civ. Code § 1751; McGill v. Citibank, N.A., 2 Cal.5th 945,
955, 965 to 966 (2017)). Where the business runs or may run a claim-batching or bellwether feature
for multiple similar arbitration filings, give every bound claimant notice of, and a genuine
opportunity to participate in or be heard on, any proceeding whose outcome will bind that claimant's
own case, matching whatever notice and participation the business gives itself; an asymmetric
feature that notifies only the business is a substantive unconscionability problem under ordinary
state contract law the Federal Arbitration Act does not preempt (Heckman v. Live Nation
Entertainment, Inc., 120 F.4th 670 (9th Cir. 2024)). Do not make the arbitration or class-waiver
clause bind a person who has only visited the site without transacting, and do not apply a
rule-change made to the clause after a dispute has already accrued to that dispute (Heckman, 120
F.4th 670). Sexual assault and sexual harassment disputes need their own carve-out: at the election
of the person alleging the conduct, or the named representative of a class or collective claim
alleging it, no predispute arbitration agreement and no predispute joint-action waiver is valid or
enforceable for that claim, whether the waiver sits inside this clause or stands alone elsewhere in
the document, for conduct occurring on or after March 3, 2022 (9 U.S.C. §§ 401(2), 402(a)). State
that a court, not the arbitrator, decides whether that carve-out applies and whether the arbitration
agreement is valid for such a claim, regardless of any delegation language elsewhere in the clause
(9 U.S.C. § 402(b)).

## Must not

- A clause may not route a predispute sexual assault or sexual harassment dispute to mandatory
  arbitration, or bar the claimant or a class/collective representative from a joint, class, or
  collective action over that conduct, for conduct accruing on or after March 3, 2022 (9 U.S.C.
  §§ 401(2), 402(a)).
- A clause may not purport to waive, in any forum, a California consumer's right to seek public
  injunctive relief under the CLRA, the UCL, or the false advertising law (Cal. Civ. Code § 1751;
  McGill, 2 Cal.5th at 965 to 966, 971 to 973).
- A clause may not let the arbitration provider batch claims or bind non-participating claimants to
  a bellwether result without giving those claimants the same notice and participation the drafting
  party gives itself, and may not apply a rule change retroactively to an accrued dispute or to a
  mere site visitor (Heckman, 120 F.4th 670).
- A clause offered to an EU consumer may not route disputes to a forum materially distant from the
  consumer's residence, may not exclude or hinder the consumer's access to courts or to judicial
  review of unfair terms, and an ADR-submission agreement made before a dispute arises does not bind
  the consumer if it would remove their right to go to court instead (UCTD Annex point (q); Guidance
  sections 5.4.2, 5.7).
- A clause may not state or imply that the EU Online Dispute Resolution platform, or Regulation (EU)
  No 524/2013, is still a live channel; that regulation is repealed and the platform discontinued
  (Art. 1, Art. 2(1), 2(2) Regulation (EU) 2024/3228).
- A clause may not fold the German § 37 VSBG case-specific notice into the standing § 36 notice, or
  deliver it informally; § 37 requires text form and is triggered only once an actual settlement
  attempt has failed (§ 37 Abs. 1, Abs. 2 VSBG).
- A clause may not promise individual arbitration while an incorporated rules document actually
  lets claims be grouped with, or bound to, another claimant's case; that mismatch is itself
  evidence of unconscionability (Heckman, 120 F.4th 670).
- A clause may not rely on a global severability provision to assume an unconscionable arbitration
  mechanism will be saved once the offending part is struck, where unconscionable terms permeate
  the agreement rather than sitting in one isolated provision (Heckman, 120 F.4th 670).

## Drafting notes

Resolved by module rather than by a single conflicts entry: an EU consumer never sees the
arbitration or individual-claims-only wording at all (dropped under the EU consumers module above), while a
US consumer sees it with the carve-outs this file lists. A document serving both markets needs the
EU consumer module and the US module as alternative blocks keyed to the user's residence, not a
single blended clause; drafting one clause that tries to satisfy both the UCTD's bar on routing
consumers away from court and the US module's arbitration-first structure in the same sentence is
not achievable and is not attempted here.

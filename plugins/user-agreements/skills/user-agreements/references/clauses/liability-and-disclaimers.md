# Liability and disclaimers

Law as of: 2026-10-20

## Purpose

This clause sets what a trader can exclude, cap, or disclaim for damage beyond the conformity remedy
itself: consequential loss, lost profit, data loss, and injury or property damage a defective product or a
breach of the agreement causes. It does not cover the repair, replacement, price-reduction, or refund
remedy for a product that fails to match what was promised (see
`references/clauses/warranty-and-conformity.md`), and it does not state the no-fault manufacturer liability
regime for a defective product that causes personal injury or property damage to a natural person;
`references/eu/pld.md` states that regime in full, and this clause names only the point where a liability
section's own wording must not narrow it. It works alongside the content-control rules for standard terms
in Germany (`references/de/bgb-agb.md`) and across the EU (`references/eu/uctd.md`), alongside
California's exculpatory-clause and unconscionability statutes (`references/us-states/ca-civil-code.md`),
and alongside the US federal warranty-disclosure regime (`references/us-federal/magnuson-moss.md`).

## Model wording

Nothing in these terms limits or excludes a liability that the law does not let [trader name] limit or
exclude. In particular, nothing here limits [trader name]'s liability for death or personal injury, for its
own fraud or intentional wrongdoing, for a grossly negligent breach of duty, for a negligent breach of a
duty essential to this contract, or for liability a specific statute states cannot be contracted away.

Outside what the paragraph above protects, [trader name]'s liability for a negligent breach of a duty that
is not essential to this contract is limited to the loss that was foreseeable and ordinary for a contract of
this kind. [Trader name] is not liable for indirect or consequential loss, including lost profit or lost
data, except where the paragraph above says this clause cannot limit that liability.

[Trader name] is not liable for a problem caused by [the consumer's / the user's] own misuse of [the
product], by unauthorized changes to it, or by a cause outside [trader name]'s control, unless the law
says this clause cannot exclude that liability.

## Modules

### EU consumers

State the floor first, before any limitation: nothing in the clause reaches liability for death or
personal injury caused by the trader's own act or omission, since excluding or limiting that liability is
itself flagged as a pattern that may be unfair (Annex point (a) UCTD). State the same floor for a clause
that would unduly exclude or limit the consumer's legal rights against the trader for non-performance or
defective performance, including denying a set-off of a debt the consumer owes against a claim the
consumer holds (Annex point (b) UCTD). Because the Annex is an indicative list rather than a closed
blacklist, do not treat a clause that falls outside both points as automatically safe: every limitation
clause is still tested under the general unfairness standard, a breach of good faith causing a significant
imbalance to the consumer's detriment, measured against what national default rules would otherwise give
the consumer (Art. 3(1), 4(1) UCTD).

Do not rely on a single stacked set of modest-looking exclusions to clear this test: several individually
narrow limitations can together still cause the same significant imbalance a single broad one would (Art.
3(1) UCTD, read with the sanction-clause proportionality standard the Guidance applies to combined sanction
terms). Write the clause in plain, intelligible language that lets the consumer work out its real economic
consequences, not merely in grammatically correct sentences; the burden of proving this transparency sits
with the trader (Art. 5 UCTD).

Where the product is a defective product within the meaning of the Product Liability Directive (a movable,
including software, an AI system, or a digital manufacturing file, placed on the market or put into service
after 9 December 2026), state plainly that nothing in these terms limits or excludes the trader's, or any
other liable economic operator's, statutory liability toward an injured natural person under that Directive,
and that a contrary provision is simply ineffective against that person (Art. 15 Directive (EU) 2024/2853).
Do not describe the compensable damage more narrowly than death, personal injury including recognised
psychological harm, damage to or destruction of property other than the product itself, or destruction or
corruption of personal-use data (Art. 6(1) Directive (EU) 2024/2853). Property damage excludes the
defective product itself, a product damaged by its own defective component where that component was
integrated by, or within the control of, the product's manufacturer, and property used exclusively for
professional purposes; do not treat these three exclusions as if they were further categories of
compensable damage (Art. 6(1) Directive (EU) 2024/2853). Do not shorten or reset the three-year or the
ten-year (or twenty-five-year) period that Directive states for bringing a claim (Art. 16, 17 Directive
(EU) 2024/2853).

### EU business customers

Drop the consumer-specific floor language tied to the Unfair Contract Terms Directive's Annex and its
Art. 3(1), 5 unfairness and transparency tests, since that Directive protects a consumer (a natural person
acting outside their trade, business, craft or profession), not a business counterparty (Art. 2(b), (c)
Directive 93/13/EEC). A broader liability cap and disclaimer is available for a business-to-business
clause, subject to the general national contract law of the parties' chosen jurisdiction, which these
reference files do not state; treat the exact wording of a B2B limitation clause as an open point for that
jurisdiction's law. Keep the Product Liability Directive floor regardless of the counterparty's status,
since that Directive protects a natural person injured by a defective product and is not limited to
consumer contracts (Art. 6(1) Directive (EU) 2024/2853).

Where the clause covers data access, data use, or liability for a data-related obligation and one enterprise imposes it on another, apply the Data Act unfair-terms test: a term that excludes or limits the imposing party's liability for intentional acts or gross negligence, excludes the other party's remedies for non-performance, or gives the imposing party the sole right to decide whether the data supplied conforms to the contract is unfair and does not bind the other party (Art. 13(1), (4)(a) to (c) Regulation (EU) 2023/2854). See `references/eu/data-act.md` for the presumed-unfair list and the scope of the test.

### Germany

State the mandatory floor using the BGB's own categories rather than the UCTD's: no clause may exclude or
limit liability for death, personal injury, or harm to health arising from a negligent breach of duty by
the Verwender or an intentional or negligent breach by its statutory representative or vicarious agent, with
no carve-out for any contract type (§ 309 Nr. 7 Buchst. a BGB). No clause may exclude or limit liability for
other damage arising from a grossly negligent breach of duty by the Verwender, or an intentional or grossly
negligent breach by its statutory representative or vicarious agent, except for the narrow approved-tariff
transport and state-approved lottery or wagering carve-outs the statute itself names (§ 309 Nr. 7 Buchst. b
BGB). A clause this strict also bars the weaker foreseeable-and-ordinary-loss cap in the model wording
above wherever the breach was grossly negligent or affected those protected interests; keep that cap only
for a negligent breach of a non-essential duty.

Where the counterparty is a business, a public-law legal person, or a public-law special fund rather than a
consumer, § 309 does not apply directly, but § 307 Abs. 1, 2 BGB still reaches a term matching a § 309 Nr. 7
category, weighing in commercial custom and usage; do not treat the B2B relaxation in the EU module as
automatically available under German law without that separate § 307 check (§ 310 Abs. 1 BGB).

Any clause letting the Verwender change the contract, including through the counterparty's silence, needs
its own check independent of this clause's liability wording; a liability section is not where that kind of
change mechanism is validated (§ 305 Abs. 2, § 307 Abs. 1 Satz 1, Abs. 2 Nr. 1 BGB; BGH, judgment of 27
April 2021, XI ZR 26/20; see `references/clauses/changes-to-terms.md` for that clause).

Where the product is within the Product Liability Directive's scope, state the Art. 15 anti-waiver floor
exactly as the EU consumers module states it; Germany has no separate, more protective national variant of
that specific rule in the reference files read for this clause.

### US

Draft a conspicuous as-is or as-available disclaimer and a liability cap (a stated dollar figure, or the
fees paid in a defined period, whichever is greater) for the US module, paired with a disclaimer of
incidental and consequential damages. Where the product carries a written warranty under the Magnuson-Moss
Warranty Act, do not let this clause's damages disclaimer stand alone: a warranty labeled "full (statement
of duration) warranty" must not exclude or limit consequential damages unless the exclusion appears
conspicuously on the warranty's own face, and it must then carry the state-variation statement at PW-US-FED-2
(15 U.S.C. 2304(a)(3); 16 C.F.R. 701.3(a)(8)). Pair any disclosed implied-warranty duration limit with
PW-US-FED-1, and include the PW-US-FED-3 legal-rights statement on every written warranty covered by part
701 (16 C.F.R. 701.3(a)(7), (9)).

A "full (statement of duration)" caption on a written warranty is not a label a liability clause can work
around: once a warranty carries it, a court hearing any claim under 15 U.S.C. 2310(d) or under state law
must treat that warranty as incorporating at least the full minimum-standards regime of 15 U.S.C. 2304,
whatever the warranty's or the liability clause's own wording says (15 U.S.C. 2304(e)). A broad liability
cap or as-is disclaimer drafted alongside a warranty that was left captioned "full (statement of duration)"
by mistake is overridden to that extent by operation of law, not merely exposed to the conspicuousness
problem above; before relying on the cap or the as-is disclaimer, confirm the warranty's own caption reads
"limited warranty" where a limited warranty is what was intended.

For a California consumer or any other California-governed contract, do not draft a release, disclaimer,
limitation-of-liability, or waiver clause, however labeled, whose effect is to exempt a party from its own
fraud, from willful injury to another's person or property, or from a violation of law, whether willful or
negligent; the statute looks at the clause's object, including an object reached only indirectly, not at
its label (Cal. Civ. Code § 1668). State the carve-out plainly in the clause itself rather than leaving it
to be read in. Check the clause for features a California court could find unconscionable at formation,
since an adverse finding lets the court strike the whole contract, sever the clause, or narrow it, at the
court's choice rather than the drafter's (Cal. Civ. Code § 1670.5(a)).

Do not draft a predispute arbitration clause that purports to waive, in any forum, a California consumer's
right to seek public injunctive relief under the Consumers Legal Remedies Act, the unfair competition law,
or the false advertising law; state separately that an individual, non-class arbitration requirement does
not reach that relief (McGill v. Citibank, N.A., Cal. Supreme Ct. No. S224086). Do not draft a clause
requiring a California consumer to waive any right or remedy the Consumers Legal Remedies Act gives; such a
clause is void regardless of the words used, because the statutory bar reaches the whole CLRA title (Cal.
Civ. Code § 1751).

## Must not

- No clause may exclude or limit liability for death, personal injury, or harm to health arising from a
  negligent breach of duty, or an intentional or grossly negligent breach by the trader's representative or
  vicarious agent, with no carve-out for any contract type (§ 309 Nr. 7 Buchst. a BGB); the same conduct is
  independently flagged as a suspect unfair-term pattern under the UCTD Annex for a consumer contract (Annex
  point (a) Directive 93/13/EEC).
- No clause may unduly exclude or limit the consumer's legal rights against the trader for non-performance
  or defective performance, including denying a set-off right (Annex point (b) Directive 93/13/EEC).
- No clause may exclude or limit liability for other damage arising from a grossly negligent breach of duty,
  except the narrow transport-tariff and lottery or wagering carve-outs the statute itself states (§ 309 Nr.
  7 Buchst. b BGB).
- No clause may limit or exclude an economic operator's liability toward a person injured by a defective
  product under the Product Liability Directive, by any wording, however prominent or separately accepted,
  and no clause may shorten the three-year or the ten-year (or twenty-five-year) period for bringing that
  claim or start either period running before its statutory trigger (Art. 15, 16, 17 Directive (EU)
  2024/2853).
- No clause may describe the Product Liability Directive's compensable damage more narrowly than death,
  personal injury including recognised psychological harm, damage to or destruction of property other than
  the product itself, or destruction or corruption of personal-use data, nor treat the exclusions from
  property damage (the defective product itself, a product damaged by its own defective component where
  that component was integrated by, or within the control of, the product's manufacturer, and property used
  exclusively for professional purposes) as further categories of compensable damage (Art. 6(1) Directive
  (EU) 2024/2853).
- No California clause may exempt a party, directly or indirectly and whatever label it uses, from its own
  fraud, from willful injury to another's person or property, or from a violation of law whether willful or
  negligent (Cal. Civ. Code § 1668).
- No California clause requiring a consumer to waive a Consumers Legal Remedies Act right or remedy is
  enforceable, regardless of the words used (Cal. Civ. Code § 1751); no predispute arbitration clause may
  waive, in any forum, a California consumer's right to seek public injunctive relief under the CLRA, the
  unfair competition law, or the false advertising law (McGill v. Citibank, N.A.).
- No US warranty labeled "full (statement of duration) warranty" may exclude or limit consequential damages
  without that exclusion appearing conspicuously on the warranty's own face, paired with PW-US-FED-2
  (15 U.S.C. 2304(a)(3); 16 C.F.R. 701.3(a)(8)).

## Drafting notes

No conflicts.md entry decides a module choice for this clause's own liability-cap language yet; add one once
a dedicated entry exists, since a US-drafted template commonly pairs a broad as-is disclaimer with a flat
dollar liability cap covering every kind of fault, including the trader's own gross negligence or wilful
breach, which the German and EU modules above must narrow rather than merely append to. The closest existing
entry, C-5 in `references/conflicts.md`, covers the same US-practice pattern for the warranty-and-conformity
clause's own remedy wording; this clause's liability cap is the companion piece C-5 flags but does not fully
resolve on its own.

Draft the foreseeable-and-ordinary-loss cap in the model wording so it narrows automatically once a breach
crosses into gross negligence, an essential duty, or one of the protected-interest categories, rather than
drafting two separate clauses that a reviewer must remember to keep in sync.

State the Product Liability Directive floor as a short, separate paragraph rather than folding it into the
general limitation-of-liability language, since Art. 15 reaches every liable economic operator, beyond just
the trader whose name sits at the top of the document, and a reader should not need to infer that scope from
context.

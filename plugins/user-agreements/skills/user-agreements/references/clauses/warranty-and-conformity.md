# Warranty and conformity

Law as of: 2026-10-20

## Purpose

This clause states what a trader owes when goods, digital content, or a digital service turns out not to
match what was promised or what a consumer may reasonably expect, and what remedy the consumer gets. In
the EU and Germany this is a statutory conformity standard the trader cannot contract below; in the
United States it is a written-warranty regime that applies once the trader chooses to give a written
warranty at all. The clause does not cover liability caps or exclusions for damages beyond the remedy
itself (see `references/clauses/liability-and-disclaimers.md`), and it does not cover the separate
manufacturer repair-and-spare-parts duty that EU law layers on top of conformity for certain product
categories (see `references/eu/repair.md`); it names that duty where it affects what this clause's own
remedy wording can say.

## Model wording

[Trader name] supplies [the goods / the digital content / the digital service] so that it matches what
this contract describes and promises, and, on top of that, has the quantity, quality, and performance a
consumer may reasonably expect for a product of this kind, including its usual functionality,
compatibility, durability, and security, taking into account any public statement [trader name] or [its
supplier / an earlier seller in the chain] made about it, for example in advertising or on packaging.

[For goods with digital elements or a continuously supplied digital content or digital service: [trader
name] keeps [the product] updated, including with security updates, for [the contract's stated period /
the period a consumer may reasonably expect for a product of this kind and purpose]. [Trader name] tells
the consumer when an update is available and what happens if it is not installed. If the consumer does not
install an update within a reasonable time after being told this, [trader name] is not responsible for a
problem that comes only from the update being missing, unless the installation instructions [trader name]
gave were themselves unclear or wrong.]

Where a specific feature of [the product] falls short of what a consumer could otherwise expect, [trader
name] names that feature and its shortfall before the consumer orders, and the consumer separately confirms
accepting it, apart from accepting these terms generally; [see PW-US-FED-4 in references/prescribed-wording.md
if also designating this a full or a limited warranty].

If [the product] does not match what this clause promises, the consumer can ask [trader name] to fix it.
[For goods: the consumer chooses between repair and replacement; trader name may decline the consumer's
choice only where it is impossible or clearly disproportionate compared with the other option, and must
then offer that other option instead.] The fix is free of charge, happens within a reasonable time, and
causes the consumer no significant inconvenience. If the fix does not happen in time, fails, or [trader
name] refuses or will clearly not do it, the consumer can ask instead for a price reduction, or can end the
contract unless the problem is only minor.

If the consumer ends the contract for this reason, [trader name] pays back what was paid within 14 days,
using the same payment method the consumer used, with no extra fee. [For goods: the consumer sends the
goods back at trader name's expense, and trader name may hold the refund until it gets them back or proof
they were sent.]

## Modules

### EU consumers

Use the core wording for a consumer sales contract (goods, including goods with digital elements) or for
digital content or a digital service supplied to a consumer for a price or for personal data, selecting the
bracketed goods or digital-content paragraphs for the product actually sold (Art. 5 to 9 Directive (EU)
2019/771; Art. 6 to 9 Directive (EU) 2019/770). State both conformity layers together: the contract's own
promise (description, quantity, quality, stated purpose, agreed accessories, instructions and updates) and
the objective floor that applies regardless of the contract's wording (normal use, usual durability,
sample or model match, the qualities a consumer may reasonably expect) (Art. 6, 7(1) Directive (EU)
2019/771; Art. 7, 8(1) Directive (EU) 2019/770). Never present the contractual promise as if it replaces
the objective floor.

Where a deviation from the objective requirements is used (a display unit, a known limitation, a feature
flag), name the specific characteristic and collect the consumer's separate, express acceptance of exactly
that deviation at conclusion, distinct from accepting the terms generally; a general sold-as-seen line or
a pre-ticked box does not meet this (Art. 7(5) Directive (EU) 2019/771; Art. 8(5) Directive (EU) 2019/770).

State the liability period as at least two years from delivery or supply (Art. 10(1) Directive (EU)
2019/771; Art. 11(2) Directive (EU) 2019/770); for goods with digital elements or digital content under
continuous supply, extend it to the whole period the contract provides for supply if that period runs
longer than two years (Art. 10(2) Directive (EU) 2019/771; Art. 11(3) Directive (EU) 2019/770). Where the
contract is concluded on or after 31 July 2026, add that choosing repair as the remedy extends the
liability period once by 12 months, and disclose that possible extension, together with the consumer's
choice between repair and replacement, before the remedy is carried out (Art. 10(2a), 13(2a) Directive (EU)
2019/771, as amended by Directive (EU) 2024/1799). For a contract concluded before that date, state the
two-year period and the choice between repair and replacement without the 12-month extension.

For goods, give the consumer the choice between repair and replacement; decline the consumer's choice only
where it is impossible, or disproportionate against the other remedy's cost, weighing the goods'
conformity-value, the defect's significance, and whether the alternative causes no significant
inconvenience (Art. 13(2), (3) Directive (EU) 2019/771). For digital content or a digital service, the
trader has a free choice of how to bring it into conformity; state this difference rather than carrying
the goods-style choice-of-remedy language into a digital-content clause (Art. 14(2) Directive (EU)
2019/770).

Keep repair and replacement free of charge, within a reasonable time, and without significant
inconvenience, with the trader bearing removal and reinstallation costs where goods were installed before
the defect appeared (Art. 14(1), (3) Directive (EU) 2019/771). On termination, state that the consumer
returns goods at the trader's expense and the trader refunds the price on receipt of the goods or proof
they were sent (Art. 16(1) to (3) Directive (EU) 2019/771); for digital content or a digital service, state
reimbursement within 14 days by the consumer's original payment method with no fee, and, for continuous
supply, a proportionate part of the price for the period it was not in conformity rather than a full refund
(Art. 16(1), 18 Directive (EU) 2019/770).

Termination is unavailable for a merely minor lack of conformity, and the trader carries the burden of
proving the shortfall is minor (Art. 13(5) Directive (EU) 2019/771; Art. 14(6) Directive (EU) 2019/770).

For goods with digital elements or continuously supplied digital content, add the update paragraph: the
trader informs the consumer of, and supplies, updates including security updates needed for conformity, for
the contract's whole term or for the period a consumer may reasonably expect, and may rely on the
consumer's own failure to install an update only where the trader disclosed the update's availability and
the consequence of not installing it, and the failure was not itself caused by defective installation
instructions (Art. 7(3), (4) Directive (EU) 2019/771; Art. 8(2), (3) Directive (EU) 2019/770).

Where this clause sits in an offer for a continuously supplied digital product, flag that an interruption
of more than a negligible time, or one that recurs, already counts as a lack of conformity; do not define
conformity so that only a total outage counts (recital 51 Directive (EU) 2019/770).

Where the trader offers a commercial guarantee (an extended warranty, a durability guarantee), give it as a
durable-medium statement at the latest at delivery, stating that the statutory remedies above are
unaffected, the guarantor's name and address, how to claim under it, what it covers, and its terms,
including duration and territorial scope; keep it at least as favourable as any advertising for it, unless
that advertising was corrected in a comparably prominent way before conclusion (Art. 17(1), (2) Directive
(EU) 2019/771).

Where goods belong to a product category covered by Annex II of Directive (EU) 2024/1799, name that a
separate manufacturer repair duty may apply on top of this clause's own remedy; see
`references/eu/repair.md` for that regime, which this clause does not restate.

### EU business customers

Drop the consumer-specific elements, in particular the forced choice between repair and replacement, the
two-year statutory floor, and the ban on shortening the liability period, since Directive (EU) 2019/771 and
Directive (EU) 2019/770 protect a consumer, not a business counterparty (Art. 2(2) Directive (EU)
2019/771 defines "consumer" as a natural person acting outside their trade, business, craft or
profession; Art. 2(6) Directive (EU) 2019/770 does the same). A business-to-business conformity and
remedy clause is a matter for the general national contract law of the parties' chosen jurisdiction, which
these reference files do not state; treat the exact wording of a B2B warranty clause as an open point for
that jurisdiction's law.

### Germany

Use the German terms for the two conformity layers (vertraglich vereinbarte Beschaffenheit, the subjective
layer the contract itself fixes, and the objective requirements that apply unless validly agreed otherwise)
(§ 434 Abs. 1 to 3 BGB for goods; § 327e Abs. 2 BGB for digital products). For a consumer sale
(Verbrauchsgüterkauf), state the deviation route the same way as the EU module: specific pre-contract
notice of the departing feature, then an express and separate agreement to it, with neither step
satisfied by a general clause inside standard terms or a term agreed after notice of an actual defect
reached the business (§ 434 Abs. 3 Satz 1, § 476 Abs. 1 Satz 2 BGB; § 327h BGB for digital products).

For a consumer sale, add the pre-cure disclosure this clause's goods paragraph assumes: before carrying out
Nacherfüllung, tell the consumer of the choice between repair and replacement and that choosing repair
extends the limitation period once by 12 months (§ 475 Abs. 4 BGB). For digital products, state instead
that the trader has free choice between cure methods, since § 327l Abs. 1 BGB gives no consumer election the
way § 439 Abs. 1 does for goods; do not carry the goods-style choice-of-remedy wording into a digital-product
clause.

State the limitation period as two years from delivery for the general case (§ 438 Abs. 1 Nr. 3 BGB), with
the consumer-sale layer adding five further floors on top where they apply: twelve months after continuous
digital-element provision ends, twelve months after an updating-duty period ends, four months after a
defect first shows up within the general period, two months after a cured item is handed back, and a
one-time twelve-month extension once Nacherfüllung is rendered by repair (§ 475e Abs. 1 to 5 BGB). For a
digital product, state the limitation start by supply type: supply itself for a one-off or repeated supply,
the end of the supply period for continuous supply, and the end of the relevant update-expectation period
for an update-duty breach outside continuous supply (§ 327j Abs. 1 to 3 BGB).

Where the product is a Ware with digital elements, fix in the contract the period the agreed updates run,
since that period itself becomes part of the subjective-requirement benchmark; separately keep the update
duty running for the period an average, reasonable consumer would expect given the product's kind and
purpose, with a two-year floor from delivery for continuous provision regardless of a shorter contractual
period (§ 475b Abs. 3 Nr. 2, Abs. 4, § 475c Abs. 1, 2 BGB).

Where the trader gives a Garantieerklärung, draft it simply and comprehensibly with the same five content
items as the EU commercial-guarantee module (reference to the consumer's statutory rights and that the
Garantie does not restrict them, the guarantor's name and address, the claim procedure, what it covers, and
its terms including duration and territorial scope), delivered on a durable medium no later than delivery
(§ 479 Abs. 1, 2 BGB).

Where the goods fall within a product group listed in Anhang II of Directive (EU) 2024/1799, name the
manufacturer's separate statutory repair duty under §§ 479a to 479g BGB as a regime alongside this clause's
own remedy, not a substitute for it, and do not let a spare-parts or repair-tools pricing clause discourage
repair (§ 479c BGB); see `references/eu/repair.md` and `references/de/bgb-digital.md` for what that duty
requires and what it does not reach.

### US

Draft a written warranty only where the business chooses to give one; the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act does
not require a seller to warrant a consumer product, but once a written warranty is given on a product
actually costing the consumer more than $15, Part 701's pre-sale disclosure items apply (15 U.S.C. 2303(d);
16 C.F.R. 700.1(g), 701.2). The separate full-or-limited designation duty below applies above $10, not $15
(15 U.S.C. 2303(a)). Draft the general conformity promise above
as a written warranty only where it is tied to a specified performance level over a specified time; an
untimed feature description or specification sheet is not a written warranty under the Act and should not
be labeled one (15 U.S.C. 2301(6); 16 C.F.R. 700.3(a)).

Caption the warranty conspicuously as either "full (statement of duration) warranty" or "limited warranty",
using PW-US-FED-4, with no other designation (15 U.S.C. 2303(a)(1), (2); 16 C.F.R. 700.6(a)). Labeling it
"full" is more than a captioning choice: a court or the Commission reads a document so labeled as
incorporating at least the federal minimum standards below regardless of what the rest of the clause says,
so do not use the full designation unless the clause actually meets them (15 U.S.C. 2304(e)).

To meet the full-warranty minimum standards, remedy the product within a reasonable time and without
charge; do not limit the duration of any implied warranty; do not exclude or limit consequential damages
unless that exclusion appears conspicuously on the warranty's face, paired with PW-US-FED-2; and let the
consumer elect a refund or free replacement if a reasonable number of repair attempts has failed
(15 U.S.C. 2304(a)(1) to (4)). Do not impose any duty on the consumer besides notifying the defect as a
condition of remedy, beyond requiring the product be free of liens as a condition of replacement or refund
(15 U.S.C. 2304(b)(1), (2)). Where a limitation on an implied warranty's duration is disclosed on the
warranty's face, pair it with PW-US-FED-1; every warranty covered by Part 701 carries PW-US-FED-3
(16 C.F.R. 701.3(a)(7), (9)); see `references/prescribed-wording.md`.

For a limited warranty, include the nine Part 701 disclosure items where the product costs the consumer
more than $15: who the warranty covers, what is covered and, for clarity, excluded, what the warrantor will
do and pay for, when the term starts and how long it runs, the step-by-step claim procedure with contact
information, any informal dispute mechanism, any disclosed implied-warranty limitation, any
consequential-damages exclusion, and the PW-US-FED-3 legal-rights statement (16 C.F.R. 701.3(a)(1) to (9)).
State whether returning an owner-registration card is a condition of coverage; if it is not, say so, since a
clause may not impose or imply that condition (16 C.F.R. 701.4, 700.7(a), (b)).

Do not condition the warranty's validity on using a particular branded repair service or replacement part,
and do not word an authorized-dealer or genuine-parts requirement as a condition of coverage for
non-warranty service; the trader may exclude liability for a defect it shows was actually caused by
unauthorized parts or service, which is different from conditioning the warranty on exclusive use of
authorized channels (16 C.F.R. 700.10(a), (c)). Do not state that the trader's own decision, or a
designated third party's decision, is final or binding in a warranty dispute (16 C.F.R. 700.8;
15 U.S.C. 2310(d)).

Where the business uses an informal dispute settlement mechanism under Part 703, disclose its availability,
contact channel, and whether resort to it is a precondition to suing under the Act, together with the
statement that resort is not required for remedies arising outside the Act (16 C.F.R. 703.2(b)(1) to (4));
this is a separate disclosure from the warranty's own content items above, not a substitute for them.

## Must not

- No clause may define conformity by the contract's own promises alone, silent on the objective floor a
  consumer may reasonably expect for the product's type (Art. 6, 7(1) Directive (EU) 2019/771; Art. 6 to 8
  Directive (EU) 2019/770; § 434 Abs. 1 to 3 BGB; § 327e Abs. 2 BGB).
- No general sold-as-seen or blanket disclaimer may substitute for the specific-disclosure-plus-
  separate-acceptance route for a named deviation; without both elements, the product is measured against
  the full objective standard as if the clause did not exist (Art. 7(5) Directive (EU) 2019/771; Art. 8(5)
  Directive (EU) 2019/770; § 434 Abs. 3, § 476 Abs. 1 Satz 2 BGB; § 327h BGB).
- No clause may shorten the two-year liability period for goods, or the two-year (or longer continuous-
  supply) window for digital content or a digital service, except for second-hand goods by agreement and
  never below one year (Art. 10(1), (6) Directive (EU) 2019/771; Art. 11(2), (3) Directive (EU) 2019/770;
  § 475c Abs. 2 BGB).
- No clause may force the consumer into one specific remedy for goods, repair only or replacement only,
  without the disproportionate-cost or impossibility test (Art. 13(2) Directive (EU) 2019/771; § 439 Abs. 4
  BGB).
- No clause may charge the consumer for return shipping on termination, or for removal or reinstallation
  costs tied to repair or replacement (Art. 14(2), (3), 16(3)(a) Directive (EU) 2019/771; § 439 Abs. 3 BGB
  (conditions apply), § 475 Abs. 7 BGB).
- No clause may present a commercial guarantee or Garantieerklärung as the consumer's only recourse; the
  statement itself must say the statutory remedies are unaffected (Art. 17(2)(a) Directive (EU) 2019/771;
  § 479 Abs. 1 BGB).
- No clause may exclude, derogate from, or vary to the consumer's detriment the national measures
  transposing either Directive before the consumer has brought the lack of conformity to the trader's
  attention (Art. 21(1) Directive (EU) 2019/771; Art. 22(1) Directive (EU) 2019/770; § 476 Abs. 1, 2 BGB).
- No US warranty may be labeled "full (statement of duration) warranty" without actually meeting the federal
  minimum standards, and no warranty of either designation may state that the trader's own decision on a
  dispute is final or binding (15 U.S.C. 2304(e), 2310(d); 16 C.F.R. 700.8).
- No US warranty may condition coverage on returning a registration card, or imply that condition
  (16 C.F.R. 700.7(a), (b)), and no limited-parts-and-labor warranty may require the consumer to use only
  warrantor-identified service to install replacement parts (16 C.F.R. 700.10(b)).
- No clause may impede an independent repairer's use of compliant original, used, compatible, or
  3D-printed spare parts for goods within the EU repair regime's reach, or set spare-parts pricing to
  discourage repair (Art. 5(6) Directive (EU) 2024/1799; § 479c, § 479e BGB).

## Drafting notes

No conflicts.md entry decides a module choice for this clause yet; add one once that file exists, since a
US-drafted warranty template commonly states a flat 90-day or 1-year limited warranty with an "as is"
disclaimer for everything else, which the EU and German modules must replace for an EU or German consumer
rather than merely append to. The EU and German modules set a floor the trader cannot contract below; the
US module applies only to the extent the trader chooses to give a written warranty at all, so a product
sold without any written warranty in the US still needs the EU and German modules wherever it reaches a
consumer there.

Keep the goods-style choice-between-repair-and-replacement language out of a digital-content or
digital-service version of this clause: EU and German law give the trader, not the consumer, the choice of
cure method there, which is the opposite of the goods rule.

Where a continuously supplied product's update or uptime commitment is drafted, state the duration and the
consequence of a missed update concretely rather than leaving the reasonable-expectation standard to carry
the whole clause; a specific notice narrows what a consumer can later claim they expected.

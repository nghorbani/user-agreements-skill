# Indemnity

Law as of: 2026-10-20

## Purpose

This clause states when the other party (a user, a customer, a business counterparty) covers the
trader's losses, defense costs, or liability arising from that party's own conduct: the content
they upload, how they use the service, or their own breach of the contract. It is distinct from
the trader's own liability caps and disclaimers toward the other party (see
`references/clauses/warranty-and-conformity.md` for the conformity and remedy regime that clause
covers) and from the separate question of which party bears a third party's product-liability
claim. An indemnity clause works in only one direction at a time: it never substitutes for, or
narrows, a right the other reference files in this set give the consumer against the trader.

## Model wording

[Counterparty name] covers [trader name] for a loss, cost, or claim a third party brings against
[trader name] because of: content [counterparty name] uploads, posts, or sends through [the
service]; [counterparty name]'s own breach of this contract; or [counterparty name]'s use of [the
service] in a way this contract does not allow. This covers [trader name]'s reasonable costs of
defending the claim and any amount [trader name] has to pay because of it, once the claim is
confirmed or settled with [counterparty name]'s agreement.

This does not cover a claim that comes from [trader name]'s own breach of this contract, from
[trader name]'s own negligence, or from [trader name] not following [trader name]'s own published
rules for using [the service]. [Trader name] tells [counterparty name] promptly about a claim this
clause might cover, lets [counterparty name] take part in defending it, and does not settle it in a
way that admits fault on [counterparty name]'s part without [counterparty name]'s agreement.

[Business-to-business module: add a stated cap on the amount covered and a stated time limit for
bringing a claim under this clause, set out in the pricing or order document [trader name] and
[counterparty name] actually negotiate.]

## Modules

### EU consumers

Drop this clause for an EU consumer. An indemnity running from the consumer toward the trader is a
term not individually negotiated, so it is tested under the significant-imbalance and good-faith
standard like any other standard term (Art. 3(1) Directive 93/13/EEC). An indemnity clause that
makes the consumer responsible for a claim regardless of whose conduct actually caused it, or that
reads as obliging the consumer to perform in full while leaving the trader's own performance
unaffected, falls within the Annex pattern of binding the consumer to obligations the trader itself
does not have to mirror (Annex point (o) Directive 93/13/EEC). An indemnity clause worded to shift
onto the consumer the burden of proving the claim did not arise from the consumer's own conduct, or
to deny the consumer a defense or set-off against the amount claimed, falls within the Annex
patterns of unduly limiting the consumer's legal rights for non-performance (Annex point (b)) and
excluding or hindering the consumer's right to pursue a remedy by shifting a burden of proof onto
them (Annex point (q)) (Directive 93/13/EEC). Where a clause of this kind is used anyway for a
reason specific to the contract, it must be in plain, intelligible language the consumer can use to
work out its actual financial exposure, not merely grammatically correct wording (Art. 5 Directive
93/13/EEC); a clause failing this is a significant factor pointing toward unfairness even apart from
its content. An indemnity clause placed in a part of the document the consumer would not expect it,
for example buried in a section about something else, risks being treated as a term the consumer
had no real opportunity to become acquainted with before conclusion, within the Annex pattern of
irrevocably binding the consumer to such a term (Annex point (i) Directive 93/13/EEC). A clause
asking the consumer to waive, in advance, the right to have a court assess this very term's
fairness is itself ineffective (Art. 6(1) Directive 93/13/EEC).

### EU business customers

An indemnity clause toward a business counterparty is not reached by Directive 93/13/EEC at all,
since the directive applies only where the other party is a consumer (Art. 2(c) Directive
93/13/EEC). Treat the cap, the notice-and-control mechanics, and the time limit as ordinary
commercial terms for the parties' chosen jurisdiction and governing law, which this reference file
does not state.

### Germany

Drop this clause for a consumer (Verbrauchervertrag) contract, for the same reason as the EU
consumer module: §§ 305c Abs. 2, 307 BGB apply to a preformulated term even meant for one-off use,
and assessing unreasonable disadvantage takes the circumstances of conclusion into account (§ 310
Abs. 3 BGB). An indemnity clause against a consumer that shifts onto the consumer the burden of
proving a circumstance within the trader's own sphere of responsibility, rather than the trader's
own conduct, is void outright: the statute names exactly this shift as a prohibited clause, with no
weighing test available to save it (§ 309 Nr. 12 Buchst. a BGB). The same prohibition reaches an
indemnity clause that has the consumer confirm particular facts bearing on the claim, for example
confirming that uploaded content was their own or that no breach occurred on the trader's side; a
separately signed or separately qualified-electronically-signed acknowledgment of receipt is carved
out of this confirmed-facts ban (§ 309 Nr. 12 Buchst. b BGB). An indemnity clause structured so
it functions as a flat compensation sum rather than cover for an actual, proven loss must not exceed
the loss ordinarily expected in the case it covers, and must let the consumer show no loss arose, or
a materially lower one, without that defense being excluded (§ 309 Nr. 5 Buchst. a, b BGB). An
indemnity clause must not, in substance, exclude or limit the trader's own liability for death,
personal injury, or health harm from the trader's own negligent breach, or from an intentional or
grossly negligent breach by the trader's own staff or vicarious agents, by routing that liability
back onto the consumer through the indemnity's wording (§ 309 Nr. 7 Buchst. a, b BGB); this is the
same ban that applies to a direct liability-exclusion clause, read onto an indemnity that reaches
the same result indirectly.

Against a business counterparty, § 309 falls away entirely and § 308 Nr. 1 to 9 fall away too, but
§ 307 Abs. 1, 2 still applies, with commercial custom and usage weighed in; a § 309 Nr. 12-style
burden-shift or a § 309 Nr. 7-style indirect liability transfer can still fail § 307 review for a
business counterparty, just without the automatic, no-weighing invalidity a consumer contract
carries (§ 310 Abs. 1 BGB). Where the business counterparty is itself a large undertaking, a named
financial-sector party, or one of the public bodies § 310 Abs. 1a lists, confirm that status before
relying on even the weighed § 307 standard, since the same provision also changes which rules apply
at all to banking, financial-services, investment, payment-services, and comparable contracts (§
310 Abs. 1a BGB, read with § 1 Abs. 1 Satz 2 KWG and the other sector statutes it cross-refers to).

An indemnity clause the trader cannot reasonably expect the other party to anticipate, given the
contract's outward appearance (for example, an indemnity tucked into a section about something
else, in a document that otherwise reads as a simple service agreement), does not become part of
the contract at all, which is a stronger result than invalidity (§ 305c Abs. 1 BGB); any remaining
doubt in how an indemnity clause is worded is resolved against the trader as the party putting it
forward (§ 305c Abs. 2 BGB).

### US

Draft the broader US-style indemnity (covering content, breach, and out-of-scope use as the model
wording states) for the US consumer and US business modules, since none of the three reference
files in this set that cover US law state a content-control rule equivalent to § 307 or § 309 BGB,
or an Annex-style unfairness test, for an ordinary commercial or consumer indemnity clause as such.
Three California-specific limits still apply wherever the clause is governed by California law or
reaches a California consumer.

First, an indemnity clause must not, however labeled, have the effect of covering the trader's own
fraud, the trader's own willful injury to a person or to property, or the trader's own violation of
law, whether that violation was willful or negligent; this reaches the clause's actual effect, not
only language that says so directly, so an indemnity broad enough to make the consumer responsible
for a claim arising from the trader's own conduct in one of these three categories is unenforceable
to that extent (Cal. Civ. Code § 1668). State the carve-out for these three categories in the clause
itself rather than leaving it to be read in.

Second, an indemnity clause, like any other clause, can expose the whole contract to
nonenforcement, beyond the clause itself, if a court finds it unconscionable as a matter of law at
the time the contract was made; this is assessed against the clause's actual commercial setting,
purpose, and effect, so keep records of that setting and purpose for a clause that shifts
substantial, open-ended cost or liability onto a consumer (Cal. Civ. Code § 1670.5(a), (b)).

Third, an indemnity clause must not have the effect of making a California consumer give up a right
or remedy the Consumers Legal Remedies Act gives, since that bar reaches the whole CLRA title and
applies regardless of how the clause is worded (Cal. Civ. Code § 1751). An indemnity requiring the
consumer to cover the trader's own cost of defending a CLRA claim the consumer brought, or
conditioning the consumer's CLRA remedy on first covering the trader's related costs, risks this
ground independently of whether the clause calls itself a waiver.

## Must not

- No indemnity clause against a consumer may make the consumer responsible for a claim regardless
  of whose conduct caused it, or oblige the consumer to perform the indemnity in full while the
  trader's own performance stays unaffected (Annex point (o) Directive 93/13/EEC).
- No indemnity clause against a consumer may shift onto the consumer the burden of proving the
  claim did not arise from their own conduct, or deny the consumer a defense or set-off against the
  amount claimed (Annex points (b), (q) Directive 93/13/EEC; § 309 Nr. 12 Buchst. a BGB for Germany
  specifically). The same German rule also bars having the consumer confirm particular facts
  bearing on the claim, subject to the statute's own carve-out for a separately signed or
  separately qualified-electronically-signed acknowledgment of receipt (§ 309 Nr. 12 Buchst. b
  BGB).
- No indemnity clause against a consumer may be placed where the consumer would not expect it, such
  as buried in a section about something else, since this risks being treated as a term the
  consumer had no real opportunity to become acquainted with before conclusion (Annex point (i)
  Directive 93/13/EEC; § 305c Abs. 1 BGB for Germany specifically).
- No indemnity clause may, in substance, route the trader's own liability for death, personal
  injury, or health harm from the trader's negligent, intentional, or grossly negligent breach back
  onto the other party (§ 309 Nr. 7 Buchst. a, b BGB).
- No indemnity clause structured as a flat sum may exceed the loss ordinarily expected in the case
  it covers, or deny the other party the right to show no loss, or a lower loss, arose (§ 309 Nr. 5
  Buchst. a, b BGB).
- No indemnity clause, however labeled, may have the effect of covering the trader's own fraud,
  willful injury to person or property, or violation of law, whether willful or negligent (Cal. Civ.
  Code § 1668).
- No indemnity clause may have the effect of making a California consumer give up a right or remedy
  the Consumers Legal Remedies Act gives (Cal. Civ. Code § 1751).
- No indemnity clause against a consumer may be presented in wording that is only formally correct;
  wording the consumer cannot use to work out the actual financial exposure fails the plain,
  intelligible language standard regardless of grammatical correctness (Art. 5 Directive 93/13/EEC).

## Drafting notes

No conflicts.md entry decides a module choice for this clause yet; add one once that file is
revisited, since the underlying pattern matches several existing entries there in direction if not
in subject (C-5's point that EU and German law give no opening for a blanket consumer-disadvantaging
clause, and C-6's point that a one-sided obligation the trader itself need not mirror is an Annex
pattern). A consumer-facing indemnity clause is, in practice, dropped outright for the EU and German
modules rather than narrowed, since an indemnity running only from the consumer to the trader is
exactly the one-sided structure Annex point (o) and § 309 Nr. 12 BGB target; narrowing it to the
consumer's own conduct (the model wording's content, breach, and misuse categories) does not, on its
own, cure the burden-of-proof and one-sidedness problems unless the clause also states who proves
what and lets the consumer raise the trader's own contribution to the loss.

A business-to-business indemnity, in any of the three jurisdictions this file covers, is the normal
commercial term and the one where negotiation (a stated cap, a stated time limit, a defense-and-
control mechanic) is both expected and the main way the clause survives a content-control or
unconscionability review; keep that negotiated structure in the model wording's bracketed
business-to-business module rather than only in a one-off recital.

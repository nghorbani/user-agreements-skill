# Governing law and jurisdiction

Law as of: 2026-10-20

## Purpose

This clause states which country's law governs the contract and, where the document fixes one,
which court may hear a dispute that goes to court. It does not cover arbitration, informal
complaint steps or Germany's consumer-arbitration-body notices; those sit in a separate dispute-
resolution clause this file does not cover.

## Model wording

This agreement and any dispute arising out of or in connection with it, including a dispute about
its existence, validity or termination, are governed by the law of [governing law jurisdiction],
without regard to its conflict-of-law rules. The courts of [named court or jurisdiction] have
[exclusive / non-exclusive] jurisdiction to settle any dispute arising out of or in connection
with this agreement.

## Modules

### EU consumers

Keep the home-jurisdiction choice of law, but add a sentence the core wording does not carry: this
choice of law does not deprive the user of the protection of any mandatory rule of the law that
would apply, in the absence of this clause, under the law of the country where the user has their
habitual residence (Rome I Art. 6(2)). Where the business pursues or directs commercial activity to
the user's country of habitual residence, that country's mandatory consumer-protection rules apply
regardless of the clause (Rome I Art. 6(1)). A clause naming the business's home law without this
disclosure, or one that otherwise creates the impression that only the named law governs, is an
unfair term (Art. 3(1) UCTD; Case C-191/15, VKI v Amazon, paragraph 71 and operative part 2).

Drop the named-court sentence and its exclusivity statement. State instead that the user may bring
proceedings in the courts of the business's member state of domicile, or, at the user's own choice,
in the courts of the user's own member state of domicile, and that the business may bring
proceedings against the user only in the courts of the user's own member state of domicile
(Brussels Ia Art. 17(1)(c), Art. 18(1), (2)). This dual-forum statement cannot be narrowed by
agreement reached before a dispute arises, except by an agreement giving the user an additional
forum, or one between parties already sharing a member state domicile at the time of contracting,
naming that state's courts (Brussels Ia Art. 19(1) to (3)). A forum clause doing anything else
read for an EU consumer is void as against this protection.

### EU business customers

Keep the model wording's choice-of-law sentence as written; Rome I's consumer-contract floor does
not reach a contract between two businesses. For the forum sentence, a jurisdiction clause naming
a member state's court binds the parties, and that court has jurisdiction unless the agreement is
null and void as to its substantive validity under that member state's own law; that jurisdiction
is exclusive unless the clause itself says otherwise, provided the clause is in writing, evidenced
in writing, in a form according with the parties' own established practice, or in a form according
with a usage the parties knew or ought to have known in that trade (Brussels Ia Art. 25(1)(a) to
(c)). State expressly whether the jurisdiction is exclusive or non-exclusive, since silence defaults to
exclusive (Brussels Ia Art. 25(1)). An electronic acceptance flow that lets the business customer
print or save the terms before the contract is concluded, such as a click to accept followed by a
separate click opening the terms in a new window, satisfies the writing requirement whether or not
that window opens automatically (Brussels Ia Art. 25(1)(a), (2); Case C-322/14, El Majdoub v
CarsOnTheWeb.Deutschland, paragraphs 39, 40 and operative part). The jurisdiction clause is treated
as independent of the rest of the contract, so a challenge to the contract's own validity does not
by itself put the jurisdiction clause in question (Brussels Ia Art. 25(5)).

### Germany

Use the EU consumers module as written; Rome I and Brussels Ia apply directly as EU regulations and
no separate German transposition changes this clause. Where the business's standard terms toward a
consumer name a single member state's court instead of stating the Brussels Ia dual forum, that
clause is exposed as an unfair term under German implementation of the UCTD in the same way as
under the directive itself (Art. 3(1) UCTD).

### US

Keep the model wording's choice-of-law and forum sentences as written, naming the business's home
state or another chosen US jurisdiction for both law and forum. State the arbitrator-appointment
method, the seat and the language of any linked arbitration proceeding in the dispute-resolution
clause, not here; this clause fixes only the law and the court for a dispute that is not
arbitrated. US federal law has no general choice-of-law rule and imposes no mandatory-protection
disclosure or dual-forum requirement for a US user; the references here do not cover a state-by-state
survey of this point, so treat any state-specific limit on a forum or choice-of-law clause as an
open point for counsel.

## Must not

- A clause naming the trader's home member state's law must not omit the disclosure that the user
  keeps the mandatory consumer-protection rules of their own habitual-residence law (Rome I Art.
  6(1), (2); Case C-191/15, VKI v Amazon, paragraph 71 and operative part 2).
- A clause must not create the impression, by what it says or what it leaves out, that only the
  named law governs the contract (VKI v Amazon, paragraph 71).
- A clause must not fix an EU consumer's forum to a single named court in place of the two fora
  Brussels Ia Art. 18(1) gives the consumer, except through an agreement reached after the dispute
  arose, one giving the consumer an additional forum, or one between parties who already shared a
  member state domicile at contracting (Brussels Ia Art. 19(1) to (3)).
- A jurisdiction or choice-of-law clause must not be used to redirect a collective injunction
  against unfair terms to the trader's own home-country law (VKI v Amazon, paragraphs 46, 47).
- A jurisdiction clause relying on Brussels Ia Art. 25(1) must not be assumed valid without one of
  its three required forms (in writing or evidenced in writing; according with the parties'
  established practice; according with a known trade usage), and must not be assumed to satisfy
  genuine consensus by form alone, since consensus is a separate, logically prior question from
  form (Brussels Ia Art. 25(1)(a) to (c); El Majdoub, paragraphs 29, 30).
- For a sexual assault or sexual harassment dispute accruing on or after March 3, 2022, no clause
  elsewhere in the document, including a predispute arbitration agreement or joint-action waiver,
  may be read to override this clause's choice of forum where the claimant elects a judicial forum
  for that dispute; the question whether such an agreement is valid or enforceable for that dispute
  is for a court, not an arbitrator, regardless of what a delegation clause in the document says (9
  U.S.C. §§ 401(2), 402(a), (b)).

## Drafting notes

See `references/conflicts.md` C-3 (governing-law clause naming the trader's home state with no
mandatory-protection carve-out) and C-4 (forum-selection clause fixing one court for all claims)
for the decision this clause follows: split the document, keep the US home-state choice of law and
single-forum clause for the US and EU-business modules, and replace both for the EU consumer
module with the mandatory-protection disclosure and the Brussels Ia dual-forum statement. The
arbitration carve-out in Must not reflects the Federal Arbitration Act's own chapter 4 rule that a
court, not this clause or an arbitration clause's delegation language, decides forum for a sexual
assault or harassment dispute; see `references/clauses/dispute-resolution.md` for the rest of that
carve-out and for the informal-complaint and arbitration wording this clause does not carry.

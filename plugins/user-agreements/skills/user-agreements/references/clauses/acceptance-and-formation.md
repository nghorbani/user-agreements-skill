# Acceptance and formation

Law as of: 2026-10-20

## Purpose

This clause is the sign-up or checkout screen's own assent notice: the sentence and control that
turn a click, a tap or continued use into a binding agreement to the terms. It sets what the
notice must say, where it must sit, and what kind of control it must be paired with, so that a
court later asked whether a contract formed at all can say yes. It covers acceptance in the United
States (where the question is whether a contract formed in the first place), the parallel German
and EU duties that apply once an order is about to be placed (which technical steps to disclose,
how to let the consumer store the terms, how to confirm the order), and the baseline that an
electronic record or signature is not denied effect merely for being electronic. It does not cover
withdrawal, cancellation buttons or what the terms themselves must say once formed; those sit in
other clause files.

## Model wording

By [clicking the button labeled "[exact button label]" / continuing to [the action that completes
sign-up or checkout]], you agree to these Terms of Service and to our Privacy Policy.

[Link to the full terms, placed immediately next to this sentence and the control it names, in a
color and weight that contrasts with the surrounding text.]

Before you complete your order, you can review and change the items in your cart, your delivery
address and your payment method. Once you submit the order, we will send you an electronic
confirmation to the email address you provided.

We will keep a copy of these terms and your order details and make them available to you on
request. This agreement, your signature and your order are not denied legal effect only because
they are in electronic form.

## Modules

### EU consumers

Add, immediately before the order button and with nothing else between the two, the four-point
disclosure: the technical steps to conclude the contract, whether the business will file the
concluded contract and whether the consumer can access it, the technical means to spot and correct
an input error before submitting, and the languages the contract can be concluded in (Art. 10(1)(a)
to (d) Directive 2000/31/EC). Pair the disclosure with a working, not merely described, way to
change the order before submission; disclosing a correction step without providing one satisfies
neither duty.

Make the full terms available to store and reproduce, not displayed once and gone, in every channel
including one that runs exclusively by email (Art. 10(3), Art. 10(4) Directive 2000/31/EC). Send an
electronic acknowledgement of the order without undue delay once it is placed through the site; the
order and the acknowledgement each count as received once the addressed party can access them, not
once sent (Art. 11(1) Directive 2000/31/EC). Where the order is placed through an online interface,
pair this with a working input-error correction step before the order is submitted (Art. 11(2)
Directive 2000/31/EC).

Where the business states a price anywhere on the path to this screen, that statement says clearly
and unambiguously whether it includes tax and delivery; a breakdown shown only elsewhere does not
cure a bare figure at the point of sale.

Keep the standing, permanently linked legal-notice page (name, geographic address, an email address
for rapid contact, trade register entry where registered, supervisory authority where one applies)
separate from this screen; it is a standing duty, not a sign-up step (Art. 5(1) Directive
2000/31/EC).

### EU business customers

Art. 10(1), 10(2) and Art. 11(1), (2) Directive 2000/31/EC fall away only where the two parties have
actually agreed otherwise and neither of them is a consumer; a one-sided label calling the
counterparty a business does not switch the duties off by itself. Keep the model wording's
assent sentence and order-confirmation mechanics for a business sign-up unless that actual agreement
exists; where it does, say in the business-facing terms which of Art. 10 and 11 the parties have
displaced.

The storable-terms duty (Art. 10(3)) and the deemed-receipt rule for when an order and its
acknowledgement count as received (the second indent of Art. 11(1)) apply even to a contract
concluded exclusively by email or an equivalent one-to-one channel; do not drop either one for an
email-only business sales channel (Art. 10(4), Art. 11(3) Directive 2000/31/EC).

### Germany

Treat the assent sentence and the order button as one unit reviewed on the button's own wording
alone. The order button, or any other control that places a paid order, carries wording that, read
on its own with nothing else on the page supplying meaning, tells an average reasonably well
informed and observant consumer that activating it creates a payment obligation; "zahlungspflichtig
bestellen" meets this on its own words, and an equally unambiguous substitute is allowed, but a
softer or reservation-flavored verb is a drafting risk precisely because its everyday meaning can
also read as a free, no-obligation step (§ 312j Abs. 3 Satz 2 BGB; PW-DE-1; Case C-249/21,
Fuhrmann-2-GmbH v B., judgment of 7 April 2022, paras. 26, 28 to 30, 33). A price shown earlier in
the flow or a nearby terms link cannot supply the missing meaning if the button's own words do not
carry it.

This order button is one of five controls across the site that the law holds to a fixed,
words-only label: the order button itself (PW-DE-1, "zahlungspflichtig bestellen"); the
cancellation button on a continuing-contract site (PW-DE-2, "Verträge hier kündigen") and its
confirmation button (PW-DE-3, "jetzt kündigen"); and the withdrawal function on a
distance-contract site (PW-DE-4, "Vertrag widerrufen") and its confirmation function (PW-DE-5,
"Widerruf bestätigen"). Check all five together wherever a site carries more than one of these
controls; only PW-DE-1 sits on this clause's own screen, and the other four belong to the
cancellation and withdrawal clause files, but a release that changes one button's wording is the
moment to confirm the other four still read correctly too.

Immediately before the order button, in a highlighted manner and not behind a link, give the EGBGB
Art. 246a § 1 Abs. 1 Satz 1 Nr. 1, 5 to 8, 11a, 14 and 15 information: price, main characteristics,
delivery, duration, minimum contract term, and the rest of that list (§ 312j Abs. 2 BGB). Give the
input-error correction step in the flow itself, not through a later support channel (§ 312i Abs. 1
Nr. 1 BGB), and state, no later than the start of the ordering process, any delivery restriction and
which payment means are accepted (§ 312j Abs. 1 BGB). None of this applies to a contract concluded
exclusively through individual communication between the parties (§ 312i Abs. 2 BGB, which does not
reach the storable-terms duty).

Inform the consumer per EGBGB Art. 246a before the contract is concluded, becoming contract content
unless expressly agreed otherwise (§ 312d BGB); for a financial-service contract, use EGBGB Art.
246b §§ 1 to 3 and the Art. 246b § 4 interface design instead. Send a confirmation on a durable
medium reproducing the contract content within a reasonable time, at the latest on delivery or
before the service starts, and where the trader called the consumer to conclude the contract,
have the caller disclose its identity and the call's commercial purpose at the start of the call
(§ 312f Abs. 2 BGB; § 312a Abs. 1 BGB). Keep records that can later show which information a given
consumer was shown and when; the trader carries the burden of proving an information duty was met
(§ 312m Abs. 2 BGB).

### US

Pair the assent sentence with whichever control actually completes the step it describes, and name
that control by its real, current label; a sentence that says "By signing up you agree" next to a
button labeled only "Continue" does not establish assent, because the words do not match the
button. Update the sentence whenever the button's label changes so the two never drift out of sync
across a release.

Place the sentence on the same screen as the control, in a font size and color comparably prominent
to the surrounding text, directly next to (not merely elsewhere on) the control, and visible without
scrolling to find it. Where the terms are reached by hyperlink rather than shown in full, render the
link in a color that contrasts with the plain text around it and make it recognizable as a link, not
merely underlined in the same shade; a user who proceeds without opening a conspicuous, well-labeled
link is still on notice of what clicking means. Put no unrelated question, offer or control between
the notice and the button it governs.

ESIGN's permission to give a notice or disclosure electronically does not excuse a placement,
posting or display requirement that some other statute, regulation or rule of law separately
imposes on it; where another law requires a warning, notice or disclosure to be posted, displayed or
otherwise made conspicuous in a particular way, that requirement survives alongside, and on top of,
the electronic-form permission ESIGN itself gives (15 U.S.C. § 7001(f)).

Where sign-up spans more than one screen, repeat the notice-and-control pairing on the one screen
where the user takes the action argued to form the contract; do not add up a notice shown on an
earlier screen to a bare button on a later one. Do not rely on a pre-checked assent box: require an
affirmative, unchecked check or an "I accept" click instead. Do not send the notice only after the
user has already completed sign-up, by email, text or chat; a disclaimer delivered after the fact
rarely cures a missing notice at the moment of the click.

Where a legally required written disclosure is to be given electronically instead of on paper,
collect, before the consumer consents: a statement covering the paper option, the withdrawal right
and any conditions or fees on withdrawing consent, whether consent covers this one transaction or a
category of records, the procedure to withdraw and to update contact information, the paper-copy
procedure and its fee, and a hardware-and-software statement, followed by the consumer's electronic
consent given in a way that demonstrates the consumer can access that electronic form. Repeat the
statement and the access check if hardware or software requirements later change in a way that
creates a material risk the consumer can no longer access the record.

Keep every record the business is legally required to retain in a format that stays accurately
reproducible and accessible, for as long as required, to everyone entitled to it; an archive only
the business itself can open does not satisfy this.

## Must not

- No clause may treat a click on a button, or mere continued use of the site, as creating a binding
  agreement where no notice on that same screen ties the specific act to acceptance; an unenforceable
  footer-link-only arrangement puts the burden of designing adequate notice on the business, not on
  the user to go looking for one (15 U.S.C. § 7001(b)(2); Nguyen v. Barnes & Noble Inc., 763 F.3d
  1171, slip op. at 16).
- No assent sentence may rely only on "I agree" or "I understand and agree to the Terms &
  Conditions," with no clause naming the actual action the user is about to take, regardless of how
  prominent the sentence is or how close it sits to the button (Berman v. Freedom Fin. Network, LLC,
  30 F.4th 849, slip op. at 13 to 14; Godun v. JustAnswer LLC, 135 F.4th 699, slip op. at 20 to 21).
- No clause may add up a notice shown on one screen of a multi-screen flow with an assent-linkage
  statement shown on a different screen to manufacture the single-screen pairing the rule requires
  (Chabolla v. ClassPass, Inc., 129 F.4th 1147, slip op. at 21); the same bullet reaches an unrelated
  prompt or offer placed between the notice and the button it governs, since it can make a reader
  take the button to refer to that prompt instead of the terms (Chabolla, 129 F.4th 1147, slip op. at
  19 to 20).
- No messages, texts, emails or chat disclaimers sent after the user has already signed up may stand
  in for the missing notice at the moment of the click (Godun v. JustAnswer LLC, 135 F.4th 699, slip
  op. at 23 to 24, n.7).
- No clause may claim that a state's own electronic-transactions rule displaces the federal baseline
  unless that state has enacted the 1999 Uniform Electronic Transactions Act or an independent,
  technology-neutral alternative that is consistent with it and, if enacted after 30 June 2000, names
  the federal statute; every other narrowing state rule is preempted (15 U.S.C. § 7002(a), (c)).
- No clause may claim the federal electronic-record baseline for a will, a codicil, a testamentary
  trust, a state family-law matter, most of the Uniform Commercial Code, a court order or official
  court document, a utility cancellation notice, a default, foreclosure or eviction notice on a
  primary residence, a health or life insurance cancellation notice, a product recall or
  safety-failure notice, or a hazardous-materials transport document, without checking the law that
  actually governs that record's form (15 U.S.C. § 7003(a), (b)).
- No order flow may bury the EGBGB Art. 246a Abs. 2 information behind a link reached only by leaving
  the checkout page, or let the order button carry generic wording in place of language that, on its
  own, tells the consumer activating it creates a payment obligation (§ 312j Abs. 2, 3 BGB).
- No clause may claim a contract was concluded despite an order button that does not meet the
  words-only test, or substitute a mere link for the required durable-medium copy or confirmation of
  the contract (§ 312j Abs. 4 BGB; § 312m Abs. 1 BGB).
- No clause may disapply the pre-order disclosure, the storable-terms duty, or the order-and-receipt
  confirmation duties for an EU business counterparty on the strength of a one-sided label alone,
  without an actual agreement between two non-consumer parties to displace them (Art. 10, 11
  Directive 2000/31/EC).

## Drafting notes

No conflicts.md entry decides a module choice for this clause yet; add one once that file exists.
A US-drafted sign-up flow commonly uses a single undifferentiated click or a passive
continued-use statement to cover the whole document; the EU and German modules above do not merely
add to that flow, they add disclosure and confirmation steps the US flow has no equivalent of, and
the US module's notice-and-control pairing is a stricter, screen-specific test than a US-style
browsewrap or sign-in-wrap notice typically meets.

Match the bracketed button label in the model wording to the real, current label of the real
control on the screen it sits on; a release that renames the button without updating this sentence
reopens the formation question this clause exists to close.

Where a flow spans checkout, registration and a separate subscription opt-in, give each its own
assent pairing under the US module rather than relying on one pairing shown earlier in the flow to
carry the rest.

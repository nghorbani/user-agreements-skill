# Children and age

Law as of: 2026-10-20

## Purpose

This clause states who may use the service by age, what happens when a user is or might be a child,
and what the provider does and does not do with a child's personal information as a condition of that
use. It does not state the lawful basis the provider relies on for processing adult users' data, which
belongs in `references/clauses/privacy-and-data.md`; it does not state the platform's content
moderation or account suspension process, which belongs in
`references/clauses/moderation-and-suspension.md`; and it does not state the advertising-targeting
rules that apply to all users regardless of age, which belong in
`references/clauses/marketing-communications.md`. It covers only the age-gating, parental consent,
and minor-specific data and advertising restrictions that attach because a user is, or may be, a
child.

## Model wording

[The service] is not directed to children under 13, and [trader name] does not knowingly collect
personal information from a child under 13 without a parent's verified consent. [User name] confirms,
on registering, that [user name] is not under 13, or, if the laws of [user name]'s country or state set
a different age for giving their own consent to an information-society or online service, that [user
name] is not under that age.

Where [trader name] learns that a user is a child under the age that applies to them, and no verified
parental consent is on file, [trader name] will [limit the account to the activity consent is not
required for / pause the account / delete the information collected], and will tell the parent, before
collecting, using or sharing any of the child's personal information, what information [trader name]
wants to collect, why, who else will see it, and that the parent can agree to the collection and use
without agreeing to the sharing, unless the sharing is necessary to provide the part of [the service]
the child is asking for.

A parent who has given this consent may ask, at any time, what categories of their child's information
[trader name] holds, may ask [trader name] to stop collecting more of it, may ask for it to be deleted,
and may review what has been collected, subject to [trader name] first confirming the requester is the
child's parent. [Trader name] will not ask a child to hand over more personal information than [the
service]'s activity actually needs in order to let the child take part in it.

consumers and Germany below.]

## Modules

### EU consumers

Where the processing rests on consent and the service is an information society service offered
directly to a child, that consent is lawful only where given or authorised by the holder of parental
responsibility, verified by reasonable effort given the technology available; the applicable floor
runs from 13 to 16 depending on the Member State the child is in, so the registration flow cannot use
one hard-coded figure for every Member State it reaches (Art. 8(1), (2) Regulation (EU) 2016/679). A
service that states it is offered only to users 18 or over is not treated as a service offered directly
to a child, unless other evidence (the service's own content or marketing) says otherwise. Where a
service is partly online and partly delivered physically, apply this test to the online part only.

A low-risk, proportionate age check (asking a new user to state a birth year, or confirm they are not
under the applicable age) is enough where the processing risk is low; the check itself must not demand
more personal data than that. Once a user who gave or had parental consent reaches the age of digital
consent that applies to them, tell them they may confirm, change or withdraw that earlier consent.
Article 8 settles only the consent question; whether a minor can validly form the underlying contract
at all is left to the contract law of the Member State concerned, and a drafter needing that answer
should raise it as an open point rather than assume Article 8's age band answers it too.

Processing a child's data under the legitimate-interest basis needs a different, more protective
balance than processing an adult's: the child's interests will often outweigh the provider's own
commercial interest, and an extensive profiling or targeted-advertising use of a child's data will
generally fail that balance unless the provider can show no negative effect on the child.

Where the service is an online platform under the Digital Services Act, put in place proportionate
measures for a high level of privacy, safety and security for any minor who can access it, and do not
show an advertisement built on profiling that uses a user's personal data where the provider is aware,
with reasonable certainty, that the user is a minor; this duty does not require collecting extra
personal data just to find out whether a user is a minor.

### EU business customers

The age-gating and parental-consent rules above apply by the nature of the user, a natural person below
the relevant age, not by whether the contract is framed as consumer-facing or business-facing; a
business customer's own employee or representative who is a minor is still covered when they use the
service directly. Where a contract is formed between the provider and a company rather than a natural
person, state in the terms that the company confirms the individuals it lets use the service meet the
applicable age, since Article 8 attaches to the individual data subject regardless of who signs the
commercial contract.

### Germany

The references here do not cover Germany's own standard-terms-control rules or its own national floor
within the Article 8 band. The German standard-terms check is in `references/checklists/de-agb.md` and
`references/de/bgb-agb.md`; Germany's specific implementing choice within the Article 8 band is not
covered by any reference here. See Drafting notes below for what a drafter must still confirm before
relying on one figure for German users.

### US

An operator running a website or online service commercially, that collects or maintains personal
information from or about its users, is covered wherever the product is directed to children under 13,
or the operator has actual knowledge it is collecting personal information from a child under 13,
regardless of whether the product is generally directed to children. Merely linking to a children's
site or service through a directory, index, reference, pointer, or hypertext link does not, on its own,
make the linking product directed to children. A mixed-audience product, one that meets the
directed-to-children factors without targeting children as its primary audience, may run a neutral age
screen before collecting any personal information beyond a narrow set of no-consent exception purposes,
provided the screen does not default to a set age and does not encourage a false answer.

Before collecting, using or disclosing a child's personal information, give the parent direct notice,
with content that depends on why notice is being given, always including a link to the online privacy
notice; a posted online notice alone does not substitute for this direct notice where consent is being
sought. Get the parent's verifiable consent before that collection, use or disclosure, including before
any material change to a practice the parent already consented to, and let the parent consent to
collection and use without consenting to third-party disclosure unless the disclosure is necessary to
provide the feature the child is asking for; a disclosure made for money or other consideration, for
advertising, or to train or develop artificial intelligence technologies is never treated as necessary
in that sense and needs its own separate, freely given, specific and affirmative consent. Match the
consent-verification method to whether the service discloses a child's data to third parties: the
lighter "email plus" or "text plus" methods are available only where it does not.

Never condition a child's part in a game, a prize, or any other activity on the child handing over more
personal information than that activity reasonably needs, even where the broader collection has
consent; consent to the broader collection does not cure this separately. State, and follow, a written,
public retention policy for a child's personal information that gives the purpose for collecting it,
the business need for keeping each category, and a deletion timeframe, rather than describing
indefinite retention or an undated retention practice as compliant. On a parent's request, after
confirming the requester, give the categories of information collected about the child, an ongoing
opportunity to refuse further use or collection and to have the information deleted, and a means to
review what has been collected that does not unduly burden the parent.

## Must not

- No clause may collect, use or disclose a child's personal information without the direct parental
  notice and verifiable parental consent this clause describes, or treat a posted online notice alone
  as satisfying that duty where consent is being sought (16 C.F.R. § 312.4(a), (b)).
- No clause may condition a child's part in a game, prize or other activity on the child disclosing more
  personal information than that activity reasonably needs, even where consent covers the broader
  collection (16 C.F.R. § 312.7).
- No clause may describe indefinite retention of a child's personal information, or a retention practice
  with no stated purpose and timeframe, as compliant (16 C.F.R. § 312.10).
- No clause may claim information collected under a no-consent exception, or for the service's own
  internal operations, may also be used to contact a specific individual, build a profile on them, or
  for any other purpose beyond what that exception or internal-operations use allows (16 C.F.R. §
  312.2 "Support for the internal operations of the website or online service"; § 312.5(c)).
- No clause may self-certify a user's age with no verification step for a service, or part of a
  service, offered directly to a child or information-society user below the applicable Article 8
  floor (Art. 8(2) Regulation (EU) 2016/679).
- No clause may rely on legitimate interest for extensive profiling or targeted advertising aimed at a
  child without being able to show no negative effect on the child (Guidelines 1/2024, paras. 92 to 96,
  cited in `references/eu/gdpr-legal-bases.md`).
- No clause may show a minor an advertisement built on profiling where the provider is aware, with
  reasonable certainty, that the user is a minor (Art. 28(2) Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).

## Drafting notes

Conflicts entry C-16 in `references/conflicts.md` decides the module split above: keep this clause with
a carve-out, built around jurisdiction rather than one global age gate. A single hard-coded age of 13,
the COPPA floor, does not clear the GDPR's own 13-to-16 Article 8 range for every Member State, and does
not clear the Digital Services Act's flat bar on profiling-based advertising to a minor; build a
jurisdiction-aware age gate rather than one global threshold, and keep the EU module's unconditional
profiling-to-minors restriction, which the US module does not carry in the same form.

Germany's own choice of figure within the Article 8 band, and whether a German-specific
standard-terms-control check applies to this clause's wording, are not stated in this clause: the
standard-terms check itself is in `references/checklists/de-agb.md` and `references/de/bgb-agb.md`,
and a drafter who needs the German figure confirmed, rather than left to that checklist, should treat
it as an open point for counsel.

Whether a minor can validly conclude the underlying contract at all, once the Article 8 consent
question is settled, runs on the contract law of the Member State in question; this clause's sources do
not state that answer for any specific state, so a drafter needing it should raise it as an open point
rather than assume consent and contractual capacity are the same question.

Whether a specific product crosses the "directed to children" threshold under COPPA, and whether a
specific third-party disclosure is "necessary" to a requested feature for the separate-consent
requirement, are both fact-specific calls that this clause's sources leave case by case; document the
factors considered for the specific product rather than treating either test as settled by the clause
text alone.

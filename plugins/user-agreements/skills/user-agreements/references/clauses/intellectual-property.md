# Intellectual property

Law as of: 2026-10-20

## Purpose

This clause states what the trader owns in the service itself (its software, trademarks, and other
material) and what rights the trader reserves against a user who only gets a right to use the
service, not a right in it. It also states, for a product that hosts, caches, links to, or carries
material at a user's direction, how a notice-and-takedown process and a designated-agent filing can
qualify the trader for the US online-service-provider safe harbor. It does not state what a user may
do with their own copy of software the trader licenses to them, the narrow exceptions that licence
cannot remove, or the resale and transfer rules for a copy sold under a one-off fee
(`references/clauses/licence-grant.md`), and it does not state what rights a user keeps in the
content the user itself posts or uploads (`references/clauses/user-content.md`).

## Model wording

[Trader name] and its licensors own all right, title, and interest in [the service], including its
software, databases, trademarks, logos, and all other material [trader name] or its licensors make
available through it, apart from content [users] post, upload, or submit. Nothing in this contract
transfers any of that ownership to [licensee name] or any other party; [trader name] reserves every
right it does not expressly grant elsewhere in this contract.

Where [the software] is protected by copyright, the acts reserved to [trader name] as rightholder
are: reproducing it, permanently or temporarily, in whole or in part, by any means and in any form,
including the incidental reproduction that loading, displaying, running, transmitting, or storing it
needs; translating, adapting, arranging, or otherwise altering it, and reproducing the result; and
distributing the original or copies of it to the public, including by rental. [Licensee name] must
not carry out any of these acts except as `references/clauses/licence-grant.md` expressly allows.

[Where the service stores, transmits, caches, or links to material at a user's direction: [trader
name] has designated an agent to receive notices of claimed copyright infringement. A notice must be
in writing and must contain: a signature of the person authorized to act for the owner of the right
claimed to be infringed; identification of the copyrighted work claimed to be infringed, or a
representative list if the notice covers more than one work; identification of the material claimed
to be infringing, with information reasonably sufficient for [trader name] to locate it; the
complaining party's contact information; a statement that the complaining party has a good faith
belief that the use is not authorized; and a statement, made under penalty of perjury, that the
notice is accurate and that the complaining party is authorized to act for the rights owner.
[Trader name] may remove or disable access to material it reasonably believes is infringing. Where
[trader name] removes material a user posted in response to such a notice, it notifies the affected
user, and that user may send a counter notice containing: the user's signature; identification of
the material removed and where it appeared before removal; a statement, made under penalty of
perjury, of a good faith belief that the material was removed by mistake or misidentification; and
the user's name, address, and phone number, with consent to the jurisdiction of the user's local
federal court or, if outside the United States, a federal court where [trader name] may be found,
and acceptance of service of process from the person who sent the original notice or an agent of
that person. Unless the original notifier informs [trader name] that it has filed a court action,
[trader name] forwards a valid counter notice to the original notifier and restores the material
between ten and fourteen business days afterward.]

## Modules

### EU consumers

State the three restricted acts the same way `references/clauses/licence-grant.md` states them for
the licence itself, so this clause's ownership statement and that clause's grant do not drift apart:
reproduction, translation, adaptation and other alteration, and distribution including rental, all
reserved to the rightholder unless the licence expressly grants them (Art. 4(1), points (a) to (c),
Directive 2009/24/EC). This clause is the ownership and reservation-of-rights statement; the
exceptions a licence cannot remove (back-up copy, study and testing, decompilation for
interoperability) live in the licence grant itself, not here, so do not restate or re-qualify them in
this clause.

The Directive leaves patent rights, trademarks, unfair competition, trade secrets,
semiconductor-topography protection, and the general law of contract untouched alongside it (Art. 8,
first paragraph, Directive 2009/24/EC); state the trader's trademark and other non-software IP
ownership as a separate sentence from the software-copyright statement, since the Directive's rules
on restricted acts and user exceptions do not extend to a trademark or logo at all.

### EU business customers

Use the same ownership and restricted-act statement as the EU consumers module: the Directive does
not distinguish a consumer licensee from a business licensee on what is reserved to the rightholder
(Art. 4(1) Directive 2009/24/EC).

### Germany

Track the UrhG's own four restricted acts instead of the Directive's three, since the German
transposition lists a fourth: reproduction, including the incidental reproduction that loading,
displaying, running, transmitting, or storing the program needs (§ 69c Nr. 1 UrhG); translation,
adaptation, arrangement, and other alteration, and reproduction of the result (§ 69c Nr. 2 UrhG);
distribution, including rental (§ 69c Nr. 3 UrhG); and public communication, including making the
program available so members of the public can access it from a place and at a time they
individually choose (§ 69c Nr. 4 UrhG). State the fourth act here if the trader's own ownership
statement is meant to reserve it, since it has no counterpart in the three-act EU list above.

Where the software is work product from an employee or a contractor under a service relationship,
this clause's ownership statement rests on who the agreement actually assigns the economic rights
to, not on a default assumption of trader ownership: absent a contrary agreement, every economic
right in a program an employee creates in carrying out their duties, or on instruction, vests in the
employer alone, and the same follows for a comparable service relationship (§ 69b Abs. 1, 2 UrhG). A
trader asserting ownership over software built by a contractor states that the contractor's own
agreement assigns those rights, since § 69b's default does not reach every contractor relationship on
its own terms.

This chapter leaves other legal regimes untouched alongside it: invention protection,
semiconductor-topography protection, trademark protection, and protection against unfair competition
including trade and business secrets (§ 69g Abs. 1 UrhG); state the trader's trademark and other
non-software IP ownership separately from the software-copyright statement, for the same reason as
the EU module.

State, alongside the ownership statement, that the rightholder may require destruction of every copy
that was unlawfully produced, distributed, or intended for unlawful distribution, and of a means whose
sole purpose is removing or circumventing a technical program-protection mechanism, with a carve-out
for a means a cultural-heritage institution uses under its own § 61d permission (§ 69f Abs. 1, 2 UrhG).
For the cases of § 44b (with § 69d Abs. 4), § 60a (with § 69d Abs. 5), § 60e Abs. 1 or 6, and § 60f
Abs. 1 or 3, § 95b applies to the technical program-protection mechanism instead, so do not cite
§ 69f Abs. 1, 2 as the remedy for those cases (§ 69f Abs. 3 UrhG).

### US

State the designated-agent and notice-and-takedown module above only where the product in fact
stores, transmits, caches, or links to material at a user's direction (hosting, file sharing,
user-generated content, search or directory services, caching, or network transit), since 17 U.S.C.
§ 512 protects exactly those four categories of conduct and each stands on its own conditions
(§ 512(a) to (d), (n)). Where the product does none of these, drop the module rather than including
an inapplicable safe-harbor statement.

Where the module applies, publish the designated agent's name, address, phone number, and email
address on the public part of the service, and separately file the same information with the
Copyright Office; both steps are required together (17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(2)(A), (B)). Do not condition
the safe harbor on monitoring the service or affirmatively seeking facts of infringement, except to
the extent a standard technical measure under § 512(i) requires it (§ 512(m)(1)). Keep the repeat-
infringer termination policy reasonably implemented and communicated, and keep the product's own
technical measures from interfering with standard technical measures that meet the § 512(i)(2)
consensus and availability conditions (§ 512(i)(1), (i)(2)).

State the ownership and reservation-of-rights sentence in the model wording as a licence, not a sale,
recital for the software itself. Section 117(a) gives its archival and essential-step copying
privilege to the "owner of a copy" of a program (17 U.S.C. § 117(a)); whether a licence-versus-sale
recital in fact decides who counts as that owner is a question courts answer, not something § 117(a)
itself states, so this clause recommends the licence recital as a drafting choice consistent with the
rest of this contract's licence structure, without citing § 117(a) as the source of that effect. The
licensee's own § 117 position, where it applies, belongs in `references/clauses/licence-grant.md`, not
here.

## Must not

- No clause may promise a payout structure, revenue share, or premium placement that pays out
  against specific user uploads the trader can contractually control, where that payout is directly
  attributable to infringing activity: that risks the financial benefit that forfeits the § 512(c)
  and § 512(d) safe harbors (17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(1)(B), § 512(d)(2)).
- No clause may claim the § 512(c) safe harbor on the strength of publishing the designated agent's
  contact details on the service alone, or of filing those details with the Copyright Office alone;
  both steps are required (17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(2)).
- No clause may state that a counter notice restores material immediately or on a shorter timeline
  than ten business days, or that the trader may skip forwarding a valid counter notice to the
  original notifier (17 U.S.C. § 512(g)(2)(B), (C)).
- No clause may state that a service provider who knowingly and materially misrepresents that
  material is infringing, or that a takedown was a mistake, has no liability for that
  misrepresentation: § 512(f) makes that person liable for the resulting damages, costs, and fees
  (17 U.S.C. § 512(f)).
- No notice-and-takedown clause may describe "service provider" with one definition for every
  purpose: § 512(a) (transitory network communications) uses a narrower definition than the rest of
  § 512, which uses a broader one (17 U.S.C. § 512(k)(1)(A), (B)).
- No clause may claim ownership or an exclusive right in the software beyond what Art. 4(1) Directive
  2009/24/EC or § 69b, § 69c UrhG actually confer, or assert trader ownership of employee- or
  contractor-created software where the development agreement assigns the economic rights elsewhere
  (§ 69b Abs. 1, 2 UrhG).
- No clause may rely on a non-transferable or licensed-not-sold label, alone, to prevent resale
  of a software copy sold for a one-off fee with an unlimited-duration right of use once the Art.
  4(2) sale classification is met; that resale and transfer boundary sits in
  `references/clauses/licence-grant.md`, and this clause's ownership statement does not override it
  (Case C-128/11, UsedSoft GmbH v Oracle International Corp., paras. 49, 77, 82 to 83).

## Drafting notes

See conflicts.md entry C-14 for the resale and transfer boundary this clause's ownership statement
sits next to: once an EU or German software sale is classified under Art. 4(2) Directive 2009/24/EC
or § 69c Nr. 3 UrhG, the trader's reservation-of-rights wording here does not block the resale that
exhaustion permits, and the mechanics of that resale belong in the licence grant clause, not in this
one.

The DMCA module and the software-ownership module serve two different kinds of product and often sit
in the same contract without connecting to each other: a product can be a software licensor with no
hosting function at all, a hosting or user-generated-content platform with no software licence to
grant, or both at once. State each module only for the function the product actually performs, and do
not let the presence of one module imply the other applies.

Where the trader's intellectual property includes trademarks, logos, or other branding alongside
software, state that ownership in its own sentence rather than folding it into the software-copyright
restricted-act list: none of the software-copyright sources this clause works from
(`references/eu/software-directive.md`, `references/de/urhg-software.md`,
`references/us-federal/copyright.md`) state a rule for trademarks as such, so a trademark-ownership
sentence here rests on the general proposition that a contract may reserve rights the law does not
otherwise grant to the other party, not on a specific citation this clause can give. Trademark law
itself is an open point for counsel; these references do not cover it.

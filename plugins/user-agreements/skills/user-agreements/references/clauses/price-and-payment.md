# Price and payment

Law as of: 2026-10-20

## Purpose

This clause states the price the customer pays, what that price includes, how it is shown before
the customer commits, which payment methods are accepted, and what the trader may and may not add
to the price afterward. It covers the single-transaction price disclosure, payment-method fees,
extra charges beyond the agreed price, and a price shown to one customer that differs from what
another customer sees. It does not cover the auto-renewal price mechanics (the pre-billing notice,
the renewal-price consent or cancellation window), which sit in
`references/clauses/term-renewal-and-cancellation.md`, and it does not cover the general unilateral
right to change contract terms, which sits in `references/clauses/changes-to-terms.md`.

## Model wording

The total price for [product or service] is [price], including all taxes. [Where the contract is a
subscription or a fixed-rate continuing service: this is the price for each [billing period]. /
Where delivery is extra: delivery costs [amount or how it is calculated] and are shown separately
before you confirm your order.]

We accept payment by [payment methods]. [Where any method carries a fee: using [payment method]
costs an extra [amount], which reflects what it costs us to accept it.] [Germany: a customary,
reasonable, free payment option must remain available alongside any surcharged method.]

We will not charge you anything beyond the price above unless you have separately and expressly
agreed to it. We do not treat your failure to uncheck a box, or your silence, as that agreement.

[Where pricing can vary by customer: the price shown to you may reflect automated processing of
information about you, including your past behaviour on this site. Where this applies to your
order, we tell you so before you pay.]

## Modules

### EU consumers

State the total price inclusive of all taxes before the consumer is bound, or, where the exact
price cannot be calculated in advance, the method used to work it out; add every extra freight,
delivery or postal charge that applies, or say that such a charge may be payable if it cannot be
calculated in advance (Art. 6(1)(e) Directive 2011/83/EU). For a subscription, state the total
price for each billing period; for a fixed-rate contract running for an indefinite period or one
that renews automatically, state the total monthly price (Art. 6(1)(e) Directive 2011/83/EU). Where
the price was set, in whole or in part, by automated decision-making based on the consumer's
behaviour, including a profile built from earlier visits, disclose that fact before the consumer is
bound (Art. 6(1)(ea) Directive 2011/83/EU).

State, at the latest at the start of the ordering process, which payment methods are accepted (§ 312j
Abs. 1 BGB); see the Germany module for the equivalent, more detailed German duty. Do not charge the
consumer, for using a given payment method, a fee that exceeds what accepting that method actually
costs the trader (Art. 19 Directive 2011/83/EU); state this as a product rule rather than a drafting
promise alone, since a fee set above actual cost is unenforceable regardless of what the clause says.

Seek the consumer's express, separate agreement to any payment beyond the agreed price for the main
contractual obligation before the consumer is bound; a pre-ticked option the consumer has to
actively reject is never treated as that agreement, and a consumer who was charged this way can
claim the payment back with no need to show they even noticed the charge (Art. 22 Directive
2011/83/EU). Where the contract is concluded over the phone on a line the trader operates to deal
with an already-concluded contract, charge no more than the basic telephone rate on that line (Art.
21 Directive 2011/83/EU).

Treat a clause fixing the price only at delivery, or letting the trader raise the agreed price,
without a matching right for the consumer to cancel if the final price is too high against the
price at conclusion, as a suspect pattern under the Unfair Contract Terms Directive's indicative
list; a lawful price-indexation clause escapes this only where it explicitly describes the method by
which the price varies (references/eu/uctd.md, Annex point (l)). A price-variation mechanism of any
kind is never shielded from the ordinary unfairness test merely because it is transparent: the main
subject matter and price-adequacy exclusion reaches only the price itself, never a mechanism for
changing it (references/eu/uctd.md, Art. 4(2)).

### EU business customers

The Art. 19, 21 and 22 Directive 2011/83/EU duties above (payment-method fee cap, phone-line rate
cap, express consent to extra payments) are consumer protections and do not extend to a business
counterparty acting in that capacity (Art. 2(1) Directive 2011/83/EU defines the protected party as
a natural person acting outside trade, business, craft or profession). The Unfair Contract Terms
Directive's unfairness test likewise runs only between a seller or supplier and a consumer
(references/eu/uctd.md, Art. 2(b), (c)); a price-variation or extra-charge clause aimed at a business
customer is priced and negotiated on ordinary contract-law terms instead. Still state the total
price and payment terms in plain, intelligible language: the plain-language duty in Art. 5
(references/eu/uctd.md) applies to every written term, including ones the unfairness test itself
does not reach.

### Germany

Price information falls under the general e-commerce disclosure duty: make the price and the other
EGBGB Art. 246a information available clearly, comprehensibly and in a highlighted manner,
immediately before the order-submission button; a link to a separate page is not enough (§ 312j Abs.
2 BGB). State, no later than the start of the ordering process, whether delivery restrictions apply
and which payment means are accepted (§ 312j Abs. 1 BGB). Costs may be demanded from the consumer
only to the extent the trader informed them of those costs under EGBGB Art. 246a § 1 Abs. 1 Satz 1
Nr. 7 (§ 312e BGB).

An extra-payment agreement beyond the main price needs the trader's express agreement; in an
e-commerce checkout, such an agreement never becomes part of the contract where it was reached
through a pre-ticked default the consumer had to actively uncheck (§ 312a Abs. 3 BGB). A
payment-method surcharge clause is void where a customary, reasonable, free payment option exists
alongside the surcharged one, or where the agreed fee exceeds the trader's own cost of that
method; either voidness leaves the rest of the contract intact (§ 312a Abs. 4 BGB). A premium-rate
support-line clause is void where its fee exceeds the pure telecommunications cost of the call; the
consumer then owes the telecom provider only the ordinary usage fee, and the telecom provider
claims the difference from the trader instead (§ 312a Abs. 5, 6 BGB).

State plainly that the order button carries only PW-DE-1 or an equally unambiguous formulation,
with nothing else on it, and that the price and payment information sits directly before that
button rather than behind a separate terms link (§ 312j Abs. 2, 3 BGB; see
`references/clauses/acceptance-and-formation.md` for the button clause itself). This clause does not
restate the button wording; it states only that the price disclosure must sit where that button
sits.

### US

The references here do not cover a US federal or state duty on the single-transaction price
disclosure, payment-method fee, or extra-payment-consent points above; draft a US price and
payment clause against ordinary contract law and whatever sector-specific disclosure rule applies to
the product, and treat the US position on these three points as an open point for counsel until a
source is added (see Open points to raise). The automatic-renewal pricing duties that the references
do cover (the pre-billing price disclosure, the price-increase consent-or-cancellation-window
rule) belong to `references/clauses/term-renewal-and-cancellation.md`, not to this clause: New York
requires either the consumer's affirmative consent to a new price before charging it or a cancellation
window of at least 14 days with a pro rata refund (N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 527-a(1)(b-1)), and both New
York and California require the recurring charge and whether and how it may change to be disclosed
before consent is taken (N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 527-a(1)(a); Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17601(a)(2)(C),
§ 17602(a)(1)); this clause's single-transaction price wording is not drafted for that renewal
mechanic.

## Must not

- No clause may fix the price only at delivery, or reserve a right to raise the agreed price, without
  a matching consumer cancellation right, outside a transparent, explicitly described price-indexation
  mechanism (references/eu/uctd.md, Annex point (l)).
- No clause may claim a price-variation mechanism is exempt from the unfairness test merely because
  it is written in plain language; the price-adequacy exclusion never reaches the mechanism for
  changing the price (references/eu/uctd.md, Art. 4(2)).
- No clause may impose a payment-method surcharge where a free, customary, reasonable alternative
  exists, or set a surcharge above the trader's own cost of that method (§ 312a Abs. 4 BGB).
- No clause may let an extra payment beyond the main price arise from a pre-ticked default the
  consumer must actively reject (Art. 22 Directive 2011/83/EU; § 312a Abs. 3 BGB).
- No clause may bury the total price, the payment methods accepted, or any delivery-cost add-on
  behind a separate terms page reached only by a link, in place of showing them directly before the
  order-submission step (§ 312j Abs. 2, 3 BGB).
- No clause may charge more than the basic telephone rate for a line the trader operates to deal
  with an already-concluded contract (Art. 21 Directive 2011/83/EU).

## Drafting notes

See C-6 in `references/conflicts.md` for the module choice behind the price-variation paragraph in
the EU consumers module: a US-style clause reserving an unlimited, no-reason price change belongs
only in the US module, and the EU and German modules need the contract-stated reason, the described
method, and the consumer's termination right instead.

This clause states the single-transaction and per-billing-period price only. Where the contract
auto-renews, hand off to `references/clauses/term-renewal-and-cancellation.md` for the pre-billing
notice, the renewal-price consent or cancellation-window mechanics, and the US state-law duties that
attach to a price increase on renewal; do not duplicate those mechanics here.

Confirm the counterparty's status (consumer or business) before drafting, since the Art. 19, 21 and
22 Directive 2011/83/EU duties and the Unfair Contract Terms Directive's unfairness test both run
only between a trader and a consumer; see `references/eu/uctd.md` and `references/de/bgb-agb.md` for
the classification rule.

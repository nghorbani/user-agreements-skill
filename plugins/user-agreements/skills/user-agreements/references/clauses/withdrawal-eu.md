# Withdrawal (EU and Germany)

Law as of: 2026-10-20

## Purpose

This clause gives a consumer the right to withdraw from a distance or off-premises contract without a
reason, states the withdrawal period and how it starts, and sets out what happens once the consumer
withdraws. It applies wherever the trader sells to a consumer at a distance or away from its business
premises inside the EU, with Germany adding its own interface duties on top of the directive floor. It
does not apply to a sale the trader makes to a business customer, and it does not apply on its own terms
in the United States, where no comparable cooling-off right exists outside a narrow set of federal and
state rules on separate topics.

## Model wording

[Trader name] gives the consumer 14 days to withdraw from this contract, without giving any reason and
without any cost beyond what this clause states.

The withdrawal period runs for 14 days from [the day the consumer, or a third party named by the
consumer other than the carrier, takes physical possession of the goods / the day this contract is
concluded], depending on what the consumer bought. Where the order has several goods delivered on
different days, the period runs from receipt of the last good. Where the goods are delivered in several
lots or pieces, the period runs from receipt of the last lot or piece. Where the goods are delivered
regularly over a set period, the period runs from receipt of the first good.

To withdraw, the consumer tells [trader name] of the decision to withdraw by a clear statement, for
example a letter sent by post, a fax, or an email to [contact details]. The consumer may use the
withdrawal form at [link], but does not have to. Sending the withdrawal statement before the period ends
is enough to meet the deadline.

[Where the contract is concluded through an online interface: the consumer may also withdraw through the
withdrawal function at [link or location], available at any time during the withdrawal period. Using
this function needs the consumer to give or confirm their name, the details that identify the contract
or the part of it withdrawn from, and a way of reaching them to confirm the withdrawal. A second, separate
step then submits the declaration. Once that second step is taken, [trader name] sends an acknowledgment
without delay, by [channel], stating what was declared and when it was received.]

If the consumer withdraws, [trader name] refunds every payment received, including the standard delivery
cost, within 14 days of being told of the withdrawal. [Trader name] uses the same payment method the
consumer used to pay, unless the consumer agrees to a different method, and charges no fee for the
refund. Where the consumer chose a delivery method more expensive than the cheapest standard delivery
[trader name] offers, the extra cost stays with the consumer.

[For goods: trader name may hold back the refund until the goods are back, or until the consumer has
shown proof of having sent them back, whichever is earlier, unless trader name offered to collect them.
The consumer sends the goods back within 14 days of telling trader name of the withdrawal, at the
consumer's own cost unless trader name agreed to bear it, or unless the goods were delivered to the
consumer's home at the start of an off-premises contract and cannot normally go back by post, in which
case trader name collects them at its own cost. The consumer only owes a payment for any loss in the
goods' value that comes from handling them beyond what was needed to check their nature, characteristics
and how they work, and owes nothing at all for that loss if trader name failed to give the information
this clause describes.]

[For digital content not supplied on a tangible medium, or a digital service: if the consumer asked for
performance to start before the withdrawal period ends, the consumer pays a share of the agreed price for
what was supplied up to the point of withdrawal. The consumer owes nothing for that early-started
performance where trader name did not get the consumer's express request and prior acknowledgment for
the early start, or did not give the information on the withdrawal right and its costs that this clause
describes.]

The consumer can get back, free of charge and in a common, machine-readable format, any content the
consumer provided or created while using the product, except content that has no use outside using the
product, content tied solely to using the product, content mixed inseparably with other data, or content
created together with other users for their continued use.

## Modules

### EU consumers

The core wording above states the general distance and off-premises regime. Fill in the goods or the
service and digital-content paragraphs depending on what the contract covers; a mixed order keeps one
withdrawal period for the whole contract, set by its main subject matter, not a separate period per item
(Art. 9(2) Directive 2011/83/EU).

Where the trader concludes the contract through an online interface, add the bracketed withdrawal-function
paragraph and label the two controls it needs with PW-EU-2 and PW-EU-3; see
`references/prescribed-wording.md`. This applies on top of the letter-or-email withdrawal route above, not
instead of it (Art. 11a(1) Directive 2011/83/EU). The duty reaches a third-party platform the trader sells
through as much as the trader's own site: where the contract is concluded on such a platform, bind its
operator by contract to provide the withdrawal function there (Art. 11a(1) Directive 2011/83/EU).

Attach the complete model withdrawal information, correctly filled in for the contract type, as the
withdrawal-information section of the terms or the order confirmation; see PW-EU-4 (or PW-EU-9 for a
German-facing document) in `references/prescribed-wording.md` (Art. 6(1)(h) Directive 2011/83/EU). Offer
the model withdrawal form alongside it as something the consumer may use, never as the only valid way to
withdraw; see PW-EU-5 or PW-EU-10 (Art. 6(1)(h), Annex I(B) Directive 2011/83/EU).

Where this contract has an ancillary contract attached to it (for example an extended-warranty or
insurance add-on sold alongside the main purchase), state that withdrawing from the main contract
automatically ends the ancillary contract too, at no cost to the consumer beyond Art. 13(2) and 14, except
that a linked credit agreement follows its own Article 15 under the Consumer Credit Directive (2008/48/EC)
instead (Art. 15(1) Directive 2011/83/EU).

Where the trader omits the Art. 6(1)(h) withdrawal-right information, the 14-day period does not simply
stay open indefinitely: it extends to 12 months from the end of the original period, then expires 14 (or
30) days after the consumer is later given that information (Art. 10(1), (2) Directive 2011/83/EU). Germany
carries the same long-stop at § 356 Abs. 4 BGB; see the Germany module.

Name every withdrawal exception this contract actually relies on, drawn only from the closed list: goods
made to the consumer's specification or clearly personalised; sealed goods unsuitable for return for
health or hygiene reasons once unsealed; goods that mix inseparably with other items after delivery;
sealed audio, video or software once unsealed; a newspaper, periodical or magazine subscription outside a
subscription agreement for the regular delivery of such publications; digital content on a non-tangible
medium whose supply has already started with the consumer's express prior consent and acknowledgment of
losing the right; and the other closed-list cases a given contract may trigger (urgent requested repair,
goods or services tied to a specific date, perishable goods, a sealed health-or-hygiene case, alcoholic
beverages priced at conclusion but deliverable only after 30 days, with a price that depends on market
fluctuations the trader cannot control). State the exception by its own facts, never by its label alone
(Art. 16 Directive 2011/83/EU).

Where the contract is a distance financial-services contract under Art. 3(1b) Directive 2011/83/EU, this
clause's 14-day period and refund timing do not apply as drafted here: that contract type runs on its own
withdrawal regime instead. Use the Art. 16a(1) information list in place of Art. 6(1)(h), and give 14
calendar days to withdraw, extended to 30 days for a personal pension contract, running from conclusion or
from receipt of the Art. 16a information if later, unless a market-fluctuation, short-term-insurance or
fully-performed-by-request exception applies (Art. 16b(1), (2) Directive 2011/83/EU). This clause's model
wording is not drafted for that information list or that period, so treat a financial-services contract as
a separate drafting task built on `references/eu/crd.md`'s own financial-services subsection rather than on
the model wording above.

### EU business customers

Drop this clause for a contract with a business customer (Unternehmer acting in that capacity). The
Directive's withdrawal right is a consumer protection; it does not reach a trade or professional
counterparty (Art. 2(1) Directive 2011/83/EU defines the protected party as a natural person acting
outside their trade, business, craft or profession). A dual-purpose buyer whose trade purpose is not
predominant in the contract is treated as a consumer for this clause regardless of a business-sounding
company name on the order.

### Germany

Add the two labelled controls for the online withdrawal function, worded exactly as PW-DE-4 and PW-DE-5
give them, or an equally unambiguous equivalent, for any distance contract concluded through an online
interface (§ 356a Abs. 1 to 3 BGB). PW-DE-1 to PW-DE-3, the order-button and cancellation-button labels
under § 312j and § 312k BGB, belong to the order and cancellation flows, not this clause; see those
clauses' own files for them. Make the function permanently
available, reachable from every subpage, and distinguishable from ordinary footer links by colour,
contrast or position where it sits near them (§ 356a Abs. 1 Satz 3 BGB). Do not gate it behind a login or
an app download the contract itself did not require. Where the trader sells through a third-party platform
as well as its own site, both channels need the function; bind the platform operator to provide it by
contract (§ 356a Abs. 1 BGB).

Use the complete Muster-Widerrufsbelehrung, PW-DE-6, filled in for the contract type, as the statutory
safe harbor for the withdrawal-information duty; a non-compliant or incomplete version does not discharge
it (Art. 246a § 1 Abs. 2 EGBGB). Offer the Muster-Widerrufsformular, PW-DE-7, alongside it with the
trader's name, address and email already filled into its first line, but never as the only accepted
channel (§ 356 Abs. 1 BGB).

State the withdrawal period's start by the specific rule the contract type triggers rather than the
14-days-from-conclusion default: receipt of the goods, or of the last good, lot or first regular delivery,
for a goods purchase; conclusion, for water, gas or electricity supplied without a fixed volume or
quantity, for district heating, and for non-tangible digital content (§ 356 Abs. 2 BGB). In every case the
period does not start at all until the gating EGBGB information has actually been given (§ 356 Abs. 3
BGB). The same long-stop in the EU consumers module runs in parallel here: where the trader omitted the
withdrawal-right information, the period extends to 12 months from the end of the original period, then
expires 14 or 30 days after the consumer is later given that information (§ 356 Abs. 4 BGB).

Where a § 312g Abs. 2 BGB (Art. 16) exception applies, or an initially existing withdrawal right can lapse
early, disclose that fact and the circumstances to the consumer rather than staying silent about it (Art.
246a § 1 Abs. 3 EGBGB). This duty sits alongside, not instead of, naming the exception itself in the EU
consumers module.

Where the trader wants early full performance to end the withdrawal right, collect, before performance
starts, the consumer's express consent to the early start, that consent on a durable medium for an
off-premises contract, a separate confirmation that the consumer understands the right is lost, and, for
non-physical digital content, the additional § 312f confirmation (§ 356 Abs. 5 Nr. 2, Abs. 6 Nr. 2 BGB).
Missing any one of these leaves the right intact despite full delivery.

State the restitution deadline as 14 days after withdrawal for goods and ordinary services, extended to 30
days for a financial-service contract (§ 357 Abs. 1 BGB, § 357b Abs. 1 BGB). Never charge value
replacement for withdrawing from non-physical digital content; the statute rules it out without exception
(§ 357a Abs. 3 BGB).

### US

These reference files cover no US withdrawal or cooling-off right, so this clause carries no US module of
its own. Do not reuse the EU 14-day period, the withdrawal function, or the model form language for a
US-facing document on the strength of this clause; a US cancellation or refund right, where one applies,
rests on separate grounds this clause does not state. Treat any US right of cancellation as an open point
for the reference file that covers it.

## Must not

- No clause may waive, restrict, or design around the consumer's withdrawal right; a term that tries,
  directly or indirectly, does not bind the consumer (Art. 25 Directive 2011/83/EU; § 361 Abs. 2 BGB).
- No clause may demand a reason for withdrawal, require a stricter form than the statute allows, or start
  the period later than the statute fixes to the consumer's disadvantage (§ 355 Abs. 1, Abs. 3 BGB).
- No clause may claim an Art. 16 exception by its label alone without the underlying facts that trigger it,
  or invent an exception outside that closed list (Art. 16 Directive 2011/83/EU).
- No clause may shift the trader's return-shipment risk onto the consumer, or shorten the 14-day (or 30-day
  for financial services) refund deadline (§ 361 Abs. 2 BGB; § 357 Abs. 1 BGB, § 357b Abs. 1 BGB).
- No clause may charge value replacement for ordinary try-it-out handling of goods, or any value
  replacement at all for non-physical digital content (§ 357a Abs. 1, Abs. 3 BGB).
- No clause may substitute a different label for the two § 356a BGB controls unless the substitute is
  equally unambiguous, or require the consumer to log in, register, or supply unrelated data before using
  the function (§ 356a Abs. 1 to 3 BGB).
- No acknowledgment of receipt may suggest the withdrawal's legal validity has already been reviewed; it
  states only that the declaration, its content, date and time were received (§ 356a Abs. 4 BGB).
- No clause may claim the trader may freely choose to keep consumer-provided or consumer-created content
  after a digital-product contract ends, outside the four named categories this clause's model wording
  lists (§ 327p Abs. 2, 3 BGB; Art. 13(5) to (8) Directive 2011/83/EU).

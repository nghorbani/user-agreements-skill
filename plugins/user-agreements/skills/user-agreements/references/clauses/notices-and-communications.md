# Notices and communications

Law as of: 2026-10-20

## Purpose

This clause sets how the business and the user give each other the notices and records a contract
needs: a cancellation confirmation, a withdrawal acknowledgment, an order confirmation, an account
notice, a complaint. It states what form a notice can take, when an electronic one counts as given
and received, what a durable medium is, and who has to prove a notice was sent and seen. It also
draws the line between a notice the contract requires and a commercial email message, since the
business's electronic channel to the user carries both. It does not cover the sign-up or checkout
assent notice and order button, which the acceptance-and-formation clause carries, notice of a
change to these terms, which the changes-to-terms clause carries, or promotional email and its
opt-out mechanics, which the marketing-communications clause carries.

## Model wording

The business will send a notice required under this agreement to the email address on the user's
account, or through a message inside the service the user is signed in to use, unless this
agreement states a different channel for that notice. A notice to the business goes to
[support/legal contact address], or through the channel this agreement names for that kind of
notice.

A notice, record or signature is not denied legal effect only because it is in electronic form. The
business will keep a copy of a notice it is required to keep, in a form the user can later retrieve
and read without further help from the business, for as long as the law requires.

Where this agreement or the law requires the business to confirm something the user did (an order,
a cancellation, a withdrawal), the business will send that confirmation without undue delay, stating
what the user did, when, and what happens next. The business can show, on request, what notice it
sent a given user and when.

## Modules

### EU consumers

State that a notice or confirmation this agreement or the law requires is given on a durable medium:
something that lets the user store information addressed personally to them, for as long as its
purpose needs, unchanged (Art. 2(10) Directive 2011/83/EU). A bare link to a webpage is never a
durable medium on its own (Case C-49/11, Content Services); the business's own account area can be,
but only where the business cannot unilaterally change what is stored there and actively tells the
user, for example by a message to the user's own mailbox, that something new is there to read (Case
C-375/15, BAWAG). Do not use a channel that can delete a sent message from the user's device within
a short window for this purpose.

Confirm a distance or off-premises contract's content on a durable medium within a reasonable time,
at the latest on delivery or before the service starts, repeating anything not already given
durably before conclusion (Art. 8(7), Art. 7(1), (2) Directive 2011/83/EU). Where the user withdraws
through the online withdrawal function, acknowledge receipt on a durable medium without undue delay,
stating the declaration's content, date and time (Art. 11a(4) Directive 2011/83/EU); do the same
where the user instead uses an electronic fill-in-and-submit option for the statement or the model
form (Art. 11(3) Directive 2011/83/EU). The business carries the burden of proving it met an
information or notice duty under this agreement, so keep the record a dispute would need (Art. 6(9)
Directive 2011/83/EU).

Where the business calls the user to conclude or discuss the contract, the caller discloses its own
identity, who it is calling for if different, and the call's commercial purpose at the start of the
call (Art. 8(5) Directive 2011/83/EU).

### EU business customers

The durable-medium confirmation duties above do not apply at all where the counterparty is in fact
a business and not a consumer: the Directive's own consumer definition requires a natural person
(Recital 13 Directive 2011/83/EU), so these duties simply never attach to a genuine business
counterparty in the first place. A label alone calling the counterparty a business, or a blanket
tick-box the counterparty checks to that effect, is not self-enforcing and does not strip protection
from someone who is in fact a consumer, including in a dual-purpose transaction where the trade
purpose is not predominant in the overall context (Recital 13, Recital 17 Directive 2011/83/EU); nor
can a clause waive or restrict a right the Directive confers on a genuine consumer, directly or by a
design that circumvents it (Art. 25 Directive 2011/83/EU). Where the counterparty is in fact a
business, keep the confirmation and record-keeping wording only if another source requires it for
that relationship; this file does not state one.

### Germany

Where a statute requires Textform (text form) for a notice under this agreement, give a readable
declaration that names the person making it, on a durable medium as defined above; no signature is
needed (§ 126b BGB). This is the form a cancellation declaration made by proxy, and the power of
attorney to make it, both need (§ 312h BGB).

Confirm a cancellation made through the cancellation button immediately, electronically, in text
form, stating its content, timestamp and the resulting end date; a declaration made by activating
the confirmation button is presumed to reach the business immediately (§ 312k Abs. 4 BGB). Let the
user store their own cancellation declaration on a durable medium, timestamped, showing it was made
by activating the confirmation button (§ 312k Abs. 3 BGB).

Confirm an off-premises contract promptly, and a distance contract within a reasonable time, at the
latest on delivery or before the service starts, on paper or another durable medium with the user's
agreement, reproducing the contract content (§ 312f Abs. 1, 2 BGB). Keep records able to show, after
the fact, what information a given user was shown and when; the business carries the burden of
proving it met an information duty (§ 312m Abs. 2 BGB). Where the business calls the user to
conclude the contract, the caller discloses its identity and the call's commercial purpose at the
start of the call, and for a financial-service contract also says whether the call is or could be
recorded (§ 312a Abs. 1 BGB).

### US

A contract, signature, notice or other record is not denied legal effect, validity or
enforceability only because it is in electronic form, or because an electronic signature or
electronic record was used to create it (15 U.S.C. § 7001(a)(1), (2)). Where another statute
requires a notice, warning or disclosure to be posted, displayed or otherwise made conspicuous in a
particular place or way, that placement requirement stands alongside the electronic-form permission
this baseline gives; it is not replaced by it (15 U.S.C. § 7001(f)).

Where the law requires a notice to a user in writing and the business wants to give it
electronically instead, collect, before the user consents: a statement covering the paper option,
the withdrawal right and any conditions or fees on withdrawing consent, whether consent covers one
transaction or a category of notices, the procedure to withdraw consent and to update contact
details, the paper-copy procedure and its fee, and a hardware-and-software statement; then capture
the user's electronic consent in a way that demonstrates the user can access that electronic form
(15 U.S.C. § 7001(c)(1)(A), (B), (C)). Repeat the hardware-and-software statement and the
access-demonstration step if requirements later change in a way that creates a material risk the
user can no longer access a later notice (15 U.S.C. § 7001(c)(1)(D)). A user's withdrawal of consent
to electronic notices is prospective only and does not undo an electronic notice already given
before the withdrawal takes effect (15 U.S.C. § 7001(c)(4)).

Keep a notice or record the business is legally required to retain in a form that accurately
reflects it and stays accessible, for the required period, to everyone entitled to it, in a form
that can be accurately reproduced later; this baseline does not reach information whose sole purpose
is enabling transmission (15 U.S.C. § 7001(d)(1), (2)). A writing requirement can still be denied
effect if the electronic record, even though stored, is not in a form capable of being retained and
accurately reproduced later; being electronic is not the same as being retrievable (15 U.S.C. §
7001(e)). An automated step in sending or confirming a notice does not by itself deny it effect, so
long as the automated action is legally attributable to the person to be bound, for example because
it is tied to an authenticated session or a payment method the user controls (15 U.S.C. § 7001(h)).

Where another statute requires a signature or record to be notarized, acknowledged, verified or
made under oath, an electronic signature of the person authorized to notarize or acknowledge it
satisfies that requirement, together with whatever other information that statute requires to
accompany it (15 U.S.C. § 7001(g)).

Sort an outbound message to a user as a notice this agreement or the law requires, or as a
commercial email message, before sending it, since the two carry different duties. A message whose
primary purpose is completing or confirming a transaction the user already agreed to, giving
warranty, recall, safety or security information about a product or service the user has, giving
notice of a change in the terms of, or the user's status under, an ongoing relationship such as a
subscription or account, or delivering something the user is entitled to under a prior transaction,
is a transactional or relationship message and carries none of the commercial-email duties below
(15 U.S.C. § 7702(17)(A)(i) to (v)). Including a reference to the business or a link to its site does
not by itself turn a notice into a commercial message; the test looks at the message's whole content
and the circumstances (15 U.S.C. § 7702(2)(D)).

Where a message's primary purpose is instead the commercial advertisement or promotion of a product
or service (15 U.S.C. § 7702(2)(A)), it carries a working return address or internet-based opt-out
mechanism, clearly and conspicuously displayed, that stays able to receive an opt-out request for at
least 30 days after the message was sent (15 U.S.C. § 7704(a)(3)(A)); clear and conspicuous
identification that it is an advertisement or solicitation; and the business's valid physical postal
address (15 U.S.C. § 7704(a)(5)(A)(i) to (iii)). The identification duty alone drops out where the
recipient already gave prior affirmative consent to the message; the opt-out notice and the postal
address still apply (15 U.S.C. § 7704(a)(5)(B)). "Affirmative consent" means the recipient expressly
agreed to receive the message, either answering a clear and conspicuous consent request or acting on
their own initiative, and, where the business is not the party the recipient originally consented
to, the recipient got clear and conspicuous notice, at the time of consenting, that the address
could be passed on for commercial email (15 U.S.C. § 7702(1)(A), (B)).

This baseline displaces a state statute that expressly regulates commercial email, apart from that
state's own anti-falsity or anti-deception rules, which stand (15 U.S.C. § 7707(b)(1), (2)(A)); it
does not displace state contract, trespass or tort law (15 U.S.C. § 7707(b)(2)(B)).

## Must not

- No notice or record may be denied effect in a clause only because it is electronic (15 U.S.C.
  § 7001(a)(1), (2)); nor may a clause claim this baseline covers a will, a codicil, a testamentary
  trust, a state family-law matter, most of the Uniform Commercial Code, a court order or official
  document, a utility cancellation notice, a default, foreclosure or eviction notice on a primary
  residence, a health or life insurance cancellation notice, a product recall or safety-failure
  notice, or a hazardous-materials transport document, without checking the law that actually
  governs that record's form (15 U.S.C. § 7003(a), (b)). Separately, this baseline never forces
  anyone to accept an electronic record or signature in the first place (15 U.S.C. § 7001(b)(2)).
- No clause may rely on a messaging or account channel the business can unilaterally alter, or from
  which a sent message can be deleted within a short window, as the durable medium for a
  confirmation or acknowledgment an EU consumer is owed (Art. 2(10) Directive 2011/83/EU; Case
  C-49/11, Content Services).
- No clause may substitute a mere link for the durable-medium copy or confirmation a distance or
  off-premises contract requires (§ 312f BGB; Art. 7(1), (2), Art. 8(7) Directive 2011/83/EU).
- No commercial email message may omit the opt-out mechanism, the advertisement identification, or
  the business's postal address on the strength of the recipient being a customer; only the
  identification duty, not the other two, drops out on prior affirmative consent (15 U.S.C. §
  7704(a)(5)(A), (B)).
- No clause may claim the business's own good-faith label for a message as a service notice
  controls whether it is in fact transactional or commercial; the primary-purpose test
  looks at the message's content and circumstances, not its label (15 U.S.C. § 7702(2)(D),
  (17)(A)).
- No clause may claim a message sent to a user after they already completed an action, standing
  alone, supplies a notice the law required to be given before or at the moment of that action; where
  a sibling clause (acceptance-and-formation) fixes a moment a notice must be given, a later message
  does not cure a missed one.

## Drafting notes

No conflicts.md entry exists yet for this clause; add one once that file is written. A US-drafted
notices clause typically names a single channel (email to the address on file) and says nothing
about durable-medium form or a burden of proof; the EU and German modules add both, since the
business, not the user, has to prove a notice was given.

Where the service runs a cancellation button, a withdrawal function, or an order-confirmation step
covered by another clause file (term-renewal-and-cancellation, withdrawal-eu, acceptance-and-
formation), that clause's own confirmation wording controls for that specific notice; this clause
is the default for every other notice the agreement or the law requires and has no more specific
clause covering it.

This clause's own confirmation duties (the § 312k Abs. 3, 4 BGB cancellation-receipt confirmation
and the Art. 11a(4) Directive 2011/83/EU withdrawal-acknowledgment duty) interlock with the
prescribed labels on the buttons that trigger them, even though the button mechanics themselves sit
in term-renewal-and-cancellation and withdrawal-eu: zahlungspflichtig bestellen (PW-DE-1), Verträge
hier kündigen (PW-DE-2), jetzt kündigen (PW-DE-3), Vertrag widerrufen (PW-EU-2, or PW-EU-7 for a
German-facing document), Widerruf bestätigen (PW-EU-3, or PW-EU-8 for a German-facing document);
see references/prescribed-wording.md for the full set.
